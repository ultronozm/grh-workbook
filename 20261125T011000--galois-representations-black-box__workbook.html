§32.1. Galois representations over CM fields Tag 22WR

What Galois representation does an automorphic representation determine, and at which places can the two be compared?

In this chapter \(F\) is any CM field (not the field \(E\) of Chapter 36), with maximal totally real subfield \(F^+\) and complex conjugation \(x \mapsto \bar x\); \(\ell\) is a prime and \(\iota \colon \mathbb{C}\cong \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) a ring isomorphism ([BLGGT] use its inverse). Let \(\epsilon_\ell\) be the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character. A cuspidal automorphic representation \(\pi\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_F)\) is polarizable if \(\pi^c \cong \pi^\vee \otimes (\chi \circ N_{F/F^+} \circ \det)\) for a continuous character \(\chi \colon \mathbb{A}_{F^+}^\times/(F^+)^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times\) with \(\chi_v(-1) = (-1)^a\) at every real place \(v\), and regular algebraic if \(\pi_\infty\) has the infinitesimal character of an irreducible algebraic representation of \(\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_a\); see [BLGGT]; the pair \((\pi, \chi)\) is then called polarized. For a finite place \(w\) with residue field of order \(q_w\) and uniformizer \(\varpi_w\), an irreducible representation \(\pi_w\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_a(F_w)\) is unramified if it has a nonzero vector fixed by \(\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathcal{O}_{F_w})\); it is then a subquotient of the normalized induction of the character \(b \mapsto \prod_{i=1}^a \chi_i(b_{ii})\) of the upper triangular Borel subgroup, where the \(\chi_i\) are unramified characters of \(F_w^\times\), and its Satake parameters are \(\chi_1(\varpi_w), \dots, \chi_a(\varpi_w)\).

Tag 22WS

Theorem 32.1.1. Let \((\pi, \chi)\) be a regular algebraic polarized cuspidal pair as above. There is a continuous semisimple \(r = r_{\ell,\iota}(\pi) \colon G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) such that: (i) \(r^c \cong r^\vee \otimes \epsilon_\ell^{1-a} r_{\ell,\iota}(\chi)|_{G_F}\); (ii) at every finite place \(w \nmid \ell\) at which \(\pi_w\) is unramified, \(r\) is unramified, and for a geometric Frobenius \(\mathrm{Frob}_w\) the eigenvalues of \(r(\mathrm{Frob}_w)\) are \(\iota(q_w^{(a-1)/2}\alpha_1), \dots, \iota(q_w^{(a-1)/2}\alpha_a)\), where \(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_a\) are the Satake parameters of \(\pi_w\); (iii) for every \(w \mid \ell\), \(r|_{G_{F_w}}\) is potentially semistable, crystalline if \(\pi_w\) is unramified, with Hodge–Tate weights determined by \(\pi_\infty\).

The construction uses the cohomology of unitary Shimura varieties and, in the generality treated by Chenevier and Harris, also \(p\)-adic families [Cl; HT; Shi; CH]. At the ramified places \(w \nmid \ell\) the published theorem gives more: the Frobenius-semisimplified Weil–Deligne representation of \(r|_{G_{F_w}}\) corresponds, after applying \(\iota^{-1}\), to \(\pi_w \otimes \lvert\det\rvert_w^{(1-a)/2}\) under the local Langlands correspondence; in this generality this is [BLGGT, Thm. 2.1.1], incorporating results of Taylor–Yoshida and Caraiani [TY; Car1]. For part (iii) see also [BLGGT, Thm. 2.1.1] and the work cited there, including [Car2]. Section 32.4 states the theorem on Galois representations of eigenforms that the proof establishes for definite unitary groups.

The smallest case is \(a = 1\) over an imaginary quadratic field \(K\), where the unitary group is \(U(1) := \ker(N \colon \mathrm{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m)\).

§32.2. The Hecke character of \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) Tag 22WW

What is the representation of Theorem 32.1.1 when \(a = 1\), and how does it compare with the curve \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\)? Let \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(\mathfrak{f} = (2 + 2\mathrm{i})\).

Tag 22WX

Problem 32.2.1. Every ideal \(\mathfrak{a}\) of \(\mathcal{O}\) prime to \(1 + \mathrm{i}\) has exactly one generator \(\alpha \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathfrak{f}}\). Let \(\psi(\mathfrak{a}) := \alpha\). Then \(\psi\) is multiplicative, \(\psi(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \overline{\psi(\mathfrak{a})}\), and \(\psi(\mathfrak{a})\psi(\mathfrak{a}^c) = N\mathfrak{a}\).

For \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) and \(\mathfrak{p} \mid p\), one has \(p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = \psi(\mathfrak{p}) + \psi(\mathfrak{p}^c)\) [IR, Ch. 18, §4].

Tag 22WY

Problem 32.2.2. \(\psi((1 + 2\mathrm{i})) = -1 - 2\mathrm{i}\) and \(\psi((3 + 2\mathrm{i})) = 3 + 2\mathrm{i}\), and \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_5) = \#E(\mathbb{F}_{13}) = 8\).

Tag 22WZ

Problem 32.2.3. Let \(n \ge 1\). If ideals \(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}\) prime to \(2\ell\) have the same class in the ray class group modulo \(\mathfrak{f}\ell^n\), then \(\psi(\mathfrak{a}) \equiv \psi(\mathfrak{b}) \pmod{\ell^n\mathcal{O}}\).

Combining Problem 32.2.3 with Artin reciprocity for the ray class fields modulo \(\mathfrak{f}\ell^n\) gives the following.

Tag 22X2

Theorem 32.2.1. There is a continuous character \(\psi_\ell \colon G_K \to (\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell)^\times\), unramified outside \(2\ell\), with \(\psi_\ell(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{p}) = \psi(\mathfrak{p})\) for every prime \(\mathfrak{p} \nmid 2\ell\), where \(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{p}\) is an arithmetic Frobenius.

By Problem 32.2.1, \(\psi\psi^c\) is the norm. For a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-algebra map \(\tau \colon \mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), the character \(\tau \circ \psi_\ell\) takes a geometric Frobenius at \(\mathfrak{p} \nmid 2\ell\) to \(\tau(\psi(\mathfrak{p}))^{-1}\); in Theorem 32.1.1(ii) the corresponding unramified local character takes \(\varpi_\mathfrak{p}\) to \(\iota^{-1}(\tau(\psi(\mathfrak{p}))^{-1})\). At the places not above \(\ell\) the theorem is then class field theory; the next problem looks at the places above \(\ell\).

Problem 32.2.4. So \(\psi_\lambda\) has Hodge–Tate weights \(1\) and \(0\) at the two places above \(\ell\) (in the convention where \(\epsilon_\ell\) has weight \(1\)), and the polarization appears as \(\mathrm{HT}_{\tau c} = 1 - \mathrm{HT}_\tau\).

The automorphism \([\mathrm{i}](x, y) = (-x, \mathrm{i}y)\) of \(E\) is defined over \(K\), so \(V_\ell(E) := T_\ell(E) \otimes \mathbb{Q}\) is free of rank one over \(K \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell\), and \(G_K\) acts on it through a character \(G_K \to (K \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell)^\times\).

Since \(E\) has good reduction at \(\mathfrak{p} \nmid 2\), \(V_\ell(E)\) is unramified at \(\mathfrak{p} \nmid 2\ell\) [Si1], and an element \(\beta \in \mathcal{O}\) acts on \(V_\ell(E)\) through \([\beta]\).

Theorem 32.2.2. The character of \(G_K\) on \(V_\ell(E)\) is \(\psi_\ell\).

Problem 32.2.6. Let \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G_K \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^\times\) be \(\psi_\ell\) followed by the two \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-algebra homomorphisms \(\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\). Then \(\psi_2 = \psi_1^c\), \(V_\ell(E) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\cong \operatorname{Ind}_{G_K}^{G_\mathbb{Q}} \psi_1\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p \mid V_\ell(E)) = 0\) for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(p \ne \ell\).

Over \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), the restriction of \(V_\ell(E) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) to \(G_K\) is \(\psi_1 \oplus \psi_1^c\), which is reducible. So the weight-two form of \(E\), base changed to \(K\), is not cuspidal, and it enters Theorem 32.1.1 only through \(\psi\), with \(a = 1\). For a cuspidal example with \(a = 2\) one needs a curve without complex multiplication by \(K\). We take \(X_0(11)\) and do the local calculations over \(\mathbb{Q}\). Its form has no complex multiplication, hence no quadratic self-twist, so its base change to \(K\) is cuspidal [AC, Ch. 3]. At a split prime of \(K/\mathbb{Q}\), the local calculation over \(\mathbb{Q}\) applies at either place of \(K\) above that prime.

§32.3. Eichler–Shimura for \(X_0(11)\) Tag 22X7

How does one get Frobenius eigenvalues out of the cohomology of a Shimura variety, and what does each method give? Let \[f = q \prod_{n \ge 1} (1 - q^n)^2 (1 - q^{11n})^2 = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n ,\] the normalized eigenform spanning \(S_2(\Gamma_0(11))\) [DS]. The curve \(X_0(11)\) has genus one and the model \(E \colon y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10x - 20\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), with good reduction away from \(11\) (LMFDB, curve 11.a2, https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/11/a/2). For \(p \ne 11\), the Hecke operator \(T_p\) acts on \(E = J_0(11)\) as \([a_p]\), and by the Eichler–Shimura relation [DS, Thm. 8.7.2], [Sh, Thm. 7.9], the reduction of \(T_p\) modulo \(p\) is \(F + V\), where \(F\) is the Frobenius endomorphism and \(FV = VF = p\).

Tag 22XA

Problem 32.3.3. Let \(\Phi \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) satisfy \(\Phi^2 - a\Phi + p = 0\) with \(a \in \mathbb{Z}\), and let \(\det\Phi = p\). Then the characteristic polynomial of \(\Phi\) is \(X^2 - aX + p\). Hence, taking \(\ell \ne p\), \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1 - a_p\) for \(p \ne 11\).

The relation of Problem 32.3.2 kills Frobenius; it does not give the multiplicities of its roots. In dimension two the determinant supplies them. In dimension three, \(\mathrm{diag}(\alpha, \alpha, \beta)\) and \(\mathrm{diag}(\alpha, \beta, \beta)\) are killed by the same quadratic polynomial. In the cohomological construction, counts of points give traces of powers of Frobenius, following Langlands and Kottwitz [Ko]. The simplest instance is the trace formula for \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p^k})\) stated before Problem 32.3.3.

For \(p \notin \{11, \ell\}\), the numbers \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p)\) and \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})\) determine the characteristic polynomial of the arithmetic Frobenius on \(V_\ell(E)\); for \(p = 2\) both equal \(5\).

By Tate’s uniformization [Si2, Ch. V], \(E\) is uniformized over \(\mathbb{Q}_{11}\) by \(\mathbb{G}_m/q^\mathbb{Z}\) for some nonzero \(q \in 11\mathbb{Z}_{11}\), and the exact sequence \(0 \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1) \to T_\ell(E) \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to 0\) has extension class given by the Kummer class of \(q\).

§32.4. Hecke polynomials on a definite unitary group Tag 237V

The input to Theorem 32.1.1 is a representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_F)\). Theorem 32.4.1 starts with forms on the unitary group of the standard definite hermitian space. What does an eigenform there determine at a split place, and in what sense is it compared with Frobenius?

Let \(F\) be a CM field, \(n \ge 1\), and \[G_f := \{ g \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{A}_{F,f}) : \bar g^{T} g = 1 \}, \qquad \mathcal{K}_0 := G_f \cap \mathrm{GL}_n(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_F).\] For a finite place \(w\) of \(F\), let \(q_w\) be the order of its residue field, \(\varpi_w\) a uniformizer of \(F_w\), and \(\mathcal{K}_w\) the group of \(k \in \mathcal{K}_0\) whose components at all places other than \(w, \bar w\) are \(1\). Let \(w\) be split, \(\bar w \ne w\). For \(a \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F_w)\) let \(b_w(a) \in G_f\) have component \(a\) at \(w\), \((\bar a^T)^{-1}\) at \(\bar w\) and \(1\) elsewhere. For \(j \ge 0\) let \[D_j := \{ a \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F_w) : a \text{ and } \varpi_w a^{-1} \text{ have entries in } \mathcal{O}_w,\ \operatorname{ord}_w \det a = j \} .\] For a function \(\phi\) on \(G_f\) fixed by \(\mathcal{K}_w\) under right translation, the Hecke operator is \((T_{w,j}\phi)(g) := \sum_{a} \phi(g\, b_w(a))\), the sum over \(a\) in a set of representatives of \(D_j/\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_w)\). For \(q, N \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(t \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}\) put \[H(q, N, t) := \sum_{j=0}^{N} (-1)^j q^{\binom{j}{2}}\, t(j)\, X^{N-j} .\] A function \(\phi\) on \(G_f\) with values in a finite-dimensional complex vector space has Hecke polynomial \(Q \in \mathbb{C}[X]\) at the split place \(w\) if \(\phi\) is fixed by \(\mathcal{K}_w\) and there is \(t \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(Q = H(q_w, n, t)\) and \(T_{w,j}\phi = t(j)\phi\) for every \(j \in \mathbb{N}\).

Problem 32.4.7. Every character \(\Gamma \to k^\times\) is trace-nondegenerate. The homomorphism \(r \colon \mathbb{Z}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(k)\), \(m \mapsto \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & m \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), is not trace-nondegenerate.

The statement used in the proof.

Fix \(\ell\) and \(\iota\) as in Section 32.1, an algebraic closure \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\), and a CM field \(F\) with an embedding \(F \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}\) and a CM type \(\Phi = (\Phi_v)_{v \mid \infty}\), an embedding \(\Phi_v \colon F \to \mathbb{C}\) inducing each infinite place \(v\). Let \(G_F = \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F)\). Assume \(F\) contains a root of unity of exact order \(5040\). Weil data on such a field consist of an element \(\jmath \in F\) with \(\bar\jmath = -\jmath\), \(\operatorname{Im}\Phi_v(\jmath) > 0\) for every \(v \mid \infty\), and \((1 - \jmath^{-1})/2\) integral; a finite idele \(\alpha\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_w(\jmath\alpha\bar\alpha) = -\operatorname{ord}_w(\mathfrak{D}_{F/\mathbb{Q}})\) at every finite \(w\), \(\mathfrak{D}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\) the different; and a continuous character \(\upsilon\) of \(\mathbb{A}_F^\times/F^\times\) with values of absolute value \(1\), such that \(\upsilon(x) = \prod_{v \mid \infty} x_v/\lvert x_v\rvert\) whenever the finite part of \(x\) lies in \(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_F^\times\) (with \(x_v \in \mathbb{C}^\times\) through \(\Phi_v\)), \(\upsilon(\bar x) = \upsilon(x)^{-1}\) for all \(x\), and \(\upsilon(\varpi_w) = -1\) at every \(w\) with \(\bar w = w\) (\(\varpi_w\) placed at \(w\), \(1\) elsewhere).

In this chapter a weight \(\kappa = (c, e)\) is the weight of a form (not the weight of a place of Chapter 36): functions \(c_v \colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) and integers \(e_v\), for \(v \mid \infty\). Let \(W_\kappa\) be the tensor product over \(v\) of the representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})\) with \(c_v(h)\) columns of height \(h\), \(0 \le h < n\), twisted by \(\det^{e_v}\), written in coordinates through Schur matrices in chosen bases of tableaux, and let \(U(F^+) := \{\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_n(F) : \bar\gamma^T\gamma = 1\}\) act on it through the complex conjugates of the \(\Phi_v(\gamma)\). A form of weight \(\kappa\) is a function \(f \colon G_f \to W_\kappa\) whose stabilizer under right translation contains a neighborhood of the identity and which satisfies \(f(\gamma g) = \gamma \cdot f(g)\) for \(\gamma \in U(F^+)\), embedded diagonally, and \(g \in G_f\). For a family \(P = (P_v)\) of polynomials in \(\mathbb{C}[X]\) indexed by the finite places of \(F\), \(f\) is an eigenform for \(P\) if \(f \ne 0\) and, for all but finitely many finite places \(v\), if \(q_v\) is prime then \(v\) is split and \(f\) has Hecke polynomial \(P_v\) at \(v\).

We define a Frobenius datum \((v, A, s)\) to consist of a finite place \(v\) of \(F\) with \(q_v\) prime, a valuation ring \(A\) of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\) lying over \(v\) (an \(x \in \mathcal{O}_F\) is in the maximal ideal of \(A\) exactly when \(x \in v\)), and \(s \in G_F\) such that \(s^{-1}\) stabilizes \(A\) and induces \(x \mapsto x^{q_v}\) on its residue field. A set \(Y\) of Frobenius data contains most Frobenius data if each nonempty member \(\mathcal{U}\) of the topology of \(G_F\) contains a nonempty member \(\mathcal{U}'\) of that topology such that, for all but finitely many \(v\), \(Y\) contains every datum \((v, A, s)\) with \(s \in \mathcal{U}'\). A homomorphism \(r \colon G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) is attached to \(P\) if it is continuous and trace-nondegenerate, and for every real \(\eta > 0\) the set of Frobenius data \((v, A, s)\) for which every coefficient of \(\det(X - r(s)) - \iota P_v\) has \(\ell\)-adic absolute value at most \(\eta\) contains most Frobenius data; here \(\iota P_v\) is \(P_v\) with \(\iota\) applied to its coefficients.

Tag 2385

Theorem 32.4.1. Let \(F\) have Weil data, let \(n \ge 1\), and let \(w\) be a finite place of \(F\) with \(w \nmid \ell\). For every family \(P\), every weight \(\kappa\) and every form \(f\) of weight \(\kappa\) that is an eigenform for \(P\):

  1. some \(r \colon G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) is attached to \(P\);

  2. for every \(r\) attached to \(P\), if \(f\) is fixed by \(\mathcal{K}_w\), then for every valuation ring \(A\) of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\) over \(w\):

    1. \(r\) is trivial on the inertia group of \(A\);

    2. if \(w\) is split, then for every \(Q \in \mathbb{C}[X]\) such that \(f\) has Hecke polynomial \(Q\) at \(w\), and every \(s \in G_F\) such that \(s^{-1}\) stabilizes \(A\) and induces \(x \mapsto x^{q_w}\) on its residue field, \((\iota Q)(r(s)) = 0\).

Used in: Problem 32.4.8; Chapter 35: Theorem 35.1.1

The full theorem gives (a) for every finite place \(w\). At \(w \mid \ell\), if \(f\) is fixed by \(\mathcal{K}_w\), then for each attached \(r\), each \(A\) over \(w\), and every local datum at \(A\), the restriction of \(r\) to the decomposition group of \(A\) is crystalline and has the labelled Hodge–Tate jumps determined by \(\kappa\) in rank \(n\). If \(w\) is also split, then for every Hecke polynomial \(Q\) of \(f\) at \(w\), the polynomial \(\iota Q\) annihilates the crystalline Frobenius operator for each geometric Frobenius lift at \(A\) and each corresponding Weil pair along that datum. Theorem 35.1.1 gives the local data, jump formula and Frobenius operator for this comparison. In (b)(ii), \(\iota Q\) kills \(r(s)\). This alone does not determine the multiplicities in the characteristic polynomial (compare \(\mathrm{diag}(\alpha,\alpha,\beta)\) in Section 32.3).

In the Lean formalization.

Theorem 32.4.1 is the case \(w \nmid \ell\) of GaloisRep.sphericalCompat_std of the Lean proof [GL] (names omit the namespace New), with conclusion GaloisRep.SphericalCompat; its local conclusion at each \(A\) over \(w\) is GaloisRep.IsCompatibleAt, which at \(w \nmid \ell\) reduces to (i) and (ii). The Lean proof obtains the theorem by lifting \(f\) along two characters to rank \(2n+1\), attaching a representation to each lift, twisting each, and cancelling the \(n+1\) extra characters on each side.

Problem 32.4.9. Let \(F\) have Weil data, let \(f\) be a form that is an eigenform for \(P\), and let \(r\) be attached to \(P\). Then the set of Frobenius data \((v, A, s)\) with \((\iota P_v)(r(s)) = 0\) contains most Frobenius data.

Thus \((\iota P_v)(r(s)) = 0\) for most Frobenius data \((v, A, s)\): the Hecke polynomial kills Frobenius. This is the step that the last part, from Chapter 36 on, takes from this chapter.
