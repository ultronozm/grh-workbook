Prerequisites.

Chapter 17, Pairings on a formal group of height two: Problems 17.3.1 and 17.3.3, and Section 17.5 entire; Problem 17.4.4. Legendre’s formula \(\operatorname{val}(n!) = (n - s_p(n))/(p-1)\), with \(s_p(n)\) the sum of the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\). Determinants over a commutative ring; the field \(\mathbb{C}_p\), its ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\), and the valuation \(\operatorname{val}\) with \(\operatorname{val}(p) = 1\) (Chapter 37).

In Chapter 57 the bound for one lift, the first part of input (iii) of Section 57.3 (Theorem 59.4.4(a)), rests on a bound for the jets of a series in the normal form along products of minors, stated in Theorem 17.5.1. For the marks at exceptional primes \(p < x_1\) (Section 57.4) the proof [GL] needs it in a stronger form, in which the columns of height one at some places are removed from the prediction and paid for by powers of \(p\) on the rows. This sheet proves the bound for two places and states the general count with charges. How much of the credit \(\gamma_p n\) of a jet of order \(n\), from the factorials and the rescaling by \(\varpi\), survives the couplings between a place and its partners?

Throughout, \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) is a prime, \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), \(p\) is a prime with \(\chi_K(p) = (\frac{p}{d}) \ne 1\), and \(c_p\), \(I_p\), \(I_p^\circ\), \(\rho(d,p)\) are as in Section 17.5: \(c_p = 1/2\) if \(p = d\) and \(1\) otherwise, \(I_p^\circ\) is the set of \(e \ge 0\) with \(p = d\), or \(\chi_K(p) = -1\) and \(e\) odd, \(I_p = I_p^\circ \smallsetminus \{0\}\), and \(\rho(d,p) = c_p/(p^2 - 1)\) for \(p\) inert, \(c_p/(p-1)\) for \(p = d\). As in Chapter 57, \(\gamma_p = c_p\sum_{e \in I_p} p^{-e}\); Problem 58.1.1 shows \(\gamma_p = 1/(p-1) - \rho(d,p)\). Let \(\pi_p := p\) if \(p \ne d\), and \(\pi_p := \eta(\sqrt{-d})\) if \(p = d\), for a fixed ring map \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\); so \(\operatorname{val}\pi_p = c_p\). Integral series and jets \((Q(\partial)G)(0)\) are as in Section 17.5.

§58.1. Two places and one row

Problem 58.1.1. \(\gamma_p = 1/(p-1) - \rho(d,p)\); it equals \(p/(p^2 - 1)\) if \(\chi_K(p) = -1\), and \(1/(2d - 2)\) if \(p = d\). If \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\) has \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = \rho(d,p)\), then \(\operatorname{val}(\varpi^{p+1}\pi_p/p) = 1/(p-1)\), both for \(p\) inert and for \(p = d\). [Check: \(\gamma_3 = 3/8\) at an inert prime \(3\).]

Used in: Chapter 57; Chapter 59; Chapter 60; Chapter 61

For the rest of this section \(\chi_K(p) = -1\), so that \(c_p = 1\), \(I_p^\circ = I_p\) is the set of odd \(e \ge 1\), and \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = \rho := 1/(p^2-1)\). The two-place model has two places \(1, 2\), one row and one input coordinate, hence two variables \(z_1, z_2\); the partner of each place, for every \(e\), is the other place. So the couplings are \(\Gamma_e = z_1^{p^e} z_2 + z_2^{p^e} z_1\), and \(F \in \mathbb{C}_p[[z_1, z_2]]\) has the normal form for \((I_p, \varpi)\) if \[F(\varpi z_1, \varpi z_2) = W(z_1, z_2)\, \exp\Bigl(\frac{\varpi^{p+1}}{p}\, \mathcal E(z_1, z_2)\Bigr),\] with \(W\) integral and \(\mathcal E\) a sum of the powers \(\Gamma_e^{p^j}\) (\(e \in I_p\), \(j \ge 0\)) with coefficients in \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). In this sheet \(a\) and \(w\) are the orders of a jet \(\partial_1^a \partial_2^w\), with \(\partial_i := \partial/\partial z_i\).

Problem 58.1.2. Let \(p = 3\). The series \(F_0 := \exp\bigl((z_1^3 z_2 + z_1 z_2^3)/3\bigr)\) has the normal form with \(W = 1\) and \(\mathcal E = \Gamma_1\). Its jets at \((a, w) = (3k, k)\) are \((3k)!/3^k\), that is \(2, 80, 13440\) for \(k = 1, 2, 3\), of valuations \(0, 0, 1\).

The credit comes from the factorials.

Problem 58.1.3. For integers \(\nu_1, \dots, \nu_q \ge 0\) with \(\nu_1 + \dots + \nu_q = n\), and \(\nu! := \nu_1! \cdots \nu_q!\), \[\operatorname{val}(\nu!) \ \ge\ \frac{n}{p-1} - q\bigl(1 + \lfloor \log_p n \rfloor\bigr),\] with \(\lfloor \log_p 0 \rfloor := 0\). [Check: \(p = 3\), \(\nu = (27, 1)\): \(13 \ge 14 - 8\).]

Used in: Problem 58.1.4

Problem 58.1.4. Let \(G\) be a power series over \(\mathbb{C}_p\) in \(q\) variables whose coefficients of degree \(n\) are integral, and let \(Q\) be a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(n\) with coefficients in \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). Then \(\operatorname{val}\bigl((Q(\partial)G)(0)\bigr)\) is at least the bound of Problem 58.1.3.

The debit comes from the couplings. One differentiates along scaled directions.

Problem 58.1.5. For \(y_1, y_2 \in \mathbb{C}_p\) and \(G(u_1, u_2) := F(\varpi y_1 u_1, \varpi y_2 u_2)\), \((\partial_{u_1}^a \partial_{u_2}^w G)(0) = \varpi^{a+w} y_1^a y_2^w\, (\partial_1^a \partial_2^w F)(0) .\)

Problem 58.1.6. Let \(F\) have the normal form, \(n \ge 0\), and \(y_1, y_2 \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\) with \(y_1^{p^e} y_2 \equiv y_2^{p^e} y_1 \equiv 0 \pmod p\) for every \(e \in I_p\) with \(p^e \le n\). Then the coefficients of degree at most \(n\) of \(F(\varpi y_1 u_1, \varpi y_2 u_2)\) are integral.

Used in: Problem 58.1.7

Problem 58.1.7. Fix \(e \in I_p\), and let \(p^{1/p^e} \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\) be a root of \(X^{p^e} = p\). The pairs \((y_1, y_2) = (1, p)\), \((p, 1)\), \((p^{1/p^e}, p^{1/p^e})\) satisfy both congruences of Problem 58.1.6 at \(e\), at the costs \(a \operatorname{val}y_1 + w \operatorname{val}y_2 = w\), \(a\), \((a + w)/p^e\). There is also an integral pair satisfying both congruences at the cost \[\min(a/p^e, w) + \min(w/p^e, a) .\] [Check: \(p = 3\), \((a, w) = (27, 1)\): \(4/3\) at \(e = 1\), \(28/27\) at \(e = 3\).]

Problem 58.1.8. If \(F\) has the normal form, then for all \(a, w \ge 0\), \[\begin{multline*} \operatorname{val}\bigl((\partial_1^a \partial_2^w F)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ \gamma_p (a + w) - \sum_{e \in I_p,\ p^e \le a + w} \Bigl[\min\Bigl(\frac{a}{p^e}, w\Bigr) + \min\Bigl(\frac{w}{p^e}, a\Bigr)\Bigr] \\ - 2\bigl(1 + \lfloor \log_p(a + w) \rfloor\bigr). \end{multline*}\]

Used in: Problem 58.1.9; Chapter 60

Problem 58.1.9. For \(a + w \ge 1\), the right side of Problem 58.1.8 is at least \(\mathcal{D}(a, w) - 2(1 + \log_p(a + w))\), where \[\mathcal{D}(a, w) := \sum_{e \in I_p} \Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{a}{p^e} - w\Bigr)_+ + \Bigl(\frac{w}{p^e} - a\Bigr)_+\Bigr]\] is the predicted divisibility of Problem 17.5.3. [Check: \(p = 3\): \(\mathcal{D}(27, 1) = 8\), \(\mathcal{D}(54, 2) = 16\), \(\mathcal{D}(243, 1) = 88\).]

Used in: Chapter 60

Problem 58.1.10. Let \(p = 3\) and \(F_1 := F_0/(1 - z_1)\). Then \(F_1\) has the normal form, and \((\partial_1^a \partial_2 F_1)(0) = a!/3\) for \(a \ge 3\), of valuation \(12\) at \(a = 27\) and \(120\) at \(a = 243\). Problem 17.4.4 gives valuations \(11\) at \((27, 1)\) and \(22\) at \((54, 2)\) for \(\exp(-Q_{c, \sigma c})\), \(c\) a unit. All four exceed \(\mathcal{D}(a, w)\).

Problems 58.1.3–58.1.9 prove the count for two places, one row and one input coordinate, with the error \(2(1 + \log_p\max(a+w, 1))\). The model is not an instance of Theorem 17.5.1: there the partners are the places \([-p^e]\tau\), and only columns of height less than the number \(n\) of rows count, so height-one columns need \(n \ge 2\); with \(n = 2\) and all boxes in row \(0\) the error is \(8f(1 + \log_p\max(a+w, 1))\). In general a direction has one component per input coordinate, and at height \(h\) the proof takes integral combinations of the first \(h\) directions by an \(h \times h\) matrix.

§58.2. Diagrams, directions and the cost

From now on we are in the setting of Theorem 17.5.1: \(q_0\), \(d\), \(s\), the set \(\Phi\) of infinite places of \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}, \zeta_{q_1})\) and \(f = \#\Phi\) are as there, \(p\) is a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), and the partner of \(\tau\) at \(e\) is \([-p^e]\tau\). There are \(n\) rows \(0 \le i < n\) and \(n'\) input coordinates \(0 \le l < n'\), and the variables are \(z_{\tau i l}\). The couplings are \(\Gamma_e = \sum_{\tau, i, l} z_{\tau i l}^{p^e}\, z_{[-p^e]\tau, i l}\), and \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\) has \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = \rho(d,p)\). We say that \(F \in \mathbb{C}_p[[z]]\) has the normal form if it has the normal form of Section 17.5 for \((I_p^\circ, [-p^e]\cdot\,, \varpi)\) and some family of integral coefficients.

Definition 58.2.1. A diagram is a function \(\beta\colon \Phi \times \{1, 2, \dots\} \to \mathbb{N}\); the diagram of the data (Definition 37.2.1) is one. \(\beta(\tau, h)\) is the number of columns of height \(h\) at \(\tau\). In sums and products over the heights of a diagram, \(h<n\) means \(1\le h<n\). Write \[\beta_1(\tau) := \beta(\tau, 1), \qquad \beta_{>1}(\tau) := \sum_{1 < h < n} \beta(\tau, h), \qquad \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau) := \sum_{1 < h < n} h\, \beta(\tau, h),\] (with \(\beta_1 := 0\) if \(n \le 1\)), and the number of boxes is \(\lvert\beta\rvert := \sum_\tau \bigl(\beta_1(\tau) + \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)\bigr)\).

Used in: Chapter 59; Chapter 62

Definition 58.2.2. For \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), define the erasure \(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi'\) to be the diagram equal to \(0\) at \((\tau, 1)\) for \(\tau \in \Phi'\) and to \(\beta\) elsewhere.

Used in: Chapter 59

Definition 58.2.3. With \(\tau' := [-p^e]\tau\) and \(\tau_0 := [-1]\tau\), define the predicted divisibility of \(\beta\) by \[\begin{align} \mathcal{D}(\beta) :={}& c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \Bigl[ \Bigl(\frac{\beta_1(\tau)}{p^e} - \beta_1(\tau') - \beta_{>1}(\tau')\Bigr)_+ \\ &\qquad\qquad + \Bigl(\frac{\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)}{p^e} - \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau')\Bigr)_+ - \frac{\beta_{>1}(\tau)}{p^e} \Bigr] \\ &- [p = d]\, \frac12 \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \Bigl[ \min\bigl(\beta_1(\tau), \beta_1(\tau_0) + \beta_{>1}(\tau_0)\bigr) + \beta_{>1}(\tau) \\ &\qquad\qquad + \min\bigl(\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau), \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau_0)\bigr) \Bigr] , \end{align}\] where \([p = d]\) is \(1\) if \(p = d\) and \(0\) otherwise.

Used in: Chapter 59

For the shape \(\kappa\) with \(\kappa(\tau, 0) = 0\) and \(\kappa = \beta\) at heights \(h \ge 1\) this is the number \(\Delta(\kappa)\) of Section 17.5, and \(a_\tau, a'_\tau, l_\tau\) there are \(\beta_1(\tau), \beta_{>1}(\tau), \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)\). For a standard filling \(\mathbf{i}\), which puts the row \(\mathbf{i}(\tau, h, c, j)\) into box \(j < h\) of the \(c\)th column (\(1 \le c \le \beta(\tau,h)\)) of height \(h\) at \(\tau\), \(1 \le h < n\), and integral directions \(y = (y_{\tau l k})\) (\(k \ge 0\)), the product of minors of Section 17.5, written \(P_\mu\) there for the family \(\mu = y\), is \[P_y(z) = \prod_{\tau \in \Phi}\ \prod_{h < n}\ \prod_{1 \le c \le \beta(\tau, h)} \det\Bigl( \sum_{l} y_{\tau l k}\, z_{\tau, \mathbf{i}(\tau, h, c, j), l} \Bigr)_{0 \le j, k < h} .\]

Three facts of the general step are read from the proof (see the remark). The proof replaces the given directions by new ones, \(y'_{\tau h k} := \sum_{k' < h} (g_{\tau h})_{k k'}\, y_{\tau \cdot k'}\), with one integral \(h \times h\) matrix \(g_{\tau h}\) for each place and height, shared by the \(\beta(\tau, h)\) columns.

Problem 58.2.1. Let \(v_1, \dots, v_h \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\) be the values of a linear form on the \(h\) rows of a matrix, and \(\varpi' \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). Some \(g \in M_h(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p})\) with \(\det g \mid \varpi'\) has \(\varpi' \mid (g v)_k\) for every \(k\). For finitely many forms and moduli there is one \(g\), with \(\det g\) dividing the product of the moduli, that works for all of them. [Over \(\mathbb{Z}_3\), for \(v = (1, 2)^t\) and \(\varpi' = 3\), there is such a \(g\) with \(gv = (3, 0)^t\) and \(\det g = 3\).]

Problem 58.2.2. Take one place (dropped from indices), \(n = 3\), \(n' = 2\), two columns of height \(2\) filled with the rows \(0, 1\), and the coordinate vectors as directions. Then \(P_y = (z_{00} z_{11} - z_{01} z_{10})^2\) and \((P_y(\partial) P_y)(0) = 12\). With the first direction scaled by \(3\), \(y = \mathrm{diag}(3, 1)\), the jet of \((z_{00} z_{11} - z_{01} z_{10})^2\) along \(P_y\) is \(108\).

In general put \(z_{\tau i l} = \varpi \sum_{h < n,\ k < h} u_{\tau h k i}\, y'_{\tau h k, l}\) in new variables \(u_{\tau h k i}\), and let \(Q\) be the product, over the columns, of \(\det(u_{\tau h k\, \mathbf{i}(\tau, h, c, j)})_{0 \le j, k < h}\). First, by multilinearity of the determinants and the chain rule, \[\bigl(Q(\partial_u)\, F(z(u))\bigr)(0) = \varpi^{\lvert\beta\rvert} \prod_{\tau \in \Phi}\ \prod_{h < n} (\det g_{\tau h})^{\beta(\tau, h)}\ \bigl(P_y(\partial) F\bigr)(0) ,\] the case of Problem 58.1.5 with matrices in place of scalars. Second, for a shift \(e\), a place \(\tau\) and its partner \(\tau' = [-p^e]\tau\), let \[\mathrm{ded}_{e, \tau}(\beta) := \min\Bigl(\frac{\beta_1(\tau)}{p^e},\ \beta_1(\tau') + \beta_{>1}(\tau')\Bigr) + \frac{\beta_{>1}(\tau)}{p^e} + \min\Bigl(\frac{\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)}{p^e},\ \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau')\Bigr) .\] The proof chooses the \(g_{\tau h}\) so that \(\pi_p\) divides \(\sum_l y'^{\,p^e}_{\tau h k, l}\, y'_{\tau' h' k', l}\) for all \(e \in I_p^\circ\) with \(e \le \lfloor \log_p \lvert\beta\rvert \rfloor\), all places, heights and directions, and so that \[\sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \sum_{h < n} \beta(\tau, h) \operatorname{val}(\det g_{\tau h}) \ \le\ c_p \sum_{e \in I_p^\circ,\ e \le \lfloor \log_p \lvert\beta\rvert \rfloor}\ \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \mathrm{ded}_{e, \tau}(\beta) .\] At the source the modulus is \(\pi_p^{1/p^e}\), at the partner \(\pi_p\), as in Problem 58.1.7; a source condition \(\sum_l y_l^{p^e} v_l \equiv 0 \pmod{\pi_p}\) follows from the linear condition \(\sum_l v_l^{1/p^e} y_l \equiv 0 \pmod{\pi_p^{1/p^e}}\), so Problem 58.2.1 applies. Each height is weighted by its number of columns: the first minimum compares entry conditions at height one on the source with pairing conditions at the partner, the middle term counts source pairing conditions at the higher heights, and the last minimum compares entry conditions at those heights on the two sides. Third, \(\pi_p\) divides \(\Gamma_e(z(u)/\varpi)\) for these \(e\), the series \(F(z(u))\) is integral through degree \(\lvert\beta\rvert\), and \(Q\) is homogeneous of degree \(\lvert\beta\rvert\).

Problem 58.2.3. \(c_p \sum_{e \in I_p^\circ} \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \mathrm{ded}_{e, \tau}(\beta) = \gamma_p \lvert\beta\rvert - \mathcal{D}(\beta)\), the series converging. The variables \(u_{\tau h k i}\) number at most \(f n^3\).

As \(\mathrm{ded}_{e,\tau} \ge 0\), the truncated sum is at most the full one. So Problems 58.1.3, 58.1.4 and 58.2.3, with the three facts read from the proof, give Theorem 17.5.1 for diagrams (with \(\lfloor\log_p 0\rfloor := 0\)).

Problem 58.2.4. Let \(p = d\) and \(n \ge 2\), let \(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2 = [-1]\sigma_1\) be two places, and let \(\beta(\sigma_1, 1) = 12\), \(\beta(\sigma_2, 1) = 4\) and \(\beta = 0\) otherwise. The shift \(e = 0\) debits \(c_p\bigl(\mathrm{ded}_{0, \sigma_1}(\beta) + \mathrm{ded}_{0, \sigma_2}(\beta)\bigr) = 4\), and the half sum in \(\mathcal{D}(\beta)\) contributes \(-4\).

§58.3. Charges

At an exceptional prime \(p < x_1\) the loss allowed in Section 57.2 need not cover the collisions between \(a\)-places. The proof then removes the columns of height one at a set \(\Phi'\) of places from the prediction and pays for their pairings with factors \(p\) on rows.

Definition 58.3.1. Let \(\beta\) be a diagram and \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\). Charges for \((\beta, \Phi')\) are numbers \(\mathfrak{c}(\tau, h, i) \in \mathbb{N}\) (\(\tau \in \Phi\), \(h \ge 1\), \(0 \le i < n\)) such that, for all \(e \in I_p^\circ\), \(\tau \in \Phi\), \(h \ge 1\) and rows \(i\), with \(\tau' = [-p^e]\tau\): if both \(\beta(\tau, 1) \ne 0\) and \(\beta(\tau', h) \ne 0\), and if either \(\tau \in \Phi'\) or both \(h = 1\) and \(\tau' \in \Phi'\), then \(\mathfrak{c}(\tau, 1, i) + \mathfrak{c}(\tau', h, i) > 0\). Here \(h\) runs over all heights, including \(h \ge n\). For a filling \(\mathbf{i}\), the sum of the charges over the boxes is \(\sum_{\text{boxes}} \mathfrak{c}:= \sum_{\tau} \sum_{h < n} \sum_{1 \le c \le \beta(\tau, h)} \sum_{j < h} \mathfrak{c}\bigl(\tau, h, \mathbf{i}(\tau, h, c, j)\bigr) .\)

Used in: Chapter 62: Problem 62.2.3

Problem 58.3.1. In the two-place model let \(F\) have the normal form. (i) \(\operatorname{val}\bigl((\partial_1^a \partial_2^w F)(0)\bigr) \ge \gamma_p(a + w) - w - 2(1 + \lfloor \log_p(a+w) \rfloor)\). (ii) With a second, empty row (\(n = 2\)), \(\beta(1,1) = a\), \(\beta(2,1) = w\), \(\beta = 0\) otherwise, \(\Phi' = \{1\}\), \(\mathfrak{c}(2, 1, i) = 1\) for both rows \(i\) and \(\mathfrak{c}= 0\) otherwise, the numbers \(\mathfrak{c}\) are charges for \((\beta, \Phi')\) for the partner rule of the model, and for the filling with all boxes in row \(0\) the main term \(\mathcal{D}(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi') + \gamma_p(\lvert\beta\rvert - \lvert\beta \smallsetminus \Phi'\rvert) - \sum_{\text{boxes}} \mathfrak{c}\) equals \(\gamma_p(a + w) - w\). [Check the main term in (ii) at \(p = 3\): \(719/8\) at \((243, 2)\), against \(\mathcal{D}(243, 2) = 86\); \(2177/8\) at \((729, 2)\), against \(\mathcal{D}(729, 2) = 267\).]

In general the substitution becomes \(z_{\tau i l} = \varpi \sum_{h, k} p^{\mathfrak{c}(\tau, h, i)}\, u_{\tau h k i}\, y'_{\tau h k, l}\), and the multiplier gains the factor \(p^{\sum_{\text{boxes}} \mathfrak{c}}\). The pairings of height one at \(\tau\) with height \(h\) at \(\tau'\), where \(\tau \in \Phi'\) or both \(h = 1\) and \(\tau' \in \Phi'\), are exempt: no congruence is required for these pairings, and the charge condition gives a factor \(p\) on one side of each row (after setting the charges to \(1\) where \(\beta(\tau, h) = 0\), which changes no box). The remaining congruences give \(\sum_{\tau}\sum_{h<n} \beta(\tau, h)\operatorname{val}(\det g_{\tau h}) \le c_p \sum_{e \in I_p^\circ,\ e \le \lfloor\log_p\lvert\beta\rvert\rfloor} \sum_\tau \mathrm{ded}_{e,\tau}(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi')\), the cut-off using \(\lvert\beta\rvert\).

Problem 58.3.2. (m) Let \(q_0 = 18480\), \(d = 138599\), \(s = 0\), \(p = 13\). Then \(F\) has the normal form, \(\operatorname{val}\bigl(F^{(A)}(0)\bigr) = (A - 1)/12 - A/168 = 302875106592239/168\), and the bound of Theorem 17.5.1 is \(13A/168 - 933120 \cdot 2^3 \cdot 13 = 302858803119613/168\).

We take the following theorem from the proof. The construction of the directions uses the simultaneous elimination of Problem 58.2.1; the proof uses both conclusions of Problem 58.2.3, applying the identity to the erased diagram; Problem 58.3.1 treats one marking in the two-place model.

Theorem 58.3.2. Let \(q_0\), \(d\), \(s\), \(\Phi\), \(f\), \(p\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(\eta\), the \(n\) rows and the \(n'\) input coordinates be as above, let \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\) with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\), and let \(F \in \mathbb{C}_p[[z]]\) have the normal form. Let \(\beta\) be a diagram, \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), and \(\mathfrak{c}\) charges for \((\beta, \Phi')\). Then for every standard filling \(\mathbf{i}\) of \(\beta\) and all integral directions \(y\), \[\operatorname{val}\bigl((P_y(\partial) F)(0)\bigr) + \sum_{\text{boxes}} \mathfrak{c}\ \ge\ \mathcal{D}(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi') + \gamma_p\bigl(\lvert\beta\rvert - \lvert\beta \smallsetminus \Phi'\rvert\bigr) - f n^3 \bigl(1 + \log_p \max(\lvert\beta\rvert, 1)\bigr) .\]
