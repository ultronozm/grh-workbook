The ring \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}\) of integers of the unramified quadratic extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), its Teichmüller roots of unity, and its Frobenius \(\sigma\), which satisfies \(\sigma(a) \equiv a^p \pmod p\). Chapter 16 found the gain at the odd levels only for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), because it needed a global element of the CM order acting by a non-unit on one eigenline and by a unit on the other. What structure, visible at \(p\) alone, forces a series to be divisible by about \(p^{\alpha p/(p^2-1)}\), that is by \(p^{\sum_{i \text{ odd}} \alpha/p^i}\), after \(\alpha\) derivatives? This chapter studies series \(\exp(-\beta(x,y))\,U(x,y)\) with \(U\) integral, where \(x\) and \(y\) are parameters of a formal group of height two and \(\beta\) is a pairing, biadditive and semilinear for Frobenius up to series with integral exponential. Section 17.5 puts \(\exp(-Q_{c,\sigma c})U\), for a unit \(c\), into the normal form of the proof [GL] by changing the partner coordinate (Problem 17.5.2), and states the count proved there for series in that form at the infinite places of a number field.

§17.1. The formal module of height two Tag 22H7

Throughout, \(p\) is an odd prime, \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_{p^2}\), \(L := \mathbb{Q}_{p^2}\), \(\sigma\) is the Frobenius of \(L\), and \(v\) is the valuation with \(v(p) = 1\). A power series over \(L\), or over a finite extension of \(L\), is integral if all its coefficients have valuation \(\ge 0\). Let \[\operatorname{lg}(X) := \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{X^{p^{2j}}}{p^j}, \qquad \operatorname{lg}^*(X) := \frac1p \operatorname{lg}(X^p) = \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{X^{p^{2j+1}}}{p^{j+1}} .\] In the notation of Chapter 13, \(\operatorname{lg}= \ell_p\) is the model logarithm for the uniformizer \(p\) of \(\mathcal{O}\), whose residue field has \(p^2\) elements.

We use Hazewinkel’s functional-equation lemma [Ha], [Ha79] in the following case. Let \(f(X) \in L[[X]]\) satisfy \(f(X) - p^{-1} f(X^{p^2}) \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) and \(f(X) \equiv X\) modulo degree \(2\). Then: \(f^{-1}(f(X) + f(Y))\) has coefficients in \(\mathcal{O}\); if \(g(X) \in X L[[X]]\) also satisfies \(g(X) - p^{-1} g(X^{p^2}) \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\), then \(f^{-1}(g(X)) \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\); and for \(\alpha, \beta \in X\mathcal{O}[[X]]\) and \(r \ge 1\), \(\alpha \equiv \beta \pmod{p^r}\) if and only if \(f(\alpha) \equiv f(\beta) \pmod{p^r}\).

Let \(F(X,Y) := \operatorname{lg}^{-1}(\operatorname{lg}X + \operatorname{lg}Y)\) and \([\alpha](X) := \operatorname{lg}^{-1}(\alpha \operatorname{lg}X)\) for \(\alpha \in \mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22H8

Problem 17.1.1. \(F \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[X,Y]]\) and \([\alpha] \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\), so \(F\) is a formal \(\mathcal{O}\)-module. Modulo degree \(p^2 + 1\), \[F(X,Y) \equiv X + Y - \frac1p \sum_{0 < k < p^2} \binom{p^2}{k} X^k Y^{p^2-k} .\] For \(\zeta \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\zeta^{p^2-1} = 1\), \([\zeta](X) = \zeta X\) and \(F(\zeta X, \zeta Y) = \zeta F(X,Y)\).

Tag 22H9

Problem 17.1.2. \([p](X) \equiv X^{p^2} \pmod p\). So \(F\) has height two.

Tag 22HA

Problem 17.1.3. Let \(\omega\) be a root of unity of order \(2(p^2-1)\), so that \(\omega^{p^2-1} = -1\) and \(\omega \in \mathbb{Z}_{p^4}\). Then \(\ell_{-p}(\omega X) = \omega \operatorname{lg}(X)\). Hence for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) the formal group of \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) is isomorphic to \(F\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^4}\).

For a series \(f\) over \(L\) let \(f^\sigma\) be the series obtained by applying \(\sigma\) to the coefficients, and \(f^\phi(X) := f^\sigma(X^p)\), where \(X^p\) raises every variable to the power \(p\).

Tag 22HB

Problem 17.1.4. For \(f\) integral over \(\mathcal{O}\) in any number of variables, \(f^\phi \equiv f^p \pmod p\). In particular \(F(X^p, Y^p) \equiv F(X,Y)^p \pmod p\).

§17.2. Pairings and their normal form Tag 22HC

In this chapter \(L\) is \(\mathbb{Q}_{p^2}\), \(U\) an integral series, and in Section 17.5 \(\kappa\) is a shape, \(\iota\) a filling, \(N\) a number of rows, \(r\) a partner rule, \(\Delta(\kappa)\) the predicted divisibility, \(B\) a number of boxes, \(t\) a height and \(S\) a set of exponents; elsewhere in the workbook these letters denote the weight, an isomorphism \(\mathbb{C}\simeq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), the level, a rank, the gap, the budget, the number \(5r\) and the source forms, and \(K\) is \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\).

For series \(f\), \(g\) over \(L\) write \(f \simeq g\) if \(f - g\) has no constant term and \(\exp(f - g)\) is integral. The basic tool is Dwork’s lemma [Ko], in the following form, which has the same proof since \(\sigma(a) \equiv a^p \pmod p\): for a series \(\Psi\) over \(L\) in finitely many variables without constant term, \[\exp\Psi \text{ is integral} \iff \Psi^\phi - p\Psi \in p\,\mathcal{O}[[X]] .\] For a series \(H\) in two variables let \(H^\mathrm{sw}(X,Y) := H(Y,X)\) and \[(d_F H)(X_0, X_1, X_2) := H\bigl(F(X_0,X_1), X_2\bigr) - H(X_0, X_2) - H(X_1, X_2),\] and for a series \(f\) in one variable let \(\partial_F f := f(F(X_0,X_1)) - f(X_0) - f(X_1)\). A pairing is a series \(\beta(X,Y)\) over \(L\) such that: (i) every monomial of \(\beta\) contains both variables; (ii) \(d_F\beta \simeq 0\) and \(d_F\beta^\mathrm{sw}\simeq 0\); (iii) \(\beta(\zeta X, Y) \simeq \beta(X, \zeta^p Y)\) for every \(\zeta \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\zeta^{p^2-1} = 1\). Condition (ii) says that \(\beta\) is additive in each variable up to \(\simeq\). Condition (iii) is semilinearity for Frobenius, since \(\zeta^p = \sigma(\zeta)\).

Tag 22HD

Problem 17.2.1. The relation \(\simeq\) is an equivalence relation, stable under sums and under substitution of series over \(\mathcal{O}\) without constant term for the variables. If \(f\) and \(g\) are series in two variables with \(f \simeq g\), then \(d_F f \simeq d_F g\).

Tag 22HE

Problem 17.2.2. (i) \(\beta(\zeta X, Y) = \beta(X, \zeta^p Y)\) for all \(\zeta\) as in (iii) if and only if every monomial \(X^m Y^n\) of \(\beta\) has \(m \equiv pn \pmod{p^2-1}\). (ii) Let \(\beta\) be a pairing, \(\zeta\) a primitive \((p^2-1)\)th root of unity, and \(G := (p^2-1)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{p^2-2} \beta(\zeta^k X, \zeta^{-pk} Y)\). Then \(G\) is a pairing, \(G \simeq \beta\), and \(G\) satisfies the condition of (i).

Used in: Problem 17.2.3

For \(c, c' \in L\) let \[A(X,Y) := \sum_{0 \le i \le j} \frac{X^{p^{2i}}\, Y^{p^{2j+1}}}{p^{\,i+j+1}}, \qquad Q_{c,c'}(X,Y) := c\,A(X,Y) - c'\,A(Y,X) .\] In every monomial of \(A\) the exponent of \(Y\) is the exponent of \(X\) times an odd power of \(p\).

Tag 22HF

Problem 17.2.3. For all \(c, c'\), \(Q_{c,c'}(\zeta X, Y) = Q_{c,c'}(X, \zeta^p Y)\) for every \(\zeta\) as in (iii). No monomial of \(\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y)\) satisfies the congruence of Problem 17.2.2(i), and \(\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y)\) fails condition (iii): \(\operatorname{lg}(\zeta X)\operatorname{lg}(Y) \not\simeq \operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}(\zeta^p Y)\) for \(\zeta\) a primitive \((p^2-1)\)th root of unity.

Tag 22HH

Problem 17.2.5. If \(c \in L\) and \(Q_{c,\sigma c} \simeq 0\), then \(c = 0\).

Tag 22HJ

For \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), is \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) itself a pairing? For \(c \ne 0\) it is not integral, and it is not biadditive on the nose. Problems 17.2.7–17.2.10 show that it is biadditive up to \(\simeq\).

Problem 17.2.10. For \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) is a pairing.

Tag 2352

Theorem 17.2.1. For every \(c \in \mathcal{O}\) the series \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) is a pairing. If \(c, c' \in \mathcal{O}\) and \(Q_{c,\sigma c} \simeq Q_{c',\sigma c'}\), then \(c = c'\). If a pairing \(\beta\) satisfies \(\beta \simeq Q_{c,\sigma c}\) with \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), then \(c \equiv p \cdot [XY^p]\,\beta \pmod p\).

The exactly biadditive series \(\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y)\) is not a pairing: it fails condition (iii) (Problem 17.2.3). The denominators of \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) sit only on the monomials \(X^{p^{2i}} Y^{p^{2j+1}}\) and \(Y^{p^{2i}} X^{p^{2j+1}}\), at odd shifts.

§17.3. Odd shifts and the credit Tag 22HQ

How much do these denominators cost after \(\alpha\) derivatives? Let \(\varpi_p\) be a root of \(\varpi_p^{p^2-1} = p\), and let \(\mathcal{O}' := \mathcal{O}[\varpi_p]\), so that \(v(\varpi_p) = \rho\) with \[\rho := \frac{1}{p^2-1}, \qquad \gamma_p := \frac{p}{p^2-1} .\]

For every odd prime \(p\) and every integer \(n \ge 0\), write \(E_p(n) := \sum_{i \ge 1,\ i \text{ odd}} \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor\).

Tag 22HR

Problem 17.3.1. \(\gamma_p = \sum_{e \ge 0} p^{-(2e+1)}\) and \(\gamma_p + \rho = 1/(p-1)\). If \(n = \sum_k \delta_k p^k\) in base \(p\), then \[\gamma_p n - E_p(n) = \gamma_p \sum_{k \text{ even}} \delta_k + \rho \sum_{k \text{ odd}} \delta_k, \qquad v(n!) - \rho n = \gamma_p n - \frac{s_p(n)}{p-1} .\] Hence \(v(n!) - \rho n \le E_p(n) \le \gamma_p n\).

Used in: Chapter 58

If \(g(\varpi_p x)\) is integral, then \(v(g^{(n)}(0)) \ge v(n!) - \rho n\). For the formal group of Chapter 15 at \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the \(n\)th invariant derivative at zero of an integral series has valuation at least \(E_p(n)\) (Theorem 15.4.1). By Problem 17.3.1, both bounds are the credit \(\gamma_p n = \sum_{i \text{ odd}} n/p^i\) up to digit terms.

Tag 22HS

Problem 17.3.2. In \(Q_{c,c'}(\varpi_p X, \varpi_p Y)\) the coefficient of \(X^{p^{2i}} Y^{p^{2j+1}}\) (\(i \le j\)) has valuation \(v(c) + (p^{2i} + p^{2j+1})\rho - (i+j+1)\). For \(i = j = 0\) this is \(v(c) + \frac1{p-1} - 1\); otherwise it is at least \(v(c) + \frac{1}{p-1} + p - 2\). The same holds for the coefficient of \(Y^{p^{2i}} X^{p^{2j+1}}\), with \(c'\) in place of \(c\).

Tag 22HT

Problem 17.3.3. Let \(\Psi\) be a series without constant term over a finite extension \(L'\) of \(L(\varpi_p)\). If every coefficient of \(\Psi\) has valuation at least \(1/(p-1)\), then every coefficient of \(\exp\Psi\) has valuation at least \(0\); for \(L' = L(\varpi_p)\) they lie in \(\mathcal{O}'\). For every series \(h\) over \(\mathcal{O}'\) without constant term, \(\exp(\varpi_p^{p+1} h/p) = \sum_{k \ge 0} (h/p)^k\, g_k\) with \(g_k \in \mathcal{O}'\).

Used in: Chapter 58; Chapter 61

For a series \(h\) without constant term, let \(\mathcal{C}(h)\) be the set of series \(\sum_{k \ge 0} p^{-k} h^k g_k\) with \(g_k\) integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\), the sum being finite in each degree. It is stable under multiplication by integral series. Problems 17.2.1 and 17.3.2–17.3.3 prove the following.

Tag 22HU

Theorem 17.3.1. Let \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), let \(\beta\) be a pairing with \(\beta \simeq Q_{c,\sigma c}\), let \(U \in \mathcal{O}[[x,y]]\), and let \(\Theta := \exp(-\beta(x,y))\, U(x,y)\). Then \[\Theta(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y) \in \mathcal{C}(h), \qquad h := \sigma(c)\, x^p y - c\, x y^p .\]

§17.4. Two local estimates Tag 22HV

Fix \(\Theta\), \(\beta\), \(c\), \(h\) as in Theorem 17.3.1. We bound the mixed derivatives at \(0\) of \(\Theta\), of order \(a\) in \(x\) and \(w\) in \(y\), in two ways: in the formal parameters, with \(\partial_x^a \partial_y^w\), and in the logarithm coordinates, with \(D_x^a D_y^w\), where \(D_x := \operatorname{lg}'(x)^{-1}\partial_x\) is the invariant derivation of Chapter 13. Thus \((D_x^a D_y^w \Theta)(0) = a!\, w!\, [\xi^a \eta^w]\, \Theta(\operatorname{lg}^{-1} \xi, \operatorname{lg}^{-1} \eta)\). We call \(x\) the top and \(y\) its partner.

Tag 22HW

Problem 17.4.1. If \(G(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y) \in \mathcal{C}(h)\), then \[v\bigl((\partial_x^a\partial_y^w G)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w) - \min\Bigl(w,\ \Bigl\lfloor \frac ap \Bigr\rfloor + \Bigl\lfloor \frac wp \Bigr\rfloor\Bigr).\]

In the formal parameters the multiplication by \(p\) on the partner, \(y \mapsto [p](y)\), is not linear. In the logarithm coordinates it is \(\eta \mapsto p\eta\), and its effect on \(D_y^w\) is the factor \(p^w\).

Tag 22HX

Problem 17.4.2. \(\beta(x, [p]y) \simeq p\,\beta(x,y)\), and \(\exp\bigl(-p\,Q_{c,\sigma c}(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y)\bigr)\) is integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\).

Tag 22HY

Problem 17.4.3. If \(G(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y)\) is integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\), then \(v\bigl((D_x^a D_y^w G)(0)\bigr) \ge v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w)\).

Applying Problem 17.4.1 to \(\Theta\), and Problem 17.4.3 to \(\Theta(x, [p]y)\), which is allowed by Problem 17.4.2, gives the following.

Tag 22HZ

Theorem 17.4.1. For all \(a, w \ge 0\): \[\begin{align*} \text{(A)}\quad & v\bigl((\partial_x^a\partial_y^w \Theta)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w) - \min\bigl(w,\ \lfloor a/p \rfloor + \lfloor w/p \rfloor\bigr), \\ \text{(B)}\quad & v\bigl(p^w (D_x^a D_y^w \Theta)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w) . \end{align*}\]

By Problem 17.3.1, both right sides are at least \(\gamma_p a - w - (s_p(a) + s_p(w))/(p-1)\): the top earns the credit \(\gamma_p a = \sum_{i \text{ odd}} a/p^i\), and the partner pays at most one factor of \(p\) per derivative. In (B) the payment is the explicit factor \(p^w\), the effect of \([p]\) on the partner. The bounds use the hypothesis \(\beta \simeq Q_{c,\sigma c}\) with \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), the integrality of \(U\) and the formal \(\mathcal{O}\)-module \(F\), not any global element of a CM order.

For the ordinary derivative of \(\Theta=\exp(-Q_{c,\sigma c})\) with \(c\) a unit, \(1\le w<p\), and \(a=p^{2e+1}w\) for an integer \(e\ge0\), the loss relative to \(v(a!)\) is \(w\) at each of the odd levels through \(2e+1\).

§17.5. Couplings and the count Tag 2353

In the normal form of the proof [GL] each variable has a place index, and the exponent uses couplings between a place and its partners. The proof defines couplings and the normal form for every prime \(p\); here \(p\) stays odd.

Let \(\Phi\) and \(V\) be finite sets, and let \(r = (r_e)_{e \ge 0}\) be a sequence of maps \(r_e \colon \Phi \to \Phi\), the partner rule. The variables are \(z_{\tau i}\), \((\tau, i) \in \Phi \times V\), and a series is integral if its coefficients lie in \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). Define the couplings by \[\Gamma_e := \sum_{(\tau, i) \in \Phi \times V} z_{\tau i}^{p^e}\, z_{r_e(\tau) i} \qquad (e \ge 0).\]

Tag 2354

Problem 17.5.1. (i) \(\Gamma_e(\alpha z) = \alpha^{p^e + 1}\, \Gamma_e(z)\) for \(\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). (ii) Let \(\Phi = (\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^\times\) with \(p \nmid q\), let \(V\) be a point, and \(r_e(\tau) := -p^e \tau\). If \(p^2 \equiv 1 \pmod q\) and \(p \not\equiv -1 \pmod q\), then for odd \(e\) the map \(r_e\) is an involution without fixed points, and \(\Gamma_e\) is the sum over its orbits \(\{\tau, -p\tau\}\) of \(z_\tau^{p^e} z_{-p\tau} + z_{-p\tau}^{p^e} z_\tau\). For \(q = 8\) and \(p = 3\), \(\Gamma_1 = z_1^3 z_5 + z_5^3 z_1 + z_3^3 z_7 + z_7^3 z_3\).

Used in: Chapter 60

Let \(S\) be a set of integers \(e \ge 0\), let \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\), and let \(\lambda = (\lambda_{ej})_{e, j \ge 0}\) be a family in \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). A series \(\Theta \in \mathbb{C}_p[[z_{\tau i}]]\) has the normal form for \((S, r, \varpi, \lambda)\) if there is an integral series \(W\) with \[\Theta(\varpi z) = W(z) \exp\Bigl( \frac{\varpi^{p+1}}{p} \sum_{e \in S} \sum_{j \ge 0} \lambda_{ej}\, \Gamma_e^{\,p^j} \Bigr) .\] The sum converges, as \(\Gamma_e^{p^j}\) is homogeneous of degree \((p^e + 1)p^j\). The \(\lambda_{ej}\) may be any elements of \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\), and \(\varpi\) any element of \(\mathbb{C}_p\).

In the next problem \(\Phi = \{1, 2\}\), \(V\) is a point, \(r_e\) is the transposition for every \(e\), \(S\) is the set of odd numbers, and \(z_1 = x\), \(z_2 = y\); so \(\Gamma_1 = x^p y + x y^p\). The series \(Q_{c,c'}\) and the root \(\varpi_p\) are those of Sections 17.2 and 17.3.

The count measures jets of a series in normal form along products of minors. Let \(\mathcal{R}\) and \(\mathcal{U}\) be finite sets, \(N := |\mathcal{R}|\), and take \(V = \mathcal{R} \times \mathcal{U}\), so that the variables are \(z_{\tau b u}\). A shape is a function \(\kappa\) on \(\Phi \times \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) with values in \(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\); \(\kappa(\tau, t)\) is the number of columns of height \(t\) at \(\tau\), and only the heights \(t < N\) will count. A filling \(\iota\) of \(\kappa\) assigns a row \(\iota_\tau(t, j, b) \in \mathcal{R}\) to every \(\tau \in \Phi\), \(t < N\), \(j < \kappa(\tau, t)\) and \(b < t\). For a family \(\mu = (\mu_{\tau u k})\) in \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\) (\(\tau \in \Phi\), \(u \in \mathcal{U}\), \(k \ge 0\)) the product of minors is the polynomial \[P_\mu(z) := \prod_{\tau \in \Phi}\ \prod_{t < N}\ \prod_{j < \kappa(\tau, t)} \det\Bigl( \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \mu_{\tau u k}\, z_{\tau, \iota_\tau(t,j,b), u} \Bigr)_{0 \le b, k < t},\] and the jet of \(\Theta\) along it is \((P_\mu(\partial)\Theta)(0) = \sum_m m!\, [z^m]P_\mu\, [z^m]\Theta\), where \(m! = \prod m_x!\) over the variables \(x\). At each place \(\tau\) the proof uses a set of standard fillings. In a matrix of independent variables \(X_{bk}\), their products of minors form a basis, over every commutative ring, of the span of the products for all fillings of that shape. For any enumeration \(b_0, \ldots, b_{N-1}\) of the rows and any complex numbers \(x_b\) indexed by the rows, let \(\chi_\tau(x)\) be the sum, over the standard fillings at \(\tau\), of \(\prod_{\text{boxes}} x_{\text{entry}}\); then \[\chi_\tau(x)\,\det\bigl(x_{b_i}^{\,k}\bigr)_{0 \le i,k < N} = \det\bigl(x_{b_i}^{\,\nu_{N-1-k}+k}\bigr)_{0 \le i,k < N}, \qquad \nu_j := \sum_{j < t < N} \kappa(\tau,t).\] Substituting \(X_{bk} = \sum_u \mu_{\tau u k} z_{\tau b u}\) gives the factors of \(P_\mu\) at \(\tau\).

The predicted divisibility also depends on a prime \(d\), which may equal \(p\). In this formula \(\chi_K(p) := \bigl(\frac{p}{d}\bigr)\) denotes the quadratic character of \(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}\), evaluated at \(p\); for \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) it agrees with the character of \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\) used above. Put \(c_p := 1/2\) if \(p = d\) and \(c_p := 1\) otherwise, and let \(I_p^\circ\) be the set of \(e \ge 0\) such that \(p = d\), or \(\chi_K(p) = -1\) and \(e\) is odd; let \(I_p := I_p^\circ \setminus \{0\}\). For a shape \(\kappa\) let \[a_\tau := \sum_{t < N,\ t = 1} \kappa(\tau, t), \qquad a'_\tau := \sum_{t < N,\ t \ne 1} \kappa(\tau, t), \qquad l_\tau := \sum_{t < N,\ t \ne 1} t\, \kappa(\tau, t)\] be the numbers of columns of height one, of the other columns, and of the boxes in these. With \(\tau_e := r_e(\tau)\) and \(x_+ := \max(x, 0)\), define the predicted divisibility as the real number \[\begin{align} \Delta(\kappa) :={}& c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \Bigl[ \Bigl(\frac{a_\tau}{p^e} - a_{\tau_e} - a'_{\tau_e}\Bigr)_+ + \Bigl(\frac{l_\tau}{p^e} - l_{\tau_e}\Bigr)_+ - \frac{a'_\tau}{p^e} \Bigr] \\ &- [p = d]\, \frac12 \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \bigl[ \min(a_\tau, a_{\tau_0} + a'_{\tau_0}) + a'_\tau + \min(l_\tau, l_{\tau_0}) \bigr], \end{align}\] where \([p = d]\) is \(1\) if \(p = d\) and \(0\) otherwise.
