This sheet serves the second moment in the proof of Theorem 39.4.2 of Chapter 39. The proof uses the second moment of the Gram operators \(\mathcal K_s\) to bound the number of projected translates of the ramified source needed to span a subspace of dimension at least \((1-4b^{-2})d_T\). The proof computes \(\mathcal K_s\) by unfolding twice, as Petersson’s formula computes the inner product of two Poincaré series: each entry of \(\mathcal K_s\) becomes a sum over the rational points whose finite components, transported by the two class representatives for that entry, are integral with reduction \(s\). What does this sum look like when the group is finite, which part of it is forced, and what is the least possible second moment \(\sum_s\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\)?

§40.1. A finite model

For a definite unitary group with one class, at principal level the forms are equivariant functions on the finite group \(\bar G\) of residues, with values fixed by the integral points that reduce to \(1\). We take this as the model.

Definition 40.1.1. A finite model consists of a finite group \(\bar G\), a subgroup \(\Gamma\subseteq\bar G\) (the integral points), a unitary representation \(\pi\) of \(\Gamma\) on a nonzero finite-dimensional complex inner product space \(V_\pi\), a subgroup \(\Gamma_X\subseteq\Gamma\), a unitary representation \(\sigma_X\) of \(\Gamma_X\) on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space \(V_X\), and a \(\Gamma_X\)-equivariant linear map \(\jmath\colon V_X\to V_\pi\) with \(\jmath^*\jmath=c_0\operatorname{id}_{V_X}\) for a constant \(c_0>0\). Its forms are the functions \(\varphi\colon\bar G\to V_\pi\) with \(\varphi(\gamma g)=\pi(\gamma)\varphi(g)\) for \(\gamma\in\Gamma\); they form a space \(\mathcal Y\) with \(\langle\varphi,\varphi'\rangle:=\sum_{g\in\Gamma\backslash\bar G}\langle\varphi(g),\varphi'(g)\rangle\), on which \(\bar G\) acts by \((\rho(s)\varphi)(g):=\varphi(gs)\). Its Poincaré map \(P\colon V_X\to\mathcal Y\) sends \(w\) to the function equal to \(c_0^{-1/2}\pi(g)\jmath w\) at \(g\in\Gamma\) and to \(0\) off \(\Gamma\).

In this sheet \(P\) without subscript is always this map. In a finite model we write \(\mathcal K_s:=P^*\rho(s)P\), \(\bar R_0:=\Gamma_X\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s):=\sigma_X(s)\) for \(s\in\Gamma_X\) and \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s):=0\) for \(s\notin\Gamma_X\), and \(D_X:=\dim V_X\): they play the parts of the objects of the same names in Chapter 39.

Problem 40.1.1. \(Pw\in\mathcal Y\), and \(\langle\varphi,Pw\rangle=c_0^{-1/2}\langle\varphi(1),\jmath w\rangle\) for \(\varphi\in\mathcal Y\): the map \(P\) represents the period \(\varphi\mapsto c_0^{-1/2}\jmath^*\varphi(1)\).

Problem 40.1.2. \(\mathcal K_s=c_0^{-1}\jmath^*\pi(s)\jmath\) for \(s\in\Gamma\), and \(\mathcal K_s=0\) for \(s\notin\Gamma\). In particular \(P\) is an isometry.

Problem 40.1.3. \(\mathcal K_s=\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)\) for \(s\in\bar R_0\). For \(t\in\bar R_0\) and \(s\in\bar G\), \(\rho(t)P=P\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) and \(\mathcal K_{st}=\mathcal K_s\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\).

Problem 40.1.4. Let \(\Gamma_X=\Gamma\), \(V_X=V_\pi\) and \(\jmath=1\). Then \(\dim\mathcal Y=[\bar G:\Gamma]\dim V_\pi\), \(\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2=\#\Gamma\dim V_\pi=\#\bar G\,(\dim V_\pi)^2/\dim\mathcal Y\), and if \(J\) is a set of representatives of the cosets \(s\Gamma\), then \(\mathcal Y\) is the orthogonal sum of the \(\rho(s)P(V_\pi)\), \(s\in J\).

Problem 40.1.5. §40.2. The least second moment

By Problem 9.2.5, if \(p\) is the orthogonal projection onto a \(\bar G\)-stable subspace \(\mathcal Y_0\subseteq\mathcal Y\) and \(pP\) is an isometry, then \(\#\bar G\,D_X^2\le\dim\mathcal Y_0\cdot\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\). When is this an equality?

Problem 40.2.1. If \(\pi\) is irreducible, then \(\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2=\#\Gamma\,D_X^2/\dim V_\pi\), and equality holds in Problem 9.2.5 with \(\mathcal Y_0=\mathcal Y\). In Problem 40.1.6: \(32\cdot1/2=16\) and \(30\cdot16=480=\#\bar G\).

Used in: Problem 40.2.2; Chapter 39

Let \(\deg:=\dim V_\pi\) and \(\mathrm{mass}:=1/\#\Gamma\). When \(\pi\) is irreducible, \(\deg\) is its formal degree for Haar measure of total mass one. Then Problem 40.2.1 says that the second moment equals \(D_X^2/(\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass})\), the quantity \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\) of Definition 40.3.2 below. Without irreducibility it is at least as large, by Problem 9.2.5, and it can be larger.

Problem 40.2.2. (a) Let \(V_\pi=\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^2)\), \(a\ge1\), with the inner product of Problem 21.3.2, the other data as in Problem 40.1.6 but \(V_X=\mathbb{C}\,\mathbf e_1^j\mathbf e_2^{a-j}\), \(0\le j\le a\), with \(\sigma_X(\operatorname{diag}(u,1))=u^j\) and the norm induced from \(V_\pi\). Then \(\sum_s\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2=16\), or \(32\) if \(2j=a\), while \(\#\Gamma\,D_X^2/\dim V_\pi=32/(a+1)\); for \(a=8\), \(j=0\) the ratio is \(9/2\). (b) Let \(\bar G=\Gamma=S_3\) act on \(V_\pi=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^3:x_1+x_2+x_3=0\}\) by permuting coordinates, \(\Gamma_X=1\), \(V_X=\mathbb{C}\) and \(\jmath(1)=(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_2)/\sqrt2\). In the lexicographic order of the permutations, written as \((s(1),s(2),s(3))\), the scalars \(\mathcal K_s\) are \(1,\frac12,-1,-\frac12,-\frac12,\frac12\), and \(\sum_s\lvert\mathcal K_s\rvert^2=3=6/2\). Here \(\pi\) is irreducible, and this is the equality of Problem 40.2.1.

In Problem 40.2.2(a), the second moment exceeds \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\) when \(a\ge2\). For the data the real group is noncompact, and the rational-point sums in Theorem 40.3.2 can have infinitely many terms.

§40.3. The block identity

In Chapter 10, unfolding \(\langle P_n,P_m\rangle\) and expanding \(P_n\) group the terms by double cosets of \(\Gamma_\infty\).

Problem 40.3.1. In this problem and the next paragraph \(k\), \(m\), \(n\), \(c\) and \(P_n\) are the weight, the two indices, the lower left entry and the Poincaré series of Chapter 10. By Problems 10.1.1 and 10.2.4, \(\langle P_n,P_m\rangle=\Gamma(k-1)(4\pi m)^{1-k}p_n(m)\). In the series for \(P_n\), the \(m\)-th Fourier coefficient of the sum of the terms with \(\gamma\in\Gamma_\infty\) is \(\delta(n,m)\), and for each \(c\ge1\) that of the sum of the terms with lower left entry \(\pm c\) is \(2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k}(m/n)^{(k-1)/2}c^{-1}S(n,m;c)J_{k-1}(4\pi\sqrt{mn}/c)\).

In Theorem 40.3.2 below, the rational points of the Bessel subgroup \(R=\mathcal U\rtimes U(X_0)\) replace \(\Gamma_\infty\), a Bessel function at each infinite place replaces \(e(mz)\), a pair of a class of \(U(X_0)\) and a Bessel coordinate replaces \(m\), and the sum over the rational \(\gamma\) outside the Bessel subgroup with reduction \(s\) replaces the Kloosterman terms \(S(n,m;c)J_{k-1}\); the term \(\delta(n,m)\) becomes \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)\). The reduction \(s\in\bar G\) has no counterpart at level one. The Bessel functions are the following.

At each infinite place \(\tau\), use the Haar measure of Theorem 41.2 on the real unitary group of \(Y_0\), and the quotient measure of Section 42.3 on \(\mathcal U_\tau\backslash G_\tau\).

Theorem 40.3.1 (Bessel functions). For the data, at each infinite place \(\tau\) there are a finite nonempty set \(I_\tau\), a number \(d_\tau>0\) equal to the formal degree of the discrete series of the real unitary group \(G_\tau\) of \(Y_0\) with doubled Harish-Chandra parameter \(2\varsigma_\tau\), an irreducible unitary representation of \(U(X_0)_\tau\) on \(\mathbb{C}^{I_\tau}\) of the source highest weight given in Definition 37.2.1 and the paragraph following it, and continuous functions \(W_\tau,W_\tau^\flat\colon G_\tau\to\mathbb{C}^{I_\tau}\) such that:

  1. \(W_\tau(xy)=\psi_{\mathcal U}(x)W_\tau(y)\) for \(x\in\mathcal U_\tau\), and \(W_\tau(hy)\) is the representation applied to \(W_\tau(y)\) for \(h\in U(X_0)_\tau\); the same holds for \(W_\tau^\flat\);

  2. \(\int_{\mathcal U_\tau\backslash G_\tau}W_{\tau,i}\overline{W_{\tau,j}}=\delta_{ij}\) for \(i,j\in I_\tau\);

  3. \(\int_{G_\tau}\bigl\lvert\int_{\mathcal U_\tau\backslash G_\tau}\langle W_\tau(y),W_\tau(yg)\rangle\,dy\bigr\rvert^2dg=(\#I_\tau)^2/d_\tau\);

  4. by construction, the Bessel truncation \(W_\tau^\flat\) is \(W_\tau\) times a function of the Bessel length of \(y\) (a measure of the size of \(y\) modulo the Bessel subgroup on the left) with values in \([0,1]\), equal to \(1\) up to half the radius \(b^{6n+2}\), \(n=\varrho+1\), and to \(0\) at and beyond the radius; and \(\int_{\mathcal U_\tau\backslash G_\tau}\lVert W_\tau-W_\tau^\flat\rVert^2\le b^{-2r^3f}\,\#I_\tau\);

with further bounds that later sheets use.

§40.4. The Bessel subgroup and near isometry

The translates by \(\bar R_0\) keep the special role they have in Problem 40.1.3. In Problems 40.4.1–40.4.5, let \(\rho\) be a unitary representation of \(\bar G\) on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space and \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) a linear map into it from a complex inner product space of finite dimension \(D_X\). Use \(\mathcal K_s={\mathcal P^\circ}^*\rho(s){\mathcal P^\circ}\), a subgroup \(\bar R_0\subseteq\bar G\), \(D_{\mathrm{id}}=\#\bar R_0\cdot D_X\), and \(b>0\).

Problem 40.4.1. For each \(t\in\bar R_0\), let \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) be a linear endomorphism of the source of \({\mathcal P^\circ}\). If \(\mathcal K_{st}=\mathcal K_s\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) for all \(s\in\bar G\) and \(t\in\bar R_0\), then \(\rho(t){\mathcal P^\circ}={\mathcal P^\circ}\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) for \(t\in\bar R_0\).

Used in: Problem 40.4.2; Chapter 39

Problem 40.4.2. Under the hypotheses of Problem 40.4.1, assume also that \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) is a unitary representation of \(\bar R_0\). Then \(\mathcal K_{ts}=\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\mathcal K_s\) for \(t\in\bar R_0\), \(s\in\bar G\), and \(\lVert\mathcal K_t-\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\rVert=\lVert{\mathcal P^\circ}^*{\mathcal P^\circ}-1\rVert\) for \(t\in\bar R_0\). Moreover \(\sum_{t\in\bar R_0}\lVert\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\rVert^2=D_{\mathrm{id}}\).

Used in: Problem 40.4.3; Chapter 43; Chapter 48

Problem 40.4.3. Under the hypotheses of Problem 40.4.2, if \(\sum_{t\in\bar R_0}\lVert\mathcal K_t-\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\rVert^2\le b^{-12}D_{\mathrm{id}}\), then \(\lVert{\mathcal P^\circ}^*{\mathcal P^\circ}-1\rVert^2\le b^{-12}D_X\).

Used in: Problem 40.1.7; Chapter 43; Chapter 47; Chapter 48

Problem 40.4.4. If \(\lVert{\mathcal P^\circ}^*{\mathcal P^\circ}-1\rVert^2\le b^{-12}D_X\) and \(b\ge1000\), then \(\lVert\mathcal K_1\rVert^2\le2D_X\).

Used in: Chapter 43; Chapter 47

Problem 40.4.5. \(s\mapsto\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\) is of positive type, and \(\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\le\lVert\mathcal K_1\rVert^2\) for every \(s\in\bar G\).

Used in: Chapter 47

Problem 40.4.6. In Problem 40.1.6, \(D_{\mathrm{id}}=4\) and \(D_{\mathrm{main}}=16\) (with \(\deg\) and \(\mathrm{mass}\) as in Section 40.2), so \(D_{\mathrm{id}}/D_{\mathrm{main}}=1/4\). For the data \(D_{\mathrm{id}}/D_{\mathrm{main}}\le b^{-11kf}\): if \(\lVert\mathcal K_1\rVert^2\le2D_X\), the elements of \(\bar R_0\) contribute at most \(2b^{-11kf}D_{\mathrm{main}}\) to \(\sum_s\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\), so a second moment of size about \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\) must come almost entirely from outside \(\bar R_0\).

For the data, the squared-error bound assumed in Problem 40.4.3 follows from Theorem 43.3.3, since each strip contains \(\bar R_0\) and the terms with \(s\notin\bar R_0\) can be dropped. In Problem 43.3.3, the squared error at each \(s\) is \(\nu^{-2}\) times the sum over class pairs of the squared norms of the amplitude sums restricted to \(U(Y_0)(F)\smallsetminus R(F)\). Theorem 43.3.3 bounds the sum of these errors on each strip.

We have proved, in the finite model, the unfolding of the Gram operators, the role of the Bessel subgroup and the least second moment; for the data we have stated the block identity (Theorem 40.3.2) from the proof and the Bessel functions (Theorem 40.3.1), with the calculation for the full function in (a)–(c) given in Chapter 42, and derived from the block identity the norm formula of Problem 40.3.2, and from \(\mathcal K_{st}=\mathcal K_s\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) the passage from the Bessel subgroup to near isometry and to \(\lVert\mathcal K_1\rVert^2\le2D_X\). Also taken as given: the two properties of \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) recalled from Definition 39.4.2, the identity \(\mathcal K_{st}=\mathcal K_s\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) for the data, and the two inequalities between \(D_{\mathrm{id}}\) and \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\). Chapter 43 bounds the second moment of the off-Bessel amplitude sums on each strip. The estimate over all of \(\bar G\) compares the full second moment \(\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\) with \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\).

Schur orthogonality: \(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\lvert\langle\pi(\gamma)v,v'\rangle\rvert^2=\#\Gamma\lVert v\rVert^2\lVert v'\rVert^2/\dim V_\pi\); apply it to an orthonormal basis of \(V_X\) and use Problem 40.1.2. For the equality, compute \(\dim\mathcal Y\).

40.2.2

A monomial matrix sends a monomial to a multiple of a monomial.

§40.3

40.3.1

Use Problems 10.2.4 and 10.2.1; for \(c\ne0\) the terms with lower left entry \(\pm c\) are indexed by \(d\) modulo \(c\) and an integer translation, and \(\Gamma_\infty\) contains \(\pm1\).

40.3.2

Problem 9.2.1 with the coordinate basis; complex conjugation does not change absolute values.

§40.4

40.4.1

Compute \({\mathcal P^\circ}^*\rho(s^{-1})d\) for \(d:=\rho(t){\mathcal P^\circ}x-{\mathcal P^\circ}\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)x\).

40.4.2

Take adjoints in \(\rho(t^{-1}){\mathcal P^\circ}={\mathcal P^\circ}\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t^{-1})\). Multiplication by a unitary does not change a Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and \(\mathcal K_t=\mathcal K_1\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\).

40.4.3

Problem 40.4.2: every term of the sum equals \(\lVert{\mathcal P^\circ}^*{\mathcal P^\circ}-1\rVert^2\).

40.4.4

\(\mathcal K_1={\mathcal P^\circ}^*{\mathcal P^\circ}\), \(\lVert1\rVert^2=D_X\), and the triangle inequality.

40.4.5

Problem 9.2.2 and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality; \(\lVert{\mathcal P^\circ}{\mathcal P^\circ}^*\rVert=\lVert{\mathcal P^\circ}^*{\mathcal P^\circ}\rVert\).
