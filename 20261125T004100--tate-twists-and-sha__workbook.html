§27.1. Tate twists over \(\mathbb{Q}\) Tag 22T3

In the previous chapter Bernoulli values gave the orders of odd eigenspaces of a class group. Which Galois cohomology groups account for the numerators and denominators of zeta values? Let \(\mathbb{Q}_S\) be the largest extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\) unramified outside \(\ell\) and \(\infty\), \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell} := \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_S/\mathbb{Q})\), and \(\chi_\ell \colon \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}) \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\) the cyclotomic character, which factors through \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\). For \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)\) is \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) with \(\sigma\) acting by \(\chi_\ell(\sigma)^k\), and similarly for \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell(k)\), \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)\) and \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell(k)\). Let \(W_e := \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(-e)\), and let \(G_\ell := \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)\), mapped to \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\) by restriction along a fixed embedding \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\).

Definition.

We define the Tate–Shafarevich group of the twist \(e\) by \[\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) := \ker\bigl( H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, W_e) \to H^1(G_\ell, W_e) \bigr) ,\] and \(h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q}) \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots, \infty\}\) is its length as a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-module. For a number field \(E\) one defines \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) and \(h_{\ell,e}(E)\) in the same way, with the local Galois groups at all primes of \(E\) above \(\ell\).

Tag 22TB

Problem 27.1.6. Let \(e \ge 1\) be odd. Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) \ne 0\) if and only if \(\ell \mid \operatorname{num}(B_{e+1}/(e+1))\). For example, \(\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q}, 11) \ne 0\) and \(\mathrm{Sha}_{37}(\mathbb{Q}, 31) \ne 0\).

We state four inputs as black boxes. By Soulé [So], \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) is finite for \(k \ge 2\). By Poitou–Tate duality [NSW, Ch. VIII, §6], \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) is dual to the kernel of \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1)) \to H^2(G_\ell, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\), and by local duality [NSW, Ch. VII, §2] \(H^2(G_\ell, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) is dual to \(H^0(G_\ell, \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1-k))\). By Tate’s global Euler characteristic formula [NSW, Ch. VIII, §7], for every finite \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\)-module \(M\) of \(\ell\)-power order, \(\lvert H^0(M) \rvert \lvert H^2(M) \rvert / \lvert H^1(M) \rvert = \lvert M^{c = 1} \rvert / \lvert M \rvert\), where \(c\) is a complex conjugation. Since \(\ell\) is odd, \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\) has \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension at most \(2\) [NSW, Ch. VIII, §3], so \(H^3(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, M) = 0\) for every \(\ell\)-primary \(M\).

§27.2. Odd characters Tag 22TG

Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the odd twists are controlled by Bernoulli numbers. How does this look over an abelian field, and what does it say about sums over all \(\ell\)?

Let \(E \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)\) and \(\Delta_E := \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\). Then \(\Delta_E\) acts on \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\), by \((\delta z)(g) := \tilde\delta\, z(\tilde\delta^{-1} g \tilde\delta)\) for a lift \(\tilde\delta\) of \(\delta\), and \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) is the direct sum of its \(\eta\)-parts over the characters \(\eta\) of \(\Delta_E\), with values in \(\mu_{\ell-1} \subset \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\). Let \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e; \eta)\) be defined as \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\), with \(W_e\) replaced by \(W_e \otimes \eta^{-1}\). Call a character \(\eta\) critical for \(e\) if \(\eta(-1) = (-1)^{e+1}\), and plus for \(e\) otherwise. We call the sum of the \(\eta\)-parts over the critical characters the minus part of \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\); it is the part on which complex conjugation acts by \((-1)^{e+1}\).

§27.3. Odd cocycles Tag 236D

In this section \(\ell\) is any prime. Fix an embedding \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \subset \mathbb{C}\), and let \(c \in \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\) be complex conjugation. Let \(E \subset \overline{\mathbb{Q}}\) be a CM field, \(G_E := \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\), and \(\chi := \chi_\ell|_{G_E}\). Then \(c G_E c = G_E\) and \(\chi(cgc) = \chi(g)\). For a place \(\mathfrak{P}\) of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\) let \(D_{\mathfrak{P}} \supseteq I_{\mathfrak{P}}\) be its decomposition and inertia groups in \(G_E\). Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be the ring of integers of a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\), with valuation \(v_\mathcal{O}\) normalized by \(v_\mathcal{O}(\pi) = 1\) on a uniformizer \(\pi\) and \(v_\mathcal{O}(0) = \infty\); for \(n \ge 0\) let \(\mathcal{O}_n := \mathcal{O}/\ell^n\mathcal{O}\), and write \(\mathcal{O}_n(-e)\) for this quotient with \(g \in G_E\) acting by \(\chi(g)^{-e}\). We write \(\operatorname{len}\) for length over \(\mathcal{O}\), with values in \(\{0, 1, \dots, \infty\}\).

Tag 236E

Definition 27.3.1. For integers \(e, n \ge 0\), \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_\ell(E, \mathcal{O}; e, n)\) is the \(\mathcal{O}\)-module of maps \(z \colon G_E \to \mathcal{O}_n\) such that:

  1. \(z\) is locally constant and \(z(gh) = z(g) + \chi(g)^{-e} z(h)\) for all \(g, h \in G_E\);

  2. \(z = 0\) on \(I_{\mathfrak{P}}\) for every \(\mathfrak{P}\) above a prime other than \(\ell\);

  3. for every \(\mathfrak{P}\) above \(\ell\): \(z = 0\) on \(I_{\mathfrak{P}}\) if \(e = 0\), and \(z = 0\) on \(D_{\mathfrak{P}} \cap \ker \chi\) if \(e > 0\);

  4. if \(e > 0\), then \(z(cgc) = -z(g)\) for all \(g \in G_E\).

Used in: Chapter 26; Chapter 28; Chapter 83; Chapter 84

These are cocycles, not classes: nothing is divided out. In (c) the kernel is that of the \(\ell\)-adic character, not of its reduction modulo \(\ell^n\). For \(e > 0\) this module is modelled on the odd part of \(\mathrm{Sha}^1(E,\mathcal{O}_n(-e))\); the proof does not compare the two. Problems 27.3.1 and 27.3.2 explain the shape of the definition.

27.1.8

Apply the Euler characteristic formula to \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)/\ell^n\) and let \(n \to \infty\); since the groups \(H^i(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)/\ell^n)\) are finite, their inverse limit is \(H^i(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) [NSW, Ch. II, §7]. The groups \(H^i(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, M)\) are finite for finite \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\)-modules \(M\) of \(\ell\)-power order [NSW, Ch. VIII, §3]; finite generation of \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) follows from finiteness of its reduction modulo \(\ell\) by Nakayama’s lemma, and \(H^3 = 0\) passes to the limit.

§27.3

27.3.1

\(\chi\) is trivial on inertia at primes other than \(\ell\), and \(\chi(cgc) = \chi(g)\); \(2\) is a unit in \(\mathcal{O}_n\).

27.3.2

\(\chi_\ell(c) = -1\). Conjugation by \(c\) carries \(D_{\mathfrak{P}}\), \(I_{\mathfrak{P}}\) to \(D_{c\mathfrak{P}}\), \(I_{c\mathfrak{P}}\).

27.3.3

A homomorphism killing all inertia groups factors through the Galois group of the Hilbert class field; \(E\) has no real places.

27.3.4

\(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}, \mathcal{O}_n) \cong \mathcal{O}/(m, \ell^n)\), and \(\mathcal{O}/\pi^k\mathcal{O}\) has length \(k\).

27.3.5

The class number of \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-23})\) is \(3\).

27.3.6

\(\chi(g) \equiv 1 \pmod \ell\) on \(G_F\).

27.3.7

27.3.8
