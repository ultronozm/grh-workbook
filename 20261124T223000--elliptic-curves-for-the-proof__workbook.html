Prerequisites.

Complex analysis: Liouville’s theorem, the argument principle, Weierstrass products, and Euler’s Gamma function with the Beta integral and the reflection formula. The Gaussian integers. Elliptic curves over \(\mathbb{C}\) at the level of the statements of [Si1, Ch. VI]. No earlier chapter is used. Chapter 13, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime, continues this one.

§11.1. Complex tori Tag 22BS

How does one compute on a curve of genus one over \(\mathbb{C}\)? One uniformizes it. Let \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}\) be a lattice, and for integers \(k \ge 3\) let \(G_k(\Lambda) := \sum'_{\omega \in \Lambda} \omega^{-k}\), where the prime means that \(\omega = 0\) is omitted. The Weierstrass function \[\wp(u) := \frac{1}{u^2} + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda}{}' \Bigl( \frac{1}{(u - \omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2} \Bigr)\] is elliptic, that is meromorphic and \(\Lambda\)-periodic. Let \(g_2 := 60 G_4(\Lambda)\) and \(g_3 := 140 G_6(\Lambda)\). The map \(u \mapsto (\wp(u), \wp'(u))\) for \(u \notin \Lambda\), extended by sending the zero class to the point at infinity, is an analytic isomorphism of groups from \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) onto the smooth projective cubic with affine equation \(y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3\), and every elliptic curve over \(\mathbb{C}\) arises in this way [Si1].

Tag 22BT

Problem 11.1.1. Let \(r := \min_{\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\lvert\omega\rvert\). For \(0<\lvert u\rvert<r\), \(\wp(u) = u^{-2} + \sum_{k \ge 1}(2k+1)G_{2k+2}(\Lambda)\,u^{2k}\). As meromorphic functions, \(\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - g_2\wp - g_3\).

Used in: Chapter 89

Let \[\zeta(u) := \frac1u + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda}{}' \Bigl( \frac{1}{u - \omega} + \frac{1}{\omega} + \frac{u}{\omega^2} \Bigr), \qquad \sigma(u) := u \prod_{\omega \in \Lambda}{}' \Bigl(1 - \frac{u}{\omega}\Bigr) e^{u/\omega + \frac{u^2}{2\omega^2}} .\] Then \(\zeta' = -\wp\) and \(\sigma'/\sigma = \zeta\). The function \(\sigma\) is entire and odd, with simple zeros exactly at the points of \(\Lambda\).

Tag 22BU

Problem 11.1.2. There is a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-linear map \(\eta \colon \Lambda \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(\zeta(u + \omega) = \zeta(u) + \eta(\omega)\).

Tag 22BV

Problem 11.1.3. If \(\omega_1, \omega_2\) is a basis of \(\Lambda\) with \(\operatorname{Im}(\omega_2/\omega_1) > 0\), then \(\eta(\omega_1)\omega_2 - \eta(\omega_2)\omega_1 = 2\pi\mathrm{i}\).

This is Legendre’s relation.

Tag 22BW

Problem 11.1.4. \(\sigma(u + \omega) = \psi(\omega)\, e^{\eta(\omega)(u + \omega/2)} \sigma(u)\) for \(\omega \in \Lambda\), where \(\psi(\omega) = 1\) if \(\omega \in 2\Lambda\) and \(\psi(\omega) = -1\) otherwise.

Tag 22BX

Problem 11.1.5. As a meromorphic identity, \(\dfrac{\sigma(u+v)\,\sigma(u-v)}{\sigma(u)^2\,\sigma(v)^2} = \wp(v) - \wp(u)\).

Tag 22BY

Problem 11.1.6. As a meromorphic identity, \(\zeta(u+v) - \zeta(u) - \zeta(v) = \dfrac12 \, \dfrac{\wp'(u) - \wp'(v)}{\wp(u) - \wp(v)}\). Consequently, as meromorphic functions, \[\wp(u+v) = \frac14 \Bigl( \frac{\wp'(u) - \wp'(v)}{\wp(u) - \wp(v)} \Bigr)^2 - \wp(u) - \wp(v) .\]

Used in: Chapter 13

Now let \(\Lambda\) be square, that is \(\mathrm{i}\Lambda = \Lambda\).

Tag 22BZ

Problem 11.1.7. If \(\mathrm{i}\Lambda = \Lambda\), then \(\wp(\mathrm{i}u) = -\wp(u)\), \(g_3 = 0\), and \(\eta(\mathrm{i}\omega) = -\mathrm{i}\,\eta(\omega)\).

Used in: Chapter 13; Chapter 89
Tag 22C2

Problem 11.1.8. There is exactly one real \(\lambda > 0\) with \(g_2(\lambda\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) = 4\).

Let \(\varpi := 2 \int_0^1 (1 - t^4)^{-1/2}\, d t = 2.62205\ldots\) be the lemniscate constant.

Tag 22C3

Problem 11.1.9. The lattice \(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) has \(g_2 = 4\) and \(g_3 = 0\). Moreover \(\varpi = \Gamma(\tfrac14)^2 / (2\sqrt{2\pi})\).

Used in: Chapter 15; Chapter 20; Chapter 89

From now on, we call \(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) the lemniscatic lattice, and \(\wp\), \(\zeta\), \(\sigma\) without further mention are its Weierstrass functions. With \(x = \wp\) and \(y = \wp'/2\) it is the lattice of the curve \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), and \(dx/2y\) pulls back to \(d u\).

§11.2. Theta functions Tag 22C4

The function \(\sigma\) is not periodic, but by Problem 11.1.4 it changes by an exponential factor under the lattice. Entire functions with such a law are theta functions. How many are there, and how are they made?

Let \(\tau\) lie in the upper half-plane, and let \(\vartheta(z) := \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{\pi\mathrm{i}n^2\tau + 2\pi\mathrm{i}n z}\).

Tag 22C5

Problem 11.2.1. \(\vartheta(z+1) = \vartheta(z)\), \(\vartheta(z + \tau) = e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\tau - 2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\vartheta(z)\), and \(\vartheta(-z) = \vartheta(z)\).

Used in: Chapter 12
Tag 22C6

Problem 11.2.2. Modulo \(\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau\), the only zero of \(\vartheta\) is \((1+\tau)/2\), and it is simple.

Tag 22C7

Problem 11.2.3. Let \(\sigma\) and \(\eta\) belong to \(\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\). Then there is a constant \(c\) with \[\sigma(u) = c\, e^{\eta(1) u^2/2 + \pi\mathrm{i}u}\, \vartheta\bigl(u + \tfrac{1+\tau}{2}\bigr) .\]

The theta functions of Chapter 15 are built from the Gaussian integers: \[\Theta(z) := \sum_{\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} e^{-\pi\lvert\lambda\rvert^2 + \lambda z}, \qquad \theta(z) := \sum_{a \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-\pi a^2 + a z} .\]

Tag 22C8

Problem 11.2.4. \(\Theta(z) = \theta(z)\,\theta(\mathrm{i}z)\), \(\Theta(\mathrm{i}z) = \Theta(z)\), and for \(\beta \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) \[\Theta(z + 2\pi\beta) = e_\beta(z)\, \Theta(z), \qquad e_\beta(z) := e^{\pi\lvert\beta\rvert^2 + \bar\beta z} .\]

Let \(T\) be the space of entire functions \(f\) with \(f(z + 2\pi\beta) = e_\beta(z) f(z)\) for all \(\beta \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

Tag 22C9

Problem 11.2.5. \(\dim T = 2\), and every \(f \in T\) is even.

Tag 22CA

Problem 11.2.6. A nonzero \(f\in T\) has exactly two zeros modulo \(2\pi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), counted with multiplicity.

Tag 22CB

Problem 11.2.7. If \(f \in T\), then \(f(\mathrm{i}z) \in T\). The \(f \in T\) with \(f(\mathrm{i}z) = f(z)\) form the line \(\mathbb{C}\,\Theta\).

Used in: Chapter 15

The last fact about \(\sigma\) that we need concerns a sigma quotient associated with the theorem of the cube [Mu, §10].

Tag 22CC

Problem 11.2.8. The quotient \[\Xi(x, y, w) := \frac{\sigma(x+y+w)\,\sigma(x)\,\sigma(y)\,\sigma(w)}{\sigma(x+y)\,\sigma(y+w)\,\sigma(w+x)}\] defines a meromorphic function on \(\mathbb{C}^3\) that is \(\Lambda\)-periodic in each variable. As a meromorphic identity, \(1/\Xi = \zeta(x) + \zeta(y) + \zeta(w) - \zeta(x+y+w)\).

Chapter 15 expresses \(\Theta\) through \(\sigma\) and \(\wp\) (Problem 15.1.3); then the \(n\)th derivative at \(0\) is \(\Theta(0)\) times a rational multiple of \((\varpi/2\pi)^n\). So one needs to know \(\varpi\), and the rational numbers that \(\wp\) produces.

§11.3. Periods Tag 22CD

By Problem 11.1.1, the coefficient of \(u^{2k}\) in the Laurent expansion of \(\wp\) is \((2k+1)G_{2k+2}(\Lambda)\) for \(k \ge 1\). For the lemniscatic lattice these coefficients are rational. Write \(\mu\) for an element of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

Tag 22CE

Problem 11.3.1. \(G_k(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) \in \mathbb{Q}\) for every integer \(k \ge 3\), and \(G_k = 0\) unless \(4 \mid k\). For each integer \(k\ge1\), define the Hurwitz number \(H_{4k}\) by \[\sum_{\mu}{}' \mu^{-4k} = \frac{(2\varpi)^{4k}}{(4k)!}\, H_{4k} .\] Then \(H_4 = \tfrac{1}{10}\) and \(H_8 = \tfrac{3}{10}\).

Used in: Chapter 23; Chapter 89

When \(\bar\mu\) also appears, \(\pi\) enters. Summed first over \(m\) and then over \(n\), Eisenstein’s series \(G_2(\tau) := \sum_n \sum_m{}' (m + n\tau)^{-2}\) satisfies \(G_2(-1/\tau) = \tau^2 G_2(\tau) - 2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau\) [We].

Tag 22CF

Problem 11.3.2. \(G_2(\mathrm{i}) = \pi\).

For integers \(j \ge 0\) and \(k > j + 2\), let \(S(j, k) := \sum'_\mu \bar\mu^j \mu^{-k}\).

Tag 22CG

Problem 11.3.3. \(S(j, k) = 0\) unless \(j + k \equiv 0 \pmod 4\), and \(S(0, k) = \varpi^k G_k(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}])\).

Damerell proved that \(S(j, k)\) is an algebraic multiple of \(\varpi^{j+k}\pi^{-j}\) [Dam]; for instance \(S(1, 7) = \tfrac{2}{315}\varpi^8/\pi\).

How big is \(\varpi\) in terms of classical constants? Let \(\chi\) be the non-trivial character modulo \(4\). In the rest of this section, \(\eta(\tau) := e^{\pi\mathrm{i}\tau/12}\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}n\tau})\) is Dedekind’s function, and \(\zeta(s)\), with argument \(s\), is Riemann’s.

Tag 22CH

Problem 11.3.4. For \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), \(\sum'_\mu \lvert\mu\rvert^{-2s} = 4\,\zeta(s)\,L(s, \chi)\).

Used in: Chapter 20; Chapter 23

Let \(\gamma\) be Euler’s constant. Kronecker’s first limit formula states that for \(\tau = x + \mathrm{i}y\) with \(y > 0\), as \(s \to 1\) from \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), \[\sum_{(m,n) \ne (0,0)} \frac{y^s}{\lvert m + n\tau\rvert^{2s}} = \frac{\pi}{s-1} + 2\pi\bigl(\gamma - \log 2 - \log(\sqrt{y}\,\lvert\eta(\tau)\rvert^2)\bigr) + O(s-1)\] [La, Ch. 20]; Theorem 20.1.1 of Chapter 20 proves it.

Tag 22CJ

Problem 11.3.5. \(L'(1, \chi)/L(1, \chi) = \gamma - 2\log 2 - 4\log\eta(\mathrm{i})\).

Used in: Chapter 20

The discriminant \(\Delta(\Lambda) := g_2^3 - 27g_3^2\) satisfies \(\Delta(\lambda\Lambda) = \lambda^{-12}\Delta(\Lambda)\) and \(\Delta(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau) = (2\pi)^{12}\eta(\tau)^{24}\) [SeA].

Tag 22CK

Problem 11.3.6. \(\eta(\mathrm{i})^2 = \varpi/(\sqrt2\,\pi)\), and so \(\eta(\mathrm{i}) = \Gamma(\tfrac14)/(2\pi^{3/4})\).

Used in: Chapter 20

Problems 11.1.9, 11.3.5 and 11.3.6 prove the following.

Tag 22CL

Theorem 11.3.1. \(\dfrac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)} = \gamma + 2\log 2 + 3\log\pi - 4\log\Gamma(\tfrac14) = 0.24560\ldots\)

Used in: Chapter 14; Chapter 23

Chowla and Selberg found the general form [CS]. Chapter 20 proves it, together with Kronecker’s limit formula, and states the period relation of Gross [Gr]: for an elliptic curve over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal{O}_K\), every nonzero period of a holomorphic differential defined over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) is a nonzero algebraic multiple of an explicit product \(\Omega_K\) of values of \(\Gamma\). For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) this product is \(\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(\tfrac14)/\Gamma(\tfrac34)\).

Tag 22CM

Problem 11.3.7. \(\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(\tfrac14)/\Gamma(\tfrac34) = 2\varpi\). Moreover \[\Omega := \frac{2\varpi^2}{\pi} = \frac{\Gamma(\tfrac14)^4}{4\pi^2} = 4\pi\,\eta(\mathrm{i})^4 .\]

Used in: Chapter 20; Chapter 23

For an imaginary quadratic field \(K\) with quadratic character \(\chi_K\), Chapter 20 relates \(L'(1,\chi_K)/L(1,\chi_K)\) to the period product \(\Omega_K\), and Theorem 11.3.1 is its value for \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\). Chapter 36 defines the period constant \(\Omega = \exp\!\left(\gamma - \operatorname{Re}\!\left(\frac{L'(1,\chi_K)}{L(1,\chi_K)}\right)\right)\cdot 2\pi/\mathrm{rd}_E\), where \(E\) is the CM field of the data and \(\mathrm{rd}_E\) its root discriminant. It also defines \(s_\infty(j) = \log(j!) + j\log(M/\Omega)\) for integers \(j \ge 0\), where \(M = p_0p_1\) is the product of the two primes of the principal level. For \(E = K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), which is not a field of the data, \(\mathrm{rd}_E = 2\), and Theorem 11.3.1 shows that the same expression is the \(\Omega\) of Problem 11.3.7.

In the Lean formalization.

The corresponding statements of the Lean proof [GL] are the definitions Parameters.bigOmega (in grh/src/New/Reduction/Data/FieldsAndPlaces.lean), Data.M and Data.jetSize (in grh/src/New/Reduction/Data/AdmissibleData.lean); bigOmega at the tower exponent of the data is \(\Omega\), and jetSize, which the code describes as the size of a derivative of order \(j\), is \(s_\infty\).

Hints

§11.1

11.1.1

Expand \((u - \omega)^{-2} - \omega^{-2}\) in powers of \(u\). The function \(\wp'^2 - 4\wp^3 + g_2\wp + g_3\) is elliptic, has no poles, and vanishes at \(0\).

11.1.2

The derivative of \(\zeta(u+\omega) - \zeta(u)\) is \(0\).

11.1.3

Integrate \(\zeta\) around a period parallelogram whose boundary avoids \(\Lambda\).

11.1.4

The logarithmic derivative of \(\sigma(u+\omega)/\sigma(u)\) is \(\eta(\omega)\). To find the constant for \(\omega \notin 2\Lambda\), use that \(\sigma\) is odd; for nonzero \(\omega \in 2\Lambda\), apply the law twice with \(\omega/2\), by induction on the power of \(2\) dividing \(\omega\).

11.1.5

For \(v\notin\Lambda\), both sides are elliptic in \(u\) with the same zeros and poles. Compare the leading terms at \(u = 0\).

11.1.6

Take the logarithmic derivative of Problem 11.1.5 in \(u\) and in \(v\), and add. For the second formula, differentiate the first in \(u\).

11.1.7

\(G_k(\mathrm{i}\Lambda) = \mathrm{i}^{-k} G_k(\Lambda)\).

11.1.8

\(g_2(\lambda\Lambda) = \lambda^{-4} g_2(\Lambda)\), and the values of \(\wp\) at the three nonzero half-period classes of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) are real, distinct, and are the roots of \(4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3\).

11.1.9

Let \(\lambda\) be as in Problem 11.1.8. Then \(\wp(\mathrm{i}\lambda/2) = -\wp(\lambda/2)\) and \(\wp((1+\mathrm{i})\lambda/2) = 0\). On \((0, \lambda/2]\), \(\wp\) is real and decreasing, and \(\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - 4\wp\). Substitute \(x = t^{-2}\). For the Gamma value use the Beta integral and \(\Gamma(\tfrac14)\Gamma(\tfrac34) = \pi\sqrt2\).

§11.2

11.2.1

Replace \(n\) by \(n + 1\) and by \(-n\).

11.2.2

Argument principle, as in Problem 11.1.3. At \(z = (1+\tau)/2\), pair the terms \(n\) and \(-1-n\).

11.2.3

Both sides have simple zeros exactly at \(\Lambda\), and the same law under \(u \mapsto u+1\) and \(u \mapsto u + \tau\), by Problem 11.1.4 and Legendre’s relation.

11.2.4

Write \(\lambda = a + b\mathrm{i}\). For the law, substitute \(\lambda = \mu + \bar\beta\) and note \(\operatorname{Im}(\mu\beta) \in \mathbb{Z}\).

11.2.5

\(e^{-z^2/4\pi} f(z)\) has period \(2\pi\). Expand it in a Fourier series \(\sum_n a_n e^{\mathrm{i}n z}\), and use \(\beta = \mathrm{i}\).

11.2.6

Integrate \(f'/f\) around a parallelogram with sides \(2\pi\) and \(2\pi\mathrm{i}\) whose boundary contains no zero of \(f\).

11.2.7

If \(f(\mathrm{i}z) = f(z)\), only powers \(z^{4k}\) occur in the Taylor series of \(f\). Use Problem 11.2.6 and \(\Theta(0) > 0\).

11.2.8

Use Problem 11.1.4 in \(x\). For fixed \(y,w\) such that \(0\), \(-y-w\), \(-y\), \(-w\) are distinct modulo \(\Lambda\), compare the zeros and poles in \(x\) of \(\Xi\) and \(1/(\zeta(x) + \zeta(y) + \zeta(w) - \zeta(x+y+w))\), then compare their leading terms at \(x=0\).

§11.3

11.3.1

\(g_2 = 4\) and \(g_3 = 0\). Differentiate \(\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - 4\wp\) to get \(\wp'' = 6\wp^2 - 2\), and compare coefficients in Problem 11.1.1.

11.3.3

Replace \(\mu\) by \(\mathrm{i}\mu\).

11.3.4

Count the \(\mu\) of norm \(n\) by unique factorization in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

11.3.5

Take \(\tau = \mathrm{i}\), and compare with the Laurent expansion of \(4\zeta(s)L(s, \chi)\) at \(s = 1\). \(L(1, \chi) = \pi/4\).

11.3.6

\(\Delta(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) = 64\), and \(\eta(\mathrm{i}) > 0\).

11.3.7

\(\Gamma(\tfrac14)\Gamma(\tfrac34) = \pi\sqrt2\), and Problems 11.1.9 and 11.3.6.
