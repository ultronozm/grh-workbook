Prerequisites.

Legendre’s formula \(v_p(r!) = \sum_{e \ge 1} \lfloor r/p^e \rfloor\), Stirling’s formula, Chebyshev’s bound (Problem 5.1.1 in Chapter 5), and the arithmetic of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\). The invariant differential and complex uniformization of an elliptic curve [Si1, Ch. VI], and supersingular reduction [Si1]. The Jacobi theta function \(\theta_1\) [DLMF], Cauchy’s inequality and the argument principle. Section 14.6 also uses the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions [MV]. From Chapter 11, Complex tori, theta functions and periods: Jacobi’s function \(\vartheta\) (Section 11.2). From Chapter 13, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime: the congruence \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\) for the invariant derivation of a one-dimensional formal group over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) (Section 13.2) and the supersingular reduction of \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) at the primes inert in \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) (Section 13.3). Chapter 15, The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime, treats the derivation \(D^p\) for the lemniscate.

§14.1. Mertens from the factorial Tag 22EB

Throughout, \(p\) denotes a prime. Let \(\theta(X) := \sum_{p \le X} \log p\). How much of \(\log r!\) comes from each prime? Chebyshev’s bound \(\theta(2n) - \theta(n) \le n \log 4\) is Problem 5.1.1 of Chapter 5.

Tag 22EC

Problem 14.1.1. \(\theta(X) < 2X\log 4\) for \(X \ge 1\).

Tag 22ED

Problem 14.1.2. For each integer \(r \ge 1\), \(-\theta(r) \le \log r! - r \sum_{p \le r} \frac{\log p}{p} \le r \sum_p \frac{\log p}{p(p-1)}\).

With Stirling’s formula, Problems 14.1.1 and 14.1.2 prove the following [Mer].

Tag 22EE

Theorem 14.1.1 (Mertens). \(\sum_{p \le X} \log p/p = \log X + O(1)\) for \(X \ge 2\).

How much of \(\log r!\) comes from each odd class modulo \(4\)? Let \[S_a(X) := \sum_{p \le X,\ p \equiv a \ (4)} \frac{\log p}{p}.\]

Tag 22EF

Theorem 14.1.2. \(S_a(X) = \frac12 \log X + O(1)\) for \(a = 1, 3\) and \(X \ge 2\).

Let \(\chi_{-4}\) be the primitive real Dirichlet character modulo \(4\). Theorem 14.1.2 is classical, and the usual proof rests on \(L(1, \chi_{-4}) \ne 0\) [MV, Ch. 4]. We replace the factorial by one determinant for each class.

§14.2. The Gaussian Vandermonde Tag 22EG

Let \(r \ge 1\) be an integer, and let \(z_1, \dots, z_r\) be distinct Gaussian integers with \(0 \le \operatorname{Re} z_j, \operatorname{Im} z_j < \lceil \sqrt r \rceil\) for each \(j\). Let \(V := \prod_{j < k} (z_k - z_j)\).

Tag 22EH

Problem 14.2.1. \(\lvert V \rvert^2\) is a positive integer, and \(\log \lvert V \rvert^2 \le \binom r2 \log(2r)\).

Tag 22EJ

Problem 14.2.2. If \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\), then \(v_p(\lvert V \rvert^2) \ge r^2/p - r\).

Problems 14.2.2, 14.2.1 and 14.1.1 imply the following. The counting of coincident pairs follows [PV].

Tag 22EK

Theorem 14.2.1. \(S_1(X) \le \frac12 \log X + O(1)\).

Tag 22EL

Problem 14.2.3. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(v_p(\lvert V \rvert^2) \ge r^2/p^2 - r\).

The estimate in Problem 14.2.3 has the summable weights \(\log p/p^2\). To obtain a weight of order \(1/p\), we use derivatives of integral regular functions on \(y^2 = x^3 - x\). Among the odd primes this curve has supersingular reduction precisely at the primes inert in \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\).

§14.3. The derivation at an inert prime Tag 22EM

Let \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\), \(A := \mathbb{Z}[x, y]/(y^2 - x^3 + x)\), \(\omega := dx/(2y)\), and \(P := (0, 0)\). The derivation \(D\) of \(A\) dual to \(\omega\) is \[Dx = 2y, \qquad Dy = 3x^2 - 1.\] The automorphism \([i](x, y) := (-x, iy)\) fixes \(P\) and pulls \(\omega\) back to \(i\omega\). Let \(d_k := (D^k x)(P)\).

Tag 22EN

Problem 14.3.1. The \(d_k\) are integers, and \(d_k = 0\) unless \(k \equiv 2 \pmod 4\). Moreover \(d_2 = -2\), \(d_6 = 144\), \(d_{10} = -96768\).

Let \(s = 1 + \frac14 t^4 + \dotsb\) be the root of \(s^4 = 1 + t^4\) with constant term \(1\). For odd \(p\) it lies in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[t]]\).

Tag 22EP

Problem 14.3.2. The point \(x = -t^2/s^2\), \(y = -t/s^3\) lies on \(E\), \(t\) is a parameter at \(P\), and \[\omega = \frac{dt}{s^3} = \sum_{h \ge 0} (-1)^h b_h \, t^{4h} \, dt, \qquad b_h := \frac{\prod_{j=1}^h (4j - 1)}{4^h h!}.\]

Thus \(E\) is a quotient of the Fermat quartic \(s^4 = 1 + t^4\), and its differential is a series in \(t^4\).

Tag 22EQ

Problem 14.3.3. Let \(p\) be an odd prime. On \(A/pA\) the operator \(D^p\) is a derivation, and \(D^p = cD\) for a constant \(c \in \mathbb{F}_p\).

Tag 22ER

Problem 14.3.4. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(c = 0\).

Tag 22ES

Problem 14.3.5. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(p^{\lfloor k/p \rfloor}\) divides \((D^k f)(P)\) for all \(f \in A\) and \(k \ge 0\). For instance \(3^2 \mid d_6\) and \(7^2 \mid d_{14} = 268240896\).

§14.4. The determinant of derivatives Tag 22ET

Write \(E(\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) with \(dz = \omega\) [Si1, Ch. VI], and let \(w(z)\) be the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P + z\). Then \(w(z) = \wp(z + u_0)\) for a lift \(u_0 \notin \Lambda\) of \(P\), \(D = d/dz\), \(d_k = w^{(k)}(0)\), and \[(w')^2 = 4(w^3 - w), \qquad w(0) = w'(0) = 0, \qquad w''(0) = -2.\] For an integer \(r \ge 1\), let \(M\) be the matrix whose row of index \(k \ge 0\) lists the \(k\)th derivatives at \(0\) of \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:columns}\tag{1} \frac{z^j}{j!} \quad (0 \le j < r), \qquad \frac{z^j}{j!}\, w(z) \quad (0 \le j < r). \end{equation}\] The first \(r\) columns are the identity followed by zeros.

Tag 22EU

Problem 14.4.1. The entry of \(M\) in row \(k\) and column \(z^j w/j!\) is \(\binom kj d_{k - j}\) for \(k \ge j\), and \(0\) otherwise.

A relation among the columns, restricted to rows \(0, \dots, 4r\), is a pair of polynomials \(a, b\) of degree \(< r\), not both zero, with \(a + bw = O(z^{4r+1})\).

Tag 22EV

Problem 14.4.2. Let \(a, b \in \mathbb{C}[z]\), and let \(Q(a, b) := (a'b - ab')^2 + 4ab(a^2 - b^2)\). Then \(Q(a, b) = b^4\bigl((h')^2 - 4(h^3 - h)\bigr)\) with \(h = -a/b\), when \(b \ne 0\). If \(a + bw = O(z^{4r+1})\) with \(\deg a, \deg b < r\), then \(Q(a, b) = 0\).

Tag 22EW

Problem 14.4.3. No nonconstant rational function \(h \in \mathbb{C}(z)\) satisfies \((h')^2 = 4(h^3 - h)\).

Tag 22EX

Problem 14.4.4. If \(h \in \{0, \pm1\}\) and \(b \ne 0\) has degree \(< r\), then \(b(w - h)\) vanishes at \(0\) to order at most \(r + 1\).

Problems 14.4.2–14.4.4 prove the following.

Tag 22EY

Theorem 14.4.1. The rows of \(M\) of index \(0, \dots, 4r\) have rank \(2r\).

In order of increasing \(k\), keep each row of \(M\) that is independent of its predecessors, until \(2r\) rows are kept. Their indices are \(I = \{0, \dots, r-1, k_1, \dots, k_r\}\) with \(r \le k_1 < \dots < k_r \le 4r\). Expanding along the identity block, \[N_r := \det M_I = \det\Bigl[\tbinom{k_i}{j} d_{k_i - j}\Bigr]_{1 \le i \le r,\ 0 \le j < r}\] is a nonzero integer. Let \(L := \sum_i k_i - \binom r2\), the total order \(\sum_i (k_i - j_i)\) of the derivatives in each term of this determinant.

Tag 22EZ

Problem 14.4.5. For \(r = 1, 2, 3\) the new rows are \(\{2\}\), \(\{2, 3\}\), \(\{3, 4, 6\}\), and \(N_r = -2\), \(12\), \(2^7 3^4\).

Tag 22F2

Problem 14.4.6. \(r^2 \le L \le 4r^2\), and \(v_p(N_r) \ge L/p - r\) for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\).

§14.5. The size of the determinant Tag 22F3

If \(\rho\) is the distance from \(0\) to the nearest pole of \(w\), then \(\limsup_{k\to\infty} (\lvert d_k\rvert/k!)^{1/k} = \rho^{-1}\). To bound \(N_r\) we first remove the poles.

Tag 22F4

Problem 14.5.1. Let \(H\) be holomorphic near \(0\) with \(H(0) = 1\). Multiplying all the functions (1) by \(H\) does not change \(\det M_I = N_r\).

Write \(\Lambda = \lambda(\mathbb{Z}+ \tau\mathbb{Z})\) with \(\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0\), and let \[\theta_\tau(v) := 2\sum_{n \ge 0} (-1)^n e^{\pi i\tau(n + 1/2)^2} \sin\bigl((2n + 1)\pi v\bigr),\] so that \(\theta_\tau(v) = \theta_1(\pi v \mid \tau)\) [DLMF]. In terms of Jacobi’s function \(\vartheta\) of Section 11.2, \(\theta_\tau(v) = -i\, e^{\pi i\tau/4 + \pi i v}\, \vartheta\bigl(v + \tfrac{1+\tau}{2}\bigr)\). It is odd, \(\theta_\tau(v + 1) = -\theta_\tau(v)\), and \(\theta_\tau(v + \tau) = -e^{-\pi i\tau - 2\pi i v}\theta_\tau(v)\).

Tag 22F5

Problem 14.5.2. The zeros of \(\theta_\tau\) are the points of \(\mathbb{Z}+ \tau\mathbb{Z}\), and they are simple.

Let \(H(z) := \theta_\tau\bigl((z + u_0)/\lambda\bigr)^2 / \theta_\tau(u_0/\lambda)^2\).

Tag 22F6

Problem 14.5.3. \(H\) and \(Hw\) are entire, and \(\lvert H(z) \rvert, \lvert H(z) w(z) \rvert \le e^{C(1 + \lvert z \rvert^2)}\) with \(C\) independent of \(z\).

Tag 22F7

Problem 14.5.4. For every \(R > 0\), \(\lvert N_r \rvert \le (2r)!\,(4r/R)^L e^{2Cr(1 + R^2)}\). Hence \(\log \lvert N_r \rvert \le \frac12 L \log r + O(r^2)\).

Problems 14.4.6, 14.5.4 and 14.1.1 imply the following.

Tag 22F8

Theorem 14.5.1. \(S_3(X) \le \frac12 \log X + O(1)\).

Theorems 14.1.1, 14.2.1 and 14.5.1 prove Theorem 14.1.2. This determinant proof does not use the nonvanishing of \(L(1, \chi_{-4})\).

§14.6. The linear term Tag 22F9

The logarithmic bounds for both determinants allow errors of order \(r^2\), which leave the constant term undetermined. For \(\chi = \chi_{-4}\) that term involves \[c_\chi := \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi) = \gamma + \log\frac{\pi^2}{2\varpi^2} = 0.2456\ldots, \qquad \varpi := 2\int_0^1 \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1 - t^4}},\] where the second expression follows from Kronecker’s limit formula and the value of \(\varpi\) in terms of \(\Gamma(\frac14)\) (Theorem 11.3.1); it is the case \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) of the formula of Chowla and Selberg [CS]. How close can a determinant come to \(c_\chi\)?

On the Vandermonde side the floors can be kept exactly. For a prime power \(q\) let \(w_q(k) := \sum_{e \ge 1} \lfloor k/q^e \rfloor\), and let \(k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} := \prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{w_{N\mathfrak p}(k)}\), the \(k\)th factorial of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) in the sense of Pólya and Bhargava. For any \(n\) Gaussian integers the Vandermonde product is divisible by \(\prod_{k < n} k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}\) [Bh]. Write \(\lvert k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} \rvert\) for the absolute value of a generator.

Tag 22FA

Problem 14.6.1. \(\log \dfrac{k!}{\lvert k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} \rvert} = \frac12 w_2(k) \log 2 + \sum_{p \equiv 3 \ (4)} \log p \sum_{e \text{ odd}} \Bigl\lfloor \dfrac{k}{p^e} \Bigr\rfloor\). For \(k = 6\) both sides are \(\log 36\).

For an integer \(n\ge 1\), let \(\beta(n):=\sum_{0\le k<n}\log\lvert k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}\rvert\).

Tag 22FB

Problem 14.6.2. \(\beta(n) = \frac12 \sum_{1 \le N < n} (n - N) \bigl( \log N + \sum_{p^e \mid N} \chi(p^e) \log p \bigr)\), the inner sum over prime powers \(p^e > 1\).

The prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions with its fixed-modulus error term [MV], together with the Euler product for \(L(s,\chi)\), gives \(\sum_{p^e \le x} \chi(p^e) \log p = o(x)\) and \(\sum_{p^e \le x} \chi(p^e) \log p / p^e = -c_\chi + o(1)\).

Tag 22FC

Problem 14.6.3. \(\beta(n) = \frac{n^2}4 \log n - \frac{n^2}{4}\bigl(\frac32 + c_\chi\bigr) + o(n^2)\).

We take the points in a disc.

Tag 22FD

Problem 14.6.4. Let \(R > 0\), and let \(\mu\) be the uniform probability measure on the disc \(\lvert z \rvert \le R\). For \(\lvert z \rvert \le R\), \(\int \log\lvert z - u \rvert \, d\mu(u) = \log R - (R^2 - \lvert z \rvert^2)/(2R^2)\), and hence \(\iint \log\lvert z - u \rvert \, d\mu \, d\mu = \log R - \frac14\).

Tag 22FE

Problem 14.6.5. Let \(V_n\) be the Vandermonde product of the \(n\) Gaussian integers nearest \(0\). Then \(\log\lvert V_n \rvert = \frac{n^2}{4} \log\frac n\pi - \frac{n^2}8 + o(n^2)\).

Tag 22FF

Problem 14.6.6. \(\beta(n) \le \log\lvert V_n \rvert\) implies \(c_\chi \ge \log\pi - 1 = 0.1447\ldots\).

So even with exact divisibility the Vandermonde loses \(c_\chi - \log\pi + 1 = 0.1008\ldots\) in the linear term. On the side of derivatives one may build Padé-type determinants from the Taylor coefficients of the lemniscatic sine, whose poles are the points of \(\varpi\bigl(\frac{1+i}2 + \mathbb{Z}[i]\bigr)\). We expect, from computation, that they give \(c_\chi \le \log(4\pi/\varpi^2) = 0.6031\ldots\), short by the absolute constant \(\log(8/\pi) - \gamma = 0.3574\ldots\); we do not know a proof of the size estimate this needs. The Vandermonde calculation uses a disc of Gaussian nodes; for the proposed derivative estimate we expect a corresponding contribution from a disc in the translated lattice of poles. The determinant argument gives a bounded error in \(S_a(X)\). Even the constant term appears to require more than one determinant. The Riemann hypotheses for \(\zeta(s)\) and \(L(s,\chi_{-4})\) predict finer error terms for these prime sums.

The comparison of size and divisibility in this chapter illustrates the comparison in the direction amount \(h_{\mathrm{dir}}\) of Chapter 36 (Section 36.4). In the Lean proof [GL], Data.jetSize gives the size term \(s_\infty(j)\) for an integer \(j \ge 0\), Data.primeSum gives the prime sum \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(y, \sigma)\) for a real \(y\) and an infinite place \(\sigma\), and Data.directionAmount is \(h_{\mathrm{dir}} = \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi} \bigl(s_\infty(\kappa(\sigma)) - s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\sigma), \sigma)\bigr)\), with \(\Phi\) and \(\kappa\) as in Chapter 36. At the long order of that chapter, Data.mainPrimeSum gives the main term \(s_{\mathrm{main}}\) of the mean of the prime sums over the \(a\)-places, and Data.jetSize_sub_mainPrimeSum_le bounds the size term at that order minus \(s_{\mathrm{main}}\) from above. In its proof the two terms proportional to \(c_1 = -1 - \gamma + \operatorname{Re} L'(1, \chi_K)/L(1, \chi_K)\) cancel; here \(\chi_K\) is the character of the quadratic field \(K\) of Chapter 36. Chapter 20 explains how a period of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication involves \(L'(1, \chi_K)/L(1, \chi_K)\).
