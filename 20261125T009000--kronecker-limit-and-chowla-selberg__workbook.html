Prerequisites.

Euler’s Gamma function: the reflection and duplication formulas, the Beta integral, and \(\psi := \Gamma'/\Gamma\) with \(\psi(1) = -\gamma\). Poisson summation. Dirichlet \(L\)-functions: the functional equation [Dav, Ch. 9], the class number formula and binary quadratic forms [Dav, Ch. 6]. The Hurwitz zeta function [Ap, Ch. 12]. Compact Riemann surfaces and their holomorphic differentials, for Section 20.4 only. From Chapter 11, Complex tori, theta functions and periods: Problem 11.1.9 of Section 11.1 and Problem 11.3.4 of Section 11.3.

Chapter 11 took Kronecker’s limit formula from the literature and computed \(L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\) for the character \(\chi\) modulo \(4\) through the discriminant of the lemniscatic lattice. Here we prove the limit formula and reach the same value through \(s = 0\), where no elliptic functions appear. Comparing the two routes is the method of Chowla and Selberg [CS]. It shows that, for the character \(\chi\) of every imaginary quadratic field, \(e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)}\) is, up to an algebraic factor, the square of a period of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication divided by \(\pi^2\).

§20.1. Eisenstein series and Kronecker’s limit formula Tag 22JX

What is the constant term at \(s = 1\) of \(\sum'_{\mu \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} \lvert\mu\rvert^{-2s}\)? The answer comes from letting the lattice vary. For \(\tau = x + \mathrm{i}y\) with \(y > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), let \[E(\tau, s) := \sum_{(m,n) \ne (0,0)} \frac{y^s}{\lvert m + n\tau\rvert^{2s}} .\] Let \(q := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}\), let \(\eta(\tau) := e^{\pi\mathrm{i}\tau/12}\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)\) be Dedekind’s function, and let \(\sigma_t(n) := \sum_{e \mid n} e^t\). For \(z > 0\), let \(K_\nu(z) := \tfrac12 \int_0^\infty t^{\nu - 1} e^{-z(t + t^{-1})/2} \, d t\).

Tag 22JY

Problem 20.1.1. \(E(g\tau, s) = E(\tau, s)\) for every \(g \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\).

Tag 22JZ

Problem 20.1.2. For real \(x\), \(y > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12\), \[\sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} \frac{1}{((m + x)^2 + y^2)^{s}} = \frac{\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(s - \frac12)}{\Gamma(s)\, y^{2s-1}} + \frac{4\pi^s}{\Gamma(s)} \sum_{k \ge 1} \Bigl(\frac{k}{y}\Bigr)^{s - \frac12} \! K_{s - \frac12}(2\pi k y) \cos(2\pi k x) .\]

Tag 22K2

Problem 20.1.3. \(E(\tau, s)\) equals \[\begin{multline*} 2\zeta(2s) y^s + \frac{2\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(s - \frac12)}{\Gamma(s)} \zeta(2s - 1) y^{1-s} \\ + \frac{8\pi^s \sqrt y}{\Gamma(s)} \sum_{n \ge 1} n^{s - \frac12} \sigma_{1 - 2s}(n) K_{s - \frac12}(2\pi n y) \cos(2\pi n x) . \end{multline*}\] In particular \(E(\tau, s)\) continues meromorphically to \(\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12\), with only a simple pole at \(s = 1\).

Tag 22K5

Problems 20.1.3–20.1.6 give Kronecker’s first limit formula [We], [La, Ch. 20, 21].

Tag 22K6

Theorem 20.1.1 (Kronecker’s first limit formula). \(E(\tau, s) = \dfrac{\pi}{s - 1} + 2\pi\bigl(\gamma - \log 2 - \log(\sqrt y\, \lvert\eta(\tau)\rvert^2)\bigr) + O(s - 1)\).

Tag 22K8

Problem 20.1.8. Let \(Z(s) := \sum'_{\mu \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} \lvert\mu\rvert^{-2s}\), and let \(\chi\) be the non-trivial character modulo \(4\). Then \(Z(s) = \pi/(s-1) + 2\pi(\gamma - \log 2 - 2\log\eta(\mathrm{i})) + O(s-1)\), and \[\frac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)} = \gamma - 2\log 2 - 4\log\eta(\mathrm{i}) .\]

The last equality is Problem 11.3.5, now deduced from the limit formula developed here. It expresses \(L'/L(1,\chi)\) through a value of a modular form. To see Gamma values instead, we move to \(s = 0\), where \(L(s,\chi)\) is a finite combination of Hurwitz zeta functions.

§20.2. Lerch’s formula and the functional equation Tag 22K9

For \(x > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), let \(\zeta(s, x) := \sum_{n \ge 0} (n + x)^{-s}\). Euler–Maclaurin summation gives \[\zeta(s, x) = \frac{x^{1-s}}{s - 1} + \frac{x^{-s}}{2} - s \int_0^\infty \frac{\{t\} - \frac12}{(t + x)^{s+1}} \, d t ,\] which continues \(\zeta(s, x)\) to \(\operatorname{Re} s > -1\) with a single pole at \(s = 1\). Write \(\zeta'(s, x)\) for \(\partial_s \zeta(s, x)\).

Tag 22KA

Problem 20.2.1. \(\zeta(0, x) = \frac12 - x\), and \(\zeta(s, \frac12) = (2^s - 1)\zeta(s)\).

Tag 22KB

Problems 20.2.1 and 20.2.2 give Lerch’s formula.

Tag 22KC

Theorem 20.2.1 (Lerch). \(\zeta'(0, x) = \log\Gamma(x) - \frac12\log 2\pi\) for \(x > 0\).

Tag 22KD

Problem 20.2.3. Let \(\chi\) be an odd character modulo \(N\). Then \(L(0, \chi) = -\frac1N \sum_{a=1}^{N} a\,\chi(a)\), and \[L'(0, \chi) = \sum_{a=1}^{N-1} \chi(a) \log\Gamma(a/N) - L(0, \chi) \log N .\]

Let \(\chi\) be a primitive odd character modulo \(N\), and let \[\Lambda(s, \chi) := (N/\pi)^{(s+1)/2}\, \Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{s+1}{2}\bigr) L(s, \chi) .\] Then \(\Lambda(s, \chi) = W(\chi)\Lambda(1 - s, \bar\chi)\) with \(\lvert W(\chi)\rvert = 1\) [Dav, Ch. 9]; moreover \(W(\chi) = \tau(\chi)/(\mathrm{i}\sqrt N)\), which is \(1\) if \(\chi\) is real, because then the Gauss sum is \(\tau(\chi) = \mathrm{i}\sqrt N\) [Dav, Ch. 6].

§20.3. The character of an imaginary quadratic field Tag 22KH

Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant \(-d\), with class number \(h\) and \(w\) roots of unity, and let \(\chi\) be its character, a primitive real odd character modulo \(d\). The class number formula is \(L(1, \chi) = 2\pi h/(w\sqrt d)\). For each \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\)-equivalence class of positive definite integral binary quadratic forms of discriminant \(-d\), choose the reduced representative \(Q = [a, b, c]\), so that \(Q(m,n) = am^2 + bmn + cn^2\), \(b^2 - 4ac = -d\), \(\lvert b\rvert \le a \le c\), and \(b \ge 0\) if \(\lvert b\rvert = a\) or \(a = c\). Let \(\tau_Q := (-b + \mathrm{i}\sqrt d)/2a\) and \(y_Q := \operatorname{Im}\tau_Q\). The forms \(Q\) together represent each \(n \ge 1\) exactly \(w \sum_{e \mid n} \chi(e)\) times [Dav, Ch. 6], so \[\sum_Q \sum_{(m,n) \ne (0,0)} Q(m,n)^{-s} = w\, \zeta(s) L(s, \chi) \qquad (\operatorname{Re} s > 1) .\] The sum over \(Q\) has \(h\) terms. When \(h > 1\), can one class be taken alone?

Tag 22KJ

Problem 20.3.1. For \(d = 20\) the reduced forms are \(Q_1 = [1, 0, 5]\) and \(Q_2 = [2, 2, 3]\). The form \(Q_1\) represents \(1\) twice and \(3\) not at all, \(Q_2\) represents \(1\) not at all and \(3\) four times, and \(w\sum_{e \mid n}\chi(e)\) is \(2\) for \(n = 1\) and \(4\) for \(n = 3\). Prove also that neither \(\sum'_{(m,n)}Q_i(m,n)^{-s}\) is a constant multiple of \(\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)\).

The sum over classes in the displayed identity leads at \(s=1\) to a formula for \(\prod_Q\sqrt{y_Q}\,\lvert\eta(\tau_Q)\rvert^2\).

Tag 22KN

§20.4. Periods Tag 22KS

How do the Gamma values in Theorem 20.3.1 enter the periods of CM elliptic curves? We first relate Beta values to integrals on Fermat curves.

Let \(N \ge 3\) be an integer, and let \(F_N\) be the smooth projective curve with affine equation \(u^N + v^N = 1\). For integers \(a,b\ge1\), let \(\omega_{a,b}:=u^{a-1}v^{b-N}\,d u\). This algebraic differential is holomorphic on \(F_N\) when \(a+b\le N-1\). Let \(\kappa\) be the path \(u \mapsto (u, (1 - u^N)^{1/N})\), \(0 \le u \le 1\), from \((0,1)\) to \((1,0)\). Let \(\zeta_N := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/N}\), and let \(A(u,v) := (\zeta_N u, v)\) and \(B(u,v) := (u, \zeta_N v)\).

Tag 22KT

Problem 20.4.1. For all integers \(a,b\ge1\), the path integral converges and \(\int_\kappa\omega_{a,b}=\frac1N\mathrm{B}(\frac aN,\frac bN)\).

Tag 22KU

Problem 20.4.2. Assume \(a+b\le N-1\). The chain \((1 - A)(1 - B)\kappa\) is a closed cycle on \(F_N\), and the integral of \(\omega_{a,b}\) over it is \(\frac1N (1 - \zeta_N^a)(1 - \zeta_N^b)\, \mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac bN)\).

So each \(\mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac bN)\) with \(a, b \ge 1\) and \(a + b \le N - 1\) is, up to an algebraic factor, a period of a holomorphic differential on \(F_N\) defined over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

Tag 22KV

Problem 20.4.3. For integers \(a \ge 1\), \(\Gamma(\frac aN)^N = \Gamma(a) \prod_{j=1}^{N-1} \mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac{ja}N)\).

The Beta path integral and the Gamma product identity imply that for every integer \(a\ge1\), \(\Gamma(a/N)^N\) is, up to a rational factor, a product of integrals along \(\kappa\) of the differentials \(\omega_{r,s}\) with \(1\le r,s\le N\).

Tag 22KW

Problem 20.4.4. The map \((u,v)\mapsto(t,y)=(u,v^2)\) on affine charts extends to a map from \(F_4\) to the smooth projective model of \(y^2=1-t^4\). Its pullback of \(d t/y\) is \(\omega_{1,2}\), and \(\mathrm{B}(\frac14,\frac12)=2\varpi\).

For \(K\) as in Section 20.3, let \[\Omega_K := \sqrt\pi \prod_{a=1}^{d-1} \Gamma\Bigl(\frac ad\Bigr)^{w\chi(a)/4h} .\] For \(K=\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), \(\Omega_K=2\varpi\) is, up to a factor in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\), a period of \(d t/y\) on the smooth projective model of \(y^2=1-t^4\). The quantity computed in Problem 11.3.7 is \(\Omega_K^2/(2\pi)=2\varpi^2/\pi\).

Tag 22KX

Problem 20.4.5. For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})\), \[\Omega_K = \sqrt\pi\, \bigl(\Gamma(\tfrac13)/\Gamma(\tfrac23)\bigr)^{3/2} = 3^{1/4}2^{-1/2}\, \mathrm{B}(\tfrac13, \tfrac13) .\]

Here \(F_3\) is itself a curve of genus one, \(\omega_{1,1}\) is its holomorphic differential, and \(A\) multiplies \(\omega_{1,1}\) by \(\zeta_3\): the curve has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\rho]\). This is the general pattern. Chowla and Selberg deduced the following from Theorem 20.3.1 [CS]; we give it in the formulation of Gross [Gr]. Every nonzero period of a nonzero holomorphic differential defined over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) on an elliptic curve over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal{O}_K\) lies in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\, \Omega_K\). Gross proved this period relation geometrically by comparing periods on a product of CM elliptic curves with periods of special differential forms on a factor of a Fermat Jacobian [Gr]. The calculation on Fermat curves uses Beta integrals of the kind in Problem 20.4.2.

Tag 22KY

Problem 20.4.6. \(e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)} = \dfrac{\Omega_K^2}{2\pi^2}\). For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) this is \(\dfrac{2\varpi^2}{\pi^2} = \dfrac{\Gamma(\frac14)^4}{4\pi^3} = 1.39320\ldots\)

Thus \(e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)} = -2\,(\Omega_K/2\pi\mathrm{i})^2\): up to a factor in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\), it is the square of the ratio of a period of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal{O}_K\) to \(2\pi\mathrm{i}\), the period of \(d z/z\) on the multiplicative group.

In the proof, with parameters as in Chapter 36 and \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), the constant \(e^{\gamma - \operatorname{Re} L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)}\) of this chapter appears in the period constant \(\Omega\) of Section 36.4. Chapter 14, Section 14.6, describes how it cancels against the corresponding term in the main term of the mean prime sum.
