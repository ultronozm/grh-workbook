Prerequisites.

Weyl modules of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) at the level of highest weights. From Chapter 37: the standard lattice stabilizer \(K^\circ(V)\) (Section 37.3), the principal level (Section 37.4), the gauge and integrality at a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) (Definition 37.5.1), the source forms \(S\) (Section 37.5) and the target lattice (Definition 37.11.1). From Chapter 49: \(Z\)-short forms (Section 49.3). From Chapter 21: the Hermitian \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)-lattice \(\mathbb{Z}[i]^n\) is alone in its genus for \(n\le4\) (Problem 21.1.4). From Chapter 36: the data. Pairing conditions of twisted lattices: Chapter 6, Section 6.1.

Input 1 of Theorem 49.3.2 (Chapter 49) rests on one statement: the \(4^{-f}e^U\)-short source forms span \(S\), with \(U\) as in Section 36.4. Why should short forms span at all? If the group \(\Gamma\) of rational unitary matrices acts freely on the cosets \(gK\) of the level \(K\), a form is determined by its values at representatives of the double classes, and those values may be prescribed arbitrarily. It is then enough to find, at each point \(g\), a basis of the Weyl module consisting of vectors that are integral at \(g\) and short at every conjugate. Section 50.1 treats such levels, Section 50.2 uses transference to find short \(E\)-bases of \(E^n\) in the lattice of \(g\), and Section 50.3 turns them into short vectors of a Weyl module.

§50.1. Free levels

Let \(E\) be a CM field with a CM type, \(f\) its number of infinite places \(\tau\), \(\Phi_\tau\) the chosen embeddings, \(z\mapsto\bar z\) complex conjugation, \(\hat{\mathcal{O}}\) the integral finite adeles. For \(n\ge1\), \(G_f\) is the finite adelic unitary group of the standard definite form \(\sum_i\bar x_iy_i\) on \(E^n\), \(\Gamma\) its group of rational points, and \(K^\circ= G_f\cap\mathrm{GL}_n(\hat{\mathcal{O}})\) the stabilizer of \(\hat{\mathcal{O}}^n\), as in Section 37.3. A level is a subgroup \(K\subseteq K^\circ\) containing a neighbourhood of the identity; its classes are the finitely many double cosets \(\Gamma gK\). For a nonzero ideal \(\mathfrak a\) of \(\mathcal{O}_E\), \(K^\circ(\mathfrak a)\) consists of the elements of \(K^\circ\) that reduce to \(1\) modulo \(\mathfrak a\).

A weight \(\kappa=(\kappa_\tau)\) is a dominant weight \(\kappa_{\tau,1}\ge\dots\ge\kappa_{\tau,n}\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\) at each \(\tau\); put \(\lvert\kappa\rvert_{<n}:=\sum_\tau\sum_i(\kappa_{\tau,i}-\kappa_{\tau,n})\). Its Weyl module \(W_\kappa\) (Section 50.3) carries the action \(\rho_\kappa\) of \(\prod_\tau\mathrm{GL}_n\), the gauge of Definition 37.5.1, and the Weyl squared norm \(\lvert\cdot\rvert^2\), invariant under \(\prod_\tau U(n)\). A form of weight \(\kappa\) and level \(K\) is a function \(F\colon G_f\to W_\kappa\) with \(F(\gamma gk)=\gamma F(g)\) for \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), \(k\in K\), where \(\gamma\) acts by \(\rho_\kappa((\Phi_\tau(\bar\gamma))_\tau)\), with conjugation and each embedding applied entrywise, as for the source forms (Section 37.5).

Definition 50.1.1. A level \(K\) is free if \(\Gamma\cap gKg^{-1}=\{1\}\) for every \(g\in G_f\).

Problem 50.1.2. \(U(2)(\mathbb{Z}[i])\) consists of the \(32\) monomial matrices whose nonzero entries belong to \(\{\pm1,\pm i\}\); \(16\) of them are \(\equiv1\bmod(1+i)\), and only \(1\) is \(\equiv1\bmod(2+i)\). Hence on \(\mathbb{Z}[i]^2\) the level \(K^\circ(1+i)\) is not free and \(K^\circ(2+i)\) is free.

Problem 50.1.3. Let \(\mathfrak a=w_1\cdots w_s\) be a product of distinct finite places with \(N\mathfrak a>(2n)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\). If \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), \(g\in G_f\) and \(g^{-1}\gamma g\in K^\circ(\mathfrak a)\), then \(\operatorname{tr}\gamma-n\in\mathfrak a\), every conjugate of \(\operatorname{tr}\gamma-n\) has absolute value at most \(2n\), and \(\gamma=1\). For \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\), \(n=2\), \(\mathfrak a=(2+i)\) one has \(N\mathfrak a=5<16\): the criterion is sufficient only.

Used in: Problem 50.1.4

This is a form of Minkowski’s lemma on torsion in congruence subgroups [Se].

Problem 50.1.4. A level contained in \(K^\circ(\mathfrak a)\), with \(\mathfrak a\) as in Problem 50.1.3, is free. For the data and \(n\le4n_m\), the product of the \(f\) chosen places above \(p_0\) is such an \(\mathfrak a\); hence every level of rank \(n\le4n_m\) whose elements reduce to \(1\) at the chosen places is free, among them the level of the source forms and the levels of the targets of the lower chains.

Used in: Chapter 37; Chapter 39; Chapter 51

Problem 50.1.5. Let \(K\) be free, with class representatives \(g_c\). Then \(F\mapsto(F(g_c))_c\) is an isomorphism from the forms of weight \(\kappa\) and level \(K\) onto \(\bigoplus_cW_\kappa\); their dimension is \(\#\{\text{classes}\}\cdot\dim W_\kappa>0\). If \(K'\subseteq K\) are free, \(K'\) has \([K:K']\) times as many classes as \(K\). On \(\mathbb{Z}[i]^2\), \(K^\circ(2+i)\) has \(15\) classes.

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 51

Definition 50.1.6. For \(x\in G_f\) and \(v\in W_\kappa\), define the delta form of level \(K\) by \(\varphi_{x,v}(y):=\sum\gamma^{-1}v\), the sum over the \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) with \(x^{-1}\gamma y\in K\).

Problem 50.1.7. At a free level the sum defining \(\varphi_{x,v}(y)\) has at most one term; \(\varphi_{x,v}\) is the form with value \(v\) at \(x\) that vanishes off \(\Gamma xK\). At every \(y\in\Gamma xK\), the gauge of \(\varphi_{x,v}(y)\) at \(y\) equals that of \(v\) at \(x\), for every \(\eta\), and \(\lvert\sigma\varphi_{x,v}(y)\rvert^2=\lvert\sigma v\rvert^2\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\).

Problem 50.1.8. At the level \(K^\circ\) of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]^2\) (not free), write \(\operatorname{Sym}^d\) as forms of degree \(d\) in \(e_1,e_2\). Then \(\sum_\gamma\gamma^{-1}e_1^4=16(e_1^4+e_2^4)\) and \(\sum_\gamma\gamma^{-1}e_1^2=0\), the sums over \(U(2)(\mathbb{Z}[i])\). The forms of level \(K^\circ\) and weight \(\operatorname{Sym}^4\) form a line, and those of weight \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\) are \(0\).

For forms \(F,F'\) of the same weight and level \(K\) put \(\langle F,F'\rangle:=\sum_c\#(\Gamma\cap g_cKg_c^{-1})^{-1}\langle F(g_c),F'(g_c)\rangle_W\), with the Weyl inner product, conjugate-linear in the first argument. We use one fact read from the proof [GL]: if \(u\) has gauge at most \(1\) at \(g\) for \(\eta\circ(z\mapsto\bar z)\) and \(v\) has gauge at most \(1\) at \(g\) for \(\eta\), then \(\lvert\eta\langle u,v\rangle_W\rvert\le1\). Status: read from the proof; not proved here.

Problem 50.1.9. At a free level, \(\langle F,F'\rangle=\sum_c\langle F(g_c),F'(g_c)\rangle_W\). If \(F\) is integral at \(\bar\iota\) and \(F'\) at \(\iota\), then \(\langle F,F'\rangle\in\mathcal{O}_\iota\). The same conclusions hold for two forms transforming on the right under the same character \(\chi\) of \(K\) with \(\lvert\chi\rvert=1\). Hence the target lattice satisfies the pairing condition of a twisted lattice (Chapter 6, Section 6.1).

Used in: Chapter 37

§50.2. Short bases

For \(g\in G_f\) let \(\Lambda(g):=E^n\cap g\hat{\mathcal{O}}^n\), and for \(y\in E^n\) let \(\lvert y\rvert^2:=\sum_\tau\sum_i\lvert\Phi_\tau(y_i)\rvert^2\). Through \(y\mapsto(\Phi_\tau(y_i))_{\tau,i}\), \(\Lambda(g)\) is a lattice of rank \(2fn\) in \(\mathbb{C}^{fn}=\mathbb{R}^{2fn}\), with the inner product \(\operatorname{Re}\sum\bar x_jy_j\). Fix a positive integer \(N'\) in the different of \(E\).

Problem 50.2.1. Every nonzero \(y\in\Lambda(g)\) has \(\lvert y\rvert\ge1\). For \(E=\mathbb{Q}(i)\), \(n=1\) and \(g\) with components \(5\) and \(5^{-1}\) at \(2+i\) and \(2-i\) and \(1\) elsewhere, \(\Lambda(g)=\frac{2+i}{2-i}\mathbb{Z}[i]\), and the bound is attained.

Problem 50.2.2. Let \(\mathfrak D_E\) be the different of \(E\). The dual of \(\Lambda(g)\) for the trace pairing \((a,\xi)\mapsto\operatorname{tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(a^*\xi)\) on \(E^n\) is \(\mathfrak D_E^{-1}\Lambda(g)\). The Euclidean dual of \((2/N')\Lambda(g)\) is \(N'\mathfrak D_E^{-1}\Lambda(g)\), and no nonzero vector of this dual is shorter than \(1\). For \(E=\mathbb{Q}(i)\) one may take \(N'=2\).

Transference. Let \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^m\) be a lattice whose dual has no nonzero vector shorter than \(r_0>0\). Then every point of \(\mathbb{R}^m\) lies within \(m^2/r_0\) of \(\Lambda\). Banaszczyk [Ba] proves the bound \(m/(2r_0)\); bounds of this shape go back to [LLS]. Status: classical.

Problem 50.2.3. For \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}(1,0)+\mathbb{Z}(0,10)\) the shortest nonzero dual vector has length \(1/10\), and the largest distance of a point from \(\Lambda\) is \(\sqrt{101}/2=5.02\ldots\le40\).

Problem 50.2.4. If every point of \(\mathbb{R}^m\) lies within \(r>0\) of a set \(\mathcal S\), the vectors of \(\mathcal S\) of length at most \(3r\) span \(\mathbb{R}^m\).

Definition 50.2.5. Put \(c_{\mathrm{sb}}:=6N'(fn)^2\). A short basis at \(g\) is a matrix \(\mathsf{B}(g)\in\mathrm{GL}_n(E)\) such that \(g^{-1}\mathsf{B}(g)\) has entries in \(\hat{\mathcal{O}}\) and \(\lvert\phi(\mathsf{B}(g)_{ij})\rvert\le c_{\mathrm{sb}}\) for all \(i,j\) and every embedding \(\phi\colon E\to\mathbb{C}\).

Problem 50.2.6. Every \(g\in G_f\) has a short basis. For \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\), \(n=2\), \(N'=2\): \(c_{\mathrm{sb}}=48\).

Problem 50.2.7. For every finite adele \(y\) of \(E\) there is \(a\in E\) with \(y-a\in\hat{\mathcal{O}}\) and \(\lvert\phi(a)\rvert\le(2f)^2N'\) for every embedding \(\phi\).

Used in: Problem 50.2.9

Problem 50.2.8. Let \(Y,Y'\in\mathrm{GL}_n(E)\) have \(Y'^*Y'=Y^*Y\), and let \(g,g'\in G_f\) be such that \((g'^{-1}Y')(g^{-1}Y)^{-1}\) has entries in \(\hat{\mathcal{O}}\). Then \(\Gamma gK^\circ=\Gamma g'K^\circ\).

Problem 50.2.9. Let \(\mathsf{B}=\mathsf{B}(g)\), \(A=g^{-1}\mathsf{B}\), and let \(\mathsf X\in M_n(E)\) have \(A^{-1}-\mathsf X\) integral and \(\lvert\phi(\mathsf X_{ij})\rvert\le (2f)^2N'\) for every embedding \(\phi\) and all \(i,j\), as in Problem 50.2.7. Put \(G:=\mathsf{B}^*\mathsf{B}\). The entries of \(G\) and \(G\mathsf X\) are algebraic integers whose conjugates have absolute value at most \(c_{\mathrm{sb}}^3\), and the pair \((G,G\mathsf X)\) determines the class \(\Gamma gK^\circ\).

Problem 50.2.10. For \(C\ge0\), at most \((1+2C)^{2f}\) algebraic integers of \(E\) have all conjugates of absolute value at most \(C\).

Problems 50.2.6–50.2.10 prove the following.

Theorem 50.2.11. Let \(E\) be a CM field with \(f\) infinite places, \(N'\) a positive integer in its different, and \(c_{\mathrm{sb}}=6N'(fn)^2\). The standard definite space of rank \(n\) has at most \((1+2c_{\mathrm{sb}}^3)^{4fn^2}\) classes \(\Gamma\backslash G_f/K^\circ\).

Used in: Chapter 66

For \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\), \(n=1\), \(N'=2\) the bound is \(3457^4\), and there is one class. Chapter 66 uses this count for the data.

§50.3. Evaluation vectors and short forms

Fix \(\tau\) and write \(\lambda=\kappa_\tau-\kappa_{\tau,n}(1,\dots,1)\), a partition whose columns have height \(h<n\). For a filling \(T\) of the diagram of \(\lambda\) by \(\{1,\dots,n\}\) and an \(n\times n\) matrix \(y\), let \(b_T(y)\) be the product, over the columns \((i_1,\dots,i_h)\) of \(T\) read from top to bottom, of \(\det(y_{i_a,b})_{1\le a,b\le h}\). The products \(b_T\) span a space \(\nabla_\lambda\) of polynomials, and those with \(T\) semistandard form a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-basis of the \(\mathbb{Z}\)-span of all the \(b_T\) [DEP], [Fu]; Status: classical. We take \(W_\kappa:=(\bigotimes_\tau\nabla_{\lambda_\tau})^*\), with coordinates dual to the products \(b_T:=\prod_\tau b_{T_\tau}(y_\tau)\) over the families of semistandard \(T_\tau\). Changing the integral basis multiplies the coordinates by a matrix in \(\mathrm{GL}_{\dim W_\kappa}(\mathbb{Z})\), which preserves the \(p\)-adic supremum norm and hence the gauge.

Definition 50.3.1. For a family \(y=(y_\tau)\) of complex \(n\times n\) matrices, the evaluation vector \(\mathrm{fl}_\kappa(y)\in W_\kappa\) is \(F\mapsto F(y)\); its coordinates are the \(b_T(y)\).

The action is \(\rho_\kappa(A)\mathrm{fl}_\kappa(y)=\prod_\tau\det(A_\tau)^{\kappa_{\tau,n}}\mathrm{fl}_\kappa((A_\tau y_\tau)_\tau)\). The restriction of \(W_\kappa\) to \(\prod_\tau U(n)\) is irreducible, so its invariant positive definite hermitian form \(H\) is unique up to a positive factor. We normalize \(H(\mathrm{fl}_\kappa(1),\mathrm{fl}_\kappa(1))=1\); the Weyl squared norm is \(\lvert v\rvert^2=H(v,v)\). For \(\mathsf X\in\mathrm{GL}_n(E)\) and a ring map \(j\colon\mathbb{C}\to L\) to a field, \(\mathsf X^{(\tau)}\) is the entrywise image of \(\mathsf X\) under \(u\mapsto j(\Phi_\tau(\bar u))\). For \(\mathsf X\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb A_{E,f})\) and \(j\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\), take the component of \(\mathsf X\) at the finite place induced by this embedding of \(E\) and apply the continuous extension to the completion, as in Definition 37.5.1.

Problem 50.3.2. For \(n=2\), \(f=1\), \(\kappa=(2,0)\), the coordinates of \(\mathrm{fl}_\kappa(y)\) are \(y_{11}^2,y_{11}y_{21},y_{21}^2\), and the vectors at matrices with first columns \((1,1)\), \((1,-1)\), \((1,i)\) have determinant \(4\).

Problem 50.3.3. The vectors \(\mathrm{fl}_\kappa(y)\), with all entries of all \(y_\tau\) roots of unity, span \(W_\kappa\).

Problem 50.3.4. Fix short bases, and read \(\mathsf X^{(\tau)}\) with \(j\) the identity. For \(\tau\) let \(\mathsf{B}_\tau:=\mathsf{B}(g^{\kappa_{\tau,n}})\) be the short basis at the point \(g^{\kappa_{\tau,n}}\in G_f\) (not a power of \(\mathsf{B}(g)\)). For \(y\) with root-of-unity entries put \(v_y:=\prod_\tau\det(\mathsf{B}_\tau^{(\tau)})\cdot\mathrm{fl}_\kappa\bigl((\mathsf{B}(g)^{(\tau)}y_\tau)_\tau\bigr)\).

  1. \(v_y\) has gauge at most \(1\) at \(g\) for every \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) and every \(p\).

  2. \(\lvert\sigma v_y\rvert^2\le c_{\mathrm{sb}}^{6(nf+\lvert\kappa\rvert_{<n})}\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\).

Problem 50.3.5. For the data, \(N'=q_1d\) lies in the different of \(E\), and with \(n=k\), \(4^fc_{\mathrm{sb}}^{6(kf+fa)}\le e^U\).

The following theorem gives the short-spanning bound for a free level and its specialization to \(S\).

Theorem 50.3.6.

  1. Let \(K\subseteq K^\circ\) be a free level, \(\kappa\) a weight and \(Z\ge c_{\mathrm{sb}}^{6(nf+\lvert\kappa\rvert_{<n})}\). The \(Z\)-short forms of weight \(\kappa\) and level \(K\) span the complex space of all forms of that weight and level.

  2. For data, the \(4^{-f}e^U\)-short forms in \(S\) span \(S\).

Used in: Chapter 49; Chapter 51
