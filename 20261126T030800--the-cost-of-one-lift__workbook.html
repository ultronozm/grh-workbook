Prerequisites.

From Chapter 5: heights of families (Section 5.3, Problem 5.3.2). From Chapter 6: sizes read at the trivial twist, valuations at a twist that depends on the prime. The data of Chapter 36 and the objects of Chapter 37. The factorization of Chapter 68. The statements of Chapters 75, 76, 77 and 79 recalled in Section 74.1.

Chapter 68 expresses the ratio of the chain Gram determinant at an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) to its value at the trivial twist as a product of single-lift Gram determinant ratios and a correction factor. The determinant \(Z_j(\vartheta)\) uses the \(j\)-th lift at \(\vartheta^j\). The proof needs a height bound for \(Z_j(1)/Z_j(\vartheta)\) when \(\vartheta\) varies with the prime \(\ell\) and the isomorphism \(\iota \mid \ell\). The proof multiplies the Gram determinant of a single lift by the determinant of the compression of a second operator to the image of the lift. The compression theorem (Theorem 75.3.1(a)) expresses this product as a fixed nonzero constant times the \(d_T\)-th power of the ratio of the Hecke value product at the twist to its value at the trivial twist. Chapter 75 gives an archimedean bound for the compression determinant at the trivial twist and a local bound at a twist when \(\ell\) is prime to its modulus norm and a clearing bound is given. The Hecke quotient is a power of the modulus norm times the square of a product of ratios attached to critical pairs (Chapter 77). For a regular family (Definition 79.1.3) the inverse ratios have bounded height, with the estimate for the selected main factors in Chapter 79 using Chapter 78. This chapter deduces Theorem 74.2.1 from these inputs and from the comparison operator of Theorem 76.5.1. The inputs are deduced in Chapters 75, 76, 77, 78 and 79 from the background and the statements taken from the proof that each of them lists.

§74.1. The inputs

We use the data of Chapter 36: the number \(f\) of infinite places, the scales \(x < x_1 < b\) and \(a = xb\), the number \(m\) of lifts, the ranks \(r\) and \(k\), the direction amount \(h_{\mathrm{dir}}\) and the denominator budget \(B = U/(mk) = fa\log^3 b/(mk)\) (Definition 36.4.1); and the objects of Chapter 37: twists \(\vartheta\) and \(m\)-fold twists, with modulus \(Q_\vartheta\) and \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\), the target dimension \(d_T\), the single lifts \(u_{\vartheta,j}\), and \(Z_j(\vartheta) = \det(u_{\vartheta^j,j}^*u_{\vartheta^j,j})\) for an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\).

Fix \(1 \le j \le m\). The Hecke value product \({\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta)\) of lift \(j\) at a twist \(\vartheta\) is that of Definition 75.3.1. Comparison operators \((\Upsilon_\vartheta)\), one operator on the target space of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) for every twist \(\vartheta\), are defined in Definition 75.3.3, their compression determinants \(D_\vartheta\) in (1), and the statement that a real \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) clears the projection at \((\iota, \vartheta, j)\) in Definition 75.3.4. A family \(\theta = (\theta_\iota)\) of \(m\)-fold twists, one for each prime \(\ell\) and each \(\iota \mid \ell\), is regular (Definitions 79.1.2 and 79.1.3) if: (i) \(\ell\) is prime to \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota}\); (ii) for every \(j\), every ramified critical pair \(P\) and \(\epsilon = \pm 1\), the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^j, \epsilon)^{-1}\) has height at most \(x_1\); (iii) for every \(j\) there are reals \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\), one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0}\mathrm{cl}(\ell) \le B\) for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota\mid\ell\), with \(\vartheta:=\theta_\iota^j\), \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) clears the projection at \((\iota,\vartheta,j)\) and, for every critical pair \(P\) main for \(j\) and any two test forms for \(\ell\) at \((P,\vartheta)\), the pairing of their projections to the line of the lift of \(P\) at \(\vartheta\) has \(\iota\)-absolute value at most \(e^{\mathrm{cl}(\ell)}\) (Definition 79.1.3). With (i) and (ii) alone the family has small ratios. Chapter 80 treats the denominators of the projection onto the image of a single lift and the sum of the clearing bounds in clause (iii). Chapter 85 states the existence of regular families.

By Theorem 76.5.1, for every \(1 \le j \le m\) the operators \(\Upsilon_\vartheta := \mathcal N h^2\, g_\vartheta g_\vartheta^*\), one for each twist \(\vartheta\), form a comparison operator; here \(g_\vartheta\) is the gluing map of Definition 76.3.1, \(h\) is the hook number of the weight of the tuples on which \(g_\vartheta\) is defined, and \(\mathcal N\) is the order of Section 37.1.

By Theorem 75.3.1, for every comparison operator every \(D_\vartheta\) is nonzero, \[\lvert\iota(D_\vartheta)\rvert_\ell \le e^{d_T r\,\mathrm{cl}(\ell)} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lvert\sigma(D_1)\rvert \le e^{3d_T fmb\log^2 b} ,\] the first for every twist \(\vartheta\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell\) prime to \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\) and every real \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) that clears the projection at \((\iota,\vartheta,j)\), the second for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\); and by Problem 75.3.2, for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \[\frac{Z_j(1)}{Z_j(\vartheta)} = \frac{D_{\vartheta^j}}{D_1}\Bigl(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta^j)}{{\mathcal{H}}_j(1)}\Bigr)^{-d_T} .\] By Problem 77.5.1, for the fixed \(j\) there are a nonnegative integer \(e\), a list of \(k/2\) critical pairs (Definition 37.10.1) main for \(j\) (Definition 77.4.1), and a list of \(5m\) ramified critical pairs with a sign \(\epsilon = \pm 1\) for each entry. For every twist \(\vartheta\), the quotient \({\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta)/{\mathcal{H}}_j(1)\) is \((\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^e\) times the square of the product of the ratios \(R_P(\vartheta,1)\) from the first list and \(R_P(\vartheta,\epsilon)\) from the second. By Theorem 79.3.1, for a regular family \(\theta\) and a critical pair \(P\) main for \(j\), the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^j)^{-1}\) has height at most \(6B - h_{\mathrm{dir}}\).

§74.2. The deduction

A height bound \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c\) for a family \(y = (y_\iota)\) is as in Section 5.3; products and powers are treated in Problem 5.3.2. We need two more operations.

Problem 74.2.1. Let \(H \ne 0\) and \(\alpha \in {\mathbb{R}}\) with \(\lvert\sigma H\rvert \le e^\alpha\) for all \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\), and let \(e(\ell) \in {\mathbb{R}}\), of any sign, one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\lvert H y_\iota\rvert_\iota \le \exp(e(\ell))\) for all \(\iota \mid \ell\) and \(\alpha + \sum_{\ell \in P_0} e(\ell) \le c\) for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes. Then \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c\).

Used in: Chapter 79

Problem 74.2.2. If \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c \le c''\) and \(\lvert y''_\iota\rvert_\iota \le \lvert y_\iota\rvert_\iota\) for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) and every prime \(\ell\), then \(\mathrm{ht}(y'') \le c''\).

Used in: Chapter 79

Let \(\theta\) be regular and \(1 \le j \le m\), let \((\Upsilon_\vartheta)\) be the comparison operator of Theorem 76.5.1, and write \(\vartheta_\iota := \theta_\iota^j\).

Problem 74.2.3. The family \(\iota \mapsto D_{\vartheta_\iota}/D_1\) has height at most \(d_T(3fmb\log^2 b + rB)\).

Problem 74.2.4. The family \(\iota \mapsto \bigl({\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta_\iota)/{\mathcal{H}}_j(1)\bigr)^{-1}\) has height at most \(10mx_1 + k(6B - h_{\mathrm{dir}})\).

Used in: Chapter 75; Chapter 79

The upper bound on \(m\) controls the sum of the two height bounds. In the one-sided bound for the prime remainder, \(m^2 \ge x\) gives the factor \(1/m \le x^{-1/2}\).

Problem 74.2.5. Let \(f \ge 1\), \(b > 1\) with \(\log b \ge 16\), \(x \ge 1\), \(0 < m \le 2\sqrt x\), \(r \ge 2m\), \(k = r + 1 - m\), \(1 \le x_1 \le b\), \(a = xb\) and \(B = fa\log^3 b/(mk)\). Then for every real \(h_{\mathrm{dir}}\) \[3fmb\log^2 b + rB + 10mx_1 + k(6B - h_{\mathrm{dir}}) \le k(B\log b - h_{\mathrm{dir}}) .\] The data satisfy these hypotheses.

Problems 74.2.3, 74.2.4, 75.3.2, 74.2.2 and 74.2.5, with Problem 5.3.2 for the product and for the \(d_T\)-th power of the family of Problem 74.2.4, prove the following.

Theorem 74.2.1. For the data, every regular family \(\theta\) of \(m\)-fold twists, and every \(1 \le j \le m\), the family \[\iota \longmapsto \frac{Z_j(1)}{Z_j(\theta_\iota)}\] has height at most \(d_T\, k\,(B\log b - h_{\mathrm{dir}})\).

Used in: Problem 74.2.6; Chapter 67; Chapter 75; Chapter 76; Chapter 85

Problem 74.2.6. For the data, \(B\log b < h_{\mathrm{dir}}\). So the bound of Theorem 74.2.1 is \(-d_Tk(h_{\mathrm{dir}}- B\log b)\) with \(h_{\mathrm{dir}}- B\log b > 0\).

So the bound of Theorem 74.2.1 is, per unit of \(d_T\), \(k\) times the budget \(B\log b\) less \(k\) times the direction amount. Chapter 67 combines these bounds for \(1\le j\le m\) with the factorization of Chapter 68 and the height bound there for the inverse of the scalar whose \(d_T\)-th power is the correction factor. The inputs are deduced in Chapters 75, 76, 77, 78 and 79 from the statements each of them lists as taken from the proof.
