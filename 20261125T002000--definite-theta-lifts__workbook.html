Prerequisites.

Hermitian forms over the Gaussian integers, at the level of definitions. The classification of positive definite unimodular \(\mathbb{Z}\)-lattices of rank at most \(8\) [CoS, Ch. 16]. Schur functors and the Weyl dimension formula for \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})\) [FH, Theorem 6.3 and Eq. (15.17)]. The values of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions at negative integers [Wa, Theorem 4.2], and the functional equations of \(\zeta(s)\) and \(L(s,\chi)\) [Dav, Ch. 8, 9]. Stirling’s formula. No earlier chapter is used.

Chapter 8 introduces a chain of linear maps. Chapter 23, Definite theta lifts and their norms, studies theta lifts between definite unitary groups; the corresponding construction of the proof is the composite lift of Chapter 36. For the definite spaces we take definite unitary groups over \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) as a model. The genus of the standard lattice has finitely many classes. At the level given by the stabilizer of that lattice, a form of a fixed weight assigns an invariant vector in the weight representation to each class. How many forms are there? For fixed positive rank, when \(4\) divides the total degree of the weight and its gaps are large, the answer is asymptotically four times the product of a mass and a Weyl dimension, and both factors carry \(\prod_{j<n} j!\), which cancels. Chapter 22 evaluates examples of genus averages and relates them to a rank-one lift norm.

§21.1. Hermitian lattices over \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) Tag 22L2

Let \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\). A Hermitian lattice of rank \(n\) is a free \(\mathcal{O}\)-module \(L\) of rank \(n\) with a positive definite Hermitian form \(h \colon L \times L \to \mathcal{O}\), linear in the first variable. Its dual is \(L^\sharp := \{y \in L \otimes_\mathcal{O}K : h(x,y) \in \mathcal{O}\text{ for all } x \in L\}\), and \(L\) is unimodular if \(L^\sharp = L\). The basic example is \(I_n := \mathcal{O}^n\) with \(h(x,y) = \sum_k x_k \bar y_k\). Let \(\Gamma_L\) be the group of \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear automorphisms of \(L\) preserving \(h\); it is finite, since it permutes the finitely many vectors of bounded length that span \(L\).

We say that two Hermitian lattices are in the same genus if they become isometric over \(\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_p\) for every prime \(p\). Let \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) be the set of isometry classes in the genus of \(I_n\). By Problem 21.1.1 below, a class gives a unimodular \(\mathbb{Z}\)-lattice of rank \(2n\) together with an automorphism of it; there are finitely many such lattices up to isometry [Ca, Ch. 9], each with a finite automorphism group, so \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) is finite. We call \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) the class set. For the unitary group of \(I_n\) it is the finite adelic double-coset set at the stabilizer of the standard lattice. Define the mass of \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) by \[M_n := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \frac{1}{\lvert \Gamma_L \rvert}, \qquad M_0 := 1 .\] What do the class sets look like in small rank?

§21.2. The mass of a genus Tag 22L7

How does \(M_n\) grow? Let \(\chi\) be the non-trivial character modulo \(4\), and let \(\chi^j\) be \(\chi\) for odd \(j\) and the trivial character for even \(j\), so that \(L(s, \chi^j) = \zeta(s)\) for even \(j\). For the genus of \(I_n\), the mass formula reads \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:mass}\tag{1} M_n = \delta_n \prod_{j=1}^{n} \frac{\lvert L(1-j, \chi^j) \rvert}{2}, \end{equation}\] where \(\delta_n\) is a positive rational number, a local factor at the prime \(2\), which is ramified in \(K\). This is the Hermitian analogue of Siegel’s mass formula for quadratic forms [Si], with the local factors computed by Gan and Yu [GY] and, at the ramified prime \(2\), by Cho [Cho]. We set \(\delta_0 := 1\), so that (1) also holds for \(n = 0\), where \(M_0 = 1\).

Tag 22L8

Tag 22L9

Problem 21.2.2. Determine the local factors from the class masses, taking the local factors as unknowns. Prove that \(\delta_1=\delta_3=1\), \(\delta_2=3\) and \(\delta_4=15\).

For all positive ranks, [JZ, Corollary 4.3] gives \(\delta_n=1\) for odd \(n\) and \(\delta_n=2^n-1\) for even \(n\).

In rank \(5\), with \(\delta_5 = 1\), the formula gives \(M_5 = 1/73728\). Two unimodular lattices of the same rank over \(\mathcal{O}\), each containing a vector \(x\) with \(h(x,x)\) odd, lie in the same genus [JZ, Corollary 2.2]; thus \(\Lambda \perp I_1 \in \mathrm{Cl}_5\).

Tag 22LB

Problem 21.2.4. For \(j \ge 1\), \[\frac{\lvert L(1-j, \chi^j) \rvert}{2} = (j-1)!\, \pi^{-j} \beta_j, \qquad \beta_j := \begin{cases} 2^{j-1} L(j, \chi) & j \text{ odd},\\ 2^{-j} \zeta(j) & j \text{ even}. \end{cases}\]

For \(r \ge 1\) define \(\kappa_r > 0\) by \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:kappa}\tag{2} \frac{M_r}{M_{r-1}} = (r-1)!\, \pi^{-r} \kappa_r . \end{equation}\] By (1) and Problem 21.2.4, \(\kappa_r = (\delta_r/\delta_{r-1})\beta_r\). Multiplying (2) over \(r \le n\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:mass-product}\tag{3} M_n = \pi^{-n(n+1)/2} \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j! \, \prod_{j=1}^{n} \kappa_j . \end{equation}\] After removing \((r-1)!\) from the mass ratio, the logarithm of the remaining factor is \(-r\log\pi+\log\kappa_r\).

Tag 22LC

Problem 21.2.5. For \(r \le 4\), \(\kappa_r = \pi^r/(4 \cdot r!)\); thus \(\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_4\) are \(\pi/4\), \(\pi^2/8\), \(\pi^3/24\), \(\pi^4/96\). With \(\delta_5 = 1\), \(\kappa_5 = \pi^5/288\).

Tag 22LD

Problem 21.2.6. For every \(r \ge 1\), \(3/4 \le \kappa_r \le 2\); in particular \(\log \kappa_r = O(1)\).

By (3), \(\log M_n\) grows like \(\tfrac12 n^2 \log n\); for \(n \ge 1\) every \(\Gamma_L\) contains \(\mu_4\), so the class set has at least \(4M_n\) elements, more than \(8 \cdot 10^5\) for \(n = 15\).

§21.3. Forms and the Weyl dimension formula Tag 22LE

On a definite group a form is a function on the class set, with values in a representation. How many are there?

For \(L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n\) let \(L_\mathbb{C}:= L \otimes_\mathcal{O}\mathbb{C}\), using the inclusion \(\mathcal{O}\subset \mathbb{C}\). The form \(h\) extends to a Hermitian inner product on \(L_\mathbb{C}\), and \(\Gamma_L\) is a finite subgroup of its unitary group. A weight is a sequence of integers \(\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n \ge 0)\); write \(\lvert \lambda \rvert := \sum_k \lambda_k\). For a finite-dimensional Hermitian space \(A\), let \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(A) \subset A^{\otimes \lvert \lambda \rvert}\) be the image of the Young symmetrizer [FH], with the inner product induced from \(A^{\otimes \lvert\lambda\rvert}\). A linear map \(x \colon A \to B\) induces \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(x) \colon \mathbb{S}_\lambda(A) \to \mathbb{S}_\lambda(B)\), the restriction of \(x^{\otimes \lvert\lambda\rvert}\). Then \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(xy) = \mathbb{S}_\lambda(x)\mathbb{S}_\lambda(y)\) and \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(x)^* = \mathbb{S}_\lambda(x^*)\), the latter because \((x^*)^{\otimes \lvert\lambda\rvert}\) intertwines the permutation actions on the two tensor powers. If \(\lambda\) has at most \(\dim A\) nonzero entries, \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(A)\) is the irreducible representation of \(\mathrm{GL}(A)\) of highest weight \(\lambda\); otherwise it is zero [FH, Theorem 6.3]. For \(\lambda = (a)\) it is \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(A)\). There, for \(u \in A\) we write \(u^a := u \otimes \dots \otimes u\); for a basis \(e_1, \dots, e_n\) of \(A\) and \(\lvert\nu\rvert = a\), the vectors \(e^\nu\) are defined by \((\sum_k t_k e_k)^a = \sum_\nu (a!/\nu!)\, t^\nu e^\nu\), and \(e^\nu e^\mu := e^{\nu+\mu}\) is the product of the symmetric algebra.

Let \(V_\lambda(L) := \mathbb{S}_\lambda(L_\mathbb{C})\). Weyl’s dimension formula reads, with \(z_k := \lambda_k + n - k\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:weyl}\tag{4} \dim V_\lambda(L) = D_n(z) := \frac{V_n(z)}{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j!}, \qquad V_n(z) := \prod_{1 \le k < l \le n} (z_k - z_l) \end{equation}\] [FH, Eq. (15.17)]. Fix one lattice in each class of \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\). A form of weight \(\lambda\) assigns to each \(L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n\) a vector \(\varphi(L) \in V_\lambda(L)\) fixed by \(\Gamma_L\). The forms make up a space \(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n)\), with the squared norm \[\lVert \varphi \rVert^2 := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \frac{1}{\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert}\, \lVert \varphi(L) \rVert^2 .\] These are the algebraic modular forms of Gross [Gr] on the unitary group of \(I_n\), of level one.

Tag 22LL

Problem 21.3.6. Let \(j \ge 1\), \(\lambda := (8j, 0)\) and \(\lambda' := (4j, 4j)\). Then \(\dim \mathcal{A}_\lambda(2) = j + 1\) and \(\dim \mathcal{A}_{\lambda'}(2) = 1\), while the scalar contributions \(4 M_2 \dim V\) of Problem 21.3.4 are \(j + \frac18\) and \(\frac18\). In both cases the non-scalar elements of \(\Gamma_{I_2}\) contribute exactly \(\frac78\).

These weights have the same total degree and different gaps.

Fix a positive rank \(n\). When \(4 \mid \lvert\lambda\rvert\) and all the gaps \(\lambda_k - \lambda_{k+1}\) tend to infinity, the Weyl character formula shows that each non-scalar \(\gamma\) contributes \(o(\dim V_\lambda)\), so \(\dim \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n) \sim 4 M_n \dim V_\lambda\). The count of forms is thus asymptotically four times the product of the mass and the Weyl dimension. By (3) and (4) each factor contains \(\prod_{j<n} j!\), once in the numerator and once in the denominator; §21.4 returns to this.

§21.4. The count of forms Tag 22LM

When \(4 \mid \lvert\lambda\rvert\) and the gaps of \(\lambda\) are large, the space of forms of weight \(\lambda\) has dimension about \(4 M_n \dim V_\lambda\) (§21.3). The mass contains the product \(\prod_{j<n} j!\), whose logarithm is about \(\tfrac12 n^2 \log n\), and the Weyl dimension contains its inverse. How does \(M_rD_r\) change when the rank increases?

Tag 22LN

Problem 21.4.1. \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \log j! = \tfrac12 n^2 \log n - \tfrac34 n^2 + O(n \log n)\).

Tag 22LP

Problem 21.4.2. Let \(r_0 < r_1\), and let \(u \in \mathbb{Z}^{r_0}\) and \(w \in \mathbb{Z}^{r_1}\) be strictly decreasing. Then \[\frac{M_{r_1} D_{r_1}(w)}{M_{r_0} D_{r_0}(u)} = \frac{V_{r_1}(w)}{V_{r_0}(u)}\; \pi^{-\sum_{r = r_0+1}^{r_1} r} \prod_{r = r_0+1}^{r_1} \kappa_r .\]

With \(r_0 = 0\), where \(M_0 = D_0 = V_0 = 1\) (empty products), Problem 21.4.2 reads as follows.

Tag 22LQ

Theorem 21.4.1 (The count identity). For every \(r \ge 1\) and every strictly decreasing \(z \in \mathbb{Z}^r\), \[M_r D_r(z) = V_r(z)\, \pi^{-r(r+1)/2} \prod_{j=1}^{r} \kappa_j .\]

No factorial survives: what remains is a product of differences of weights, a power of \(\pi\) and the factors \(\kappa_j\).

The simplest instance adds one rank, with one large new weight.

Tag 22LR

Problem 21.4.3. Fix \(r_0\ge1\) and a strictly decreasing \(u\in\mathbb{Z}^{r_0}\). For an integer \(w_1>u_1\), let \(w=(w_1,u_1,\dots,u_{r_0})\). Then \[\log \frac{M_{r_0+1} D_{r_0+1}(w)}{M_{r_0} D_{r_0}(u)} = \sum_{i=1}^{r_0} \bigl( \log(w_1 - u_i) - \log \pi \bigr) - \log\pi + \log \kappa_{r_0+1} .\]

For fixed \(r_0\) and \(u\), the sum in this formula grows like \(r_0\log w_1\) as \(w_1\to\infty\), and the remaining term \(\log\kappa_{r_0+1}-\log\pi\) is constant.
