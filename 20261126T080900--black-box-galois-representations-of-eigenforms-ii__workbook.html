To study denominators of projections in Chapter 80, we need local conditions on the Galois representations of definite eigenforms. Chapter 32 gives attachment and the spherical comparison away from \(\ell\). Here we give the comparison above \(\ell\), tame conditions and auxiliary-level inputs. The literature gives analogous local comparisons for automorphic representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\). For definite eigenforms the statements below use polynomials that kill Frobenius.

Prerequisites.

Section 32.4 for weights and Hecke polynomials, and Sections 32.2 and 32.3 for the Gaussian CM character and \(X_0(11)\). Use Definitions 34.1.1 and 34.1.2 for periods and jumps. A uniformizer gives the geometric Frobenius class under local reciprocity into the topological abelianization.

§35.1. Spherical places above \(\ell\) Tag 239Y

What do crystalline periods recover from the weight and Hecke polynomial?

Keep the notation of Section 32.4. Thus \(F\) is CM, \(\Phi\) is its CM type, \(n\) is the rank, \(\kappa=(c,e)\) is the weight of a form, and \(\iota:\mathbb C\simeq\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) for the prime \(\ell\). The cyclotomic character has jump \(-1\) in the convention of Definition 34.1.2.

Tag 239Z

Definition 35.1.1. For \(\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) inducing the infinite place \(v\), set \[\lambda_j=e_v+\sum_{j\le h<n}c_v(h)\qquad(1\le j\le n).\] The jumps of the weight are \[\mathrm{HT}_\sigma(\kappa)= \begin{cases} \{j-1-\lambda_j:1\le j\le n\},&\sigma=\Phi_v,\\ \{\lambda_j+n-j:1\le j\le n\},&\sigma\ne\Phi_v . \end{cases}\] Braces in the jump formula denote multisets.

The Gaussian CM character tests the two signs in rank one.

Tag 23A2

Problem 35.1.1. For \(n=1\) the weight has jump \(-e_v\) at \(\Phi_v\) and \(+e_v\) at its conjugate.

Tag 23A3

Problem 35.1.2. Use \(F=\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\), \(\ell=\lambda\bar\lambda\) split, and the character \(\psi_\lambda\) of Problem 32.2.4. Write \(\psi_\lambda^c\) for the character obtained by conjugating the argument by the nontrivial automorphism of \(F/\mathbb Q\). The ratio \(\psi_\lambda/\psi_\lambda^c\) has jumps \(-1\) at \(\lambda\) and \(+1\) at \(\bar\lambda\). With \(\Phi_v\) chosen so \(\iota\Phi_v\) induces \(\lambda\), the jumps agree with \(e_v=1\). The ratio of the corresponding complex Hecke characters descends to \(U(1)\).

In this section \(\Delta=q\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^j)^{24}=\sum_{j\ge1}\tau(j)q^j\) denotes Ramanujan’s level-one weight-twelve cusp form. In the cohomological normalization, the local representation at \(\ell\) is crystalline with jumps \(0,11\) and crystalline Frobenius polynomial \(X^2-\tau(\ell)X+\ell^{11}\). Use Scholl’s good-reduction model and Frobenius comparison [Sch], with Tsuji’s crystalline comparison [Ts].

For the general comparison, let \(A\) be a valuation ring of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) over a place \(w\mid\ell\). Write \(G_F=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/F)\) and let \(D_A\) be the stabilizer of \(A\) in \(G_F\). A local datum consists of an embedding \(\varepsilon:\overline{\mathbb Q}\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) pulling the valuation ring back to \(A\), and a topological isomorphism \(j_A\) from \(D_A\) onto the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell/\mathbb Q_\ell)\) fixing \(\varepsilon(F)\). The maps satisfy \(j_A(s)(\varepsilon(x))=\varepsilon(sx)\) for \(s\in D_A\) and \(x\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\). An automorphism \(g\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell/\mathbb Q_\ell)\) gives a coefficient embedding by composition with \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_\ell\), where \(\mathbb C_\ell\) is the completion. Write \(\sigma=\iota^{-1}g^{-1}\varepsilon|_F\).

For a representation \(r\) on \(V_r=\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell^n\), let \(\gamma:\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\to\mathbb C_\ell\) be the coefficient embedding given by \(g\). The multiplicity of a jump \(u\in\mathbb Z\) at \(\gamma\) is the dimension over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) of the space of \(\gamma\)-semilinear maps \(a:V_r\to\mathbb C_\ell\) such that for each \(v\in V_r\) and all \(h\in D_A\) sufficiently close to the identity for that vector, \[a(r(h)v)=\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(j_A(h))^{-u}\,j_A(h)(a(v)).\] Here \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is cyclotomic and \(j_A(h)\) acts by continuity on \(\mathbb C_\ell\). Infinite dimension counts as zero.

For \(s\in D_A\) and a nonnegative integer \(k\), \((j_A(s),k)\) is a Weil pair if \(j_A(s)^{-1}\) acts on the residue field of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) by the \(\ell^k\)-power map. Use \(B_{\max}\) as in Definition 34.1.1. The restriction is crystalline along the datum if the periods fixed by all \(j_A(h)\otimes r(h)\), \(h\in D_A\), span \(B_{\max}\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}V_r\) over \(B_{\max}\). Potential crystallinity uses periods fixed for \(h\) in a neighborhood of the identity which may depend on the period.

For a Weil pair and a potentially crystalline restriction, \(\mathrm{Fr}^{\mathrm{cris}}_{s,k}\) is the unique \(B_{\max}\)-linear endomorphism extending \((\varphi^k j_A(s))\otimes r(s)\) on those potentially crystalline periods. Here \(\varphi\) is the Frobenius of the period ring, and coefficients over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) act on the factor \(V_r\).

Tag 23A8

Theorem 35.1.1 (Comparison above \(\ell\)). Let \(F\) have Weil data, let \(n\ge1\), let \(f\) be an eigenform of weight \(\kappa\) for \(P\) on the standard definite group, and let \(r\) be attached to \(P\), as in Theorem 32.4.1. If \(f\) is fixed by \(\mathcal K_w\) at \(w\mid\ell\), then at every \(A\) over \(w\) and for every local datum:

  1. \(r|_{D_A}\) is crystalline.

  2. For each \(g\) the jumps at the coefficient embedding given by \(g\) are \(\mathrm{HT}_\sigma(\kappa)\), with \(\sigma=\iota^{-1}g^{-1}\varepsilon|_F\).

  3. If \(w\) is split, each Hecke polynomial \(Q\) of \(f\) at \(w\) gives \((\iota Q)(\mathrm{Fr}^{\mathrm{cris}}_{s,k})=0\) for every \(s\in D_A\) whose inverse induces the \(q_w\)-power map and every \(k\) giving a Weil pair. Here \(q_w\) is the residue-field size.

Used in: Chapter 32

The comparison is read from the rewritten Lean proof [GL]. For regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_n\), see [BLGGT, Theorem 2.1.1] and [BLGGT2]. Zhang gives the literature statement in these Frobenius and jump conventions [Zh, Theorem 4.1].

The rewritten Lean proof uses Hecke eigensystems in Shimura variety cohomology, the congruence relation and crystalline comparison [GL]. In odd rank it glues restricted representations from quadratic weak base changes. To treat general rank, two theta lifts to rank \(2n+1\) then give representations whose twists have disjoint lists of \(n+1\) extra characters to cancel. At split places the extra factors are coprime [GL].

Let \(E_{11}=X_0(11)\) with equation \(y^2+y=x^3-x^2-10x-20\), as in Section 32.3. For \(p\notin\{11,\ell\}\), write \(a_p=p+1-\#E_{11}(\mathbb F_p)\). The next model restricts the Tate-module representation to \(\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\) and compares split and inert primes. Use arithmetic Frobenius on the Tate module, or geometric Frobenius on its dual as in Problem 32.4.4.

§35.2. Tame level Tag 23AC

At smaller level we use the inertia action and its annihilating polynomials.

At a split place \(w\), the local group is \(\mathrm{GL}_n(F_w)\), acting by translation \(\rho\). The pro-Iwahori subgroup \(I_1\) is the preimage of the upper unitriangular subgroup in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(k_w)\) under reduction of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_w)\), where \(k_w\) is the residue field. Write \(M_{I_1}\) for averaging with probability Haar measure over \(I_1\). A diagonal tuple \(t\) is dominant if the additive valuations of its entries are nonincreasing. For characters \(\xi_i:F_w^\times\to\mathbb C^\times\) the corresponding torus character is \[\xi(t)=\prod_{i=1}^n\xi_i(t_i)|t_i|_w^{n-i}, \qquad |x|_w=q_w^{-\operatorname{ord}_w(x)} .\] Use \(D_A\) for the stabilizer of \(A\) at any finite place \(w\), and \(\operatorname{Art}_w:F_w^\times\to D_A^{\mathrm{ab}}\) for the local Artin map. A representation is of finite type at \(A\) if inertia acts trivially in some neighborhood of the identity when \(w\nmid\ell\), or if the restriction is potentially crystalline along every local datum when \(w\mid\ell\). For a continuous representation the inertia condition means finite inertia image. For \(s\in G_F\), a polynomial \(Q'\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell[X]\) kills \(s\) at \(A\) if it satisfies the following tests. Away from \(\ell\), \(Q'(r(s))=0\). Above \(\ell\), if \(s\in D_A\), then for each local datum and each Weil pair \((j_A(s),k)\) the restriction \(r|_{D_A}\) is potentially crystalline along the datum and \(Q'(\mathrm{Fr}^{\mathrm{cris}}_{s,k})=0\).

Two inertia tests distinguish finite tame image from unipotent inertia.

Tag 23AF

Theorem 35.2.1 (Tame comparison). Let \(F\) have Weil data and let \(n\ge1\). Let \(f\) be a weight-\(\kappa\) eigenform for \(P\) on the standard definite group, and let \(r\) be attached to \(P\). Let \(w\) be split and \(A\) lie above \(w\). Assume \(I_1\) fixes \(f\) and \(M_{I_1}\rho(\operatorname{diag}t) f=\xi(t)f\) for dominant \(t\), with \(|\xi_i(\varpi_w)|\) nonincreasing in \(i\) for the chosen uniformizer \(\varpi_w\). Let \(s\in D_A\), \(k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) and \(x\in F_w^\times\) satisfy \(s^{-1}\) inducing the \(q_w^k\)-power map on the residue field of \(A\), \(\operatorname{ord}_w(x)=k\) and \(\operatorname{Art}_w(x)=[s]\) in the topological abelianization of \(D_A\). Then \(r\) is of finite type at \(A\) and \(\prod_i(X-\iota\xi_i(x))\) kills \(s\) at \(A\). Above \(\ell\), the jumps at each coefficient embedding along every local datum are \(\mathrm{HT}_\sigma(\kappa)\).

For the parahoric version, let \(w\nmid\ell\) be split, let \(A\) lie over \(w\), and let \(f\ne0\) belong to a Hecke isotypic space of weight-\(\kappa\) forms on the standard definite group. Use the representation \(r\) chosen for the Hecke polynomials of that space. Assign nonnegative integer labels \(b_i\) to the slots. The parahoric is the subgroup of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_w)\) whose reduction has zero \(ij\)-entry when \(b_i>b_j\). Suppose \(I_1\) fixes \(f\) and the parahoric acts on \(f\) by scalars. For a local unit \(u\), let \(d_i\) be the scalar of the diagonal with \(u\) in slot \(i\) and ones elsewhere. Assume \(d_i=d_j\) implies \(b_i=b_j\).

Let \(\varpi\) be a uniformizer. Let \(s,s'\in D_A\) be a geometric Frobenius and an inertia element, respectively, with \(\operatorname{Art}_w(\varpi)=[s]\) and \(\operatorname{Art}_w(u)=[s']\). Then \(r(s)\) and \(r(s')\) commute and every polynomial over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) vanishing at all \(\iota(d_i)\) kills \(r(s')\). There are characters \(\theta_i:F_w^\times\to\mathbb C^\times\) and a complex linear functional \(L\) on the space of weight-\(\kappa\) forms, with \(L(f)\ne0\), such that \[L(\rho(\operatorname{diag}t)M_{I_1}z)=\xi_\theta(t)L(z)\] for all dominant \(t\) and all forms \(z\) of that weight. Here \(\xi_\theta\) is the torus character obtained using the \(\theta_i\). For every integer \(b\ge0\), \[\prod_i\bigl(X-\iota(\theta_i(\varpi)d_i^b)\bigr) \quad\text{kills}\quad r(s)r(s')^b.\]

§35.3. Auxiliary places and root chains Tag 23AG

First we state the integral operator whose conjugates sum to a scalar. Then we give the bound for chains of roots of a spherical Hecke polynomial.

Tag 23AH

Theorem 35.3.1 (Integral operator). Take Taylor–Wiles places \(w_j\) of level \(\ell^\nu\) as in Definition 84.3.1 over \(F\). Fix a definite hermitian space of rank \(n\) over \(F\) with an adelic basis in which the Gram matrix is the identity at every place. Use the basis for maximal level, reduction and coordinates of forms. Fix a weight \(\kappa\) and a subspace of its forms with local type supported at a finite set of places. Assume a subgroup containing a neighborhood of the adelic identity acts on the subspace by a character through the components at the support and its conjugate. Every complex linear operator on the full space commuting with the maximal level through those components must preserve the subspace. The base-level forms are the vectors in the subspace fixed by maximal level outside the support and its conjugate.

Take \(w_j\nmid\ell\) with \(w_j,\bar w_j\) outside the support. In each Hecke isotypic space meeting the base level nontrivially, assume all base-level forms are spherical at each \(w_j\) with the eigenvalues chosen for that isotypic space. Within the same subspace, principal level replaces the maximal level at each \(w_j\) by the kernel of reduction. A form is integral if its coordinates under \(\iota\) are integral at every adelic point whose components above \(\ell\) are in the maximal level. Let \(n_{\mathrm{st}}\) be a nonnegative integer multiple of the orders of the stabilizers of maximal-level cosets under the group of rational points.

For any finite group \(H\) embedded in \(\prod_j\mathrm{GL}_n(k_{w_j})\), there is a complex linear endomorphism \(\Psi\) of the principal-level space preserving its integral forms such that \[\sum_{h\in H}h^{-1}\Psi h=n_{\mathrm{st}}\operatorname{id}.\] The residue group acts by right translation through the lift components off the support and its conjugate.

Used in: Chapter 84

The operator is read from the rewritten Lean proof [GL]. For group-ring freeness in patching, see [TW; CHT].

A two-point orbit models the conjugate sum.

Theorem 35.3.2 (Chains of Hecke roots). Let \(n\ge1\) and let \(x\ne0\) be fixed by \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_w)\) in a smooth unitary complex representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(F_w)\). For each nonnegative integer \(j\), assume the \(j\)-th spherical Hecke operator acts on \(x\) by \(t_j\), and write \(P=H(q_w,n,t)\) as in Section 32.4.

  1. If \(P(c)=0\) and \(P(q_wc)\ne0\), then \(|c|^2\ge q_w^n/q_w^2\).

  2. If \(P(c)=0\) and \(|c|^2q_w^k=q_w^n\) for an integer \(k\ge0\), then \(P(cq_w^i)=0\) for each integer \(0\le i<k\).

§35.4. Avatars of Hecke characters Tag 23AM

The avatar formula gives the value at an Artin class.

Tag 23AN

Theorem 35.4.1 (Avatar). Let \(F\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}\) be a totally complex number field and \(\chi\) an algebraic Hecke character with finite part \(\chi_f\) and infinite part \(\prod_\sigma\sigma^{a_\sigma}\), with integer exponents indexed by all embeddings \(F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\). The kernel of \(\chi_f\) contains a neighborhood of the identity. On principal finite ideles \(x\in F^\times\), the finite part is \(\chi_f(x)=\prod_\sigma\sigma(x)^{-a_\sigma}\). Its continuous \(\ell\)-adic avatar \(\chi_\ell:G_F\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell^\times\) satisfies \[\chi_\ell(s)=\iota(\chi_f(x))\prod_\sigma(\iota\sigma)(x)^{a_\sigma} \qquad\text{if }[s]=\operatorname{Art}_F(x).\] Here \(x\) is a finite idele, \(s\in G_F\), and \(\operatorname{Art}_F\) is geometric reciprocity into the topological abelianization. The expression \((\iota\sigma)(x)\) evaluates the component at the place induced by \(\iota\sigma\) using the extended local embedding. At \(w\nmid\ell\) where \(\chi_f\) is unramified, the avatar takes the value \(\iota(\chi_f(\varpi_w))\) on each geometric Frobenius at every \(A\) over \(w\), with \(\varpi_w\) viewed as an idele supported at \(w\).

The formula follows from class field theory [Ser]. Above \(\ell\), the restriction of the avatar to \(G_{F_w}\) is potentially crystalline. It is crystalline if \(\chi_f\) is trivial on the units at \(w\). The classical rank-one criterion is [Con, Proposition B.4], in arithmetic reciprocity.
