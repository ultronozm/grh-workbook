Prerequisites.

Valuations on \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) [Ne, Ch. II]; roots of unity of prime power order [Wa, Ch. 1–2]. No earlier chapter.

Chapter 85, Twists with few congruences, describes how the proof bounds Selmer lengths and the valuations of ratios of Hecke \(L\)-values at each prime \(\ell\) through values of polynomials at points \((\zeta_w - 1)_w\), with the \(\zeta_w\) of \(\ell\)-power order. Suppose finitely many integral polynomials have starting points whose coordinates have valuation at least a fixed rational \(\rho > 0\), and the sum of their starting valuations is bounded. Can one replace all the points by one tuple \((\zeta_w - 1)_w\) while increasing each valuation by at most a prescribed \(\varepsilon > 0\)? Theorem 86.2.1 gives a level depending only on the prime, the bound on the number of variables, \(\rho\), the sum bound and \(\varepsilon\). Corollary 86.2.2 treats the first \(m\) powers simultaneously.

Throughout, \(\ell\) is a prime, \(\operatorname{val}\) is the valuation of \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) with \(\operatorname{val}(\ell) = 1\) and \(\operatorname{val}(0) = \infty\), \(\lVert y\rVert = \ell^{-\operatorname{val}(y)}\), and \(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell= \{y : \operatorname{val}(y) \ge 0\}\). For \(h \ge 0\) put \[\rho_h := 1/\varphi(\ell^h),\] with Euler’s function \(\varphi\). For a polynomial \(g = \sum_i y_i Y^i \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell[Y]\) and a rational \(\rho \ge 0\), we define the Gauss valuation by \(v_\rho(g) := \min_i \bigl(\operatorname{val}(y_i) + \rho i\bigr)\), and \(R_\rho(g)\) is the multiset of roots of \(g\) in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) of valuation exactly \(\rho\), counted with multiplicity; for the zero polynomial \(v_\rho(0) = \infty\) and \(R_\rho(0) = \varnothing\). Below, \(g\) denotes a nonzero polynomial in \(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell[Y]\). “Of order \(\ell^h\)” means of exact order \(\ell^h\). We write \(\zeta_n\) for a root of unity of exact order \(n\).

§86.1. One variable

Given \(g\) and a point \(x\) of positive valuation, can one replace \(x\) by \(\zeta - 1\), with \(\zeta\) a root of unity of \(\ell\)-power order, without making \(g\) much more divisible?

Problem 86.1.1. If \(h \ge 1\) and \(\zeta\) has order \(\ell^h\), then \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1) = \rho_h\). For \(\ell = 3\) the radii \(\rho_1, \rho_2, \rho_3\) are \(1/2, 1/6, 1/18\); for \(\ell = 2\) they are \(1, 1/2, 1/4\). The \(\rho_h\) with \(h \ge 1\) are distinct and tend to \(0\).

Used in: Chapter 87

Problem 86.1.2. Let \(0 \ne g \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell[Y]\), and write \(g = c\prod_k (Y - \alpha_k)\) in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell[Y]\). For rational \(\rho \ge 0\), \[v_\rho(g) = \operatorname{val}(c) + \sum\nolimits_k \min\bigl(\operatorname{val}(\alpha_k), \rho\bigr).\] Hence \(v_\rho(g_1g_2) = v_\rho(g_1) + v_\rho(g_2)\), and \(\rho \mapsto v_\rho(g)\) is nondecreasing.

Used in: Chapter 87

Problem 86.1.3. (a) If \(\operatorname{val}(x) \ge \rho\), then \(\operatorname{val}(g(x)) \ge v_\rho(g)\). (b) If \(\operatorname{val}(y) = \rho\), then \[\operatorname{val}(g(y)) = v_\rho(g) + \sum\nolimits_{\alpha \in R_\rho(g)} \bigl(\operatorname{val}(y - \alpha) - \rho\bigr).\]

Used in: Chapter 87

Problem 86.1.4. Let \(\ell = 3\) and \(g = Y^2 + 3Y + 3\). Then \(v_\rho(g) = \min(1, 2\rho)\), and for \(\zeta\) of order \(3^h\) the valuation \(\operatorname{val}(g(\zeta - 1))\) is \(\infty\) if \(h = 1\) and \(2\rho_h = v_{\rho_h}(g)\) if \(h \ge 2\).

We bound the root counts on the radii \(\rho_h \le \rho\), each weighted by \(\rho_h\). For a rational \(\rho > 0\) and an integer \(h_0 \ge 1\), let \(H(\rho, h_0)\) be the set of \(h\) with \(1 \le h \le h_0\) and \(\rho_h \le \rho\).

Problem 86.1.5. \(\sum_{h \in H(\rho, h_0)} \# R_{\rho_h}(g) \cdot \rho_h \le v_\rho(g)\).

Problem 86.1.6. Let \(\delta > 0\) and \(\beta \ge 0\) (in this sheet \(\beta\) is a real bound), with \(\beta < \delta \sum_{h \in H(\rho, h_0)} 1/(h + 1)\). If \(v_\rho(g) \le \beta\), then some \(h \in H(\rho, h_0)\) has \(\# R_{\rho_h}(g) \cdot \rho_h \le \delta/(h + 1)\).

Next, how close can the \(\zeta - 1\) of a given order be to a fixed point?

Problem 86.1.7. Let \(\alpha \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\) with \(\operatorname{val}(\alpha) > 0\), and \(h \ge 1\). Then \[\sum\nolimits_{\zeta} \min\bigl(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1 - \alpha), 1\bigr) \le h + 1,\] the sum over the \(\zeta\) of order \(\ell^h\). For \(\ell = 3\), \(h = 1\) and \(\alpha = \zeta_0 - 1\), with \(\zeta_0\) of order \(3\), the sum is \(3/2\).

Problem 86.1.8. For \(\ell = 3\), \(h = 2\) and \(\alpha = \zeta_9 - 1\), the six terms \(\min(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1 - \alpha), 1)\), for \(\zeta = \zeta_9^e\) with \(e = 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8\), are \(1, \tfrac16, \tfrac12, \tfrac16, \tfrac12, \tfrac16\); their sum is \(\tfrac52 \le h + 1\).

Problem 86.1.9. Let \(R\) be a finite multiset of elements of \(\bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\) of positive valuation, and \(h \ge 1\). Some \(\zeta\) of order \(\ell^h\) has \(\sum_{\alpha \in R} \min\bigl(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1 - \alpha), 1\bigr) \le \#R \cdot \rho_h (h + 1)\).

Problem 86.1.10. Let \(0 < \delta < 1\), \(\rho > 0\) rational, and \(\beta < \delta\sum_{h \in H(\rho, h_0)} 1/(h + 1)\). If \(v_\rho(g) \le \beta\), then for some \(h \in H(\rho, h_0)\) and some \(\zeta\) of order \(\ell^h\), \[\operatorname{val}\bigl(g(\zeta - 1)\bigr) \le v_{\rho_h}(g) + \delta .\]

Used in: Problem 86.1.12

Problem 86.1.11. For rational \(\rho > 0\), real \(\delta > 0\) and \(\beta \ge 0\), some \(h_0\) has \(\beta < \delta\sum_{h \in H(\rho, h_0)} 1/(h + 1)\). For \(\ell = 2\), \(\rho = \delta = 1/2\) and \(\beta = 1\), the least such \(h_0\) is \(18\).

Problem 86.1.12. Let \(\delta\), \(\rho\), \(\beta\), \(h_0\) be as in Problem 86.1.10. If \(\operatorname{val}(x) \ge \rho\) and \(\operatorname{val}(g(x)) \le \beta\), then some \(\zeta\) with \(\zeta^{\ell^{h_0}} = 1\) and \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1) \le \rho\) has \(\operatorname{val}(g(\zeta - 1)) \le \operatorname{val}(g(x)) + \delta\).

§86.2. Several variables and several powers

Let \(\rho > 0\) be rational, and \(\mathcal V\) and \(I\) finite sets. For \(i \in I\) let \(F_i \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell[X_j : j \in \mathcal V]\), and let \(x_i \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell^\mathcal V\) be a point. We seek one tuple of roots of unity that works for every \(i\).

Problem 86.2.1. Fix \(j \in \mathcal V\), and assume \(\operatorname{val}(x_{ij}) \ge \rho\) for all \(i\) (the other coordinates are only integral). Let \(0 < \delta < 1\), \(\beta < \delta\sum_{h \in H(\rho, h_0)} 1/(h + 1)\), and \(\sum_i \operatorname{val}(F_i(x_i)) \le \beta\). There is \(\zeta\) with \(\zeta^{\ell^{h_0}} = 1\) and \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1) \le \rho\) such that, if the coordinate \(x_{ij}\) is replaced by \(\zeta - 1\) for every \(i\), each \(\operatorname{val}(F_i(x_i))\), and also their sum, grows by at most \(\delta\).

Problem 86.2.2. Let \(0 < \delta < 1\), \(\#\mathcal V \cdot \delta \le 1\), \(\beta + 1 < \delta\sum_{h \in H(\rho, h_0)} 1/(h + 1)\), \(\operatorname{val}(x_{ij}) \ge \rho\) for all \(i, j\), and \(\sum_i \operatorname{val}(F_i(x_i)) \le \beta\). There are \(\zeta_j\), \(j \in \mathcal V\), with \(\zeta_j^{\ell^{h_0}} = 1\), \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta_j - 1) \le \rho\), and \(\operatorname{val}\bigl(F_i((\zeta_j - 1)_j)\bigr) \le \operatorname{val}(F_i(x_i)) + \#\mathcal V\cdot\delta\) for all \(i\).

Problem 86.2.3. Let \(\ell = 3\), \(F_1 = X + Y\), \(F_2 = XY + 3\), \(F_3 = X^2 + 3X + 3\), with starting points \((3, 0)\), \((0, 3)\), \((0, 0)\), at which each \(F_i\) has valuation \(1\). For \(\zeta = \zeta_{27}\), the common point \((\zeta - 1, \zeta^2 - 1)\) gives \(\operatorname{val}(F_i) = \tfrac19\) for \(i = 1, 2, 3\).

The following theorem is proved by the problems of this section. In Theorem 86.2.1, the level \(h_0\) depends only on \(\ell, s_0, \rho, \beta, \varepsilon\).

Theorem 86.2.1. Let \(s_0 \ge 0\) be an integer, \(\rho > 0\) rational, \(\beta \ge 0\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\). There is \(h_0\) with the following property. Let \(\mathcal V\), \(I\) be finite sets with \(\#\mathcal V \le s_0\), let \(F_i \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell[X_j : j \in \mathcal V]\) and \(x_i \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell^\mathcal V\) for \(i \in I\), with \(\operatorname{val}(x_{ij}) \ge \rho\) for all \(i, j\) and \(\sum_i \operatorname{val}(F_i(x_i)) \le \beta\). Then there are \(\zeta_j \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\), \(j \in \mathcal V\), with \[\zeta_j^{\ell^{h_0}} = 1,\quad \operatorname{val}(\zeta_j-1)\le\rho \quad(j\in\mathcal V),\qquad \operatorname{val}\bigl(F_i((\zeta_j-1)_j)\bigr) \le \operatorname{val}\bigl(F_i(x_i)\bigr)+\varepsilon \quad(i\in I).\]

Used in: Chapter 85

A twist will be used through its first \(m\) powers at once, so the roots of unity must also serve for \(\zeta_j^n - 1\), \(1 \le n \le m\).

Problem 86.2.4. Let \(n \ge 1\) and \(x \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\) with \(\operatorname{val}(x) \ge \rho\). Some \(x' \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell\) has \((1 + x')^n = 1 + x\) and \(\operatorname{val}(x') \ge \rho/n\).

Problem 86.2.5. Let \(0 < \rho < 1\), \(1 \le n \le m\), and let \(\zeta\) have \(\ell\)-power order and \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta - 1) \le \rho/m\). Then \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta^n - 1) \le \rho\); in particular \(\zeta^n \ne 1\). For \(\ell = 2\), \(\zeta\) of order \(8\), \(m = n = 2\) and \(\rho = 1/2\), equality holds.

Used in: Chapter 85

Problem 86.2.6. Let \(\ell = 3\) and \(\Phi_3(Y) = Y^2 + Y + 1\). The values \(\operatorname{val}(\Phi_3(\zeta_n^j))\) for \(j = 1, 2, 3\) are \(\tfrac13, \tfrac13, \infty\) for \(n = 9\); \(\tfrac19, \tfrac19, \tfrac13\) for \(n = 27\); and \(\tfrac1{27}, \tfrac1{27}, \tfrac19\) for \(n = 81\). For \(m = 3\), \(\rho = 1/2\), one variable, \(F(X) = \Phi_3(1 + X)\), \(x = 0\) and \(\beta = 1\), no root of order \(9\) satisfies the conclusion of Corollary 86.2.2, for any \(\varepsilon > 0\).

Problems 86.2.4 and 86.2.5 and Theorem 86.2.1 give the form used in Chapter 85:

Corollary 86.2.2. Let integers \(s_0 \ge 0\), \(m \ge 1\), a rational \(\rho > 0\), \(\varepsilon > 0\), a finite set \(I\) and numbers \(\beta_i \ge 0\), \(i \in I\), be given. There is \(h_0\) such that, for every finite set \(\mathcal V\) with \(\#\mathcal V \le s_0\), all \(F_i \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell[X_j : j \in \mathcal V]\) and all \(x_i \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell^\mathcal V\) satisfying \(\operatorname{val}(x_{ij}) \ge \rho\) for every \(i, j\) and \(\operatorname{val}(F_i(x_i)) \le \beta_i\) for every \(i\), some \(\zeta_j\) with \(\zeta_j^{\ell^{h_0}} = 1\) and \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta_j - 1) \le \rho/m\) satisfy \[\operatorname{val}\bigl(F_i((\zeta_j^n - 1)_j)\bigr) \le \operatorname{val}\bigl(F_i(x_i)\bigr) + \varepsilon \qquad (i \in I,\ 1 \le n \le m).\]

The exercises derive Theorem 86.2.1 and Corollary 86.2.2 from the stated prerequisites and the divergence of the harmonic series. Chapter 87 states the polynomial input, and Chapter 85 uses Corollary 86.2.2 to construct the twist and bound its powers.
