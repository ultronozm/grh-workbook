Prerequisites.

The zeta function and Dirichlet \(L\)-functions as in [Dav]: orthogonality of characters and \(L(1, \chi) \ne 0\) for nonprincipal \(\chi\) (Ch. 4), the class number formula (Ch. 6), the Hadamard product and the constant \(B\) (Ch. 12), nonvanishing on \(\operatorname{Re}s = 1\) (Ch. 13 and 14), the explicit formula for Chebyshev’s function \(\psi(x)\) and its contour shift (Ch. 17), their analogues for \(L(s, \chi)\) (Ch. 12 and 19), and the prime number theorem in progressions to a fixed modulus (Ch. 20). Chebyshev’s bound \(\psi(x) \ll x\) [MV]. Mellin transforms. Landau’s theorem is stated in §1.2. Section 1.4 uses the auxiliary-prime theorem stated as Theorem 2.2.2 in Chapter 2. Chapter 16 is used only for motivation, in §1.3.

A comparison of divisibility with size, as in Chebyshev’s method, gives upper bounds for weighted sums over primes. Can an upper bound for a sum over primes prove the Riemann hypothesis? This chapter shows that it can, for a suitable weight. After the zeta function, Theorem 1.4.1 gives the criterion for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions in the form used in the proof. The upper bound itself is not treated here.

§1.1. The ramp sum Tag 2223

Write \(v_+ := \max(v, 0)\) for real \(v\), and let \(\Lambda\) be the von Mangoldt function. We define the ramp at \(x\) to be the function \(n \mapsto (x/n - 1)_+\): it is large for small \(n\), decreases, and vanishes for \(n \ge x\). Let \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:G}\tag{1} S(x) := \sum_{n} \Lambda(n) \Bigl(\frac{x}{n} - 1\Bigr)_+ , \qquad G(x) := S(x) - x \log x + (1 + \gamma) x , \end{equation}\] where \(\gamma\) is Euler’s constant, and let \(\psi(x) := \sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n)\) be Chebyshev’s function. In §1.4, \(\psi\) denotes a Dirichlet character. The two subtracted terms are the main term of \(S\), as the problems show.

§1.2. One side suffices Tag 222D

We use the following measurable version of Landau’s theorem [Lan]. Montgomery and Vaughan [MV, Lemma 15.1] assume local Riemann integrability and use \(x^{-s}\); their proof applies to the Lebesgue integral below after replacing \(s\) by \(s+1\). Let \(A \colon [1, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}\) be measurable and bounded on compact sets, with \(A(x) \ge 0\) for \(x \ge X_0\). Let \(\sigma_c\) be the infimum of the real \(\sigma\) with \(\int_1^\infty \lvert A(x) \rvert x^{-\sigma-1} \, d x < \infty\), and suppose that \(-\infty < \sigma_c < \infty\). Then \(H(s) := \int_1^\infty A(x) x^{-s-1} \, d x\) is holomorphic in \(\operatorname{Re}s > \sigma_c\), and it has no holomorphic continuation to a neighborhood of \(\sigma_c\).

§1.3. Smoothing, and the triangle weight Tag 222P

Why the ramp, and not another weight? Let \(\phi \colon (0, \infty) \to \mathbb{R}\) be measurable, vanish on \((1, \infty)\), be bounded on compact subsets of \((0, 1]\), and satisfy \(\phi(t) = O(1/t)\) as \(t \to 0\). Let \[\psi_\phi(x) := \sum_n \Lambda(n) \phi(n/x) , \qquad \tilde\phi(s) := \int_0^\infty \phi(t) t^{s-1} \, d t \quad (\operatorname{Re}s > 1) .\] We use \(\tilde\phi\) on its half-plane of absolute convergence and, for the three weights below, also for the meromorphic continuation given by their rational formulas. The sharp cutoff \(\phi = \mathbf{1}_{(0,1]}\) gives \(\psi_\phi = \psi\) and \(\tilde\phi(s) = 1/s\). The ramp is \(\phi(t) = (1/t - 1)_+\), with \(\tilde\phi(s) = 1/(s(s-1))\) by Problem 1.1.1. We define the triangle weight by \(\phi(t) = (1 - t)_+\), with \(\psi_\phi(x) = x^{-1}\int_0^x \psi(t) \, d t\).

Tag 222Q

Problem 1.3.1. For \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1\), \(\int_0^\infty \psi_\phi(x) x^{-s-1} \, d x = -\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s) \, \tilde\phi(s)\).

Tag 222R

Problem 1.3.2. For the triangle weight \(\tilde\phi(s) = 1/(s(s+1))\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s > 0\). If \(\phi_1(t) := \phi(t)/t\), then \(\tilde\phi_1(s) = \tilde\phi(s - 1)\) wherever either integral converges absolutely; for the triangle weight \(\phi_1\) is the ramp.

The following theorem is proved as Theorem 1.1.1: by Problem 1.3.1 with \(\tilde\phi(s) = 1/(s(s+1))\), shift the line of integration to \(\operatorname{Re}s = -3/2\) as in [Dav, Ch. 17], along horizontal segments at heights \(T\) where \(\zeta'/\zeta \ll \log^2 T\). The factor \(1/(s(s+1))\) makes every integral converge absolutely, and the poles of \(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)\, x^s/(s(s+1))\) to the right of \(-3/2\) are at \(1\), \(0\), \(-1\) and the zeros \(\rho\).

Tag 222S

Theorem 1.3.1. For \(x > 1\), \[\sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n) \Bigl(1 - \frac{n}{x}\Bigr) = \frac{x}{2} - \log 2\pi - \sum_\rho \frac{x^\rho}{\rho(\rho + 1)} + O\Bigl(\frac1x\Bigr) .\]

As a check, the left side minus \((x/2 - \log 2\pi)\) is approximately \(0.106\), \(7.975\) and \(-12.115\) at \(x = 10^4\), \(10^6\) and \(2 \cdot 10^6\). For the ramp and the triangle weight \(\lvert \tilde\phi(\rho) \rvert \ll \lvert \rho \rvert^{-2}\), so the sums over zeros converge absolutely. For the sharp cutoff they do not, since \(\sum_\rho 1/\lvert \rho \rvert\) diverges.

Tag 222T

Problem 1.3.3. Let \(0 < \theta < 1\). If \(\sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n)(1 - n/x) \le x/2 + x^\theta\) for all large \(x\), then \(\zeta\) has no zeros in \(\operatorname{Re}s > \theta\).

We return to real zeros in §1.4.

The ramp itself is forced by the arithmetic. Theorem 16.3.1 gives, at primes \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), lower bounds for \(p\)-adic valuations of the form \(\sum_{i \text{ odd}} (\lfloor a/p^i \rfloor - w)_+\). Up to an error of \(1\) for each odd \(i\) with \(p^i \le a\), and with \(a = wx\), the summand is \((a/p^i - w)_+ = w(x/p^i - 1)_+\), which is \(w\) times the ramp at \(x\). Bounds of this form at every inert prime, summed with weights \(\log p\), give the ramp sum over odd powers of primes inert in an imaginary quadratic field; Chapter 2 says what this sum counts.

§1.4. Dirichlet \(L\)-functions Tag 222U

What replaces \(G\) for the \(L\)-functions of the characters modulo \(q\)? The arithmetic bounds sums over primes that do not split in an imaginary quadratic field. For a prime \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the Legendre symbol \(\chi_d(n) = (n/d)\) is the character of \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), and at a prime \(p\) the weight \(1 - \chi_d(p)\) is \(2\) if \(p\) is inert, \(1\) if \(p = d\), and \(0\) if \(p\) splits. This weight brings in the twisted \(L\)-functions \(L(s, \psi\chi_d)\), whose orders of vanishing are subtracted from the untwisted ones in the residues below. The prime \(d\) will be chosen so that they do not vanish where it matters.

For a primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet character \(\chi\) let \(c_\chi := \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi)\) and \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:Gchi}\tag{3} G_\chi(x) := \sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n) \chi(n) \Bigl(\frac{x}{n} - 1\Bigr) + c_\chi x . \end{equation}\] Let \(d\) be an odd prime, \(\chi_d(n) := (n/d)\) the Legendre symbol, and \(q \ge 1\) an integer prime to \(d\). Let \(\varphi\) be Euler’s function. For \(n \ge 1\) let \[\lambda(n) := \Lambda(n)\bigl(1 - \chi_d(n)\bigr) \ \text{if } n \equiv 1 \ (\mathrm{mod}\ q), \qquad \lambda(n) := 0 \ \text{otherwise}.\] The analytic definitions below apply to every odd prime \(d\). For a Dirichlet character \(\psi\) modulo \(q\), let \(L(s, \psi)\) be the meromorphic continuation of \(\sum_{n \ge 1} \psi(n) n^{-s}\) from \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1\); its Euler product there runs over the primes \(p \nmid q\). Let \(E_\psi := L(\,\cdot\,, \psi)\) if \(\psi\) is not principal, and if \(\psi\) is principal let \(E_\psi\) be the entire continuation of \((s - 1)L(s, \psi)\), with \(E_\psi(1) = \prod_{p \mid q}(1 - p^{-1})\). Let \(m_\psi(\rho) \ge 0\) be the order of vanishing of \(E_\psi\) at \(\rho\). The product \(\psi\chi_d\) is a character modulo \(qd\); it is not principal, and we write \(E_{\psi\chi_d} := L(\,\cdot\,, \psi\chi_d)\) and \(m_{\psi\chi_d}\) for its order. Let \[G_{d,q}(s) := \frac{1}{\varphi(q)} \Bigl( \sum_{\psi} \Bigl( \frac{E_{\psi\chi_d}'}{E_{\psi\chi_d}}(s) - \frac{E_\psi'}{E_\psi}(s) \Bigr) - 1 \Bigr),\] the sum over the characters \(\psi\) modulo \(q\). The \(E\)’s do not vanish on \(\operatorname{Re}s \ge 1\), so \(G_{d,q}\) is holomorphic there. Let \(c_{d,q} := \operatorname{Re}G_{d,q}(1)\), and for real \(x > 0\) let \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:R}\tag{4} R_{d,q}(x) := \sum_{1 \le n \le x} \lambda(n) \Bigl( \frac1n - \frac1x \Bigr) - \frac{\log x}{\varphi(q)} - c_{d,q} . \end{equation}\] The simplest case is \(q = 1\), where \(\lambda(n) = \Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_d(n))\) for all \(n\) and \(E_{\mathbf{1}}(s) = (s - 1)\zeta(s)\) for \(s \ne 1\), with \(E_{\mathbf{1}}(1) = 1\).
