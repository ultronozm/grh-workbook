Prerequisites.

Hadamard’s and Fischer’s inequalities for positive definite Hermitian matrices [HJ]. From Chapter 5 heights, covolume and Problem 5.3.2; from Chapter 6 the theorem on a twist for each prime; from Chapter 36 the data and Problem 36.4.6; from Chapter 37 the objects and the target lattice; the auxiliary forms and the turn ratios (Sections 73.4 and 73.2); the factorization of the chain determinant and the bound for small ratios (Chapter 68); the cost of one lift (Theorem 74.2.1); families with small ratios and regular families (Definitions 79.1.2 and 79.1.3); the denominator budget (Definition 36.4.1); and the existence of a regular family (Theorem 85.2.1). Section 67.1 uses no earlier chapter.

Theorem C gives the lower bound used in Chapter 38, The main inequality, for the covolume plus twice the index of a linearly independent family of target forms spanning almost all of the target. For each prime \(\ell\) and each field isomorphism \(\iota\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_\ell\), the proof chooses a twist \(\theta_\iota\). It compares the chain Gram determinant at the trivial twist with its value at \(\theta_\iota\), using a height bound for the family of ratios for each single lift. We first compare index and Gram determinant in a lattice, then state and prove Theorem C in the form used in the proof. This chapter heads Part IX; the chapters that follow it develop its inputs.

§67.1. The comparison in a lattice

Let \(N \ge 1\) be an integer, and give \({\mathbb{C}}^d\) the Hermitian form \(\langle v, w \rangle := N\, \bar v^{\mathsf T} w\). For \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in {\mathbb{Q}}^d\) write \(\operatorname{Gram}(y) := \det(\langle y_i, y_j \rangle)_{i,j \le n}\), and let \(H := \log N\).

Problem 67.1.1. If \(y_1, \dots, y_d \in {\mathbb{Z}}^d\) are linearly independent, then \(\operatorname{Gram}(y) \ge N^d\), so \(\log \operatorname{Gram}(y) \ge dH\). For \(N = 5\), \(y_1 = (1, 2)\), \(y_2 = (2, -1)\) one has \(\operatorname{Gram}(y) = 625\).

Problem 67.1.2. Let \(y_1, \dots, y_d \in {\mathbb{Q}}^d\) be linearly independent, and let \(u \in M_d({\mathbb{Z}})\) with \(\det u \ne 0\) be such that \(\sum_k u_{ik} y_k \in {\mathbb{Z}}^d\) for every \(i\). If \(\log \operatorname{Gram}(y) \le c\) and \(\log\lvert\det u\rvert \le \delta\), then \(c + 2\delta \ge dH\). For \(N = 5\), \(y_1 = (\tfrac12, 1)\), \(y_2 = (0, \tfrac13)\), \(u = \mathrm{diag}(2, 3)\), \(c = \log \operatorname{Gram}(y)\) and \(\delta = \log 6\) there is equality.

Now let the family be shorter than the dimension. Suppose it is completed to a basis by vectors of bounded length.

Problem 67.1.3. Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) and \(z_1, \dots, z_{n'}\) in \({\mathbb{Z}}^d\) together form a basis of \({\mathbb{Q}}^d\), with \(\langle z_i, z_i \rangle \le e^{\beta}\) for every \(i\). Then \(\log \operatorname{Gram}(y) + n'\beta \ge dH\). For \(d = 3\), \(N = 2\), \(y_1 = (1, 1, 0)\), \(y_2 = (0, 1, 1)\), \(z_1 = (1, 0, 0)\): \(\operatorname{Gram}(y) = 12\), \(\operatorname{Gram}(y, z) = 8\), and \(8 \le 12 \cdot 2\).

Problem 67.1.4. Every linearly independent family \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in {\mathbb{Z}}^d\) is completed to a basis of \({\mathbb{Q}}^d\) by \(d - n\) of the standard basis vectors. Hence \(\log \operatorname{Gram}(y) \ge nH\).

In Section 67.2 the main amount (Section 36.4) enters through the height of the family \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\), where the twist \(\theta_\iota\) is chosen for each pair \((\ell, \iota)\) (Problem 67.2.5).

§67.2. Theorem C

We use the parameters \(q_0,d\) and the data of Chapter 36, and the objects and notation of Chapter 37: the target forms \(T\), of dimension \(d_T\); the set \(\mathrm{Tw}\) of \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\), with the trivial twist \(1\) and the norm \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\) of the modulus; the lower chain \(\Theta_\vartheta\); the Gram determinants \(Z(\vartheta)\) of the chain and \(Z_j(\vartheta)\) of the \(j\)th single lift at \(\vartheta^j\); and families of twists \(\theta = (\theta_\iota)\), one twist for each \(\iota \mid \ell\). For \(1 \le i < j \le m\), the ratios \(\rho_{ij}(\vartheta)\) of Definition 72.3.2 occur in inputs (c) and (d). For \(0 \le i,j < m\), the turn ratios \(\rho'_{ij}(\vartheta)\) of Definition 73.2.1 occur in (a) and (d). Both are products, over \(1 \le z \le t\), of ratios of absolute values of Hecke \(L\)-values. Among the conditions on the data, the scales \(x < x_1 < b\) are integers with \[(\log b)^{100} \le x , \qquad x^{10^6} \le x_1 , \qquad x_1^{10^6} \le b , \qquad (\log x_1)^6 \le \log b , \qquad \log\log b \ge 10^{12} ,\] the last inequality following from \(10^{12}C\le\log\log b\) and \(C\ge1\), with \(C\) as in Chapter 36, Section 36.3. Further \(a = xb\), \(\sqrt x \le m \le 2\sqrt x\), \(x^5 \le r \le 3x^5\), \(k = r + 1 - m\), \(t = 5r\) and \(f \ge 1\). With \(U = fa\log^3 b\) write \(F := U\log b\). The main amount is \(h_{\mathrm{main}}= mk\,h_{\mathrm{dir}}\), and \(h_{\mathrm{main}}\ge 0\) by Problem 36.4.6. Heights of families \(\iota \mapsto u_\iota\) are those of Chapter 5, Section 5.3; we write \(\operatorname{ht}(u) \le c\).

The target lattice. The target lattice on \(T\), with twists \(\mathrm{Tw}\), the lifts \(\Theta_\vartheta\), injective by Problem 72.4.3, the twists allowed at \(\ell\), the lattices \(\mathfrak L_{\vartheta,\iota}\), the squared lengths \(\lVert v \rVert_\sigma^2\) of the \(\sigma\)-conjugated coordinate vectors of \(\Theta_1 v\), and index and covolume of a family in \(T\), is that of Definition 37.11.1; its volume at \(\vartheta\) is \(Z(\vartheta)\).

Theorem 6.2.1, for this lattice, reads as follows. Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'}\) be a basis of \(T\), where \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) and index at most \(\delta\); let \(\theta\) be a family of twists with \(\theta_\iota\) allowed at \(\ell\), and \(\gamma \colon \mathrm{Tw} \to {\mathbb{C}}^\times\); suppose (i) \(\gamma(\vartheta)\Theta_\vartheta z_i \in \mathfrak L_{\vartheta,\iota}\) for every \(i\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) and every \(\vartheta\) allowed at \(\ell\); (ii) \(\lVert z_i \rVert_\sigma^2 \le e^{c_{\mathrm{short}}}\) for every \(i\) and \(\sigma\); (iii) \(\operatorname{ht}(\lvert\gamma(\theta_\iota)\rvert^{-2}) \le c_{\mathrm{den}}\); (iv) \(\operatorname{ht}(Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)) \le c_{\mathrm{vol}}\). Then \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:twist}\tag{1} 0 \le c + 2\delta + n'(c_{\mathrm{short}} + c_{\mathrm{den}}) + c_{\mathrm{vol}} . \end{equation}\]

Four inputs supply the hypotheses of the twist inequality. (a) Auxiliary forms (Theorem 73.4.1). There are an integer \(e_\gamma\) and a function \(\gamma \colon \mathrm{Tw} \to {\mathbb{C}}\), with \(\gamma(\vartheta) \ne 0\) for every \(\vartheta\), \(\gamma(1) = 1\) and \(\lvert\gamma(\vartheta)\rvert = (\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta)^{e_\gamma/2}\prod_{0 \le i, j < m} \rho'_{ij}(\vartheta)\) for \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\), such that \(T\) is spanned over \({\mathbb{C}}\) by the forms that are auxiliary for \(\gamma\) (Definition 73.4.1): the \(z \in T\) with \(\gamma(\vartheta)\Theta_\vartheta z \in \mathfrak L_{\vartheta,\iota}\) for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) and every \(\vartheta\) with \(\gcd(\ell, \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta) = 1\), and with \(\lVert z \rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp(2x_1^2b^2)\) for every \(\sigma\). (b) Single lifts. By Theorem 85.2.1 there is a regular family \(\theta\) (Definition 79.1.3); it has small ratios (Definition 79.1.2), so each \(\theta_\iota\) is allowed at \(\ell\); and by Theorem 74.2.1, \(\operatorname{ht}(Z_j(1)/Z_j(\theta_\iota)) \le d_T k(B\log b - h_{\mathrm{dir}})\) for \(1 \le j \le m\), with \(B = U/(mk)\) the budget of Definition 36.4.1. (c) Factorization (Theorem 68.4.1). There is an integer \(e_Z\) such that for every \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) \[\frac{Z(\vartheta)}{Z(1)} = \Bigl( (\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta)^{e_Z/2} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) \Bigr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{Z_j(\vartheta)}{Z_j(1)} .\] (d) Small ratios (Theorem 68.5.1). If \(\theta\) has small ratios, then for every integer \(e\) both families \(\bigl((\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{0 \le i,j < m} \rho'_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\bigr)^{-1}\) and \(\bigl((\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{1 \le i < j \le m} \rho_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\bigr)^{-1}\) have height at most \(m^2tx_1\).

Problem 67.2.1. \(d_T k(B\log b - h_{\mathrm{dir}}) = d_T(F/m - kh_{\mathrm{dir}})\).

Problem 67.2.2. The data satisfy \(2m^2tx_1 \le b\), \(4(2x_1^2 + b^{-1}) \le b\), \(b \le F\) and \(4/b^2 \le 1/2\).

Problem 67.2.3. If \(n \ge (1 - 4/b^2)d_T\) and \(d_T = n + n'\), then \(n'(2x_1^2b^2 + b) \le d_T b\).

So the allowance for one completing auxiliary form is \(2x_1^2b^2 + b\), and the total allowance for the \(n'\) completing forms is at most \(d_T b\).

Problem 67.2.4. For \(\gamma\) as in (a) and \(\theta\) as in (b), \(\operatorname{ht}(\lvert\gamma(\theta_\iota)\rvert^{-2}) \le b\).

Problem 67.2.5. For \(\theta\) as in (b), \(\operatorname{ht}(Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)) \le d_T(b + F - h_{\mathrm{main}})\).

Used in: Chapter 38

Problem 67.2.6. Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in T\) be linearly independent, with \(n \ge (1 - 4/b^2)d_T\), covolume at most \(c\) and index at most \(\delta\). Then \(T\) has a basis \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'}\) with each \(z_i\) as in (a), and \(c + 2\delta \ge d_T(h_{\mathrm{main}}- 3F)\).

Problems 67.2.1–67.2.6 prove the following.

Theorem 67.2.1 (Theorem C). Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) be linearly independent target forms with \((1 - 4/b^2)d_T \le n\), of index at most \(\delta\) and covolume at most \(c\). Then \[d_T(h_{\mathrm{main}}- 3fa\log^4 b) \le c + 2\delta .\]

Used in: Chapter 38; Chapter 68
