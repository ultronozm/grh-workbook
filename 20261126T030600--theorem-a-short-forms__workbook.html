Theorem A says that the ramified source is spanned by integral forms whose normalized translates have untwisted lifts of uniformly bounded squared class norm at every complex conjugate. It also bounds the index of every finite family of translated images of integral ramified forms in terms of the number of translates used. Chapter 38 uses both bounds. The normalization multiplies by a positive rational number \(c_B\), with exponent \(-\lfloor\mathrm{gain}(p)-\mathrm{err}(p)\rfloor\) at each prime \(p\) in the product, including the level prime \(N\); the price of the shortness is paid in index. We first do this with rational vectors, and then follow the exponents in the proof; the size of the composite lift is the subject of Chapter 66.

§49.1. Index and colength over \(\mathbb{Z}\)

Let \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\) be the ring of rationals with denominator prime to \(p\), and \(v_p\) the \(p\)-adic valuation, normalized by \(v_p(p) = 1\), with \(v_p(0) = +\infty\). Let \(y = (y_i)_{i \in \mathcal{J}}\) be a finite family in \(\mathbb{Q}^n\). A square matrix \(u = (u_{ik})_{i,k \in \mathcal{J}}\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\) carries \(y\) with loss \(\delta_p\) if every \(\sum_k u_{ik}y_k\) lies in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^n\) and \(v_p(\det u) \le \delta_p\). The family has index at most \(\delta\) if there are real numbers \(\delta_p \ge 0\) with \(\sum_p \delta_p\log p \le \delta\) such that for every prime \(p\) some matrix carries \(y\) with loss \(\delta_p\).

Problem 49.1.1. The family \((1/6)\) in \(\mathbb{Q}\) has index at most \(\log 6\), and not at most \(\delta\) for any \(\delta < \log 6\).

Problem 49.1.2. A finite family \(y\) in \(\mathbb{Q}^n\) has index at most \(\delta\) if and only if \(\delta \ge \log\,[\Lambda_y : \mathbb{Z}^n]\), where \(\Lambda_y = \mathbb{Z}^n + \sum_i \mathbb{Z}y_i\). For the family \(((\tfrac12, \tfrac12), (0, \tfrac14))\) in \(\mathbb{Q}^2\) one has \([\Lambda_y : \mathbb{Z}^2] = 8\).

Problem 49.1.3. If \(\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}' \sqcup \mathcal{J}''\) and the two subfamilies have index at most \(\delta'\) and \(\delta''\), then \(y\) has index at most \(\delta' + \delta''\). Hence a family that is the union of \(h\) subfamilies of index at most \(\delta\) has index at most \(h\delta\). For the families \((\tfrac12, \tfrac13)\) and \((\tfrac12, \tfrac12)\) in \(\mathbb{Q}\), split into singletons, the bound is \(\log 6\) and \(2\log 2\), and the index is \(\log 6\) and \(\log 2\).

Problem 49.1.4. Let \(W\) be a submodule of \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k\) with \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k/W\) of order \(p^j\). For every finite family \((\varphi_i)_{i \in \mathcal{J}}\) in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k\) there is a square matrix \(u\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\) with \(v_p(\det u) \le j\) and \(\sum_k u_{ik}\varphi_k \in W\) for every \(i\).

Problem 49.1.5. Let \(A\colon \mathbb{Q}^k \to \mathbb{Q}^n\) be linear. Suppose that for every prime \(p\) there is a submodule \(W_p \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k\) of index \(p^{j_p}\) with \(A(W_p) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^n\), and \(j_p = 0\) for almost all \(p\). Then every finite family in \(A(\mathbb{Z}^k)\) has index at most \(\sum_p j_p\log p\).

§49.2. A model of Theorem A

Let \(B\colon \mathbb{Q}^k \to \mathbb{Q}^n\) be linear; it stands for the chain of lifts. Let \(\Pi_0\) be a finite set of primes, and suppose that for each \(p \in \Pi_0\) we are given a real number \(\gamma_p\) and a submodule \(W_p \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k\) of index \(p^{j_p}\) on which \(B\) is divisible: \(v_p(B\varphi) \ge \gamma_p\) coordinatewise for \(\varphi \in W_p\). For \(p \notin \Pi_0\) put \(\gamma_p = j_p = 0\) and \(W_p = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k\), and suppose \(B(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^k) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^n\) for these \(p\). Put \[g_p = \lfloor \gamma_p \rfloor, \qquad c_B = \prod_{p \in \Pi_0} p^{-g_p}, \qquad A = c_B B .\]

Problem 49.2.1. For every prime \(p\), \(v_p(c_B) = -g_p\) and \(A(W_p) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}^n\). Hence every finite family in \(A(\mathbb{Z}^k)\) has index at most \(\sum_p j_p \log p\), and \(\lvert A\varphi\rvert^2 = c_B^2\lvert B\varphi\rvert^2\) with \(2\log c_B \le -2\sum_{p \in \Pi_0} \gamma_p\log p + 2\sum_{p \in \Pi_0}\log p\).

Problem 49.2.2. Let \(k = n = 2\), \(\Pi_0 = \{2, 3\}\) and \(B(\varphi_1, \varphi_2) = (4\varphi_1 + 6\varphi_2, 12\varphi_2)\).

  1. With \(\gamma_2 = 1\), \(W_2 = \mathbb{Z}_{(2)}^2\) and \(\gamma_3 = 1\), \(W_3 = \{\varphi : 3 \mid \varphi_1\}\), one has \(c_B = 1/6\); the least \(\delta\) such that \((Ae_1, Ae_2)\) has index at most \(\delta\) is \(\log 3\), and \(\lvert Ae_1\rvert^2 = 4/9\), \(\lvert Ae_2\rvert^2 = 5\).

  2. With \(\gamma_2 = 2\), \(W_2 = \{\varphi : 2 \mid \varphi_2\}\) and the same choice at \(3\), one has \(c_B = 1/12\); this least \(\delta\) is \(\log 6\), and \(\lvert Ae_1\rvert^2 = 1/9\), \(\lvert Ae_2\rvert^2 = 5/4\).

Problem 49.2.3. The vectors of \(\mathbb{Z}^k\) with all coordinates in \(\{0, \pm1\}\) span \(\mathbb{C}^k\). If \(T_1, \dots, T_f\) are commuting complex-linear isometries of \(\mathbb{C}^k\) preserving \(\mathbb{Z}^k\), the image of \(\prod_i(1 - T_i)\) is spanned by integral vectors of squared norm at most \(4^f k\).

In Problem 49.2.2, increasing \(\gamma_2\) by one halves \(c_B\), shortens the image, and increases the index of the family by \(\log 2\). For each prime \(p\) and each ring homomorphism \(\mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\), the divisibility theorem of Chapter 57 gives a submodule of the integral ramified source forms of colength exponent at most \(\mathrm{err}(p)\) times the dimension of the full source. On this submodule the composite lift has valuation at least \(\mathrm{gain}(p) - \mathrm{err}(p)\) for every twist of level prime to \(p\) and every chain datum for that twist.

§49.3. Theorem A

Put \(\Sigma := \sum_{p \in \Pi}\mathrm{gain}(p)\log p\), as in Chapter 66. The composite lift \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) of Chapter 52 is defined on all source forms; for a twist \(\vartheta\) it depends on a chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\), a finitely supported function on the translations. Write \(G_f(V)\) for the group of finite adelic points of the unitary group of a space \(V\). For a form \(F\) on \(X_0\) or on \(Y_m\) and \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), \(\lvert\sigma F(g)\rvert^2\) denotes the Weyl squared norm of the conjugate value. By Theorem 66.3.6, if \(\Psi\) is a chain datum for the trivial twist, \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), \(\varphi \in S\), \(c \in \mathbb{R}\), and \(\lvert\sigma\varphi(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for every \(g \in G_f(X_0)\), then for every \(y \in G_f(Y_m)\) \[\lvert\sigma(B_{1,\Psi}\varphi)(y)\rvert^2 \le \exp\bigl(c + h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ 2\Sigma + U\bigr) .\] This is a statement at the trivial twist only.

The spaces \(S \supseteq S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) of source forms and ramified source forms, the set \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}}\) of ramified forms integral at every \(\iota\), the dimension \(D_X = \dim S\), the group \(\bar G\) and its action \(\rho\) on the principal forms \(H(Y_0)\), the target \(T\), tame characters \(\xi\), twists and \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\) with their moduli \(Q_\vartheta\), and the lower chain \(\Theta_\xi\) are as in Chapter 37; the Poincaré map \(\mathcal{P}\colon S \to H(Y_0)\) is that of Chapter 39. A form is integral at a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\) if all its values have gauge at most \(1\) at \(\eta\) (Definition 37.5.1); integrality at \(\iota\) is integrality at the homomorphism induced by \(\iota\), and \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\) is the ring of \(z \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\lvert\iota(z)\rvert_p \le 1\). For \(Z > 0\) a form \(\varphi\) is \(Z\)-short if it is integral at every \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\), for every prime \(p\), and \(\lvert\sigma\varphi(g)\rvert^2 \le Z\) for all \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) and all \(g\). For a form \(F\) on \(Y_m\) of specified level \(K\) and character, \(\lVert F\rVert_\sigma^2\) is the sum over the double classes at level \(K\) of \(\lvert\sigma F(g)\rvert^2\) at the chosen representatives \(g\), each weighted by the reciprocal of the order of its stabilizer. For \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\) the squared norm of \(\sigma(\lambda F(g))\) is \(\lvert\sigma(\lambda)\rvert^2\) times that of \(\sigma F(g)\), and the gauge of \(\lambda F(g)\) at \(\eta\) is \(\lvert\eta(\lambda)\rvert_p\) times that of \(F(g)\).

In addition to the pointwise size bound of Theorem 66.3.6, we use the following five inputs.

  1. The \(e^U\)-short forms in \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) span \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\). Problems 49.3.1 and 49.3.2 derive this from the statement before them.

  2. Seesaw. With the seesaw constant \(c_{\mathrm{ss}} \in \mathbb{C}^\times\) of Chapter 52: for every twist \(\vartheta\) there is a chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(\Theta_\vartheta(\mathcal{P}\varphi) = c_{\mathrm{ss}}B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) for all \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) (Theorem 52.4.1, at the point and the linear form that define \(\mathcal P\)).

  3. Good part. Let \(z \in H(Y_0)\) and \(s \in \bar G\). If \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1 z)(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for all \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\), with \(c \in \mathbb{R}\) and a fixed \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), then \(\lVert\Theta_1(\#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z)\rVert_\sigma^2 \le e^{x^{99} + c}\). For every prime \(p\), every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\), every tame character \(\xi\) and every \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\), if \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi z\) is integral at \(\eta\), so is \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi(\#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z)\). Both estimates are proved in Theorem 51.2.2.

  4. Divisibility. Theorem 57.6.2. Its module \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) consists of the forms in \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) integral at \(\eta\). We write \(j_{p,\eta}\) and \(S'_{p,\eta}\) for the integer \(l\) and the module \(S'\) of its part (d) at \((p, \eta)\). That the colength of \(S'_{p,\eta}\) in \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) divides \(p^{j_{p,\eta}}\) means, by the definition of colength divisibility, that every finite family in \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) is carried into \(S'_{p,\eta}\) by a square matrix over \(\mathcal{O}_\eta\), the ring of \(z \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\lvert\eta(z)\rvert_p \le 1\), whose determinant divides \(p^{j_{p,\eta}}\).

  5. \(\dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}\ge (1 - f/(N - 1))D_X\), and \(f/(N - 1) \le b^{-8}\) (Theorem 51.3.1).

Moreover \(b \ge 2\) and \(N \ge x_1\) (Definition 36.3.1, Problems 36.3.1 and 36.5.4).

Theorem 50.3.6(b) gives the starting point for input 1: the \(4^{-f}e^U\)-short forms in \(S\) span \(S\).

Problem 49.3.3. For data, \(\sum_{p \in \Pi \cup \{N\}}\log p \le b\) and \(x^{99} + 4U + 2b \le fa\log^4 b\).

Problem 49.3.4. \(\log c_B \le -\Sigma + U + b\).

Problem 49.3.5. For every prime \(p\) and every \(\iota\) above \(p\), \(\lvert\iota(c_B)\rvert_p = p^{g_p}\), and \(g_p = 0\) for \(p \notin \Pi \cup \{N\}\).

Problem 49.3.6. \(U + (h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ 2\Sigma + U) + x^{99} + 2\log c_B \le h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ x^{99} + 4U + 2b\).

Problem 49.3.7. Every \(e^U\)-short \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) lies in \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}}\) and satisfies \(\lVert\Theta_1 A_s\varphi\rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp(h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ fa\log^4 b)\) for all \(s \in \bar G\) and \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\).

Problem 49.3.8. \(D_X \le 2\dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}\).

Vectors of \(T\) integral at \(\iota\), and the index of a finite family in \(T\), are those of the target lattice (Definition 37.11.1): \(y \in T\) is integral at \(\iota\) above \(p\) if \(\Theta_\vartheta y\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) of level prime to \(p\). A finite family \((y_i)\) has index at most \(\delta\) if there are numbers \(\delta_p \ge 0\), one for each prime, with \(\sum_{p\in F}\delta_p\log p\le\delta\) for every finite set \(F\) of primes, such that for every \(p\) and every \(\iota\) above \(p\) there is a square matrix \(u\) over \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\), indexed by the family, with \(\lvert\iota(\det u)\rvert_p\ge p^{-\delta_p}\) and \(\sum_j u_{ij}y_j\) integral at \(\iota\) for every \(i\). At a given \(\iota\), the same matrix serves every allowed twist.

Problem 49.3.9. Let \(\iota\) lie above \(p\), and let \(\eta\) be the homomorphism induced by \(\iota\). Then \(A_s\varphi\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(\varphi \in S'_{p,\eta}\) and every \(s \in \bar G\).

Problem 49.3.10. For each \(s \in \bar G\), every finite family in \(A_s(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}})\) has index at most \(\dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}\cdot 2U\).

Problems 49.3.2 and 49.3.7 give part (a) of the following theorem using the five inputs, Theorem 66.3.6 and the bounds on the data. For the target lattice, if every finite family in each of \(h\) subsets of \(T\) has index at most \(\delta\), then every finite family in their union has index at most \(h\delta\); this is the form, for the target lattice, of the bound modeled in Problem 49.1.3, and with Problem 49.3.10 it gives part (b).

Theorem 49.3.2 (Theorem A).

  1. The complex span of the set of \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}}\) such that \[\lVert\Theta_1 A_s\varphi\rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp\bigl(h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ fa\log^4 b\bigr) \quad\text{for all } s \in \bar G\text{ and } \sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\] is \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\).

  2. Let \(J \subseteq \bar G\) be finite, \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), and \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in T\). If each \(y_i\) lies in \(A_s(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}})\) for some \(s \in J\), then \((y_1, \dots, y_n)\) has index at most \(\#J\cdot\dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}\cdot 2fa\log^3 b\).

Used in: Chapter 38; Chapter 50; Chapter 51; Chapter 66

Neither part asks the forms to be independent or the images to span; Theorem B of Chapter 39 supplies few translates whose images span almost all of \(T\). In the exponent of (a) the gains have disappeared and the remainder appears with the factor \(m\). For the independent family obtained using Theorem B, Chapter 38 uses (a) to bound the logarithmic covolume and (b) to bound the index. Theorem C bounds the logarithmic covolume plus twice the index from below, with main term \((\dim T)h_{\mathrm{main}}\) and no remainder term.
