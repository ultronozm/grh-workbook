Prerequisites.

Linear algebra over \(\mathbb{C}\): traces, characteristic polynomials and Newton’s identities [Mac, Ch. I, §2]. Modular forms of weight two on \(\Gamma_0(N)\) and their Hecke operators [DS, Ch. 5]. From Chapter 24, Hecke operators and theta lifts: Section 24.4, with Problems 24.4.2 and 24.4.3. From Chapter 37, Spaces, forms and twists: the chosen places \(\mathcal{M}\), the group \(\bar G\), good families of labels and the idempotent \(e_0\) (Section 37.1); the gap \(\Delta\) and the target parameter \(\varsigma_\tau\) (Section 37.2, Problem 37.2.1); the spaces \(Y_0\), \(W_j\) and their signs (Sections 37.3 and 37.4, Problem 37.3.1); principal and good forms \(H(V) \supseteq H_g(V)\) and the target \(T = H_g(Y_0)\) of dimension \(d_T\); tame characters (Section 37.6). From Chapter 36: \(r = 3^{e'}\) is odd, \(x^5 \le r\), \(m\) is even, and the weights \(\kappa(\tau)\) are integers. From Chapter 69: the Rallis operators \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; V)\) (Definition 69.3.2). Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first variable. Problems marked “by machine” use Sage [Sage].

The single lifts of Chapter 68, The determinant of a chain, start from the spaces \(W_j\), which have the rank of \(Y_0\) but other signs at the real places. How can a determinant on \(H_g(W_j)\) be compared with one on \(T = H_g(Y_0)\), when no map between the two spaces is given? In the proof one Hecke algebra acts on both spaces with equal traces. The equality implies that the two spaces have the same dimension, that each element of the Hecke algebra has the same characteristic polynomial on both, and that each Hecke eigensystem occurs with the same multiplicity.

§70.1. Hecke operators of a finite group

Here \(K\) is a subgroup (the field \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\) does not occur). Let the symmetric group \(S_3\) act on \(\mathbb{C}^3\) by \((\sigma v)_i = v_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}\), let \(K = \{1, (12)\}\) and \(g = (23)\).

Problem 70.1.1. The double coset \(KgK\) has \(4\) elements. For a \(K\)-fixed vector \(v = (v_1, v_1, v_3)\), the average of \(\sigma v\) over \(\sigma \in KgK\) is \(\bigl(\tfrac{v_1+v_3}2, \tfrac{v_1+v_3}2, v_1\bigr)\); in the coordinates \((v_1, v_3)\) of the \(K\)-fixed vectors it is \((v_1, v_3) \mapsto \bigl(\tfrac{v_1+v_3}2, v_1\bigr)\).

Used in: Problem 70.1.3

Problem 70.1.2. Let a group \(G\) act on a complex inner product space \(U\) by unitary operators, let \(K \subseteq G\) be a subgroup and \(g \in G\) be such that \(K_g := K \cap gKg^{-1}\) has finite index in \(K\). On the \(K\)-fixed vectors \(U^K\) the operator \(\mathrm{av}_K(g) := [K : K_g]^{-1} \sum_{\delta K_g \in K/K_g} \delta g\) does not depend on the representatives, maps \(U^K\) to itself, and satisfies \(\langle z, gy \rangle = \langle z, \mathrm{av}_K(g) y\rangle\) for all \(z, y \in U^K\). If \(K\) is finite, \(\mathrm{av}_K(g) y\) is the average of \(\sigma y\) over \(\sigma \in KgK\).

Problem 70.1.3. The operator of Problem 70.1.1 has traces \(2, \tfrac12, \tfrac54, \tfrac78\) in its powers \(0, 1, 2, 3\), characteristic polynomial \((X - 1)(X + \tfrac12)\) and determinant \(-\tfrac12\).

Problem 70.1.4. Let a monoid act linearly, with its unit acting as the identity and products as composites, on finite-dimensional complex spaces \(U\) and \(U'\), and suppose \(\operatorname{tr}_U h = \operatorname{tr}_{U'} h\) for every element \(h\). Then \(\dim U = \dim U'\), and every element \(h\) has the same characteristic polynomial, hence the same determinant, on \(U\) and on \(U'\).

Used in: Problem 70.1.6

Problem 70.1.5. The matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)\) and \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) both have traces \(3, 5, 9\) in their powers \(1, 2, 3\), and \(\det(h + 1) = 6\) for both.

Problem 70.1.6. In Problem 70.1.4, suppose moreover that the monoid is commutative and acts diagonalizably on \(U\) and on \(U'\). Then for every function \(\lambda\) from the monoid to \(\mathbb{C}\) the joint eigenspaces \(\{y : hy = \lambda(h) y \text{ for all } h\}\) in \(U\) and in \(U'\) have the same dimension.

§70.2. Brandt matrices: a model

Where do two spaces with the same Hecke traces come from? The classical case compares a definite quaternion algebra with \(\mathrm{GL}_2\). Let \(B\), \(\mathcal{O}_1\), \(\mathcal{O}_2\), the Brandt matrices \(B(\ell)\) for primes \(\ell \ne 11\), and the cusp form \(f\) be as in Section 24.4 and Problem 24.4.3; \(B(\ell)\) acts on functions \(y\) on the two classes by \((B(\ell) y)(i) = \sum_j B_{ij}(\ell)\, y(j)\). The space of cusp forms of weight \(2\) on \(\Gamma_0(11)\) is \(\mathbb{C}f\) [DS], and \(T_\ell f = a_\ell(f) f\).

Problem 70.2.1. (By machine.) For \(\ell = 2, 3, 5, 7, 13\): \(\operatorname{tr}B(\ell) = 1, 3, 7, 6, 18\), and \(\operatorname{tr}B(\ell) - (\ell + 1) = a_\ell(f) = -2, -1, 1, -2, 4\).

Problem 70.2.2. (a) If an idempotent \(e\) on a finite-dimensional space \(U\) commutes with an operator \(h\), then \(eU\) is stable under \(h\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(h \mid eU) = \operatorname{tr}_U(he)\). (b) For a prime \(\ell \ne 11\), the constant function is an eigenvector of \(B(\ell)\) with eigenvalue \(\ell + 1\), and the line \(U_0 := \{y : y(1)/\lvert\mathcal{O}_1^\times\rvert + y(2)/\lvert\mathcal{O}_2^\times\rvert = 0\}\) is stable under \(B(\ell)\); hence \(\operatorname{tr}(B(\ell) \mid U_0) = \operatorname{tr}B(\ell) - (\ell + 1)\).

For the five primes of Problem 70.2.1, Problems 70.2.1 and 70.2.2 give \(\operatorname{tr}(B(\ell) \mid U_0) = a_\ell(f)\): once the constants are removed, the Brandt matrices have the traces of the Hecke operators on cusp forms. The equality for every prime \(\ell \ne 11\) is the level-\(11\) case of the correspondence of Eichler and of Jacquet and Langlands [Ei73], [JL]; Status: classical. It is a model only: the group of units of \(B\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) differ at \(11\) as well as at infinity, whereas the proof compares unitary groups that differ only at the real places.

Problem 70.2.3. (By machine.) The quaternion algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}\) ramified at \(37\) and \(\infty\) has three classes of left ideals of a maximal order; its Brandt matrix \(B(2)\) has characteristic polynomial \((X - 3)X(X + 2)\), and \(T_2\) on the cusp forms of weight \(2\) on \(\Gamma_0(37)\) has characteristic polynomial \(X(X + 2)\).

At level \(37\) the factor of the constants is \(X - 3\), and what remains is the characteristic polynomial of \(T_2\) on cusp forms.

§70.3. Equal traces

Let \(V\) be a framed space of rank \(r\) over \(E\) with numbered signs \(s = (s(0), \dots, s(r-1))\) (in this sheet \(s\) is a system of signs and, below, \(a\) an element of a group algebra; the numbers \(s\) and \(a\) of the data do not occur), such as \(Y_0\) or \(W_j\) (Sections 37.3 and 37.4). Through the frames, all such \(V\) have the same finite adelic unitary group \(G_f\), that of the identity form; it acts on forms on \(V\) by right translation. \(H(V)\) and its subspaces carry the Petersson inner product \(\langle y', y\rangle\) of Definition 37.4.1, conjugate-linear in the first argument.

Definition 70.3.1. The numbered signs \(s\) of \(V\) satisfy the gap hypothesis if at every infinite place \(\tau \in \Phi\) the entries \(2\varsigma_\tau(0), \dots, 2\varsigma_\tau(r-1)\) are distinct, are congruent to \(r - 1\) modulo \(2\), and satisfy \(\lvert 2\varsigma_\tau(i) - 2\varsigma_\tau(i')\rvert \ge 2\Delta\) whenever \(s(i) \ne s(i')\).

Under the gap hypothesis the proof shows that \(H(V)\) is finite-dimensional.

Problem 70.3.1. The gap hypothesis holds for \(Y_0\) and for \(W_j\), \(1 \le j \le m\).

Used in: Chapter 37

Definition 70.3.2. In this sheet \(G_f^{\mathcal{M}}\) is the subgroup of the \(g \in G_f\) whose component at every \(w \in \mathcal{M}\) is \(1\); the component at \(w\) determines that at \(\bar w\), so these components are \(1\) as well. An element \(a\) of the group algebra \(\mathbb{C}[G_f^{\mathcal{M}}]\) is spherical if, in every representation of \(G_f\), it maps the vectors fixed by the principal level to vectors fixed by the principal level. A spherical \(a\) acts on \(H(V)\) for every framed \(V\) of rank \(r\); we define the Hecke algebra away from \(\mathcal{M}\), \(\mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\), to be the algebra of the families of operators on the spaces \(H(V)\), one for each \(V\), given in this way by one spherical element. For a finite place \(w\) and \(i \in \mathbb N\), let \(h_{w,i} \in \mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\) be the Hecke operator (the sum of the translates by representatives of its single cosets) of the \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathcal{O}_w)\)-double coset of \(\operatorname{diag}(\varpi_w 1_i, 1_{r-i})\) in \(\mathrm{GL}_r(E_w)\), with \(\mathcal{O}_w\) the local ring of integers and \(\varpi_w\) a uniformizer, if \(w\) is split, \(w, \bar w \notin \mathcal{M}\) and \(i \le r\); in all other cases \(h_{w,i} := 0\).

In this sheet we call a family \(\lambda = (\lambda_{w,i})\) of complex numbers indexed by the finite places \(w\) and \(i \in \mathbb N\) an eigensystem, and write \(H(V)_\lambda := \{y \in H(V) : h_{w,i} y = \lambda_{w,i} y \text{ for all } w, i\}\). The proof lets \(\bar G\) act on \(H(V)\) through integral lifts whose components outside \(\mathcal{M}\cup \overline{\mathcal{M}}\) are \(1\).

Problem 70.3.2. An element of \(G_f^{\mathcal{M}}\) commutes with every element of \(G_f\) whose components outside \(\mathcal{M}\cup \overline{\mathcal{M}}\) are \(1\). Hence every \(h \in \mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\) commutes with the action of \(\bar G\), and so with \(e_0\), on \(H(V)\), and maps \(H_g(V)\) to itself.

For \(V\) satisfying the gap hypothesis and \(h \in \mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\), write \(\operatorname{tr}(h \mid H_g(V))\) for the trace of the restriction of \(h\) to \(H_g(V)\). The proof establishes the following.

Theorem 70.3.3. Let \(V\) and \(V'\) be framed spaces of rank \(r\) over \(E\) whose numbered signs satisfy the gap hypothesis. Then every \(h \in \mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\) has \(\operatorname{tr}(h \mid H_g(V)) = \operatorname{tr}(h \mid H_g(V'))\).

Used in: Chapter 41

The proof deduces Theorem 70.3.3 from Theorem 41.7, which compares the traces on two unitary groups that differ only at the real places. Problem 41.1.8 checks the local character identity in rank two. For a classical comparison with a cuspidal component, see [HL, Props. 1.10.5–1.10.6].

The data meet these conditions: the proof shows that \(E\) contains the \(5040\)th roots of unity and has an even number of infinite places, and \(\Delta = 2r^8 \ge 500 r^3 + 10 r^5\) and \(r \ge 2\) because \(r \ge x^5 \ge 9\), \(x\) being large. The idempotent \(e_0\) is the sum of the orthogonal idempotents \(e_c := \bigotimes_w e_{c_w}\) over the good families \(c\), so by Problem 70.2.2(a) the trace of \(h \in \mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\) on \(H_g(V)\) is \(\sum_c \operatorname{tr}_{H(V)}(h e_c)\). The proof shows that \(h e_c\) acts on \(H(V)\) for every \(V\) through one element of \(\mathbb{C}[G_f]\) that keeps the principal-level vectors, and that its test function is cuspidal at a chosen place \(w\) where \(c_w\) is primitive (irreducible with a root of order \((\#k_w)^r - 1\)); every good family has such a place (Section 37.1), and chosen places are split. The comparison applies to each term, with \(K_f\) the principal level and \(l_\tau = 2\varsigma_\tau\).

§70.4. Dimensions, eigenspaces and determinants

Problem 70.4.1. \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T\) for \(1 \le j \le m\).

When \(H(V)\) is finite-dimensional, the proof shows that it is the direct sum of the spaces \(H(V)_\lambda\) (the operators \(h_{w,i}\) are normal and commute).

Problem 70.4.2. Let \(\lambda\) be an eigensystem and fix finitely many eigensystems \(\lambda' \ne \lambda\). A polynomial in finitely many of the \(h_{w,i}\), an element of \(\mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\), acts on \(H(V)\), for every framed \(V\) of rank \(r\), as the identity on \(H(V)_\lambda\) and as zero on each of these \(H(V)_{\lambda'}\).

Problem 70.4.3. For every eigensystem \(\lambda\) and \(1 \le j \le m\), the spaces \(T \cap H(Y_0)_\lambda\) and \(H_g(W_j) \cap H(W_j)_\lambda\) have the same dimension.

Let \(\xi\) be a tame character and \(j \in \mathbb N\). By Definition 69.3.2 and the formula for its matrix coefficients proved with it, for every framed \(V\) of rank \(r\) and all \(z, y \in H(V)\), \[\langle z, \mathcal{R}_j(\xi; V)\, y \rangle = \sum_\gamma \mathrm{mc}(\gamma)\, \langle z, \gamma y\rangle ,\] a sum, convergent in any order, over the countable family of representatives \(\gamma \in G_f\) of that definition, the same family for every \(V\), whose components at the places of \(\mathcal{M}\) are \(1\), with the coefficients \(\mathrm{mc}(\gamma)\) of Chapter 69, which depend on \(\xi\), \(j\) and \(\gamma\) but not on \(V\); here \(\gamma y := y(\cdot\,\gamma)\). For each \(\gamma \in G_f^{\mathcal{M}}\) the proof supplies an element \(\mathrm{av}(\gamma) \in \mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\), an average of translates as in Problem 70.1.2, such that \(\langle z, \mathrm{av}(\gamma) y\rangle = \langle z, \gamma y\rangle\) for every framed \(V\) of rank \(r\) and all \(z, y \in H(V)\).

Problem 70.4.4. Let \(U\) and \(U'\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, and let pairs \((h_i, h'_i) \in \operatorname{End} U \times \operatorname{End} U'\), for \(i\) in a countable set, lie in one complex linear subspace. Let \(h_\infty \in \operatorname{End} U\) and \(h'_\infty \in \operatorname{End} U'\) be such that \(\sum_i \langle z, h_i y\rangle = \langle z, h_\infty y\rangle\) for all \(z, y \in U\) and \(\sum_i \langle z', h'_i y'\rangle = \langle z', h'_\infty y'\rangle\) for all \(z', y' \in U'\), the sums converging in any order. Then \((h_\infty, h'_\infty)\) lies in that subspace. In particular, let a complex algebra act on \(U\) and on \(U'\) through complex-linear algebra homomorphisms to \(\operatorname{End} U\) and \(\operatorname{End} U'\); if each \((h_i, h'_i)\) is the action of one element, then one element acts as \(h_\infty\) on \(U\) and as \(h'_\infty\) on \(U'\).

Problem 70.4.5. Let \(V\) and \(V'\) be framed spaces of rank \(r\) satisfying the gap hypothesis, \(\xi\) a tame character and \(j \in \mathbb N\). One element of \(\mathbb{T}^{\mathcal{M}}\) acts as \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; V)\) on \(H(V)\) and as \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; V')\) on \(H(V')\). In particular \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; V)\) maps \(H_g(V)\) to itself.

Problem 70.4.6. For \(1 \le j \le m\) and every tame character \(\xi\), \(\det\bigl(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; W_j) \mid H_g(W_j)\bigr) = \det\bigl(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; Y_0) \mid T\bigr)\).

Problems 70.4.1, 70.4.3, 70.4.5 and 70.4.6 prove the following.

Corollary 70.4.1. Let \(1 \le j \le m\). Then \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T\); for every eigensystem \(\lambda\) the \(\lambda\)-eigenspaces of the \(h_{w,i}\) in \(T\) and in \(H_g(W_j)\) have the same dimension; and for every tame character \(\xi\), \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; Y_0)\) maps \(T\) to itself, \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; W_j)\) maps \(H_g(W_j)\) to itself, and \(\det\bigl(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; W_j) \mid H_g(W_j)\bigr) = \det\bigl(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; Y_0) \mid T\bigr)\).

The problems deduce Corollary 70.4.1 from Theorem 70.3.3, which rests on the comparison of trace formulas taken as given, and from facts read from the proof: finite-dimensionality under the gap hypothesis, the decomposition into joint eigenspaces, the series for the matrix coefficients of \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; V)\), and the operators \(\mathrm{av}(\gamma)\). Chapter 68 combines the corollary with Theorem 69.4.1, which writes \(\hat u_{\xi,j}^*\hat u_{\xi,j}\) as a multiple of \(\mathcal{R}_j(\xi; W_j)\), to express the volume of a single lift by a determinant on \(T\) (Problem 68.2.1).
