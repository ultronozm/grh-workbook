Theorem B (Chapter 39) is deduced from three estimates for the scaled Poincaré map: a second moment over \(\bar G\), the near isometry of \(e_0{\mathcal P^\circ}\), and an upper bound for \(d_T\). This sheet proves the last two from Chapters 40 and 43 and from equal multiplicities. Why does cutting \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) down to the good forms lose so little, and why is the target no larger than \(\#\bar G\cdot\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r\)? The first answer is a trace against the Bessel representation, small because the conjugacy average of \(\psi_{\mathcal U}\) is small; the second is a count of classes on the definite space \(W_m\), where the level is free.

§48.1. Central idempotents and their shares

For a finite group \(H\), an element of \(\mathbb{C}[H]\) is written \(g = \sum_{t \in H} g(t)\,t\); in a representation \(\rho\) it acts by \(\rho(g) := \sum_t g(t)\rho(t)\). It is a central idempotent if \(g^2 = g\) and \(t \mapsto g(t)\) is a class function.

Definition 48.1.1. We define the share of a central idempotent \(g \in \mathbb{C}[H]\) to be its coefficient \(g(1)\) at the identity.

In this sheet \(q\) is the number of elements of a residue field.

Problem 48.1.1. The labels of degree \(2\) over \(\mathbb{F}_3\) are \(X^2+1\), \(X^2+2\), \(X^2+X+2\) and \(X^2+2X+2\). The multiplicative orders of their roots in \(\mathbb{F}_9^\times\) are \((4,4)\), \((1,2)\), \((8,8)\) and \((8,8)\). Three of them have no root \(1\), and two are irreducible with a root of order \(8 = 3^2-1\).

Green’s degrees. For a label \(c\) of degree \(r\) over a finite field \(k\) with \(q = \#k\), the value \(d := \Xi_c(1)\) of the character of Section 37.1 satisfies \[\operatorname{Re} d \ge 0, \qquad \frac{\lvert d\rvert^2}{\#\mathrm{GL}_r(k)} \ge \frac{1 - r/q}{q^r} .\] Status: classical; Green [Gr, Thms. 12–14]. For \(r = 2\) the degree is \(q+1\) for a label with two roots in \(k\), \(q-1\) for an irreducible one [FH].

Problem 48.1.2. In \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)\), of order \(48\), the idempotent \(e_c\) of the label \(X^2+2\) has share \(1/3\), and that of each irreducible label has share \(1/12 \ge (1 - 2/3)/9\). The sum of the \(e_c\) over the three labels without root \(1\) is a central idempotent of share \(1/4\).

Problem 48.1.3. Let \(g\) be a central idempotent of \(\mathbb{C}[H]\), \(H\) finite. In every unitary representation of \(H\), \(g\) acts as an orthogonal projection, and \(g(1)\) is the share \(\dim(g\mathbb{C}[H])/\#H\) of the regular representation that \(g\) keeps. Moreover \(\sum_{t \in H}\lvert g(t)\rvert^2 = g(1)\), and \(1 - g\) is a central idempotent.

The idempotents of the data.

For \(i = 0, 1\) let \(g_i \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_i]\) be the sum, over the families \((c_w)_{w \in \mathcal M_i}\) of labels such that no \(c_w\) has a root \(z \in k_w\) with \(z^{q_2} = 1\) and some \(c_w\) is irreducible with a root of order \(p_i^r - 1\), of the tensor products \(\bigotimes_w e_{c_w}\). These are central idempotents, and \(g_0 \otimes g_1 \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G]\) acts on \(H(Y_0)\) as \(e_0\). Call a label of degree \(r\) over \(k_w\) allowed if it has no root \(z \in k_w\) with \(z^{q_2} = 1\), and primitive if it is irreducible with a root of order \(q^r - 1\); at one place \(w \in \mathcal M_i\), with \(q = p_i\), let \(A\) be the set of allowed labels, \(B\) the set of monic polynomials of degree \(r\) over \(k_w\) that are not primitive labels, and \(m' := (1 - r/q)/q^r\). For \(1 < r < q\) the proof shows that each tensor product \(\bigotimes_w e_{c_w}\) has share at least \(m'^f\) (by Green’s degrees), that \(\#A\cdot m' \ge 1 - \varepsilon'\) whenever \(\varepsilon' \ge (1 + q_2 + 2r)/q\), and that \(\#B\cdot m' \le \eta := \exp\bigl(-1/(2r(2r\log q + 1))\bigr)\).

Problem 48.1.4. Let \(f \ge 1\) be an integer, \(A\), \(B\) finite sets, \(m' > 0\), \(0 \le \varepsilon' \le 1\) and \(\eta \ge 0\), with \(\#A\cdot m' \ge 1 - \varepsilon'\) and \(\#B\cdot m' \le \eta\). Then \((1 - \varepsilon')^f \ge 1 - f\varepsilon'\) and \(m'^f(\#A^f - \#B^f) \ge 1 - f\varepsilon' - \eta^f\). Hence, in the paragraph above, the families counted in \(g_i\) include every family of allowed labels not all in \(B\), and \(\operatorname{Re}(1 - g_i)(1) \le f\varepsilon' + \eta^f\).

With one place, \(\eta\) is close to \(1\); only its \(f\)-th power is small. The proof takes \(\varepsilon' = (1 + q_2 + 2r)/p_i\) and checks, for the data, \(1 < r < p_i\), \(\varepsilon' \le 1\), \(2r(2r\log p_i + 1)\cdot 9\log b \le f\) and \(f\varepsilon' + e^{-9\log b} \le b^{-8}/2\). So \(\operatorname{Re}(1 - g_i)(1) \le b^{-8}/2\) for \(i = 0, 1\).

Problem 48.1.5. Let \(\mathcal X\) and \(\mathcal Y\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, and let \(L\colon\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) be linear. If \(p\) is an orthogonal projection and \(q'\) is a linear operator on \(\mathcal Y\), then \(\lVert(1-pq')L\rVert^2\le\lVert(1-p)L\rVert^2+\lVert(1-q')L\rVert^2\). In the regular representation of \(C_5 \times C_5\), with \(p\) and \(q'\) the projections removing one character of the first and of the second factor and \(L = 1\), the two sides divided by \(25\) are \(9/25 \le 2/5\).

§48.2. The trace on the Bessel subgroup

In this section, as in Theorem 43.3.3, consider an admissible filling over a CM field \(E\) with Weil data, a unitary representation \(\rho\) of \(\bar G\) on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, and a linear map \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) from the coordinate space of Definition 39.4.1 to it that satisfies the block identity of Theorem 40.3.2; \(\mathcal K_s = \mathcal P^{\circ*}\rho(s){\mathcal P^\circ}\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\), \(\bar R_0\), \(D_X\) and \(D_{\mathrm{id}}= \#\bar R_0\cdot D_X\) are as there. In this setting, as for the data, \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s) = 0\) for \(s \notin \bar R_0\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) is a unitary representation of \(\bar R_0\), and \(\mathcal K_{st} = \mathcal K_s\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\), \(\mathcal K_{ts} = \rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\mathcal K_s\) for \(t \in \bar R_0\) (read from the proof; Problem 40.4.2). In this sheet \(\iota_i\colon\bar G_i\to\bar G\) is the inclusion with identity component in the other factor, and \(\rho(g) := \sum_t g(t)\rho(\iota_i(t))\) for \(g \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_i]\).

Problem 48.2.1. (a) Let \(\varphi\colon H'\to H\) be a homomorphism of finite groups, \(g\) a central idempotent of \(\mathbb{C}[H']\), and \(F\) a function on \(H\) with values in a complex inner product space. Then \(\sum_{s\in H}\lVert\sum_{t\in H'}g(t)F(s\varphi(t))\rVert^2\le\sum_{s\in H}\lVert F(s)\rVert^2\).

(b) Let \(g \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_i]\) be a central idempotent and \(D_g := \sum_{t \in \bar G_i} g(t)\bigl(\mathcal K_{\iota_i(t)} - \rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(\iota_i(t))\bigr)\). Then \(\#\bar R_0\cdot\lVert D_g\rVert^2 \le b^{-12}D_{\mathrm{id}}\), so \(\lVert D_g\rVert^2 \le b^{-12}D_X\) and \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}D_g\rvert \le b^{-6}D_X\).

There remains the trace of \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) against \(g\). The proof shows: if \(s \in \bar G\) has component \(1\) in \(\bar G_0\) or in \(\bar G_1\), then \[\operatorname{tr}\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s) = D_X\,\psi_{\mathcal U}(s).\] Here \(\psi_{\mathcal U}(s)\) is the reduced character of the filling [GL] (Filling.reducedChar). (The trace uses the diagonal amplitude blocks over \(s^{-1}\). For these blocks the definite part of each Bessel term is trivial by the freeness argument of Problem 48.3.2, and the amplitude block over \(s^{-1}\) is \(\psi_{\mathcal U}(s)\) times the one over \(1\).)

Definition 48.2.1. Define the conjugacy average of a function \(\psi\) on a finite group \(H\) by \(\psi^\natural(s) := (\#H)^{-1}\sum_{y \in H}\psi(ysy^{-1})\).

Problem 48.2.2. In \(S_3\) let \(\varphi = 1\) at \(1\), \(-1\) at \((12)\) and \(0\) elsewhere. Its conjugacy average is \(1\), \(-1/3\), \(0\) on the identity, the transpositions and the \(3\)-cycles, and \(\sum_s\lvert\varphi(s)\rvert^2 = 2\), \(\sum_s\lvert\varphi^\natural(s)\rvert^2 = 4/3\).

Problem 48.2.3. For a character \(\psi\) of a subgroup \(R \subseteq H\), extended by zero to \(H\), \(\sum_{s \in H}\lvert\psi^\natural(s)\rvert^2 = \#R^2\cdot\dim\operatorname{End}_H(\operatorname{Ind}_R^H\psi)/\#H\).

Problem 48.2.4. Let \(U \subset \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\) be the upper unitriangular group and \(\psi_U(u) := e^{2\pi\mathrm i u_{12}/p}\). By Problem 43.1.2, \(\sum_s\lvert\psi_U^\natural(s)\rvert^2 = p^2/(p^2-1)\): \(9/8\) for \(p = 3\), \(25/24\) for \(p = 5\). For the upper unitriangular group of \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)\) and \(\psi_U(u) = (-1)^{u_{12}+u_{23}}\), Problem 43.1.3 gives \(32/21\).

The conjugacy average of \(\psi_{\mathcal U}\). For an admissible filling with Weil data, \[\sum_{s \in \bar G}\lvert\psi_{\mathcal U}^\natural(s)\rvert^2 \le 2 .\] Status: read from the proof. There the conjugacy average is the normalized Haar average of the extended character on the conjugates of a lift, over the integral unitary group at the chosen places and their conjugates. The bound for the sum of squares comes from an integral of the squared modulus of this average, estimated by counting shells.

Problem 48.2.5. Let \(g \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_i]\) be a central idempotent with \(\operatorname{Re} g(1) \le b^{-8}/2\). Then \(\bigl\lvert\sum_t g(t)\operatorname{tr}\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(\iota_i(t))\bigr\rvert \le D_X\sqrt{2\operatorname{Re} g(1)} \le b^{-4}D_X\), and \(\lVert\rho(g){\mathcal P^\circ}\rVert^2 \le 2b^{-4}D_X\).

Recall from Chapter 9 that \(L\colon\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\) if \(\dim Z-\varepsilon\dim\mathcal X\le\lVert L\,\mathrm{pr}_Z\rVert^2\) for every subspace \(Z\subset\mathcal X\), where \(\mathrm{pr}_Z\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(Z\), and that by Problem 9.3.1 this holds if \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and \(\lVert L^*L-1\rVert^2\le\varepsilon^2\dim\mathcal X\).

Problem 48.2.6. If \(L\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\), \(p\) is an orthogonal projection and \(\lVert(1-p)L\rVert^2\le\varepsilon'\dim\mathcal X\), then \(pL\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon+\varepsilon'\).

For central idempotents \(g_0 \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_0]\), \(g_1 \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_1]\), the element \(g_0 \otimes g_1 \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G]\) has coefficient \(g_0(s_0)g_1(s_1)\) at \((s_0, s_1)\), and \(\rho(g_0 \otimes g_1) = \rho(g_0)\rho(g_1)\), a product of two commuting orthogonal projections. Problems 48.1.3, 48.1.5 and 48.2.5 prove part (a) of the following theorem; Problem 43.3.9, whose proof (Theorem 43.3.3 on \(\bar R_0\) and Problem 40.4.3) holds in the setting of this section, and Problems 9.3.1 and 48.2.6 then prove part (b). Part (c) follows from (b) and the paragraph on the idempotents of the data.

Theorem 48.2.2 (good forms). In the setting of this section, let \(g_i \in \mathbb{C}[\bar G_i]\) be central idempotents with \(\operatorname{Re}(1 - g_i)(1) \le b^{-8}/2\) for \(i = 0, 1\), and \(e := \rho(g_0 \otimes g_1)\). Then

  1. \(\lVert(1 - e){\mathcal P^\circ}\rVert^2 \le 4b^{-4}D_X\);

  2. \(\lVert e{\mathcal P^\circ}\,\mathrm{pr}_Z\rVert^2 \ge \dim Z - 5b^{-4}D_X\) for every subspace \(Z\) of the coordinate space: \(e{\mathcal P^\circ}\) is a near isometry with error \(5b^{-4}\).

(c) For the data, with \(\rho\) the action of \(\bar G\) on \(H(Y_0)\) and \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) the scaled Poincaré map, which satisfy the hypotheses of this section (Chapter 40), the idempotents \(g_0, g_1\) of Section 48.1 satisfy these hypotheses and \(e\) is the action of \(e_0\): \(e_0{\mathcal P^\circ}\) is a near isometry with error \(5b^{-4}\).

§48.3. The count of the target

In this section \(K\), \(K'\) are levels, not the field \(K\). On a definite group a form is a vector of the weight at each class, fixed by the stabilizer of the class. First the smallest case.

Problem 48.3.1. Let \(E = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm i)\), and let the unitary group of rank one over \(E\), at the level \(K\) of the lattice \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm i]\), have class number one, stabilizer \(\Gamma \cap K = \mu_4\) (\(\Gamma\) the rational points) and mass \(\mathrm{mass}_1 = 1/4\). At the split prime \(13 = (3+2\mathrm i)(3-2\mathrm i)\) take the place \(w = (3+2\mathrm i)\), where \(\mathrm i \equiv 5\). Then \(\mu_4\) reduces to \(\{1, 5, 12, 8\} \subset \mathbb{F}_{13}^\times\), no unit other than \(1\) reduces to \(1\), and the level of integral elements reducing to \(1\) at \(w\) has \(12/4 = 3\) classes, which is \(\#\bar G\cdot\mathrm{mass}_1\) for \(\bar G= \mathbb{F}_{13}^\times\).

In Problem 48.3.2, \(n \ge 1\) is a size.

Problem 48.3.2. Let \(E\) be a CM field, \(\gamma\) a rational unitary matrix of size \(n\) of the standard definite space over \(E\), and \(h\) a finite adelic unitary matrix such that \(h^{-1}\gamma h\) is integral at every finite place and reduces to \(1\) at distinct finite places \(w_1, \dots, w_l\) with \((2n)^{[E:\mathbb{Q}]} < \prod_i \#k_{w_i}\). Then \(\gamma = 1\). In particular this holds if \((2n)^2 < p\), \([E:\mathbb{Q}] = 2f\), and \(w_1, \dots, w_f\) have residue fields with \(p\) elements.

The proof checks this inequality at the \(f\) chosen places above \(p_0\) for the rank \(r\) of \(W_m\). So every class of \(W_m\) at the principal level has trivial stabilizer.

Problem 48.3.3. Let \(G\) be the unitary group of a definite space. Let \(G_f\) be its group of finite adelic points and \(\Gamma\subseteq G_f\) the diagonal image of its rational points. Let \(K'\subseteq K\) be compact subgroups of \(G_f\) that are neighborhoods of \(1\), and let \(\Gamma_h(K) := \Gamma\cap hKh^{-1}\). (a) \(\sum_{[h] \in \Gamma\backslash G_f/K'} 1/\#\Gamma_h(K') = [K : K']\sum_{[h] \in \Gamma\backslash G_f/K} 1/\#\Gamma_h(K)\). (b) If every \(\Gamma_h(K')\) is trivial and \(\pi\) is a representation of \(\Gamma\) on a finite-dimensional complex vector space \(A\), the functions \(\varphi\colon G_f \to A\) with \(\varphi(\gamma h k) = \pi(\gamma)\varphi(h)\) for \(\gamma \in \Gamma\), \(k \in K'\) form a space of dimension \(\#(\Gamma\backslash G_f/K')\cdot\dim A\). (c) For \(W_m\), \(K = K^\circ(W_m)\) and \(K'\) the principal level, \([K : K'] = \#\bar G\), and, since the principal level is free and \(\mathrm{mass}_r\) is the sum of (a) at the level \(K\), the number of classes at the principal level is \(\#\bar G\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r\).

Harish-Chandra’s formal degree. Let \(r\ge1\), let \(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_r)\) be signs, and let \(l=(l_1,\dots,l_r)\) be distinct integers \(\equiv r-1 \pmod 2\) with \(\lvert l_i-l_j\rvert\ge2(500r^3+10r^5)\) whenever \(\varepsilon_i\ne\varepsilon_j\). The formal degree of the discrete series of the unitary group of the signs \(\varepsilon\) with doubled parameter \(l\) is \[\prod_{l_i > l_j}\frac{l_i - l_j}{2}\Bigm/\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}a! ,\] in the compact-dual Haar normalization of Theorem 41.2. For definite signs, write \(l_{(1)} > \cdots > l_{(r)}\). The degree is the Vandermonde quotient in Weyl’s formula (4) at \(z_k = l_{(k)}/2 + (r-1)/2\). A common integer shift of the \(z_k\) leaves the quotient unchanged and can make the highest weight nonnegative. For the rank-two parameter \(l=(-2\mathsf a-1,-2\mathsf a-2\mathsf N+1)\) with integers \(\mathsf a\ge1\) and \(\mathsf N\ge2\), Problem 41.1.5 computes the degree \(\mathsf N-1\) for both \(U(1,1)\) and \(U(2)\).

Problem 48.3.4. For the doubled parameter \((6, 2, -2)\) the quotient is \(8\), the dimension of \(\mathbb S_{(2,1,0)}(\mathbb{C}^3)\).

In the proof the degree of the Bessel system at each infinite place \(\tau\) is this product for the doubled parameter \(2\varsigma_\tau\), so \(\deg\) is the product over \(\tau\) of the formal degrees. On the definite space \(W_m\), the space of principal forms is isomorphic over \(\mathbb{C}\) to the space of functions in Problem 48.3.3(b) at the principal level, with values in a representation of dimension \(\deg\).

Problem 48.3.5. Hence \(\dim H(W_m) = \#\bar G\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r\cdot\deg\), and with Corollary 70.4.1 for \(j = m\), \(d_T \le \#\bar G\cdot\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r\). In the toy of Chapter 40, the \(32\) elements of \(\Gamma_{I_2}\) have distinct reductions modulo \(2 + \mathrm i\) in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_5)\) (order \(480\)). For weight \(\mathbb{C}^2\), the space of principal forms has dimension \(30 = 480\cdot\tfrac1{32}\cdot2\).

Problems 48.3.2, 48.3.3 and 48.3.5, with Corollary 70.4.1, Harish-Chandra’s formal degree and the facts read from the proof stated after Problems 48.3.2 and 48.3.4, prove the following.

Theorem 48.3.1 (the count). For all data, \(d_T \le \#\bar G\cdot\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r\).
