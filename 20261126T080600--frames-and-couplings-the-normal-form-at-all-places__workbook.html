Frames and couplings: the normal form at all places

Chapter 59 bounds the derivatives of a theta function by using a normal form for its Taylor series. Chapter 60 starts with the theta function of one curve. Here we show how a product of copies of that function produces the normal form over all infinite places. The main calculation turns powers of linear forms into the couplings used to count divisibility.

Prerequisites.

Chapter 60, Sections 60.1 and 60.3: the CM theta function \(\mathrm{th}\), its negative logarithm \(g\), the parameter \(y\), the one-curve expansion and the ring closed under differentiation. Chapter 17, Section 17.5 and Problem 17.3.3: normal form, couplings and integral exponentials. Chapter 36, Section 36.2: \(E=K(\zeta_{q_1})\) and its infinite places \(\Phi\). Chapter 58, Problem 58.1.1: the credit \(\gamma_p\) and the scaling valuation. Chapter 37: the ring map \(\eta\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_p\), the normalized valuation \(\operatorname{val}\), the ring \(\mathcal O_\eta\) and the integral vacuum. Chapter 59, Section 59.4: standard data, jet series and the jet scale. For cyclotomic fields see [Wa]. A series is integral if its coefficients belong to \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}\), the ring of integers of \(\mathbb C_p\).

§61.1. A frame for two places

How can the same pair of linear forms give different couplings at different primes? We start with the matrix identity that fixes the pair.

Definition 61.1.1. Use the resolution of the identity of Definition 60.3.1, with vectors \(w_i\in E^n\) and multiplicities \(c_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) indexed by a finite set. In this notation the weighted identity and the associated linear forms are \[\sum_i c_i\,\tau(w_{i,a})\,\overline{\sigma(w_{i,b})} =[\tau=\sigma][a=b],\qquad \mu_i(z)=\sum_{\tau,a}\eta\bigl(\overline{\tau(w_{i,a})}\bigr)z_{\tau,a}.\] The coordinate indices \(a,b\) run from \(1\) to \(n\), and \(\tau,\sigma\) run over \(\Phi\), viewed as embeddings over \(K\). The vectors with their multiplicities form a tight frame of bound \(B>0\) if the weighted matrix in the first identity is \(B\) times the identity. For a resolution of the identity, \(B=1\). We also call the associated family of linear forms, with those multiplicities, a frame. A resolution is integral above \(p\) if every coordinate of every \(w_i\) is integral at all places above \(p\).

For the model take \(E_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7},\mathrm i)\), with embeddings over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})\) sending \(\mathrm i\) to \(\mathrm i\) and \(-\mathrm i\), in that order. Take \(n=1\), \(w_1=1/2\), \(w_2=\mathrm i/2\), \(c_1=c_2=2\) and variables \(z_1,z_2\). Write \(\mathrm i\) also for its image through \(\eta\). We use \(E_0\) only for the model.

Problem 61.1.1. The matrix \(A=\frac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mathrm i\\1&-\mathrm i\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) satisfies \(2AA^*=I_2\). The vectors \(w_1,w_2\) with multiplicities \(c_1,c_2\) give a resolution integral above every odd prime, with forms \(\mu_1=(z_1+z_2)/2\) and \(\mu_2=\mathrm i(z_2-z_1)/2\).

Problem 61.1.2. The sum \(2(\mu_1^6+\mu_2^6)=\frac38(z_1^5z_2+z_1z_2^5)+\frac54 z_1^3z_2^3\) reduces to \(z_1^5z_2+z_1z_2^5\) modulo \(5\).

Used in: Problem 61.2.1

Problem 61.1.3. At \(3\), the sum \(2(\mu_1^4+\mu_2^4)=(z_1^4+6z_1^2z_2^2+z_2^4)/4\) reduces to \(z_1^4+z_2^4\). At \(7\), the sum \(2(\mu_1^8+\mu_2^8)\) reduces to \(z_1^8+z_2^8\). In degree two the exact sum is \(2(\mu_1^2+\mu_2^2)=2z_1z_2\).

Used in: Problem 61.2.1

§61.2. From powers to couplings

The next problems relate powers of the linear forms to the couplings by a congruence. Use \(E,\Phi,q_1,d\) of Chapter 36, and take \(p\) non-split in \(K\). By Problem 36.1.2, \(p\) is odd and \(p\nmid q_1\). For \(e\in I_p^\circ\) of Section 17.5, the partner of \(\tau\) is \(\tau_e=[-p^e]\tau\) and the coupling is \(\Gamma_e=\sum_{\tau,a}z_{\tau,a}^{p^e} z_{\tau_e,a}\). Use the modulus \(\pi_p\) of Chapter 58 and take congruences coefficientwise. For resolutions integral above \(p\), the weighted power sums of degree \(p^e+1\) are independent of the resolution modulo \(\pi_p\).

Ring of integers. For the field \(E\) above, \(\mathcal O_E=\mathcal O_K[\zeta_{q_1}]\). Status. Read from the proof as part of the computation of the relative different [GL]. For the cyclotomic background see [Wa].

Problem 61.2.1. For \(x\in E\) integral above \(p\), \(\eta(\overline{\tau(x)})^{p^e}\equiv\eta(\tau_e(x))\pmod{\pi_p}\). In the model, for odd \(e\) the partner exchanges the places at \(5\) and fixes both at \(3\). At \(7\) it exchanges the places for even \(e\ge0\) and fixes both for odd \(e\). The degree-\(p+1\) sums in Problems 61.1.2 and 61.1.3 are congruent to \(\Gamma_1\) modulo \(\pi_p\) for \(p=3,5,7\). In degree two at \(7\) the sum is congruent to \(\Gamma_0\) modulo \(\pi_7\).

For the frame of roots of unity, use the finite idele \(\alpha_0\) of the Weil data from Section 60.3. At the places dividing \((1+\sqrt{-d})/2\) its inverse is the derivative of the \(q_1\)-th cyclotomic polynomial evaluated at \(\zeta_{q_1}\). At all other finite places its inverse is \(1\). Status. The formula for the idele is read from the proof [GL].

Problem 61.2.2. Every prime dividing \(q_1\) splits in \(K\). The \(q_1\) powers \(\zeta_{q_1}^k\), \(0\le k<q_1\), each taken once, form a tight frame of bound \(q_1\). That is, their weighted matrix in the resolution identity for one coordinate is \(q_1\) times the identity and every finite image \(\alpha_0^{-1}\zeta_{q_1}^k\) is integral at every finite place.

Problem 61.2.3. For a resolution integral above \(p\), \[\sum_i c_i\,\eta(\tau_e(w_{i,a}))\,\mu_i(z)=z_{\tau_e,a}.\] Deduce \(\sum_i c_i\mu_i^{p^e+1}\equiv\Gamma_e\pmod{\pi_p}\), and \(\sum_i c_i(\mu_i^{p^e+1})^{p^j}\equiv\Gamma_e^{p^j}\pmod{\pi_p}\) for every \(j\ge0\).

The next two problems use the one-curve expansion of Section 60.1. Use its \(\varpi\), \(c_\varpi=\varpi^{p+1}/p\), and integral series \(\mathcal E\) with zero constant term in the exponent \(c_\varpi\mathcal E\). Write \(\mathfrak l_{e,j}\) for the coefficients \(\lambda_{ej}\) in the one-curve congruence for \(\mathcal E\) modulo \(\pi_p\). Let \(\mathrm{th},g,y\) belong to the curve of lattice \(\Omega_0\mathcal O_K\) in Definition 59.4.3, read through \(\eta\). As in Definition 60.3.1, \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}[[y(\mu_i(z)) : i]]\) denotes the substitutions of the series \(y(\mu_i(z))\) into integral series.

Problem 61.2.4. For a resolution integral above \(p\), \[\sum_i c_i\mathcal E(\mu_i(z))\equiv \sum_{e\in I_p^\circ}\sum_{j\ge0}\mathfrak l_{e,j}\Gamma_e^{p^j}\pmod{\pi_p}.\]

Problem 61.2.5. For any integral series \(G\) in finitely many variables with \(G(0)=0\), the series \(\exp(c_\varpi\pi_p G)\) is integral. With \(\varpi\) and \(\mathfrak l\) fixed by the one-curve expansion, for every \(n\ge0\), every resolution on \(E^n\) integral above \(p\), and every \(H\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}[[y(\mu_i(z)):i]]\), the product \(\bigl(\prod_i\mathrm{th}(\mu_i(z))^{c_i}\bigr)H\) has the normal form for \((I_p^\circ,([-p^e]\cdot)_e,\varpi,\mathfrak l)\). If \(p\) is inert, then \(\mathfrak l_{e,0}\) is a unit for every odd \(e\).

§61.3. Checking the exponential on two variables

We return to the model to check how adding a multiple of the first coupling to the exponent changes the coefficient valuations. Use the curve of conductor \(49\) and the point \(t=(2,-1)\) of Chapter 60. In the unscaled coordinate \(u\) of Problem 60.1.1, let \(\wp_0(u)=\wp(u+t)-\wp(t)\). Use a formal negative logarithm \(g_0\) with \(g_0(0)=g_0'(0)=0\), \(g_0''=2+\wp_0\), choosing \(2\) for the constant of integration in \(g_0''\). The equation of Problem 60.1.2 in the unscaled coordinate is \(\wp_0''=6\wp_0^2+21\wp_0+14\), with \(\wp_0(0)=\wp_0'(0)=0\). Let \(\mathrm{th}_0=\exp(-g_0)\) and \(\mathrm{Th}(z_1,z_2)=\mathrm{th}_0(\mu_1(z))^2\mathrm{th}_0(\mu_2(z))^2\).

Problem 61.3.1. Through total degree six, the product \(\mathrm{Th}\) is \[1-2z_1z_2+\tfrac98 z_1^2z_2^2-\tfrac7{48}(z_1^4+z_2^4) -\tfrac3{32}z_1^3z_2^3+\tfrac{133}{960}(z_1^5z_2+z_1z_2^5).\] By machine, the jets \(\partial_1^5\partial_2\mathrm{Th}(0)\), \(\partial_1^6\mathrm{Th}(0)\) and \(\partial_1^8\mathrm{Th}(0)\) are \(133/8\), \(0\) and \(7105/32\). At \(5\) the first is a unit and the last has valuation \(1\).

At an inert prime \(p\), the valuation of a coefficient of degree \(a+b\) after rescaling is its original valuation plus \((a+b)/(p^2-1)\), by the inert scale of Section 17.5.

Problem 61.3.2. At \(5\) the only non-integral coefficients of \(\mathrm{Th}\) through degree eight are those of \(z_1^5z_2,z_1z_2^5,z_1^6z_2^2,z_1^2z_2^6\), each of valuation \(-1\). By machine, for \(W=\mathrm{Th}\exp\bigl(\frac{49}{120}(z_1^5z_2+z_1z_2^5)\bigr)\), \[\operatorname{val}[z_1^a z_2^b]W+\frac{a+b}{24}\ge0\qquad(a+b\le29).\] For \(\mathrm{Th}\) the same expression has minimum \(-3\) in that range.

Problem 61.3.3. At \(3\) the only non-integral coefficients of \(\mathrm{Th}\) through degree four are those of \(z_1^4,z_2^4\), and \(\partial_1^4\mathrm{Th}(0)=-7/2\) is a unit. By machine, \(W=\mathrm{Th}\exp(\frac7{12}(z_1^4+z_2^4))\) satisfies \(\operatorname{val}[z_1^a z_2^b]W+(a+b)/8\ge0\) for \(a+b\le11\).

§61.4. The jet series of a standard datum

A product formula transfers the calculation from the curve to the jet series used in Chapter 59. Use the standard data and scales of Definitions 59.4.1 and 59.4.3 on a standard positive definite space \(V\) over \(E\) with \(n\) coordinates, all signs \(+1\) and the identity matrix as adelic point (Section 37.3).

The theta product of a standard datum. Fix a prime \(p\), a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_p\), a nonzero denominator \(\delta\in\mathcal O_K\) and a standard datum \(\varphi\) of denominator \(\delta\) on \(V\). There is a resolution \((w_i,c_i)\) on \(E^n\) whose finite adelic vectors \(\alpha_0^{-1}w_i\) are integral above \(p\), where \(\alpha_0\) is the finite idele of the Weil data. The norm \(\delta\bar\delta\) is a positive integer. The jet series of \(\varphi\) at scale \(\bar\delta\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt d}/\Omega_0\), read through \(\eta\), is \[\left(\prod_i \mathrm{th}(\mu_i(z))^{c_i}\right)^{\delta\bar\delta} H, \qquad H\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}[[y(\mu_i(z)):i]].\] For \(p\nmid q_1\) the vectors \(w_i\) are integral above \(p\) and \(\lvert\eta(c_{\mathrm{per}})\rvert=1\). Status. Read from the proof [GL]. For background on the theta group see Mumford [Mu], and for related integrality results on CM theta functions see Finis [Fi].

The next problems give the two conclusions on standard data.

Problem 61.4.1. If \(F\) has the normal form with \(\varpi,\mathfrak l\), and \(c'\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}\), then \(F(c'z)\) has the normal form for the same partners, exponents and \(\varpi\), with coefficients \((c')^{(p^e+1)p^j}\mathfrak l_{e,j}\). For \(c'=0\) the resulting series is integral and constant.

Problem 61.4.2. For \(p\) non-split and \(c\in\mathcal O_\eta\), the jet series at scale \(cc_{\mathrm{jet}}\) of a standard datum of denominator \(1\) has the normal form asserted in Theorem 61.4.1.

Problem 61.4.3. Let \((\nu_i)_i\) be a finite family of linear forms in the variables \(z\), with coefficients in \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}\). The ring generated by \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}[[y(d\nu_i(z)):i]]\) and by the series \(\mathrm{th}(d\nu_i(z))\) and \(g'(d\nu_i(z))\) is closed under partial differentiation and has integral constant terms. Deduce Theorem 61.4.2 for \(p\nmid q_1\).

Problem 61.4.2 gives the normal form used in Chapter 59. Problem 61.4.3 gives jet integrality for \(p\nmid q_1\).

Theorem 61.4.1 (Normal form). Let \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(\varphi\) a standard datum of denominator \(1\) and \(c \in \mathbb C\) with \(\lvert \eta c \rvert \le 1\). Then the jet series of \(\varphi\) at scale \(cc_{\mathrm{jet}}\), read in \(\mathbb C_p\) through \(\eta\), has the normal form of Section 17.5 for the exponents \(I_p^\circ\), the partner rule \(([-p^e]\cdot)_e\), some \(\varpi\) with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\), and some integral family of coefficients.

Used in: Problem 61.4.2; Chapter 59

Theorem 61.4.2 (Integrality). If \(\delta \ne 0\) and \(\varphi\) is standard of denominator \(\delta\), then every jet of its jet series at scale \(\bar\delta d c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), along a polynomial with coefficients in \(\mathcal O_\eta\), is integral at \(\eta\).

Used in: Problem 61.4.3; Chapter 59

Status. The case \(p\mid q_1\) of integrality is read from the proof.
