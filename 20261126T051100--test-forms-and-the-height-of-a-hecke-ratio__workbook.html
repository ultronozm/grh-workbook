We begin with a line in \(\mathbb{C}^2\).

§79.1. Two tests and a line

Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, conjugate-linear in the first variable, let \(0 \ne v \in V\), and let \(\operatorname{pr}\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathbb{C}v\).

Problem 79.1.1. For all \(\Xi, \Xi' \in V\), \[\langle \operatorname{pr}\Xi, \operatorname{pr}\Xi'\rangle = \frac{\langle\Xi, v\rangle\langle v, \Xi'\rangle}{\lVert v\rVert^2} .\]

Problem 79.1.2. In \(V = \mathbb{C}^2\) with the standard inner product let \(v = (1,2)\), \(\Xi = (1,0)\) and \(\Xi' = (0,1)\). Then \(\langle\Xi,v\rangle\langle v,\Xi'\rangle = 2\) and \(\langle\operatorname{pr}\Xi, \operatorname{pr}\Xi'\rangle = 2/5\), so \(\lvert\langle\operatorname{pr}\Xi,\operatorname{pr}\Xi'\rangle\rvert_\iota = 5\) for every \(\iota \mid 5\), although \(v\), \(\Xi\) and \(\Xi'\) have integral coordinates.

So projecting integral vectors to a line can create a denominator, a congruence in the sense of Chapter 29. Given an upper bound for \(\lvert\langle\operatorname{pr}\Xi,\operatorname{pr}\Xi'\rangle\rvert_\iota\), a lower bound for \(\lvert\langle\Xi,v\rangle\langle v,\Xi'\rangle\rvert_\iota\) gives one for \(\lvert\lVert v\rVert^2\rvert_\iota\). For a critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) with \(z \ge 2\), Theorem 77.4.1(i) states that for every tame character \(\xi\) \[R_P(\xi) = \frac{\lVert\phi_{P,\xi}\rVert^2/(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi)^{2z-1}}{\lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2} .\]

Problem 79.1.3. Let \(P = (\nu, z)\) be a critical pair with \(z \ge 2\), \(\vartheta\) a twist, \(\operatorname{pr}_{P,\vartheta}\) the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathbb{C}\phi_{P,\vartheta}\), \(\Xi, \Xi'\) forms with \(\langle\Xi,\phi_{P,\vartheta}\rangle\langle\phi_{P,\vartheta},\Xi'\rangle \ne 0\), and \(P'\) a critical pair with \(R_{P'}(\vartheta) \ne 0\). Then \[R_P(\vartheta)^{-1} = (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{2z-1} \cdot \lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2 \cdot \langle\operatorname{pr}_{P,\vartheta}\Xi, \operatorname{pr}_{P,\vartheta}\Xi'\rangle \cdot R_{P'}(\vartheta)^{-2} \cdot \frac{R_{P'}(\vartheta)^2}{\langle\Xi,\phi_{P,\vartheta}\rangle\langle\phi_{P,\vartheta},\Xi'\rangle} .\]

Used in: Problem 79.3.5

In the application \(P\) is main for \(j\), \(\vartheta = \theta_\iota^j\) for a regular family \(\theta\) (Definition 79.1.3), \(\Xi\) and \(\Xi'\) are test forms and \(P'\) is ramified; Problem 79.3.5 bounds the five factors.

Definition 79.1.1. Let \(P = (\nu, z)\) be a critical pair with \(z \ge 2\), \(\vartheta\) a twist and \(\ell\) a prime. We define a test form for \(\ell\) (for \(P\) and \(\vartheta\)) to be a form \(\Xi\) in the space of forms of \(\phi_{P,\vartheta}\), on the definite space of rank \(2z\) (Definition 77.2.2), such that:

  1. \(\Xi\) is integral at every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_\ell\) (Definition 37.5.1);

  2. \(\Xi\) is orthogonal to the false lifts of the rank-one lift: for every finite place \(w\) of \(E\) with \(\bar w \ne w\) that is not a place of the lift or the conjugate of one, \(\Xi\) is orthogonal to every form in the image of \(e_{X-1}\) that is killed by the Hecke polynomial at \(w\) at its test value (as in Definition 75.3.2) and by the descent, the adjoint of the lift. Here \(e_{X-1}\) is the idempotent with label \(X - 1\) at every chosen place; since the source has rank one, it is the mean over \(\prod_{w \in \mathcal M} k_w^\times\).

Let \(\theta = (\theta_\iota)\) be a family of \(m\)-fold twists, one for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(\iota \mid \ell\).

Definition 79.1.2. The family \(\theta\) has small ratios if (i) \(\ell\) is prime to \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota}\) for every \(\iota \mid \ell\), and (ii) for every \(1 \le j \le m\), every ramified critical pair \(P\) and \(\epsilon = \pm 1\), the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^j, \epsilon)^{-1}\) has height at most \(x_1\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 68: Theorem 68.5.1; Chapter 74; Chapter 77; Chapter 85

Definition 79.1.3. The family \(\theta\) is regular if it has small ratios and (iii) for every \(1 \le j \le m\) there are reals \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\), one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0} \mathrm{cl}(\ell) \le B\) for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota \mid \ell\), with \(\vartheta := \theta_\iota^j\): \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) clears the projection at \((\iota, \vartheta, j)\) (Definition 75.3.4); and for every critical pair \(P\) main for \(j\) and any two test forms \(\Xi\), \(\Xi'\) for \(\ell\) for \(P\) and \(\vartheta\), \(\lvert\langle\operatorname{pr}_{P,\vartheta}\Xi, \operatorname{pr}_{P,\vartheta}\Xi'\rangle\rvert_\iota \le e^{\mathrm{cl}(\ell)}\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 74; Chapter 75; Chapter 77; Chapter 85; Chapter Overview

That regular families exist is Theorem 85.2.1.

§79.2. A large pairing from characters

Where does a test form with a large pairing come from? In the proof of (1) its pairing with \(\phi_{P,\vartheta}\) is a scalar multiple of a normalized character sum of a theta kernel with twelve coordinates at each \(\tau\); after scaling, the value of the kernel at the identity is the coefficient estimated in Theorem 78.5.1. Two elementary facts carry the passage.

Problem 79.2.1. Let \(\mathrm{Cl}\) be a finite abelian group of order \(N_c\), \(\mathrm{kn}\colon \mathrm{Cl}\to \mathbb{C}\), and for a character \(\psi\) of \(\mathrm{Cl}\) let \(\mathrm{av}_\psi := N_c^{-1}\sum_{g \in \mathrm{Cl}}\psi(g)\,\mathrm{kn}(g)\). Then \(\sum_\psi \mathrm{av}_\psi = \mathrm{kn}(1)\), the sum over all characters, and for every prime \(\ell\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) some \(\psi\) has \(\lvert\eta(\mathrm{av}_\psi)\rvert \ge \lvert\eta(\mathrm{kn}(1))\rvert\). If \(N_c = 2\) and \(\mathrm{kn}\) is the indicator of \(1\), both \(\mathrm{av}_\psi\) equal \(1/2\), of \(2\)-adic absolute value \(2 \ge \lvert\mathrm{kn}(1)\rvert_2\).

Problem 79.2.2. Let \(G\) be a finite group, \(\mathcal A_3, \mathcal A_1\colon G \to \mathbb{C}\), and \(L_* \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(L_*^{\#G} = \prod_{\chi \in G} \mathcal A_3(\chi)\mathcal A_1(\chi)\). Let \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to F_0\) be a ring homomorphism into a field with an absolute value. Then for every \(\psi \in G\) there are \(\chi_3, \chi_1 \in G\) with \(\chi_3\chi_1 = \psi\) and \(\lvert\eta(\mathcal A_3(\chi_3)\mathcal A_1(\chi_1))\rvert \ge \lvert\eta(L_*)\rvert\). For \(G = \{1, \psi\}\), \(\mathcal A_3 = (1, 9)\), \(\mathcal A_1 = (9, 1)\) and \(L_* = 9\), exactly one factorization of \(\psi\) satisfies the inequality for \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_3\).

For a main pair the proof of (1) splits the \(2z\) coordinates into blocks of sizes \(12\), \(4\), \(4\) and \(2z - 20\): the jet lives on the first block, the twist on the second, and the last two carry auxiliary lifts.

Problem 79.2.3. For an integer \(z \ge 2\), \(12 + 4 + 4 + \max(2z - 20, 0) = 2z\) if and only if \(z \ge 10\). For a pair main for \(j\), \(10000 \le m < z \le r\) and \(4 \le 2z - 20 \le t\).

Let \(P\) be main for \(j\), \(\lambda := \lambda_P\) its row, and \({\mathbf{h}}= (h_{\tau,i})\) a multi-index on the twelve copies with \(\sum_i h_{\tau,i} = \lambda(\tau)\) for every \(\tau\) (Chapter 78). For the twelve coordinates of the first block the proof uses an integer vector in the Weyl representation of the lift; over every field of characteristic zero it is fixed by every family of invertible matrices fixing those twelve basis vectors at each \(\tau\), and its Fock polynomial in these coordinates is \(T_{\mathbf{h}}X^{\mathbf{h}}\), with \[T_{\mathbf{h}}:= \prod_{\tau\in\Phi}\frac{\lambda(\tau)!}{\prod_{i=1}^{12} h_{\tau,i}!} .\] This is read from the proof; not proved here. Give the polynomials in the \(X_{\tau,i}\) the Fock inner product of Problem 5.4.4 in each variable: \(\lVert X^{\mathbf{h}}\rVert^2 = \prod_{\tau,i}h_{\tau,i}!\), and monomials are orthogonal.

Problem 79.2.4. \(T_{\mathbf{h}}\) is the coefficient of \(X^{\mathbf{h}}\) in \(\prod_\tau (X_{\tau,1} + \dots + X_{\tau,12})^{\lambda(\tau)}\), and \(T_{\mathbf{h}}\in \mathbb{Z}\). For a polynomial \(f = \sum_{{\mathbf{h}}'} a_{{\mathbf{h}}'} X^{{\mathbf{h}}'}\), \(\langle T_{\mathbf{h}}X^{\mathbf{h}}, f\rangle = \prod_\tau \lambda(\tau)! \cdot a_{\mathbf{h}}\). For one place, \(\lambda = 13\) and \({\mathbf{h}}= (2, 1, \dots, 1)\), \(T_{\mathbf{h}}= 13!/2\).

Theorem 78.5.1, for \(\lambda\) with \(\kappa(\tau) \le \lambda(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau\), gives \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\in \mathbb{C}^\times\) with \(\lvert\sigma(c_{\mathrm{att}})\rvert \le \exp(fmb/k)\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) such that for the modulus \(\mathfrak m := (\pi_M)\) (Definition 71.2.1), every prime \(p\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) there are \({\mathbf{h}}\) with \(\sum_i h_{\tau,i} = \lambda(\tau)\) for every \(\tau\) and \(y_0 \in (\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m)^{12}\) with \[\operatorname{val}\Bigl(\eta\Bigl(\frac{c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}}{c_{\mathrm{att}}}\prod_{\tau\in\Phi}\lambda(\tau)!\cdot[z^{\mathbf{h}}]\mathrm{Jet}_{y_0}\Bigr)\Bigr) \le s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p} .\] For a pair \(P\) main for \(j\), the row \(\lambda_P\) satisfies these bounds (Problem 77.4.2).

Now the objects read from the proof. In this sheet \(G_{\mathcal M}\) is the finite group of pairs \(\psi\) consisting of a continuous unitary character of the norm-one ideles of \(E\), trivial on the norm-one elements of \(E^\times\), of weight \(0\), and its tame part, which is trivial off \(\mathcal M\); \(\psi(g)\) is the value of the character at the norm-one idele given by a finite adelic point \(g\) of \(U(1)\). For every \(\psi \in G_{\mathcal M}\) and every even \(n\) with \(4 \le n \le t\) there is a critical pair \((\nu, n/2)\) with empty row, of rank \(n\) (the rank \(2z\) of the space of its lift, Definition 77.2.2), with the tame part of \(\psi\), whose character becomes that of \(\psi\) after multiplication by \(\upsilon^{n/2}\). For a pair \(P = (\nu_P, z)\) main for \(j\), let \(P^{(4)}_\psi\) and \(P^{(2z-20)}_\psi\) be such pairs of ranks \(4\) and \(2z - 20\), and fix \(L_*\) with \[L_*^{\#G_{\mathcal M}} = \prod_{\psi \in G_{\mathcal M}}\mathrm{alg}_{P^{(4)}_\psi}(1)\,\mathrm{alg}_{P^{(2z-20)}_\psi}(1) .\] For a pair \(P_0\) with empty row, \(\mathrm{alg}_{P_0}(1)\) is a positive real number (Chapter 77), and \[\lvert\sigma(\mathrm{alg}_{P_0}(1))\rvert \le e^{x^{34}} \qquad\text{for every } \sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}) ;\] this bound is read from the proof; not proved here. Hence \(L_* \ne 0\) and \(\lvert\sigma(L_*)\rvert \le e^{2x^{34}}\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\). The classes of the finite adelic points of \(U(1)\) modulo the rational points and the principal level at the chosen places form a finite abelian group \(\mathrm{Cl}\), of order \(N_c\); every character of \(\mathrm{Cl}\) has the form \(g \mapsto \psi(g)\) for some \(\psi \in G_{\mathcal M}\). Fix representatives \(g_c\) of the classes \(c \in \mathrm{Cl}\).

The exact pairing. Let \(P\) be main for \(j\), \(P'\) a critical pair with empty row of rank four, \(\ell\) a prime, \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) a ring homomorphism and \(\vartheta\) a twist with \(\ell \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\). Let \({\mathbf{h}}\) and \(y_0\) be given by Theorem 78.5.1 for \(\lambda = \lambda_P\), \((\ell, \eta)\) and the modulus \((\pi_M)\). Write \(\nu_{P'}\) for the character of \(P'\) and put \(\chi_0 := (\nu_P/\nu_{P'})\,\upsilon^{z-2}\). Let \(\mathrm{kn}(g)\) be the scalar theta kernel on the twelve-block, with characters \(1\) and \(\chi_0\), polynomial \(X^{\mathbf{h}}\) and the indicator of \(y_0\), evaluated at the point of \(U(1)\) with finite part \(g\) and trivial archimedean part, and at the identity of \(U(12)\). There is a number \(c_{\mathrm{kn}} \ne 0\) such that \(c_{\mathrm{kn}}\,\mathrm{kn}(1) = c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}c_{\mathrm{att}}^{-1}\prod_\tau\lambda_P(\tau)!\cdot[z^{\mathbf{h}}]\mathrm{Jet}_{y_0}\), so that \(\lvert\eta(c_{\mathrm{kn}}\,\mathrm{kn}(1))\rvert \ge \ell^{-s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},\ell}}\); and for every \(\psi \in G_{\mathcal M}\), every factorization \(\psi = \psi_3\psi_1\) in \(G_{\mathcal M}\) and every finite adelic point \(g_0\) of \(U(1)\) there is a test form \(\Xi\) for \(\ell\) for \(P\) and \(\vartheta\) with \[N_c^2\,\langle\Xi, \phi_{P,\vartheta}\rangle = c_{\mathrm{att}}\,\mathrm{alg}_{P'}(1)\,\mathrm{alg}_{\psi_3,\psi_1}\,R_{P'}(\vartheta)\,c_{\mathrm{kn}}\,\mathrm{av}_{\psi,g_0}, \qquad \mathrm{av}_{\psi,g_0} := \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{c \in \mathrm{Cl}}\psi(g_cg_0)\,\mathrm{kn}(g_cg_0) ,\] where \(\mathrm{alg}_{\psi_3,\psi_1} := \mathrm{alg}_{P^{(4)}_{\psi_3}}(1)\,\mathrm{alg}_{P^{(2z-20)}_{\psi_1}}(1)\). Here \(\Xi\) is a class sum, against the lift of \(P'\) at \(\vartheta\) and the untwisted lifts of the two auxiliary pairs, of a point form with the integer vector above. Status. We take this identity as an input. The Lean proof obtains it for its chosen character, factorization and translation. The stated form follows from the pointwise kernel identity by summing over translated classes, as in the final remark.

Problem 79.2.5. Let \(P\) be main for \(j\) and \(P'\) a critical pair with empty row of rank four, and put \(c_{\mathrm{test}}:= c_{\mathrm{att}}\,\mathrm{alg}_{P'}(1)\,L_*\). Then \(c_{\mathrm{test}}\ne 0\), \(\lvert\sigma(c_{\mathrm{test}})\rvert \le \exp(fmb/k + 3x^{34})\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), and for every prime \(\ell\), every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) and every twist \(\vartheta\) with \(\ell\nmid\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\) there is a test form \(\Xi\) for \(\ell\) for \(P\) and \(\vartheta\) with \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:test-pairing}\tag{1} \lvert\eta(c_{\mathrm{test}}\,R_{P'}(\vartheta))\rvert\cdot\ell^{-s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},\ell}} \le \lvert\eta\langle\Xi, \phi_{P,\vartheta}\rangle\rvert . \end{equation}\]

§79.3. The height of a Hecke ratio

Let \(\theta\) be regular, \(1 \le j \le m\), \(P = (\nu, z)\) a critical pair main for \(j\), and put \(\vartheta_\iota := \theta_\iota^j\). Fix a ramified critical pair \(P'\) with empty row of rank four; the proof takes one whose tame part is that of \(\chi_M\) and whose character becomes \(\chi_M\) after multiplication by \(\upsilon^2\). Let \(c_{\mathrm{test}}\) be as in Problem 79.2.5 for \(P\) and \(P'\). The ratio \(R_{P'}(\vartheta_\iota)\) is a positive real number.

Problem 79.3.1. Let \(\iota \mid \ell\). There are test forms \(\Xi_\iota\), \(\Xi'_\iota\) for \(\ell\) for \(P\) and \(\vartheta_\iota\) such that, with \(y_\iota := \langle\Xi_\iota, \phi_{P,\vartheta_\iota}\rangle\langle\phi_{P,\vartheta_\iota}, \Xi'_\iota\rangle\), \[\lvert c_{\mathrm{test}}\,\bar c_{\mathrm{test}}\,R_{P'}(\vartheta_\iota)^2\rvert_\iota\cdot\ell^{-2s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},\ell}} \le \lvert y_\iota\rvert_\iota ;\] in particular \(y_\iota \ne 0\).

For each \(\iota\) fix such \(\Xi_\iota\), \(\Xi'_\iota\), and let \(y_\iota\) be the resulting product.

Problem 79.3.2. For every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, \[\sum_{p \in P_0} 2\,s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}\log p \le \sum_{\tau\in\Phi}s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) .\]

Problem 79.3.3. For the data, \(fmb/k + 3x^{34} \le B\) and \(x_1 \le B\).

Problem 79.3.4. The family \(\iota \mapsto R_{P'}(\vartheta_\iota)^2/y_\iota\) has height at most \(2B + \sum_{\tau\in\Phi}s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\tau),\tau)\).

Problem 79.3.5. In Problem 79.1.3 at \(\vartheta_\iota\), with \(\Xi_\iota, \Xi'_\iota\) and \(P'\), the five factors have \(\lvert(\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\vartheta_\iota})^{2z-1}\rvert_\iota = 1\) and, as families in \(\iota\), heights at most \(B - \sum_\tau s_\infty(\kappa(\tau))\), \(B\), \(2x_1\) and \(2B + \sum_\tau s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\tau),\tau)\) for the last four. The sum of these four bounds is \(4B + 2x_1 - h_{\mathrm{dir}}\le 6B - h_{\mathrm{dir}}\).

Problems 79.1.3 and 79.3.1–79.3.5 prove the following.

Theorem 79.3.1. Let \(\theta\) be regular, \(1 \le j \le m\), and \(P\) a critical pair main for \(j\). Then the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^j)^{-1}\) has height at most \(6B - h_{\mathrm{dir}}\).

Used in: Problem 79.3.6; Chapter 74; Chapter 77

Problem 79.3.6. For the data, \(6B < h_{\mathrm{dir}}\). So the bound of Theorem 79.3.1 is negative.

We have proved Theorem 79.3.1 from Theorems 77.4.1 and 78.5.1 and the exact pairing of Section 79.2, taken as an input with the auxiliary pairs, the conjugate bound for their algebraic parts, the integer vector on the twelve-block, and the finite class group \(\mathrm{Cl}\) with the lifting of its characters to \(G_{\mathcal M}\); the bounds on ramified ratios and on denominators of projections enter as the definition of regular families. By Problem 77.5.1, at \(\vartheta_\iota\) the inverse ratio of Hecke value products is, up to a unit at \(\ell\), the square of a product of \(k/2\) factors \(R_P(\vartheta_\iota)^{-1}\), \(P\) main, and \(5m\) factors \(R_{P'}(\vartheta_\iota, \epsilon)^{-1}\), \(P'\) ramified, with signs depending only on \(j\); so its height is at most \(k(6B - h_{\mathrm{dir}}) + 10mx_1\) (Problem 74.2.4).
