Theorem A (Chapter 49) compares the size of the composite lift with its divisibility. Chapter 57 treats the divisibility; this sheet treats the size. How large can \(B_{1,\Psi}\varphi\) be on every conjugate, if \(\varphi\) is small? The answer has two halves. At one pair of points the kernel of lift \(j\) multiplies conjugate Weyl norms by at most \(\exp(x^{50} + \mathrm{rs}_j/2)\), where \(\mathrm{rs}_j\) sums \(s_\infty(l)\) over its rows at all places (Definition 66.2.6): a Gaussian integral, and one factorial \(l!\) per row of length \(l\). The finite prime sums cancel exactly against the gains (Problem 66.1.1), and at each long place (a place in \(\Phi_a\)) the level \(N\) pays for the excess of \(m\,s_\infty(a)\) over \(m\,s_{\mathrm{main}}\) up to \(32a\log b\) (Problem 66.1.4).

§66.1. The exponent

We recall the global prime sums and sizes from Chapter 36 and the local gains from Chapter 57. The places \(\Phi\) are identified with \((\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times\), \([\nu]\) is the place of the residue class of an integer \(\nu\) if \(\gcd(\nu, q_1) = 1\), and the identity place otherwise, and \(\Phi_a\) is the set of \(a\)-places. Recall \(s_\infty(j) = \log j! + j\log(M/\Omega)\) with \(\Omega = e^{\mathrm{CS}(d)}\,2\pi/\mathrm{rd}_E\), \(c_1 = -1 - \mathrm{CS}(d)\), \(s_{\mathrm{main}}= a(\log a - \frac1f\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}\log\kappa(\tau) + c_1)\), \(h_{\mathrm{main}}= mk\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}(s_\infty(\kappa(\tau)) - s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\tau), \tau))\) and \(\mathrm{rem}= \sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a}(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) - s_{\mathrm{main}})\). Let \(\Pi\) be the set of primes \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) (Section 57.1), and put, in Part VIII, \[\Sigma = \sum_{p \in \Pi}\mathrm{gain}(p)\log p .\] We use the following consequences of Chapter 36 (Definition 36.3.1 and Problems 36.2.3, 36.4.1, 36.4.4 and 36.5.4): \(x_1 \le a\); \(a/x_1 \le \kappa(\tau) \le b \le a\) for every \(\tau\); \(20\log b \le \log(M/\Omega) \le 23\log b\) (Problem 36.4.3); \(s_\infty(j) \ge 0\) for integers \(0 \le j \le a\); \(m(1 + \frac12\log a) \le a\log b\); and \(\log(M\mathrm{rd}_E/2\pi) \le 23\log b\). By Definition 36.3.1, \(\log N \ge L_-(\kappa, m, M, \log b)\).

By Problems 57.1.3 and 57.1.4, and the sum over primes immediately after the latter, \[2\Sigma = m\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a}s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) + mk\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) .\]

Problem 66.1.1. \(h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ 2\Sigma = m\,\#\Phi_a\, s_{\mathrm{main}}+ mk\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}s_\infty(\kappa(\tau))\).

The prime sums have cancelled.

Problem 66.1.2. For every integer \(n \ge 1\), \(\log n! \le n\log n - n + 1 + \frac12\log n\).

Problem 66.1.3. \(\log(M/\Omega) = 1 + c_1 + \log(M\mathrm{rd}_E/2\pi)\), and \[s_\infty(a) - s_{\mathrm{main}}\le a\Bigl(\frac1f\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}\log\kappa(\tau) + \log\frac{M\mathrm{rd}_E}{2\pi}\Bigr) + 1 + \tfrac12\log a .\]

Used in: Chapter Overview

Problem 66.1.4. \(m(s_\infty(a) - s_{\mathrm{main}}) - a\log N \le 32\,a\log b\).

Used in: Chapter 36; Chapter Overview

Put \(C_{\mathrm{ch}}= m\bigl(\#\Phi_a\,s_\infty(a) + k\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}s_\infty(\kappa(\tau))\bigr)\).

Problem 66.1.5. \(C_{\mathrm{ch}}- \#\Phi_a\,a\log N \le h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ 2\Sigma + 32\,fa\log b\).

For \(n = 6\) the two sides of Problem 66.1.2 compare as follows.

Problem 66.1.6. \(\log 6! + 6(1 - \log 6) = 1.8286\ldots \le 1 + \frac12\log 6 = 1.8958\ldots\).

§66.2. One kernel in one coordinate

Where does \(s_\infty\) come from? Moving a finite translate of the integral vacuum gives a finite translate of the moved vacuum times a scalar whose conjugates have modulus one. For an automorphism over \(K\), the proof writes the conjugate of a theta jet of this latter translate as an integral of a conjugated polynomial against the Gaussian \(e^{-\sum_i\lvert y_i\rvert^2/2}\) and a bounded function [GL]. In this sheet \(l\) denotes the length of a row, \(L \ge 1\) a growth rate, and \(v\), \(t_0\), \(u\) are real numbers.

Problem 66.2.1. For \(\rho \ge 0\), \(l \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(v \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\rho^l \le l!\,\exp(lv + \rho e^{-v})\).

Problem 66.2.2. In the one-coordinate model let \(l \ge 0\) be an integer, \(t_0 > 0\), \(L \ge 1\) and \(u \ge 0\). If \(z \in \mathbb{C}\) and \(\lvert z\rvert^2 \le t_0(2L + 2)u\), then \[\lvert z\rvert^l \le \exp\Bigl(\tfrac12\bigl(l/L + \log l! + l\log t_0\bigr) + Lu\Bigr) .\]

For a matrix the same device is applied to each row of a diagram. In one variable the Fock norm \(\lVert z^l\rVert^2 = l!\) of Problem 5.4.4 shows the same factorial.

Problem 66.2.3. Let \(n \ge 1\) and let \(Z\) be a complex \(n \times n\) matrix and \(\rho_0 \ge \rho_1 \ge \dots \ge \rho_{n-1}\) the eigenvalues of \(Z^*Z\). For \(1 \le h \le n\) the operator norm of \(\wedge^h Z\), for the inner product induced on \(\wedge^h\mathbb{C}^n\), satisfies \(\lVert\wedge^h Z\rVert^2 = \rho_0\rho_1\cdots\rho_{h-1}\). For \(l \ge 1\), with the inner product on \(\operatorname{Sym}^l\mathbb{C}^n \subseteq (\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes l}\) induced from the tensor power, \(\lVert\operatorname{Sym}^l Z\rVert^2 \le \rho_0^l\).

A column of height \(h\) carries \(\wedge^h\), a row of length \(l\) carries \(\operatorname{Sym}^l\); a general diagram mixes both, and the proof uses the following bound. Schur norm. For each \(\tau\) in a finite set let \(z_\tau\) be a complex \(n'_\tau \times n_\tau\) matrix with \(n_\tau \le n'_\tau\), let \(b_{\tau,h}\) be numbers of columns of height \(h\), with \(b_{\tau,h} = 0\) for \(n_\tau < h < n'_\tau\), and put \(l_{\tau,i} = \sum_{i < h < n'_\tau} b_{\tau,h}\). There are \(\rho_{\tau,i} \ge 0\), \(0 \le i < n_\tau\), with \(\sum_{i < n_\tau}\rho_{\tau,i}\) at most the sum of the squared absolute values of the entries of \(z_\tau\) for each \(\tau\), such that the Schur matrix of \((z_\tau)\) multiplies the squared Weyl norm of every vector by at most \(\prod_\tau\prod_{i < n_\tau}\rho_{\tau,i}^{l_{\tau,i}}\). Status: classical linear algebra (singular values); read from the proof [GL].

Problem 66.2.4. Let \(L \ge 1\) and \(s' \in \mathbb{R}\). Then \(Ls'/(2L + 2) - s'/2 = -s'/(2L + 2)\), and \(\frac1\pi\int_\mathbb{C}e^{-\lvert y\rvert^2/c}\,dA(y) = c\) for \(c > 0\). For \(L = 2\), \(\int_0^\infty 2r e^{-r^2/6}\,dr = 6 = 2L + 2\).

Problem 66.2.5. Let \(l \ge 0\) be an integer, \(t_0 > 0\), \(L \ge 1\), \(\mathrm{gr}(l) = \frac12(l/L + \log l! + l\log t_0)\), and \(H\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}\) measurable with \(\lvert H\rvert \le 1\). Then \[\Bigl\lvert\frac1\pi\int_\mathbb{C}(\sqrt{t_0}\,y)^l e^{-\lvert y\rvert^2/2}H(y)\,dA(y)\Bigr\rvert \le e^{\mathrm{gr}(l)}(2L + 2) ,\] and the Gaussian \(\exp(-\sum_i\lvert y_i\rvert^2/(2L + 2))\) in \(n_{\mathrm{c}}\) complex coordinates has integral \((2L + 2)^{n_{\mathrm{c}}}\) for the measure \(\pi^{-n_{\mathrm{c}}}dA\).

The lift takes its Taylor series at the scale \(\pi_M c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), with \(c_{\mathrm{jet}}\) the jet scale of Definition 59.4.3. For each \(\sigma\) over \(K\) the conjugation result in the proof supplies a scale \(t_\sigma\) with \(\lvert t_\sigma\rvert^2 = 1/\Omega\). Set \(t_0 := \lvert\sigma(\pi_M)t_\sigma\rvert^2\). Since \(\pi_M\bar\pi_M = M\), we have \(t_0 = M/\Omega\), so \(2\,\mathrm{gr}(l) = l/L + s_\infty(l)\). Every automorphism of \(\mathbb{C}\) gives the same absolute values as one over \(K\) [GL]. With \(L = a + 1\), \(l/L \le 2\) for \(l \le 2a\). The computation of \(\lvert t_\sigma\rvert^2\) uses the area of the conjugate lattice, the Chowla–Selberg unit, and the fact that the principal \(f\)-th root in Definition 59.4.3 has squared modulus \(\mathrm{rd}_E/\sqrt d\) on every conjugate over \(K\) [GL]; the next two inputs enter it.

Conjugates of translated theta functions. Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be the prime of the data. We call a lattice \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}\) stable under \(\mathcal{O}_K\), with a point \(t' \in \mathbb{C}\) of exact order two modulo \(\Lambda\) such that \((1 \pm \sqrt{-d})t'/2 \notin \Lambda\), scaled so that \(\wp_\Lambda''(t') = 2\), with a chosen \(a_1\) such that \(a_1^2 = 12\wp_\Lambda(t')\), a normalized CM lattice; for a lattice \(D\) write \(T^D_v f(z) = \exp(-\pi(z\bar v + \lvert v\rvert^2/2)/\operatorname{area}(D))f(z + v)\), and let \(\theta\) be the normalized theta section of \(\Lambda\) of [GL] (the twisted translate by \(t'\) of the sigma function of [GL], which differs from the classical Weierstrass sigma function by a factor \(e^{cz^2}\), divided by the value of that translate at \(0\)). Suppose \(\Lambda = \omega_0\mathcal{O}_K\), and let \(\sigma\) be an automorphism of \(\mathbb{C}\) over \(K\). There are \(\omega' \in \mathbb{C}^\times\), a nonzero ideal \(\mathfrak a\) of \(\mathcal{O}_K\) and a finite adele \(\mathfrak b\) of \(K\) such that the lattice \(\Lambda'\) of the conjugate curve is \(\omega'\mathfrak a^2/\mathrm{Nm}\,\mathfrak a\), has area \(\lvert\omega'\rvert^2\sqrt d/2\) and contains \(n_0\omega'\) for some integer \(n_0 \ge 1\); and, with \(\theta'\) defined in the same way from the conjugate marked lattice, for every \(y \in K\) and every \(y' \in K\) close enough to \(\mathfrak b y\) in the finite adeles, applying \(\sigma\) coefficientwise to the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(T^\Lambda_{y\omega_0}\theta\) gives the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(T^{\Lambda'}_{y'\omega'}\theta'\). Status: this theta formulation is read from the proof [GL]; for the classical main theorem of complex multiplication see [Sh, Thm. 5.4].

The Chowla–Selberg unit. For every normalized CM lattice for \(K\), in any ideal class, there is \(u_{\mathrm{CS}} \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\lvert\eta(u_{\mathrm{CS}})\rvert_p = 1\) for every prime \(p\) and every ring map \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\), such that for every automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\mathbb{C}\) over \(K\) the area of the lattice of the conjugate curve times \(\lvert\sigma(u_{\mathrm{CS}})\rvert^2\) is \(\pi e^{\mathrm{CS}(d)}\,2\pi/\sqrt d\). Status: Theorem 20.3.1 is the classical Chowla–Selberg product over ideal classes [CS]; the statement above is read from the proof [GL].

Let \(\beta\) be the diagram of Definition 37.2.1. Put \(a_\tau = a\) if \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) and \(a_\tau = 0\) otherwise.

Definition 66.2.6. Fix \(j < m\). For \(\tau \in \Phi\) and \(0 \le i < n_j\) let \[\mathrm{row}_\tau(i) = \sum_{i < h < n_{j+1}} \beta(\tau, h),\] the length of row \(i\), counted from \(0\), of the diagram at \(\tau\) formed by the columns of height less than \(n_{j+1}\). We call these the row lengths of lift \(j\), drop \(j\) from the notation, and put \(\mathrm{rs}_j = \sum_{\tau \in \Phi}\sum_{0 \le i < n_j}s_\infty(\mathrm{row}_\tau(i))\).

Write \(G_f(V)\) for the group of finite adelic points of the unitary group of a space \(V\). For \(j < m\), a translation \(\mathfrak t\), \(g \in G_f(X_j)\) and \(g' \in G_f(X_{j+1})\), consider the kernel of lift \(j\) of Section 59.4, at the trivial twist and at the datum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) moved by \((g, g')\): a linear map from the Weyl vectors of the weight of \(X_j\) to those of \(X_{j+1}\), in which the values of forms lie. The proof establishes [GL]: on every conjugate this kernel multiplies Weyl norms by at most \(\exp(x^{50} + \mathrm{rs}_j/2)\); that is, for every value \(\omega\) and every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), the Weyl norm of \(\sigma\) applied to the image of \(\omega\) is at most \(\exp(x^{50} + \mathrm{rs}_j/2)\) times the Weyl norm of \(\sigma\omega\). Here \(L = a + 1\), \(t_0 = M/\Omega\) and there are \(n_{\mathrm{c}} = fn_jn_{j+1}\) coordinates; besides \(\mathrm{rs}_j/2\) the exponent contains the logarithm of the vacuum bound, \(n_{\mathrm{c}}\log(2a + 4)\) and \(\sum_{\tau,i}\mathrm{row}_\tau(i)/(2L)\), and their sum is at most \(x^{50}\).

§66.3. The chain

The kernel bounds multiply along each tuple of classes, so their exponents involve \(\sum_{j<m}\mathrm{rs}_j\). We bound this sum, count the classes, and restrict to \(Y_m\).

Problem 66.3.1. For integers \(l, l_0, l_1 \ge 0\) with \(l \le l_0 + l_1\), \(s_\infty(l) \le s_\infty(l_0) + l_1\bigl(\log(l_0 + l_1 + 1) + \log(M/\Omega)\bigr)\).

Problem 66.3.2. Let \(j < m\), \(\tau \in \Phi\) and \(0 \le i < n_j\). Then \(\mathrm{row}_\tau(0) \le a_\tau + b + r^{16}\) (the first-row slack in the proof is \(b + a/x_1 + r^{16}\)), \(\mathrm{row}_\tau(i) \le \kappa(\tau) + r^{16}\) for \(0 < i < k\), and \(\mathrm{row}_\tau(i) \le r^{16}\) for \(i \ge k\). Each row length is at most \(2a\).

Problem 66.3.3. \(\sum_{j < m} \mathrm{rs}_j \le C_{\mathrm{ch}}+ U/2\).

Class count. Theorem 50.2.11 bounds the number of classes at the standard level using short bases. For the data, at a level containing the principal congruence subgroup of \(\pi_M\pi_N\), in rank at most \(n_m\), there are at most \(e^{x^{30}}\) classes. Status: read from the proof [GL].

Branching norm. The restriction from \(X_m\) to \(Y_m\) of Section 52.4 applies an integral branching matrix; its squared operator norm for the Weyl norms is at most \(e^{x^{70}}\). Status: read from the proof [GL].

For a form \(F\) on \(X_j\) or on \(Y_m\) and a ring homomorphism \(\sigma\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}\), \(\lvert\sigma F(g)\rvert^2\) denotes the Weyl squared norm of the conjugate value.

Problem 66.3.4. For a form \(F\) on \(X_m\), a ring homomorphism \(\sigma\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}\) and \(h \in G_f(Y_m)\), the restriction of \(F\) to \(Y_m\) satisfies \[\lvert\sigma(\mathrm{res}\,F)(h)\rvert^2 \le \exp\Bigl(x^{70} - \log N\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}(a_\tau - mr^8)\Bigr)\,\lvert\sigma F(\operatorname{diag}(h, 1))\rvert^2 ,\] and \(\sum_{\tau \in \Phi}(a_\tau - mr^8) = \#\Phi_a\,a - fmr^8\). Formally, for one place with \(a_\tau - mr^8 = 1\) and \(\pi_N = 1 + 2\sqrt{-1}\) (so \(N = 5\), which is not the level of any data), the scalar contributes \(\lvert 1/\bar\pi_N\rvert^2 = 1/5\) to the squared norm.

Problem 66.3.5. \(x^{70} + fmr^8\log N + 2(mx^{30} + x^{60} + mx^{50}) + 32fa\log b \le U/2\).

Let \(\Psi\) be a chain datum for the trivial twist. By Theorem 57.3.4, choose a weak chain datum \(\Psi'\) with \(B_{1,\Psi} = R_{1,\Psi'}\) on \(S\); then \(\sum_{\mathfrak t}\lvert\sigma(\Psi'(\mathfrak t))\rvert \le e^{x^{60}}\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\). Fix \(\varphi \in S\) and \(y \in G_f(Y_m)\). Write \(C_{\mathfrak t}\varphi\) for the chain of the \(m\) lifts of \(\varphi\) with the data \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\). Before restriction the value at \(\operatorname{diag}(y, 1)\) is \(\sum_{\mathfrak t}\Psi'(\mathfrak t)(C_{\mathfrak t}\varphi)(\operatorname{diag}(y, 1))\), and the proof expands it as a sum over the \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of \(\Psi'\) and over the tuples of classes at the levels of \(X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}\), of \(\Psi'(\mathfrak t)\) times products of \(m\) kernels applied to values of \(\varphi\). There are at most \(e^{mx^{30}}\) tuples of classes. The resulting estimate for the restricted chain, whose exponent is controlled by Problems 66.1.5, 66.3.3, 66.3.4 and 66.3.5, gives the following theorem. The problems prove the arithmetic of its exponent; the estimate for the restricted chain is read from the proof [GL].

Theorem 66.3.6. Let \(\Psi\) be a chain datum for the trivial twist, \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), \(\varphi \in S\) and \(c \in \mathbb{R}\). If \(\lvert\sigma\varphi(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for every \(g \in G_f(X_0)\), then for every \(y \in G_f(Y_m)\) \[\lvert\sigma(B_{1,\Psi}\varphi)(y)\rvert^2 \le \exp\bigl(c + h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ 2\Sigma + U\bigr) .\]

Used in: Chapter 49

The theorem is a statement at the trivial twist only. The problems give the arithmetic estimates in its exponent; taken as given are the kernel bound with the Schur norm, the conjugation of translated theta functions and the Chowla–Selberg unit behind it, the class count at the congruence level, the branching norm, and the expansion of a chain datum. In the exponent \(\Sigma\) has the coefficient \(2\); Chapter 49 sets the divisibility of Chapter 57 against it in Theorem 49.3.2.
