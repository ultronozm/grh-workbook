§43.1. Characters that disagree

Problem 43.1.1. Let \(U,U'\) be subgroups of a finite group \(H\) with characters \(\psi,\psi'\), and let \(\phi\colon H\to{\mathbb{C}}\) satisfy \(\phi(ugu')=\psi(u)\phi(g)\psi'(u')\) for \(u\in U\), \(u'\in U'\). If \(ugu'=g\) for some \(u,u'\) with \(\psi(u)\psi'(u')\ne1\), then \(\phi(g)=0\).

Used in: Chapter 48

Let \(G=\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{F}}_p)\), and let \(U\subset G\) be the upper unitriangular group (upper triangular matrices with diagonal entries \(1\)), \(\psi\) a nontrivial character of \({\mathbb{F}}_p\), and \(\psi_U(u):=\psi(\sum_i u_{i,i+1})\). A double coset \(UgU\) supports if \(\psi_U(u)\psi_U(u')=1\) whenever \(ugu'=g\) (this does not depend on the representative \(g\)); by Problem 43.1.1 the functions \(\phi\) with \(\phi(ugu')=\psi_U(u)\phi(g)\psi_U(u')\) vanish off the supporting double cosets. By the Bruhat decomposition every double coset is \(UwtU\) with \(w\) a permutation matrix and \(t\) diagonal.

Problem 43.1.2. In \(\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p)\), \(U\operatorname{diag}(t_1,t_2)U\) supports if and only if \(t_1=t_2\), and every double coset in the big cell supports. So \((p-1)+(p-1)^2=p(p-1)\) of the \(2(p-1)^2\) double cosets support: \(6\) of \(8\) for \(p=3\), \(20\) of \(32\) for \(p=5\).

Used in: Chapter 48: Problem 48.2.4

Problem 43.1.3. By machine: in \(\mathrm{GL}_3({\mathbb{F}}_2)\), \(4\) of the \(6\) double cosets support, of sizes \(8,32,32,64\) (the two simple reflections do not); in \(\mathrm{GL}_3({\mathbb{F}}_3)\), \(18=2+8+8\) of the \(48\) support, of sizes \(27\), \(243\), \(729\) respectively.

Used in: Chapter 48: Problem 48.2.4

In all four cases the number of supporting double cosets is \(p^{n-1}(p-1)\). By Mackey’s formula it is \(\dim\operatorname{End}_G(\operatorname{Ind}_U^G\psi_U)\), and since the Gelfand–Graev representation \(\operatorname{Ind}_U^G\psi_U\) is multiplicity free [Ca85], it is the number of its irreducible constituents.

Problem 43.1.4. On \({\mathbb{F}}_p^2\) put \(h(y,y'):=y_1y'_2+y_2y'_1\), and for \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_2({\mathbb{F}}_p)\) let \(\langle g\rangle_1:=h(g\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_1)\). Then \(\langle g\rangle_1=g_{21}\); \({\mathbb{F}}_pg\mathbf e_1={\mathbb{F}}_p\mathbf e_1\) if and only if \(\langle g\rangle_1=0\); and the \(g\) with \(\langle g\rangle_1=0\) whose double coset supports are exactly the scalar multiples of elements of \(U\).

§43.2. The Bessel tuple, discriminants and links

In the proof the roles of \(U\), \(\psi_U\) and \(\mathbf e_1\) are played by \({\mathcal{U}}\), the character \(\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}\) and the flag of \(v_1,\dots,v_\varrho\). The group \(U(Y_0)(F)\) of rational points contains the rational Bessel subgroup \(R(F)\).

Definition 43.2.1. The Bessel tuple is \((v_1,\dots,v_\varrho,v_{\varrho+1})\) with \(v_{\varrho+1}:=z\). For \(\gamma\in U(Y_0)(F)\) the pairing matrix is \(\bigl(h(\gamma v_b,v_a)\bigr)_{1\le a,b\le\varrho+1}\). Its entries are the Bessel pairings. For \(a\le\varrho\), the entry in row \(a\) and column \(b\) is the coefficient of \(v'_a\) in \(\gamma v_b\), and for \(a=\varrho+1\) it is \(-N\) times the coefficient of \(z\). Its leading minors are \(\langle\gamma\rangle_j:=\det\bigl(h(\gamma v_b,v_a)\bigr)_{1\le a,b\le j}\) for \(1\le j\le\varrho+1\), and \(\langle\gamma\rangle_0:=1\).

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 44; Chapter 45; Chapter 46; Chapter 47; Chapter Overview

Definition 43.2.2. Define the discriminant of \(\gamma\) by \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma):=\langle\gamma\rangle_{\varrho+1}\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle_{\varrho+1}}-N^2\langle\gamma\rangle_\varrho\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle_\varrho}\in E\).

Used in: Chapter 45

In this sheet \(\mathrm{Fl}:=Ev_1+\dots+Ev_\varrho\), and for \(y\in Y_0\) we write \([y]\in X_0\oplus Ez\) for its component in \(X_0\oplus Ez\); for \(y\perp\mathrm{Fl}\) it represents the class of \(y\) in \(\mathrm{Fl}^\perp/\mathrm{Fl}\).

Definition 43.2.3. Define the link of \(\gamma\) to be the subspace \(\operatorname{lk}(\gamma):=\{([y],[\gamma^{-1}y]):y\perp\mathrm{Fl},\ y\perp\gamma\mathrm{Fl}\}\) of \((X_0\oplus Ez)^2\).

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 45

The characters agree on \(\gamma\) if, for all \(u,u'\in{\mathcal{U}}(F)\) with \(u\gamma=\gamma u'\), the character that the proof fixes on the finite adelic points of \({\mathcal{U}}\) takes the same value at the finite components of \(u\) and \(u'\). In this sheet \[\Gamma^*:=\{\gamma:\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\}\cup\{\gamma:\text{the characters agree on }\gamma,\ \operatorname{disc}(\gamma)=0,\ \gamma\mathrm{Fl}\ne\mathrm{Fl}\}\] is the set of remaining terms.

Definition 43.2.4. For \(\zeta\in\mathcal O_E\) with \(\zeta\bar\zeta=1\), the scalar coset \(\zeta\bar R_0\) is the set of \(s\in\bar G\) for which some \(t\in\bar R_0\) has \(s_w=(\zeta\bmod w)\,t_w\) for every \(w\in\mathcal M\). A set \(\mathcal B\subseteq\bar G\) avoids the scalar cosets if it meets no \(\zeta\bar R_0\) with \(\zeta\ne1\).

Used in: Chapter 47

Theorem 43.2.1 (the terms that survive). If \(\mathcal B\subseteq\bar G\) avoids the scalar cosets and \(s\in\mathcal B\), then for all classes \(c,c'\), for \(W\) and for \(W^\flat\), \[\mathrm{Amp}\bigl(U(Y_0)(F)\smallsetminus R(F);c,s,c'\bigr)=\mathrm{Amp}(\Gamma^*;c,s,c').\]

Used in: Chapter 44

Problem 43.2.1. In a three-dimensional model over \(E\), let \(\varrho=1\) and \(X_0=0\), with basis \(v_1,z,v'_1\), \(h(v_1,v'_1)=1\), \(h(v_1,v_1)=h(v'_1,v'_1)=0\), \(h(z,z)=-N\) and \(z\perp v_1,v'_1\). The two maps \(\gamma_\pm\) exchanging \(v_1\) and \(v'_1\) with \(z\mapsto\pm z\) are isometries of square \(1\) with \(\langle\gamma_\pm\rangle_1=1\), \(\langle\gamma_\pm\rangle_2=\mp N\), \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma_\pm)=0\) and \(\gamma_\pm\mathrm{Fl}\ne\mathrm{Fl}\); the characters agree on them, whatever the character of \({\mathcal{U}}\), so both lie in \(\Gamma^*\), and \(\operatorname{lk}(\gamma_\pm)\) is spanned by \((z,\pm z)\).

Reductions act on the lattice modulo \(w\) in the lattice basis, in which the members of the Bessel tuple, \(v_1,\dots,v_\varrho,z\), are basis vectors. For \(s\in\bar G\) and \(w\in\mathcal M\), let \(s\) act modulo \(w\) by \(s_w\), and modulo \(\bar w\) by the adjoint inverse of \(s_w\) for the bilinear form whose matrix is the Gram matrix of the lattice basis reduced modulo \(w\), which is invertible. For \(w\in\mathcal M_i\), \(\#k_w=p_i\) (Problem 37.1.1).

Definition 43.2.5. The element \(s\) meets at \(w\) (or at \(\bar w\)) if, modulo that place, the span of the Bessel tuple and the span of its image under \(s\) share a nonzero vector. The meet norm \(\operatorname{mt}(s)\) is the product, over \(w\in\mathcal M\), of a factor \(\#k_w\) if \(s\) meets at \(w\) and a further factor \(\#k_w\) if \(s\) meets at \(\bar w\).

Used in: Chapter 44; Chapter 47

Definition 43.2.6. The minor condition holds for \(s\) if at some \(w\in\mathcal M_0\) the leading minors of orders \(1,\dots,\varrho\) of the matrix \(\bigl(h(s_wv_b,v_a)\bigr)_{a,b\le\varrho+1}\), formed with the reduced form, are all nonzero.

Used in: Chapter 45; Chapter 47; Chapter Overview

Definition 43.2.7. Let \(\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal M_0\), and for each \(w\in\mathcal D\) choose \(w\) or \(\bar w\) and a nonzero vector \(y_w\) in the span of the Bessel tuple modulo the chosen place. The fixer of these vectors is the subgroup of the \(s\in\bar G\) whose action modulo the chosen place fixes \(y_w\), for every \(w\in\mathcal D\).

Used in: Chapter 45: Theorem 45.3.3; Chapter 46; Chapter 47

For \(j=0,1\), the strip \(\mathcal B_j\) is the set of \(s\in\bar G\) whose component in \(\bar G_j\) equals that of an element of \(\bar R_0\).

Problem 43.2.2. If \(\zeta\in\mathcal O_E\), \(\zeta\bar\zeta=1\) and \(\zeta\equiv1\) modulo every \(w\in\mathcal M_j\), then \(\zeta=1\). For example, in \({\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm i]\) reduction modulo \(2+\mathrm i\) is injective on \(\mu_4\).

Problem 43.2.3. Every \(s\in\mathcal B_j\) meets at \(w\) and at \(\bar w\) for every \(w\in\mathcal M_j\), so \(\operatorname{mt}(s)\ge p_j^{2f}\ge(M/b)^f\); and \(\mathcal B_j\) avoids the scalar cosets.

Problem 43.2.4. Every fixer contains the reduction of the finite adelic points of \(U(X_0)\) that preserve the lattice, and each element of a fixer meets at the chosen place over every \(w\in\mathcal D\).

§43.3. The congruence saving

The congruence saving is the saving from congruences satisfied by terms above a fixed reduction. On the strip \(\mathcal B_j\) every reduction meets at the \(2f\) places \(w,\bar w\), \(w\in\mathcal M_j\). In Petersson’s formula the same mechanism appears when the terms are sorted by their reduction modulo \(p\).

Problem 43.3.1. Let \(p\) be a prime and \(k'\ge2\), \(m',n'\ge1\) integers (primed, since \(k\) and \(m\) belong to the data); for even \(k'\ge4\) these are the weight and the indices of Theorem 10.2.1. If \(\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2({\mathbb{Z}})\) is upper triangular modulo \(p\) and \(\gamma_{21}\ne0\), then \(\lvert\gamma_{21}\rvert\ge p\) and \(\lvert J_{k'-1}(4\pi\sqrt{m'n'}/\lvert\gamma_{21}\rvert)\rvert\le(2\pi\sqrt{m'n'}/p)^{k'-1}/(k'-1)!\).

Problem 43.3.2. Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2\) be the standard basis of \({\mathbb{Z}}^2\). The integral columns \(\mathbf e_1\) and \(\mathbf e_1+p\mathbf e_2\) agree modulo \(p\); the lattice they span has determinant \(p\) and Gram determinant \(p^2\) (\(2,4\) for \(p=2\); \(3,9\) for \(p=3\)).
