Prerequisites.

From Chapter 40: the degrees \(d_\tau\) of Theorem 40.3.1, \(\deg=\prod_\tau d_\tau\), and the sizes \(D_{\mathrm{id}}\), \(D_{\mathrm{main}}=D_X^2/(\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r)\) of Definition 40.3.2. From Chapter 43: the Bessel tuple \((v_1,\dots,v_\varrho,z)\) and the leading minors \(\langle\gamma\rangle_j\) (Definition 43.2.1), \(\operatorname{disc}\) (Definition 43.2.2), links, open reductions, fixers and link second moments (Definitions 43.2.3, 43.2.6, 43.2.7, 43.3.1). From Chapter 22: the densities \(\alpha_\infty\), \(\alpha_p\) of Section 22.2 and Braun’s formula (3). Problem 21.1.4; Problem 10.5.5. Fourier analysis on finite abelian groups; Hensel’s lemma; Schur orthogonality for square-integrable representations [Kn, Ch. IX]. Hermitian forms are linear in the first variable, and \(\lvert\cdot\rvert^2\) is the product with the complex conjugate. In this sheet \(y\) is a vector, \(\omega\) is an isometry, \(\mathrm e(\cdot):=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\cdot}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\), and \(K'\) is a level (not the field \(K\)). In the finite examples \(p\) denotes a prime.

Theorem 47.3.1 bounds the second moment of the Gram operators of Chapter 39 by a bootstrap over fixers. In the nonterminal case the proof splits a maximizing fixer’s reductions according to whether they satisfy the minor condition of Definition 43.2.6. This sheet treats the reductions that satisfy that condition. Let \(\mathcal{D}\) be a set of chosen places above \(p_0\). For each \(w\in\mathcal{D}\), prescribe a nonzero vector in the span of the Bessel tuple modulo either \(w\) or \(\bar w\). Over the open reductions that fix these vectors, the link second moment of the terms with \(\operatorname{disc}\ne0\) is at most \((1+b^{-4})\,b^{-6k\#\mathcal{D}}\,D_{\mathrm{main}}\,\nu^2\) (Theorem 45.3.3). Where does \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\) come from? The open cell here is the part over reductions satisfying Definition 43.2.6. There the head (Definition 45.1.1) and a residual isometry determine a double coset (Section 45.1). For each last entry that contributes, the proof bounds the contributions over each class and vector of the prescribed norm. It then applies Siegel’s formula to the weighted sum over classes and vectors. The heads are summed by Poisson summation, one entry at a time: the zero frequencies give an integral, and the other frequencies are small. The bound has no small instance: in the proof \(\varrho\ge10^5\). The examples use a residual isometry, six squares, hermitian forms over finite fields, and Fourier analysis on \(\mathbb{Z}/5\) and \(\mathbb{Z}/3\).

§45.1. Head, last entry and residual isometry

Let \(V=\bigoplus_{a\le\varrho}Ev_a\oplus X\oplus Ez\oplus\bigoplus_{a\le\varrho}Ev'_a\) be a hermitian space of the shape of Section 39.1, with \(X\) of rank \(k\) and totally positive definite, and \(h(z,z)=-N\). A residual isometry is an isometry \(\omega\) of \(V\) with \(\omega v_a=v_a\) and \(\omega v'_a=v'_a\) for all \(a\). It preserves the orthogonal complement \(X\oplus Ez\) of the \(v_a\), \(v'_a\).

Problem 45.1.1. Let \(\omega\) be a residual isometry, and write \(\omega z=y+c_\omega z\) with \(y\in X\), \(c_\omega\in E\). Then \(\lvert h(z,\omega z)\rvert^2=N^2\lvert c_\omega\rvert^2\) and \[N\,h(y,y)=\lvert h(z,\omega z)\rvert^2-N^2 .\] Hence \(\lvert\tau h(z,\omega z)\rvert\ge N\) at every infinite place \(\tau\), with strict inequality at every \(\tau\) if \(y\ne0\) and equality at every \(\tau\) if \(y=0\).

Now let \(V=Y_0\), with the Bessel tuple and the leading minors \(\langle\gamma\rangle_j\), \(0\le j\le\varrho+1\), of Definition 43.2.1, so that \(\langle\gamma\rangle_0=1\) and \(\langle\gamma\rangle_{\varrho+1}\) is the determinant of the pairing matrix of \(\gamma\), whose entries are the Bessel pairings \(h(\gamma v_b,v_a)\).

Definition 45.1.1. Let \(\gamma\in U(Y_0)(F)\) have \(\langle\gamma\rangle_j\ne0\) for \(1\le j\le\varrho\). Define the head of \(\gamma\) by \(\operatorname{hd}(\gamma):=\bigl(\langle\gamma\rangle_{a+1}/\langle\gamma\rangle_a\bigr)_{0\le a<\varrho}\in(E^\times)^\varrho\). If also \(\langle\gamma\rangle_{\varrho+1}\ne0\), define the last entry of \(\gamma\) by \(\operatorname{le}(\gamma):=\langle\gamma\rangle_{\varrho+1}/\langle\gamma\rangle_\varrho\). A residual isometry of the last floor is a residual isometry of \(Y_0\).

Used in: Chapter 46; Chapter Overview

By Definition 43.2.2, \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)=\lvert\langle\gamma\rangle_\varrho\rvert^2\bigl(\lvert\operatorname{le}(\gamma)\rvert^2-N^2\bigr)\) whenever \(\operatorname{le}(\gamma)\) is defined. In the proof, if \(s\) is open, then every term \(\gamma\) over \((c,s,c')\) has \(\langle\gamma\rangle_j\ne0\) for \(j\le\varrho\). Moreover \(\gamma=n^{-1}\mathfrak w\,\omega\,n'\) with \(n,n'\in\mathcal{U}(F)\), \(\omega\) a residual isometry of \(Y_0\), and \(\mathfrak w\) a Weyl element that exchanges each line \(Ev_a\) with \(Ev'_a\), fixes \(X_0\oplus Ez\) and depends only on \(\operatorname{hd}(\gamma)\). The ratio \(\langle\gamma\rangle_{\varrho+1}/\langle\gamma\rangle_\varrho\) is the \((z,z)\) entry of the pairing matrix of \(\omega\). By Problem 45.1.1 this is nonzero, so the last entry is defined and equals this ratio. Thus \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\) exactly when the \(X_0\)-part \(y\) of \(\omega z\) is nonzero.

Consider a block of the link second moment for \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\) over an open reduction \(s\), and suppose its sum of truncated amplitudes is nonzero. These amplitudes use \(W^\flat\), the Bessel truncation of \(W\) by length (Definition 43.3.1). In the proof one residual isometry \(\omega\) names the link, and the heads of the terms with nonzero truncated amplitude lie in a finite set \(\mathcal E^\varrho\), where \(\mathcal E\subset E^\times\) consists of quotients of two nonzero algebraic integers of \(E\), each with squared absolute value at most \(b^{270(\varrho+1)^2}\) at every infinite place. The block is the sum, over \(\mathfrak h\in\mathcal E^\varrho\), of the sums of the truncated amplitudes of the \(n^{-1}\mathfrak w\,\omega\,n'\) over \((c,s,c')\), for \(n,n'\in\mathcal{U}(F)\), where \(\mathfrak w\) is the Weyl element of \(\mathfrak h\). Different nonzero blocks give different \((c,c',s,\omega)\). So the heads are summed inside the square, and the residual isometries outside it. For each contributing last entry, group the blocks by the class \(c\) and the \(X_0\)-part \(y\) of \(\omega z\). By Problem 45.1.1, these vectors satisfy \(N h(y,y)=\lvert\operatorname{le}\rvert^2-N^2\). A bound for the contribution over each pair \((c,y)\) reduces the estimate to a weighted sum over such pairs.

§45.2. Siegel’s formula for six squares

The smallest rank to which Braun’s formula (3) applies is \(3\), and for \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]^3\) the class set is \(\{I_3\}\) (Problem 21.1.4). For every positive integer \(j\) the mean over the class set is therefore a count: \(r_{I_3}(j):=\#\{y\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]^3:y^*y=j\}\) equals \(\alpha_\infty(j)\prod_p\alpha_p(j)\), with the densities of Section 22.2 for the form \(Q(y)=y^*y\), a sum of six squares on \(\mathbb{Z}^6\). Let \(\chi_{-4}\) be the nontrivial character modulo \(4\).

Problem 45.2.1. By machine, \(r_{I_3}(1)=12\), \(r_{I_3}(2)=60\) and \(r_{I_3}(5)=312\).

Problem 45.2.2. For odd \(p\), the number of \(x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^6\) with \(\sum_ix_i^2=1\) is \(p^5-\chi_{-4}(p)p^2\), and the same holds with \(1\) replaced by any \(j\) prime to \(p\): by machine \(252\), \(3100\), \(16856\) for \(p=3,5,7\). For right-hand side \(1\), by machine there are \(61236=3^5\cdot252\) solutions modulo \(9\), and \(3\cdot2^{5e}/4\) solutions modulo \(2^e\) for \(e=2,3\) (\(768\), \(24576\)). For \(e\ge3\) the count modulo \(2^{e+1}\) is \(2^5\) times the count modulo \(2^e\). Hence \(\alpha_p(1)=1-\chi_{-4}(p)p^{-3}\) for odd \(p\), and \(\alpha_2(1)=3/4\).

For \(p\equiv3\pmod4\), \(y\mapsto y^*y\) modulo \(p\) is the hermitian form \(\sum_iy_i^{p+1}\) on \(\mathbb F_{p^2}^3\).

Problem 45.2.3. Let \(q\) be a prime power. The form \(\sum_{i\le n}x_i^{q+1}\) on \(\mathbb F_{q^2}^n\) has \((q^n-(-1)^n)(q^{n-1}-(-1)^{n-1})\) nonzero isotropic vectors: \(9\), \(27\), \(135\) for \(q=2\) and \(n=2,3,4\). Divided by \(q^{2n-1}\) this is \((1-(-1)^nq^{-n})(1+(-1)^nq^{1-n})\).

Problem 45.2.4. For real \(j>0\), \(\operatorname{vol}\{y\in\mathbb{C}^3:\lvert y\rvert^2\le j\}=\pi^3j^3/6\), so \(\alpha_\infty(j)=\pi^3j^2/2\).

Problem 45.2.5. With \(L(3,\chi_{-4})=\pi^3/32\): \(\frac{\pi^3}2\cdot\frac34\cdot\prod_{p\text{ odd}}\bigl(1-\chi_{-4}(p)p^{-3}\bigr)=12=r_{I_3}(1)\). For \(j=5\) the factor at \(5\) is \(\alpha_5(5)=3224/3125\), the factor at infinity is \(\alpha_\infty(5)=25\pi^3/2\), the other factors are those of \(j=1\), and the product is \(312=r_{I_3}(5)\).

Choose one embedding \(\tau\colon E\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}\) at each infinite place of the CM field \(E\), and let \(f\) be the number of these places. For Weil data, \(E\) contains a primitive \(5040\)th root of unity. There is a \(j_E\in E\) with \(\bar j_E=-j_E\), \(\operatorname{Im}\tau(j_E)>0\) for each chosen \(\tau\), and \((1-j_E^{-1})/2\) integral. A finite idele \(a\) makes \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E\) self-dual for \((x,y)\mapsto\psi_f(j_E a\bar a xy)\), with \(\psi_f\) the finite part of the standard additive character. The splitting character \(\upsilon\) is a unitary Hecke character satisfying \(\upsilon(\bar x)=\upsilon(x)^{-1}\). For an idele \(x\) whose finite part belongs to \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E^\times\), its value is \(\prod_\tau x_\tau/\lvert x_\tau\rvert\). Its value at the chosen uniformizer of each finite place fixed by conjugation is \(-1\). In the chosen measure, \(\operatorname{vol}(\hat{\mathcal O}_E)=2^f\lvert d_E\rvert^{-1/2}\), where \(d_E\) is the discriminant of \(E\).

Theorem 45.2.1 (Siegel’s formula for one vector). Let \(E\) be a CM field with Weil data, \(k\ge3\), \(K'\) an open subgroup of the stabilizer of \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E^k\) in the finite adelic unitary group of \(y^*y\), \(\phi\ge0\) a locally constant, compactly supported, \(K'\)-invariant function on \(\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^k\), and \(\mathfrak n\in E\) with \(\bar{\mathfrak n}=\mathfrak n\) and \(\tau\mathfrak n>0\) at every infinite place. Then each sum \(\sum_{y\in E^k,\,y^*y=\mathfrak n}\phi(g^{-1}y)\) converges, and \[\begin{multline*} \sum_{[g]}\frac{1}{\#\mathrm{Aut}(g)}\sum_{\substack{y\in E^k\\ y^*y=\mathfrak n}}\phi(g^{-1}y)\\ =\Bigl(\sum_{[g]}\frac{1}{\#\mathrm{Aut}(g)}\Bigr)\lvert d_E\rvert^{1/4}\prod_\tau\frac{\pi^k(\tau\mathfrak n)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!} \int\!\!\int\phi(u)\,\psi_f\bigl(\tfrac12\zeta(u^*u-\mathfrak n)\bigr)\,du\,d\zeta , \end{multline*}\] where \([g]\) runs over the classes of level \(K'\), \(u\) over \(\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^k\) with \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E\) of volume \(\operatorname{vol}(\hat{\mathcal O}_E)\) in each coordinate, and \(\zeta\) over the self-adjoint finite adeles, with Haar measure giving the self-adjoint part of the finite adelic codifferent of \(E/\mathbb{Q}\) volume \(1\).

Status: classical; due to Siegel [Si] for quadratic forms and Braun [Br] for hermitian forms, and a case of Weil’s theorem [We65, Thm. 5]. The last integral is the finite adelic density of \(\phi\) at \(\mathfrak n\). For product test functions it factors into local densities with compatible measures. We leave the comparison of measures with Chapter 22 aside. With one class its automorphism weight cancels. With trivial automorphism groups each class has weight one. For a lattice indicator the inner sums count vectors. Problem 45.2.1 illustrates the one-class case of the classical formula over \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\).

In the proof, Theorem 45.2.1 is applied, for each last entry \(\operatorname{le}\) of a contributing block, for which \(\lvert\tau\operatorname{le}\rvert>N\) at every infinite place, to the vectors \(y\in X_0\) with \(N\,h(y,y)=\lvert\operatorname{le}\rvert^2-N^2\), that is at \(\mathfrak n=(\operatorname{le}\,\overline{\operatorname{le}}-N^2)/N\). It is used as an upper bound, with an extra factor \(1+b^{-6}\) from the local densities.

§45.3. Poisson summation, one entry at a time

In the proof, each double-coset sum of Section 45.1 is a Kloosterman sum at finitely many finite places times an orbital integral at the infinite places. Squaring the sum over heads gives a sum over pairs of heads, and the pairs of different heads are kept. Poisson summation in the common next entry gives a zero-frequency sum over all pairs of shorter heads. At positive remaining length, pairs whose last entries differ are bounded using the difference of the inverses of those entries. For a function \(\mathfrak f\) on \(\mathbb{Z}/p\) let \(\hat{\mathfrak f}(l):=p^{-1}\sum_j\mathfrak f(j)\,\mathrm e(-lj/p)\), so that \(\mathfrak f(j)=\sum_l\hat{\mathfrak f}(l)\,\mathrm e(lj/p)\).

Problem 45.3.1. On \(\mathbb{Z}/5\), \(\mathfrak f(j)=2+\cos(2\pi j/5)\) has \(\hat{\mathfrak f}=(2,\tfrac12,0,0,\tfrac12)\). So \(\mathfrak f(0)=3\), while the zero frequency alone gives \(2\).

Problem 45.3.2. Let \(\mathfrak f\) be a function on a finite abelian group \(\mathcal G\), and \(\hat{\mathfrak f}(\psi'):=(\#\mathcal G)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal G}\mathfrak f(j)\overline{\psi'(j)}\) for the characters \(\psi'\) of \(\mathcal G\). Then \(\lvert\mathfrak f(j)\rvert\le\sum_{\psi'}\lvert\hat{\mathfrak f}(\psi')\rvert\) for every \(j\), and \(\hat{\mathfrak f}(\psi_0)\) is the mean of \(\mathfrak f\), for the trivial character \(\psi_0\). If \(\mathfrak f\) is real and \(\sum_{\psi'\ne\psi_0}\lvert\hat{\mathfrak f}(\psi')\rvert\le c\), then \(\mathfrak f(j)\le\hat{\mathfrak f}(\psi_0)+c\).

For \(j\in\mathbb{Z}/p\) let \(\mathrm{Kl}(j):=\sum_{l\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times}\mathrm e\bigl((l+jl^{-1})/p\bigr)\), a Kloosterman sum.

Problem 45.3.3. For \(p=5\) the values \(\mathrm{Kl}(0),\dots,\mathrm{Kl}(4)\) are \[-1,\quad \tfrac{3-\sqrt5}2,\quad -1-\sqrt5,\quad \sqrt5-1,\quad \tfrac{3+\sqrt5}2,\] and \(\sum_j\lvert\mathrm{Kl}(j)\rvert^2=20\).

Problem 45.3.4. Prove \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/p}\lvert\mathrm{Kl}(j)\rvert^2=p(p-1)\) and \(\bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/p}\mathrm e(j(l^{-1}-l'^{-1})/p)\bigr)_{l,l'\in(\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times}=pI_{p-1}\).

The next problem compares operator norms at zero and nonzero frequency.

Problem 45.3.5. Let \(\mathfrak A_l:=\bigl(\mathrm e(ljj'/3)\bigr)_{j,j'\in\mathbb{Z}/3}\). Then \(\mathfrak A_1\mathfrak A_1^*=3\cdot1\), and \(\mathfrak A_0\mathfrak A_0^*\) is the matrix with all entries \(3\). The squared operator norms of \(\mathfrak A_1\) and \(\mathfrak A_0\) are \(3\) and \(9\), respectively. For each integer \(k\ge1\), the squared operator norms of \(\mathfrak A_1^{\otimes k}\) and \(\mathfrak A_0^{\otimes k}\) are \(3^k\) and \(9^k\), respectively.

In the proof, the rank \(k\) of \(X_0\) enters the exponent in the estimate for nonzero frequencies. At each infinite place the bound at a nonzero frequency decays at least like the \((k-(\varrho+1)^7)\)-th power of its inverse size. We do not derive these local bounds here.

Problem 45.3.6. The entrywise product of two positive semidefinite matrices of the same size is positive semidefinite.

Used in: Chapter 46

In the proof, the form on pairs of heads with a common last entry is the entrywise product of positive semidefinite kernels, one at each infinite place and one at the finite places. By Problem 45.3.6 the product kernel is positive semidefinite on these heads. At each stage the sum of the kernel over all pairs with a common entry is real and nonnegative. Nonnegativity is what Theorem 45.3.1 below needs.

Problem 45.3.7. Let \(\pi\) be an irreducible unitary representation of a unimodular group \(\mathcal G'\) (whose left Haar measure is also right invariant) with square-integrable matrix coefficients and formal degree \(d_\pi\), so that \(\int_{\mathcal G'}\langle\pi(g)u_1,u_3\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(g)u_2,u_4\rangle}\,dg=d_\pi^{-1}\langle u_1,u_2\rangle\overline{\langle u_3,u_4\rangle}\). For every vector \(u\) with \(\lVert u\rVert^2=D'\), \[\int_{\mathcal G'}\lvert\langle\pi(g)u,u\rangle\rvert^2\,dg=\frac{D'^2}{d_\pi} .\] For the representation of Problem 10.5.5 and \(D'=1\), this is the reciprocal of the formal degree found there.

Identity (c) of Theorem 40.3.1 at each infinite place \(\tau\) is the relation of Problem 45.3.7 for the single vector \(W_\tau\), whose squared norm is \(\#I_\tau\), with \(d_\pi=d_\tau\). In the proof, at the zero frequency the integral of a form over a common entry is exactly the form of one length less, and the form of length zero has modulus at most \((1+b^{-10})/d_\tau\) at the infinite place \(\tau\). This is the factor \(\prod_\tau1/d_\tau=1/\deg\) of \(c_{\mathrm{bot}}\) below. Poisson summation is used in the following one-sided form.

Theorem 45.3.1 (one-sided Poisson summation). Let \(E\) be a totally complex number field with chosen complex embeddings, \(\psi\) the standard character of \(\mathbb{A}_E/E\), and \(du\) the Haar measure on \(\mathbb{A}_E\) for which \(E\) has covolume \(1\). Let \(\mathfrak f\colon\mathbb{A}_E\to\mathbb{R}\) be nonnegative and integrable, and lower semicontinuous at each point of a finite set \(\mathcal{Z}\subset E\), and put \(\hat{\mathfrak f}(l):=\int\mathfrak f(u)\psi(-lu)\,du\). If \(\sum_{l\in\mathcal{Z}'}\lvert\hat{\mathfrak f}(l)\rvert\le c_{\mathfrak f}\) for every finite \(\mathcal{Z}'\subset E\), then \(\sum_{u\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathfrak f(u)\le c_{\mathfrak f}\).

Used in: Chapter 46

Status: an inequality form of Poisson summation on \(\mathbb{A}_E/E\) [Ta], for functions that need not be continuous. In the proof a bump on the compact quotient is pulled back to the adeles and multiplied by the test function before integration.

Theorem 45.3.2 (the sieve). For the data, fix \(\mathcal{D}\) and the vectors of Theorem 45.3.3. Use the product sieve system of Section 46.4 for this fixer. Use the prefix weights of that section. Complete each rational tuple with entries \(1\), then read it at the finite places of the sieve and in the scaled Bessel tuple at each infinite place, as in Definition 46.2.2. There are real numbers \(\Sigma_0,\dots,\Sigma_{\varrho+1}\), the real parts of the sums of the form over all pairs of length-\(i\) heads in \(\mathcal E^i\) whose last entries agree (for \(i=0\), the real part of the form at the empty pair), and \(\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_\varrho\ge0\), the sums of the weights of the heads of length \(i\) with entries in \(\mathcal E\). They satisfy, for \(0\le i\le\varrho\), \[\Sigma_0\le c_{\mathrm{bot}},\qquad \Sigma_{i+1}\le c_i\bigl(\Sigma_i+2c_{\mathrm{fr}}\lambda_i^2\bigr),\qquad \lambda_i^2\le c_{\mathrm{wt}}\prod_{i'<i}c_{i'} ,\] and for every \(U\subseteq\mathcal E\), \(\Sigma_{\varrho+1}\) bounds the real part of the sum of the top form over all \(t\in U\) and all ordered pairs of heads in \(\mathcal E^\varrho\) completed by \(t\). Here \(c_i>0\) is the constant of Poisson summation in the entry \(i\): \(\operatorname{vol}(\hat{\mathcal O}_E)\) divided by the Jacobian, at the infinite places, of the rescaling that the proof applies to that entry. Further \(c_{\mathrm{bot}}=\prod_\tau\bigl((1+b^{-10})/d_\tau\bigr)\cdot(1+b^{-6})\,b^{-6k\#\mathcal{D}}\), \(c_{\mathrm{fr}}=b^{2002(\varrho+1)^9f}c_{\mathrm{dec}}\), and \(c_{\mathrm{wt}}=b^{400(\varrho+1)^9f}\). Here \(c_{\mathrm{dec}}>0\) is the decay constant of the proof.

Used in: Problem 45.3.8; Chapter 46

Chapter 46 derives the recurrence from the six properties of a sieve system and Poisson summation (Theorem 46.2.1), with one step worked out modulo \(25\) in Problem 46.1.5. Theorem 46.3.1 bounds the sum over frequencies. Section 46.4 applies these results using the local systems, the decay estimate and the bound for the sum of the head weights read from the proof.

Problem 45.3.8. Under Theorem 45.3.2, \(\Sigma_i\le\bigl(\prod_{i'<i}c_{i'}\bigr)\bigl(c_{\mathrm{bot}}+2i\,c_{\mathrm{wt}}c_{\mathrm{fr}}\bigr)\) for \(0\le i\le\varrho+1\).

Let \(C_S:=\lvert d_E\rvert^{1/4}\operatorname{vol}(\hat{\mathcal O}_E)^k\bigl(\pi^k/((k-1)!N^k)\bigr)^f\). The constant of Siegel’s step is \(C_S\) times the number of classes, the order of the reduction of the integral points of \(\mathcal{U}\), and \(\#\bar R_0\) (Definition 39.4.2). For each finite set of nonzero blocks in the link second moment of Theorem 45.3.3, the sum of their squared norms, multiplied by the square of the proof’s normalizing constant, is at most \((1+b^{-6})\) times the constant of Siegel’s step times \(\Sigma_{\varrho+1}\). Apply Problem 45.3.8 with \(i=\varrho+1\). The product of the \(c_i\), of the constant of Siegel’s step and of \(c_{\mathrm{bot}}\) is at most \((1+b^{-9})^2(1+b^{-6})\,b^{-6k\#\mathcal{D}}D_{\mathrm{main}}\nu^2\), after multiplying the right-hand side by that same square. The factors are: \(1+b^{-9}\) for the constants, \((1+b^{-10})^f\le1+b^{-9}\) for the degrees (in the proof \(f\le b/1000\)), and \(1+b^{-6}\) from \(c_{\mathrm{bot}}\). After division by that square, the error inside the outer factor \(1+b^{-6}\) from Siegel’s step is at most \(b^{-10}\cdot b^{-6k\#\mathcal{D}}D_{\mathrm{main}}\nu^2\), because the rank bound and \(b^{11kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}\le D_{\mathrm{main}}\) (Chapter 40) absorb the powers of \(b\) from the error estimate. Since \(b\ge1000\), the following inequality collects these factors.

Problem 45.3.9. For \(b\ge1000\): \((1+b^{-6})\bigl((1+b^{-9})^2(1+b^{-6})+b^{-10}\bigr)\le1+b^{-4}\).

Problems 45.1.1, 45.3.2, 45.3.6, 45.3.7, 45.3.8 and 45.3.9 model the steps of the following theorem. The proof proves it by these steps, with Theorems 45.2.1, 45.3.1 and 45.3.2 and the statements read from the proof in this sheet.

Theorem 45.3.3. For the data, let \(\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_0\) be a set of chosen places above \(p_0\), and for each \(w\in\mathcal{D}\) choose \(w\) or \(\bar w\) and a nonzero vector in the span of the Bessel tuple modulo it, as in Definition 43.2.7; let the fixer of \(\mathcal{D}\) be the fixer of these vectors. Then the link second moment of the terms \(\gamma\) with \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\), over the reductions that are open and lie in the fixer of \(\mathcal{D}\), is at most \[(1+b^{-4})\,b^{-6k\#\mathcal{D}}\,D_{\mathrm{main}}\,\nu^2 .\]

Used in: Theorem 45.3.2; Chapter 47

Status: read from the proof. It applies to any admissible filling over a CM field with Weil data and a family of sided vectors at all the chosen places above the first prime. For the data, extend the family outside \(\mathcal{D}\) by the first member of the Bessel tuple, \(v_1\), at each unused place; the fixer tests only the vectors in \(\mathcal{D}\). The comparison of constants is read from the proof, as are the local bounds used in Chapter 46.

What has been proved.

In the open cell a term has the form \(n^{-1}\mathfrak w(\mathfrak h)\omega n'\) of Section 45.1. The \(X_0\)-part \(y\) of \(\omega z\) satisfies \(N h(y,y)=\lvert\operatorname{le}\rvert^2-N^2\) by Problem 45.1.1. For a contributing block with \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\), this norm is totally positive. Siegel’s formula bounds the resulting weighted sum over classes and vectors of this norm (Theorem 45.2.1, modelled in Problems 45.2.1–45.2.5). The proof applies Poisson summation to one common entry at a time. The zero-frequency integrals give shorter forms, whose length-zero bounds contain the factors \(1/d_\tau\). The rank \(k\) enters the bounds for nonzero frequencies and for unequal last entries (Problems 45.3.1–45.3.7, Theorems 45.3.1 and 45.3.2). Taken as given: Theorems 45.2.1 and 45.3.1, the local estimates used in Chapter 46, and the statements marked “in the proof”.
