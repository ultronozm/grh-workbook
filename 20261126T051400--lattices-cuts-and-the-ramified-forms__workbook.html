From Chapter 39: the Bessel subgroups (Section 39.1). The Poincaré map \(\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\) is that of Definition 39.2.1. The seesaw identity (Theorem 52.4.1) is proved by pairing \(\Theta_{\xi,m}\cdots\Theta_{\xi,1}\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi\) with a form on \(Y_m\) and transferring the Bessel period up the lower chain. Chapter 54 computes what is left after the transfers as a function of the outer variables \((g_k,y_k)\in U(Y_k)(\mathbb{A})\). At stage zero the finite sum is over classes in \(K_M\). At the subsequent stages the Bessel periods must be integrated over the remaining outer variables. Which finite sets of level classes suffice to compute these integrals, and with which weights? These sets are the cuts (Definition 55.3.2). The answer uses the lattices of the finite points and, at one place, the hypothesis \(\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\). The result is Theorem 55.4.1.

§55.1. The first lift

Problem 55.1.1. Let \(y\) and \(z'\) be functions on \([U(Y_0)]\), fixed by right translation by \(K_M\) and by \(K^{Y}_{0}(\xi)\) respectively, with \(y z'\) integrable. Then \[\int_{[U(Y_0)]} y(h)\sum_{c\in K_M/K^{Y}_{0}(\xi)} z'(hc)\,dh=[K_M:K^{Y}_{0}(\xi)]\int_{[U(Y_0)]}y\,z' .\] Hence, for every tame \(\xi\), every \(\varphi\in S\) and every form \(z\) on \(Y_1\) as in Problem 54.2.3, \[\int_{[U(Y_1)]} z\;\Theta_{\xi,1}\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi=[K^{Y\circ}_{0}:K_M]\sum_{c\in K_M/K^{Y}_{0}(\xi)}\ \int_{R_1(F)\backslash U(Y_1)(\mathbb{A})}\mathcal{W}_1\,z ,\] where the summand uses \((g,\tilde c)\) in outer slot \(0\), with \(\tilde c\in K_M\) any representative of \(c\), and the identity in every other slot.

§55.2. Lattices of finite points

The lattice of the finite point enters the support condition. The classes used for the evaluated data are taken modulo \(K^{Y}_{j}(\xi)\). For \(0\le j\le m-1\), write \(X_j\) for the definite summand of \(Y_j\) and \(X_j^\perp\) for the span of its string in the rational basis.

Definition 55.2.1. Let \(B_j\) be the finite component of the adelic frame of Section 37.3. The lattice \(\Lambda(x)\) of \(x\in U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A}_f)\) consists of the finite adelic vectors whose coordinates in the frame \(B_jx\) are integral. In rational coordinates, \(\Lambda(x)=(B_jxB_j^{-1})\Lambda(1)\). Define the string parts of \(\Lambda(x)\) by \(\Lambda^-(x):=\Lambda(x)\cap X_j^\perp\) and \(\Lambda^+(x):=\operatorname{pr}\Lambda(x)\), where \(\operatorname{pr}\) is the orthogonal projection to \(X_j^\perp\). We write \(\Lambda^\pm_j\) when the stage must be named.

Definition 55.2.2. A lattice is graded if it contains the part of each grade of each of its vectors.

As the frame of \(x\) is orthonormal, \(\Lambda(x)\) is self-dual. The standard lattice \(\Lambda(1)\) is graded. At a place \(w\mid\pi_N\), the standard lattice of an odd string is spanned, in the last definite coordinate and the middle string coordinate, by vectors \((1,\epsilon/N)\) and \((0,\epsilon')\) with units \(\epsilon,\epsilon'\). In the toy \(N=5\), the place has degree one, \(\epsilon=\epsilon'=1\). In \(\mathbb{Q}_5^2=\mathbb{Q}_5e_1\oplus\mathbb{Q}_5e_2\) call \(e_1\) the definite coordinate and \(e_2\) the string coordinate, and define \(\Lambda^\pm\) as in Definition 55.2.1 with \(X^\perp=\mathbb{Q}_5e_2\); no form is fixed here.

Problem 55.2.3. For \(\Lambda=B\mathbb{Z}_5^2\), \(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1/5&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\): \(\Lambda^-=\mathbb{Z}_5e_2\), \(\Lambda^+=5^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_5e_2\) and \(\#\Lambda^+/\Lambda^-=5\). For \(\Lambda=D'\mathbb{Z}_5^2\), \(D'=\operatorname{diag}(5,1/5)\): \(\Lambda^-=\Lambda^+=5^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_5e_2\).

Problem 55.2.4. Over a DVR, or a product of two exchanged by the involution, let \(\Lambda\) be a lattice, self-dual for a nondegenerate hermitian form, in a space \(W\perp W'\). Then the dual of \(\Lambda\cap W\) in \(W\) is the projection of \(\Lambda\) to \(W\). Hence, for \(x,x'\in U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A}_f)\) and a finite idele \(\varpi\), \(\varpi\Lambda^-(x)\subseteq\Lambda^-(x')\) implies \(\bar\varpi\Lambda^+(x')\subseteq\Lambda^+(x)\).

Problem 55.2.5. A function on \(\mathbb{Z}/25\) invariant under translation by \(5\mathbb{Z}/25\) has Fourier transform supported on \(5\mathbb{Z}/25\).

Definition 55.2.6. Let \(\varpi_\xi\) and \(\varpi_M\) be the generator ideles fixed in the proof for the ideals \((\pi_M)\prod_{w\in Q_\xi}w\) and \((\pi_M)\); each has component \(1\) away from the places dividing its ideal. Define the idele of the modulus by \(\mathfrak{u}:=\varpi_\xi/\varpi_M\). It is a uniformizer at every \(w\in Q_\xi\) and a unit at every other finite place.

Used in: Chapter 56: Definition 56.3.1

In this sheet \(\mathfrak{a}:=\varpi_W/\bar\varpi_M\), where \(\varpi_W\) is the self-dual scale of the Weil data. Throughout this paragraph let \(0\le j\le m-2\). The finite datum of lift \(j+1\) is fixed by the finite Heisenberg translations by \(\varpi_\xi v\) for every integral vector \(v\) in the finite space of maps from \(Y_j\) to \(Y_{j+1}\). The proof shows: let \(\mathcal{W}_{j+1}(g_\infty,x')\ne0\), so that the integrand defining it (Definitions 53.4.2 and 54.2.1) is nonzero at some \(r\in R_j(\mathbb{A})\), and let \(x\) be the finite part of \(r(g_j,y_j)\); if \(r_1\in R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\) and \(r_2\in R_{j+1}(\mathbb{A}_f)\) make \(\Lambda(r_1x)\) and \(\Lambda(r_2x')\) graded, then \[(\bar{\mathfrak{u}}/\mathfrak{a})\,\mathrm{st}_{j+1}\Lambda^+_j(r_1x)\subseteq\Lambda^-_{j+1}(r_2x') .\] For the standard lattices it shows \(\mathrm{st}_{j+1}\Lambda^+_j(1)=\mathfrak{a}\,\Lambda^-_{j+1}(1)\).

Problem 55.2.7. Use the string matrices \(J_3=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0&1\\0&-5&0\\1&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(J_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(\mathrm{st}_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-5&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) of Chapter 54 (\(N=5\), string constant \(1\)), and let \(\hat{\mathcal{O}}\) be the integral finite adeles of \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\). On the first string let \(L^-=\hat{\mathcal{O}}^3\) and \(L^+=\operatorname{diag}(1,\tfrac15,1)\hat{\mathcal{O}}^3\), on the second \(L'=\hat{\mathcal{O}}^2\). Then \(L^-\) and \(L^+\) are dual for \(J_3\), \(L'\) is self-dual for \(J_2\), and \(\mathrm{st}_1L^+=L'\).

Definition 55.2.8. A point \(x\in U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A}_f)\) has the lattice bounds at stage \(j\) if \(\bar{\mathfrak{u}}^{\,j}\Lambda^-_j(1)\subseteq\Lambda^-_j(x)\) and \(\mathfrak{u}^{\,j}\Lambda^-_j(x)\subseteq\Lambda^-_j(1)\).

Problem 55.2.9. Let \(0\le j\le m-2\), and suppose \(\mathfrak{u}^{\,j}\Lambda^-_j(r_1x)\subseteq\Lambda^-_j(1)\) and \((\bar{\mathfrak{u}}/\mathfrak{a})\,\mathrm{st}_{j+1}\Lambda^+_j(r_1x)\subseteq\Lambda^-_{j+1}(r_2x')\). Then \(\bar{\mathfrak{u}}^{\,j+1}\Lambda^-_{j+1}(1)\subseteq\Lambda^-_{j+1}(r_2x')\).

Definition 55.2.10. \(\Lambda(x)\) is split at a finite place \(w\) if \(1_w\Lambda^+(x)\subseteq\Lambda^-(x)\), where \(1_w\) is the idempotent finite adele that is \(1\) at \(w\) and \(0\) elsewhere.

The proof shows that \(\Lambda^+_j(1)=\Lambda^-_j(1)\) for an even string, and that for an odd string the two differ only in the middle coordinate, by a simple pole at the places above \(N\): at each \(w\mid N\) the quotient is the residue field \(k_w\), as in Problem 55.2.3.

Problem 55.2.11. Let \(1\le j\le m-1\) and \(\bar{\mathfrak{u}}^{\,j}\Lambda^-_j(1)\subseteq\Lambda^-_j(x)\), but \(\mathfrak{u}^{\,j}\Lambda^-_j(x)\not\subseteq\Lambda^-_j(1)\). Then some \(\mathfrak{u}^{\,j}v\), \(v\in\Lambda^-_j(x)\), lies in \(\Lambda^+_j(1)\) but not in \(\Lambda^-_j(1)\). Moreover, the string of \(Y_j\) is odd, \(j\) is even, and \(\Lambda(x)\) is split at a place \(w\) dividing \(\pi_N\).

Problem 55.2.12. For \(z=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\), the scaling of the definite coordinate by a generator of \((\mathbb{Z}/5)^\times\): \(B^{-1}zB=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&0\\-1/5&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) is not integral, and \(D'^{-1}zD'=z\).

In general, if \(\Lambda(x)\) is split at \(w\mid\pi_N\), then \(x^{-1}z_wx\), with \(z_w\) acting as a scalar on \(X_j\) and trivially on the string, is integral and \(1\) off \(w,\bar w\). It belongs to \(K^{Y}_{j}(\xi)\).

Problem 55.2.13. For \(\mathrm{sh}\,e_k=e_{k+1\bmod 4}\) on \(\mathbb{C}^4\): \((1-\mathrm{sh})e_0=(1,-1,0,0)\) and \((1+\mathrm{sh}+\mathrm{sh}^2+\mathrm{sh}^3)(1-\mathrm{sh})e_0=0\).

Problem 55.2.14. Suppose \(\omega^X(z_w,z_w)\) acts trivially on the data of every lift \(X_k\to X_{k+1}\), and \(\omega^X(1,z_w)\), acting on one factor alone, fixes a tensor \(v\) of data. Then the (partial) chain \(\Theta^X_\xi(v)\) satisfies \(\Theta^X_\xi(v)\,T_w=\Theta^X_\xi(v)\), and it vanishes on \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\).

Used in: Chapter 52

For \(w\mid\pi_N\), the diagonal scaling \(\omega^X(z_w,z_w)\) is the identity on every lift: the base changes of the characters of the lifts are unramified at the places above \(N\). For \(0\le i<\varrho\), embed \(U(X_{2i+2})\) into \(U(Y_{2i+2})\) by the identity on the string. If \(y_{2i+2}^{-1}z_wy_{2i+2}\in K^{Y}_{2i+2}(\xi)\), then \(\omega^X(1,z_w)\) on the second tensor factor fixes \(\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_i(\xi,y)\).

Problem 55.2.15. Let \(\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), \(2\le j\le m-1\) even, and \(\Lambda(x)\) split at some \(w\mid\pi_N\). Then \(\mathcal{W}_j(\gamma(g_\infty,x))=0\) for every \(\gamma\in R_j(\mathbb{A})\) and every archimedean point \(g_\infty\).

Problem 55.2.16. Let \(\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), \(y_0\in K_M\), \(1\le j\le m-1\), and \(\mathcal{W}_j(g_\infty,x)\ne0\). Then \(r_2x\) has the lattice bounds at stage \(j\) for every \(r_2\in R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\) with \(\Lambda(r_2x)\) graded.

Used in: Chapter 52

This is where the hypothesis \(\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) enters. We use two facts for the field \(E\) of the data, which contains a primitive \(5040\)th root of unity. (i) Every finite point \(x\) has a translate \(ux\) with \(u\) in the unipotent subgroup of \(R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\) and \(\Lambda(ux)\) graded. This is the lattice form of the Iwasawa decomposition [Ti]. (ii) There is a finite set \(\mathcal Z_j\) of finite points equal to \(1\) outside \(Q_\xi\cup\bar Q_\xi\) such that, if the \(R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\)-orbit of \(x\) contains a graded point satisfying the bounds, then \(rx\in \mathcal Z_jK^{Y\circ}_{j}\) for some \(r\in R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\). Fact (ii) is read from the proof; it is not proved here. Two classical lattice facts used there are that only finitely many lattices lie between two fixed full lattices, and that a lattice is determined by its local components [OM].

§55.3. Cuts and multiplicities

In this sheet let a group \(G_0\) act on a set, let \(G_1\subseteq G_0\) be a subgroup and \(\mathrm{Cut}\) a finite \(G_1\)-stable subset, and put \(\mathrm{mult}(t):=\#\{rG_1\in G_0/G_1 : r^{-1}t\in\mathrm{Cut}\}\), with the value \(0\) for an infinite count. For a finite group acting on a finite set, if the cut meets every orbit, this is the setting of Problem 52.3.2 with translates counted modulo \(G_1\).

Problem 55.3.1. Let \(G_0=H=S_3\) act on \(H/H_0\), \(G_1=H_0:=\langle(1\,2)\rangle\), \(x=(1\,2\,3)\), \(\mathrm{Cut}:=\{h xH_0:h\in H_0\}\subset H/H_0\). Then \(\#\mathrm{Cut}=2\), the multiplicities of the three cosets are \(2,2,2\), and for \(f_1(hH_0):=h(3)\), \[\sum_{t\in H/H_0}f_1(t)=6=\sum_{rH_0\in H/H_0}\ \sum_{c\in\mathrm{Cut}}\frac{f_1(rc)}{\mathrm{mult}(rc)} .\]

Definition 55.3.2. \(\mathrm{Cut}_0(\xi)\) is the set of classes modulo \(K^{Y}_{0}(\xi)\) of the elements of \(K_M\), with \(\mathrm{mult}_0:=1\). For \(1\le j\le m-1\), let \(G_0=R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\) and \(G_1=R_j(\mathbb{A}_f)\cap K^{Y\circ}_{j}\) act on \(U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A}_f)/K^{Y}_{j}(\xi)\); write \(\mathrm{mult}_j\) for the multiplicity above. A cut at stage \(j\) is a finite set \(\mathrm{Cut}_j(\xi)\) of classes such that: (a) it is \(G_1\)-stable; (b) its points are integral at every place not above a prime dividing \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi\); (c) on it \(\mathrm{mult}_j\) divides a power of \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi\); (d) \(\mathrm{mult}_j(x)\ne0\) whenever the \(G_0\)-orbit of \(x\) contains a point with graded lattice and the lattice bounds at stage \(j\). The proof fixes one cut at each stage.

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 56

For \(1\le j<m\) the proof shows that a cut exists by choosing, inside the image of \(\mathcal Z_jK^{Y\circ}_{j}\) modulo \(K^{Y}_{j}(\xi)\), one class in each \(G_0\)-orbit meeting that image, and taking the \(G_1\)-saturation. For \(x\) integral off \(Q_\xi\cup\bar Q_\xi\), such as the points of \(\mathcal Z_jK^{Y\circ}_{j}\), it shows that there is \(n\) with \((xux^{-1})^{(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi)^n}\in K^{Y\circ}_{j}\) for all \(u\in K^{Y}_{j}(\xi)\).

Problem 55.3.3. Let \(C\) meet each \(G_0\)-orbit in at most one point, and \(\mathrm{Cut}=G_1\cdot C\). For \(c\in C\), \(\mathrm{mult}(c)=[\operatorname{Stab}_c:\operatorname{Stab}_c\cap G_1]\) with \(\operatorname{Stab}_c:=\operatorname{Stab}_{G_0}(c)\) (index \(0\) if infinite); and \(\mathrm{mult}(st)=\mathrm{mult}(t)\) for \(s\in G_0\).

Problem 55.3.4. (a) Let \(q,n\) be nonnegative integers. A finite group in which every element has order dividing \(q^n\) has order dividing a power of \(q\). (b) For \(u\equiv1\pmod p\) in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)\) and \(x=\operatorname{diag}(p^{-2},1)\), \(xu^px^{-1}\) is integral; for \(p=3\), \(u=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4&3\\3&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(xux^{-1}\) is not. (c) For the \(G_1\)-saturation of the classes chosen in the construction above, every \(c\) has \(\mathrm{mult}_j(c)\mid(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi)^n\) for some \(n\ge0\).

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 56

Problem 55.3.5. For \(0\le j<m\), if \(Q_\xi=\varnothing\), every finite point representing a class of \(\mathrm{Cut}_j(\xi)\) lies in \(K^{Y\circ}_{j}\), and \(\#\mathrm{Cut}_j(\xi)\le\#\mathrm{GL}_{r+jt}(\mathcal{O}_E/\pi_M)\le M^{f(r+jt)^2}\le M^{fn_m^2}\).

Used in: Chapter 56

§55.4. Summing over the cuts

Fix \(1\le j\le m-1\), and let \(G_0,G_1\) be as in Definition 55.3.2. A level \(K'\) here means a subgroup of \(K^{Y\circ}_{j}\) containing a neighbourhood of the identity.

Problem 55.4.1. Let \(F_\infty\) be a function on \(U(Y_j)_\infty\). There is \(k(F_\infty)\in\mathbb{C}\) with the following property, for every level \(K'\), every finite \(G_1\)-stable set \(\mathrm{Cut}\) of classes modulo \(K'\), every complex function \(h_1\) on \(U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A}_f)/K'\) and every continuous left \(R_j(F)\)-invariant function \(f_1\) on \(U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A})\), integrable on \(R_j(F)\backslash U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A})\), with \(\mathrm{mult}(x_f)\ne0\) wherever \(f_1(x)\ne0\). If \(\int_{[R_j]}f_1(rx)\,dr=[K^{Y\circ}_{j}:K']\,F_\infty(x_\infty)\,h_1(x_fK')\) whenever \(x_fK'\in\mathrm{Cut}\), then \[\int_{R_j(F)\backslash U(Y_j)(\mathbb{A})}f_1=k(F_\infty)\sum_{c\in\mathrm{Cut}}\frac{h_1(c)}{\mathrm{mult}(c)} .\]

Problem 55.4.1 uses quotient integration in stages [Bou, Ch. VII, §2]; Status: classical. Problems 55.1.1 and 55.4.1, with Problem 54.2.3 and Problem 55.2.16 for the support, give the following by induction. Fix \(g,\lambda\), a stage \(0\le j\le m-1\), and the grading of \(Y_{j+1}\) used in the stage function. For every complex function \(F_\infty\) of the tuple of archimedean outer points there is \(c_j(F_\infty)\in\mathbb{C}\) with the following property. Let \(\xi\) be tame, \(\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), \(z\) a form on \(Y_{j+1}\) as in Problem 54.2.3, and \(S_1\) a complex function of the tuple of finite level classes. Suppose that for every tuple \((g_k,y_k)_k\) with \(y_0\in K_M\) and \(y_m=1\), the integral of \(\mathcal{W}_{j+1}z\) over \(R_{j+1}(F)\backslash U(Y_{j+1})(\mathbb{A})\) equals \(F_\infty((g_k)_k)\,S_1((y_kK^{Y}_{k}(\xi))_k)\). Then the integral of \(z\,\Theta_{\xi,j+1}\cdots\Theta_{\xi,1}\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi\) over \([U(Y_{j+1})]\) equals \[c_j(F_\infty)\sum_{c_0\in\mathrm{Cut}_0(\xi),\dots,c_j\in\mathrm{Cut}_j(\xi)}\frac{S_1(c_0,\dots,c_j,1,1,\dots)}{\prod_{k=0}^j\mathrm{mult}_k(c_k)} ,\] where each \(1\) denotes the identity class at its stage.

For interlacing highest weights, restriction from \(U(n+1)\) to \(U(n)\) has multiplicity one [GW, Thm. 8.1.1]; Status: classical. For the weights and integral Weyl modules of \(X_m,Y_m\), the proof fixes an integer matrix whose complex span is the space of intertwiners for the block embedding.

Problem 55.4.2. Let \(l\) be a linear form on the value space of the algebraic forms on \(X_m\) of the prescribed weight. There is \(c(l)\in\mathbb{C}\) with the following property, for every such form \(\psi\) and every \(h'\) with \(h'(g_\infty,x)=\langle v^+,\rho(g_\infty^{-1})P'(x)\rangle\) for an algebraic form \(P'\) on \(Y_m\) of the weight of the lower chain. Identify \(Y_m\) with the complement of the last basis vector of \(X_m\). If \(F'(k)\) is the coefficient of \(\psi\) at \(l\), evaluated at \(\operatorname{diag}(k,1)\), for every \(k\in U(Y_m)(\mathbb{A})\), then \[\int_{[U(Y_m)]}F'\,\overline{h'}=c(l)\int_{[U(Y_m)]}(\mathrm{res}\psi)\,\overline{h'} ,\] where \(v^+\) is the highest-weight vector, \(\rho\) the action on the value space, the product is conjugate-linear in the first variable, and on the right \(\mathrm{res}\psi\) is read in the same way.

Definition 55.4.3. Define the seesaw tensor of a tame character \(\xi\) by \[\mathrm{ss}_\xi:=\sum_{c_0\in\mathrm{Cut}_0(\xi),\dots,c_{m-1}\in\mathrm{Cut}_{m-1}(\xi)}\Bigl(\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac1{\mathrm{mult}_j(c_j)}\Bigr)\,\bigl(\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_\varrho(\xi,c)\otimes\dots\otimes\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_0(\xi,c)\bigr),\] with \(c_m:=1\), in the order of Chapter 54; the evaluated data do not depend on the representatives of the classes.

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 56

In the proof \(\Theta^\top_{\xi,j+1}\) preserves the forms of Problem 54.2.3, and both sides below have the shape of \(h'\) in Problem 55.4.2, where pairing with such \(h'\) is nondegenerate. So Problems 55.1.1, 55.2.16, 55.4.1 and 55.4.2, with Theorem 54.4.1 and the facts quoted, prove the following.

Theorem 55.4.1. Let \(\lambda\) be a linear form on the values of source forms and \(g\) a point of the archimedean unitary group of \(Y_0\). There is \(c'\in\mathbb{C}\), possibly zero, such that for every tame character \(\xi\) and every \(\varphi\in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) \[\Theta_{\xi,m}\cdots\Theta_{\xi,1}\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi=c'\,\mathrm{res}\,\Theta^X_\xi(\mathrm{ss}_\xi)\varphi ,\] an equality of automorphic forms on \(Y_m\), with \(\mathrm{res}\) read in automorphic forms.

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 56
