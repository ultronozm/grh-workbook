Prerequisites.

From Chapter 21: the lattices \(I_n\) over \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), the groups \(\Gamma_L\), forms of weight \((a)\) and their norm (Sections 21.1 and 21.3), and Problems 21.1.2 and 21.1.4. From Chapter 23: the lifts \(\Theta_{(a)}\), the moments \(c_a\), and Problems 23.1.4, 23.1.5, 23.2.1, 23.2.3, 23.2.4, 23.2.5, 23.4.2 and 23.5.1. From Chapter 39: the finite Poincaré map of Section 39.3, the Poincaré maps \(\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\) and \(\mathcal{P}\) (Definition 39.2.1), and Theorem 39.4.2. From Chapter 49: the normalized translates \(A_s\) (Definition 49.3.1). From Chapters 36 and 37: the data, and the spaces, forms and twists, in the notation fixed there. Inner products of forms on definite lattices are linear in the first variable.

In the proof, Theorem A concerns lifts of the normalized translates \(A_s\) along the chain \(Y_0 \to \dots \to Y_m\), whose first space is indefinite, while the estimates of size and divisibility are proved along the chain \(X_0 \to \dots \to X_m\) of definite spaces. How does the proof pass between the chains? For each twist, the seesaw identity expresses the lift along the first chain of the Poincaré image of a ramified form \(\varphi\) as one nonzero constant times a combination of lifts of \(\varphi\) along the definite chain, restricted to \(Y_m\). This chapter states the identity. Chapters 53–56 develop the four theorems and the properties of the seesaw tensor used in its deduction, each from statements read from the proof that are listed there. We first study an analogue for one lift between lattices of ranks one and two.

§52.1. A Poincaré map between definite lattices

We use the finite Poincaré map of Chapter 39, Section 39.3. Let \(X := \mathcal{O}e_1\) and \(Y := I_2 = X \perp \mathcal{O}e_2\) (in Section 23.4 the line \(X\) is called \(Y\)). By Problem 21.1.4 a form of weight \((a)\) on \(I_n\), \(n \le 2\), is a single vector of \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^n)\) fixed by \(\Gamma_{I_n}\), which we identify with the form; the inner product of forms is \(\langle\psi,\psi'\rangle = \lvert\Gamma_{I_n}\rvert^{-1}\langle\psi,\psi'\rangle_{\operatorname{Sym}}\), with \(\langle\,,\rangle_{\operatorname{Sym}}\) the inner product of \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^n)\). Let \(0 \le j \le a\) be integers divisible by \(4\), and let \(\varphi_j \in \mathcal{A}_{(j)}(1)\) be the form with value \(e_1^j\). For \(\varphi \in \mathcal{A}_{(j)}(1)\) let \[P\varphi := \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_Y / \Gamma_{e_2}} \gamma\,\bigl(\varphi\, e_2^{a-j}\bigr) \in \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2) ,\] where \(\Gamma_{e_2}\) is the stabilizer of \(e_2\); it is the map \(P\) of Chapter 39 with \(\jmath(\varphi) = \varphi\,e_2^{a-j}\). By Problem 39.2.1, \(\langle\psi, P\varphi\rangle\) is the period \(\lvert\Gamma_X\rvert^{-1}\langle\psi, \varphi\,e_2^{a-j}\rangle_{\operatorname{Sym}}\) of \(\psi \in \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2)\) along \(X\), that of Section 23.4; and by Problem 39.3.1, \(P\varphi_j = 4\bigl(e_1^j e_2^{a-j} + e_1^{a-j} e_2^{j}\bigr)\).

§52.2. One lift

Now lift \(P\varphi\) from \(Y\) to a lattice \(L' = I_{n'}\), \(n' \in \{1,2\}\), with standard basis \(e'_1, \dots, e'_{n'}\); write \(\mathcal{A}_{(d)}(L') := \mathcal{A}_{(d)}(n')\). For \(x \colon Y \to L'\) write \(x_k := x e_k\), and also view \(x_1\) as the map \(\mathbb{C}e_1 \to L'_{\mathbb{C}}\), \(e_1 \mapsto x_1\). Put \[\mathrm{th}_{L'} := \sum_{v \in L'} v^{a-j}\, e^{-\pi h(v,v)} \in \operatorname{Sym}^{a-j}(L'_{\mathbb{C}}) ,\] the vector obtained from the line \(\mathcal{O}e_2\) alone. Products are taken in the symmetric algebra, as in Chapter 21.

Problem 52.2.1. For \(x = (x_1, x_2)\) and \(\varphi \in \operatorname{Sym}^j(\mathbb{C}e_1)\), \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(x)(\varphi\, e_2^{a-j}) = \bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^j(x_1)\varphi\bigr)\, x_2^{a-j}\).

Problem 52.2.2. For \(\varphi \in \mathcal{A}_{(j)}(1)\), with \(\Theta_{(a)} \colon \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2) \to \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(L')\) and \(\Theta_{(j)} \colon \mathcal{A}_{(j)}(1) \to \mathcal{A}_{(j)}(L')\) the lifts of Chapter 23, \[\Theta_{(a)}(P\varphi) = \bigl(\Theta_{(j)}\varphi\bigr)\, \mathrm{th}_{L'} .\]

Used in: Chapter 54

The right side lifts \(\varphi\) from the smaller space \(X\), with the kernel multiplied by the fixed vector \(\mathrm{th}_{L'}\).

Problem 52.2.3. \(\mathrm{th}_{L'}\) is fixed by \(\Gamma_{L'}\). For \(L' = I_1\), \(\mathrm{th}_{I_1} = c_{a-j}\, {e'_1}^{a-j}\) and \(\Theta_{(a)}(P\varphi_j) = \tfrac14 c_j c_{a-j}\, {e'_1}^a\), in agreement with Problem 23.2.5. For \(L' = I_2\), \(a = 8\) and \(j = 4\), \[\Theta_{(8)}(P\varphi_4) = \tfrac14 c_0^2 c_4^2\,\bigl({e'_1}^8 + 2 {e'_1}^4 {e'_2}^4 + {e'_2}^8\bigr) .\]

Problem 52.2.4. For \(\psi \in \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(L')\), let \(\Theta'_{(a)}\psi \in \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2)\) be its lift back to \(Y\). Then \[\bigl\langle \Theta'_{(a)}\psi,\ P\varphi \bigr\rangle = \bigl\langle \psi,\ (\Theta_{(j)}\varphi)\, \mathrm{th}_{L'} \bigr\rangle .\] For \(L' = I_1\) this is Problem 23.4.2.

§52.3. Chains, weighted sums, and translates of the vacuum

Problem 52.3.1. In this problem take \(a = 8\) and \(j = 4\). Let \(\Theta^{(2)}\) be the composite \(\mathcal{A}_{(8)}(2) \to \mathcal{A}_{(8)}(1) \to \mathcal{A}_{(8)}(2)\) of two lifts. Then \(\Theta^{(2)}P\varphi_4 = \Theta_{(8)}\bigl((\Theta_{(4)}\varphi_4)\,\mathrm{th}_{I_1}\bigr) = \tfrac1{16} c_4^2\bigl(c_0c_8\, {e'_1}^8 + 70\, c_4^2\, {e'_1}^4 {e'_2}^4 + c_0c_8\, {e'_2}^8\bigr)\).

Used in: Chapter 54

By Problem 23.5.1, the kernel of \(\Theta^{(2)}\) is the sum over the middle classes of the products of the two weight-eight kernels, each divided by the order of the middle stabilizer. In general the data of a chain of lifts form a tensor, with one factor for each lift, and the seesaw produces a particular tensor.

The general proof pairs both sides with a form on \(Y_m\) and transfers the Bessel period along the lower chain one lift at a time (Chapter 53). The finite variables in the resulting outer integrals are summed over finite sets of cosets of a level, the cuts, with reciprocal multiplicities as weights (Chapter 55). Here is a finite model for those weights.

Problem 52.3.2. Let a finite group \(G_0\) act on a finite set \(\mathcal X\), let \(\mathrm{Cut} \subset \mathcal X\) meet every orbit, and put \(\mathrm{mult}(y) := \#\{\gamma \in G_0 : \gamma^{-1}y \in \mathrm{Cut}\}\). Then for every function \(f \colon \mathcal X \to \mathbb{C}\), \[\sum_{y \in \mathcal X} f(y) = \sum_{\gamma \in G_0}\, \sum_{c \in \mathrm{Cut}} \frac{f(\gamma c)}{\mathrm{mult}(\gamma c)} .\] If \(f\) is \(G_0\)-invariant, the right side is \(\lvert G_0\rvert \sum_{c \in \mathrm{Cut}} f(c)/\mathrm{mult}(c)\); if \(\mathrm{Cut}\) has exactly one point in each orbit, then \(\mathrm{mult}(c) = \lvert\operatorname{Stab}_{G_0}(c)\rvert\) for \(c \in \mathrm{Cut}\). For \(G_0 = (\mathbb{Z}/5)^\times\) acting on \(\mathcal X = \mathbb{Z}/5\) by multiplication, \(\mathrm{Cut} = \{0,1,2\}\) and \(f = 1\), the multiplicities are \(4, 2, 2\) and both sides equal \(5\).

Used in: Chapter 55

In the proof the sums are formed for a tame character \(\xi\) (Definition 55.3.2), and each multiplicity that occurs divides a power of \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi\) (Problem 55.3.4).

The data for the definite chain in the seesaw are finite sums of tensors built from translates of the integral vacuum of each lift (Definition 52.4.2). In the simplest local model the vacuum is the characteristic function \(\mathbf 1_{\Lambda_0}\) of a lattice \(\Lambda_0 \subset \mathbb{Q}_p^n\), and the elementary translation operators are the shifts \(f(v) \mapsto f(v-x)\) and the multiplications \(f(v) \mapsto \chi(v)f(v)\) by additive characters \(\chi\) of \(\mathbb{Q}_p^n\).

Problem 52.3.3. Let \(\Lambda \subset \Lambda_0 \subset \mathbb{Q}_p^n\) be lattices with \([\Lambda_0 : \Lambda] = p^e\). Then \(\mathbf 1_{\Lambda_0} = \sum_{x \in \Lambda_0/\Lambda} \mathbf 1_{x + \Lambda}\), and \(\mathbf 1_\Lambda = p^{-e} \sum_{\chi} \chi\, \mathbf 1_{\Lambda_0}\), where \(\chi\) runs over one extension to \(\mathbb{Q}_p^n\) of each of the \(p^e\) characters of \(\Lambda_0/\Lambda\). For every lattice \(\Lambda'\) commensurable with \(\Lambda_0\) (their intersection has finite index in both), \(\mathbf 1_{\Lambda'}\) is a finite \(\mathbb{Z}[1/p]\)-linear combination of shifts of functions \(\chi\mathbf 1_{\Lambda_0}\), where the additive characters \(\chi\) of \(\mathbb{Q}_p^n\) may depend on \(\Lambda'\).

§52.4. The seesaw identity

We use the field \(E\), the levels \(M = p_0p_1\) and \(N\), the elements \(\pi_M\) and \(\pi_N\), the spaces \(X_j\) and \(Y_j\), the source forms \(S \supseteq S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), tame characters \(\xi\) and twists \(\vartheta\) with their moduli \(Q\), and the characters \(\mu_j^0\) of the lifts, as in Chapter 37. Recall \(\varrho = m/2 - 1\) (Chapter 36).

Definition 52.4.1 (the lower chain). For \(0 \le j \le m\) let \(K^{Y\circ}_j := K^\circ(Y_j)\) be the standard lattice stabilizer of \(Y_j\) (Section 37.3) and, for a tame character \(\xi\), \(K^Y_j(\xi) \subseteq K^{Y\circ}_j\) its subgroup of elements that reduce to the identity at the places dividing \(\pi_M\) and at those in \(Q_\xi\); \(K_M\) is the principal level of \(Y_0\) (Section 37.4), so that \(K^Y_0(\xi) \subseteq K_M \subseteq K^{Y\circ}_0\). Let \(R_0 = \mathcal U \rtimes U(X_0)\) be the Bessel subgroup of \(Y_0\) and \(\psi_{\mathcal U}\) its character (Chapter 39). The Bessel function \(\mathcal{W}_{\lambda,\varphi}\) of a source form \(\varphi\) formed with a linear form \(\lambda\), and the Poincaré map \(\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\colon S\to H(Y_0)\) for a point \(g\) of the archimedean unitary group of \(Y_0\), are those of Definition 39.2.1. Its defining identity is \(\langle \mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi, y\rangle = \int_{[R_0]} \mathcal{W}_{\lambda,\varphi}(r)\,\overline{y(r(g,1))}\,dr\) for \(y\in H(Y_0)\), with the Petersson product linear in the first variable, as in Chapter 39. Let \(\upsilon\) be as in Section 37.7. For a tame character \(\xi\) and \(1 \le j \le m\), lift \(j\) of the lower chain, \(\Theta_{\xi,j}\), goes from forms on \(Y_{j-1}\) to forms on \(Y_j\). Its Weil representation \(\omega_j\), of \(U(Y_{j-1})(\mathbb A) \times U(Y_j)(\mathbb A)\) on the data on \(\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{j-1}, Y_j)\), uses on each of the two spaces, of rank \(n\), the character \(\mu_j^0\xi^j\upsilon^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\). Its datum \(\phi_j(\xi)\) is the product of the joint harmonic at infinity for the parameters of \(Y_{j-1}\) and \(Y_j\) and the finite datum \(\mathbf 1_j(\xi)\), given at the places dividing \(\pi_M\) and at those in \(Q_\xi\) by the indicator of the reduction of the inclusion matrix and at the other finite places by the vacuum. The lift is the theta lift with this datum multiplied by the index \([K^{Y\circ}_{j-1} : K^Y_{j-1}(\xi)]\). The composite \(\Theta_{\xi,m}\cdots\Theta_{\xi,1}\), followed by the passage to algebraic forms on \(Y_m\), is the lower chain \(\Theta_\xi\) of Chapter 37.

Used in: Chapter 54

The proof checks that this datum sends forms of the prescribed source type to forms of the prescribed target type.

Definition 52.4.2 (the definite chain). A finite datum for a lift is its vector in the finite part of the corresponding Weil representation (Section 59.3). Let \(\mathcal{T}\) be the set of tuples \(\mathfrak t = (\mathfrak t_j)_{j \ge 0}\) of matrices \(\mathfrak t_j\) of finite adeles of \(E\), with rows indexed by the basis of \(X_{j+1}\) and columns by that of \(X_j\). Write \(\mathrm{Heis}(\cdot)\) for the finite Heisenberg operators on the finite data on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\). The translated vacuum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) is the integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\) (Section 37.8) translated by \(\mathrm{Heis}(\mathfrak t_j)\). For a tame character \(\xi\), \(1 \le j \le m\) and a finite datum \(v\) on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_{j-1}, X_j)\), \(\Theta^X_{\xi,j}(v)\) is lift \(j\) of the definite chain: the algebraic theta lift from \(X_{j-1}\) to \(X_j\), of the weights of Definition 37.2.1. Its kernel uses the rank twists of \(\mu_j^0\xi^j\) on the two spaces and the matrix of Schur jets of the jet series of the datum at the scale \(\pi_M c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), with \(c_{\mathrm{jet}}\) the jet scale of Definition 59.4.3; the integral over the source quotient uses Haar measure on \(U(X_{j-1})(\mathbb A_f)\) normalized by \(\operatorname{vol}(K^X_{j-1})=1\). The lift is linear in \(v\), and lift \(j\) uses data on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_{j-1}, X_j)\), such as \(v_{j-1}(\mathfrak t)\). For \(j \ge 0\), \(\omega^X_j\) is the finite Weil action of \(U(X_j)(\mathbb A_f) \times U(X_{j+1})(\mathbb A_f)\) on these data for the tame character \(\xi\) (written \(\omega^X\) when the lift is clear), and \(K^X_j\) is the level of \(X_j\): the integral finite adelic unitary matrices that reduce to the identity at every \(w \in \mathcal M\) and have last row congruent to \((0, \dots, 0, 1)\) modulo \(\pi_N\); \(K^X_0\) is the level of the source forms. A pair tensor for pair \(i\) \((0 \le i \le \varrho)\) is a tensor of finite data on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_{2i}, X_{2i+1})\) and on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_{2i+1}, X_{2i+2})\). For pair tensors \(\mathsf T_0, \dots, \mathsf T_\varrho\), \[\Theta^X_\xi(\mathsf T_\varrho \otimes \dots \otimes \mathsf T_0)\] is the composite of the \(\varrho + 1\) double lifts on the smooth algebraic forms of the specified weight on \(X_0\), with values in forms on \(X_m\), pair \(i\) acting through \(v \otimes v' \mapsto \Theta^X_{\xi,2i+2}(v') \circ \Theta^X_{\xi,2i+1}(v)\), extended linearly; in a tensor over pairs, later pairs stand on the left.

Used in: Chapter 54; Chapter 57: Definition 57.3.3; Chapter 59; Chapter 65

Definition 52.4.3 (gluing, restriction, the composite lift). For \(0 \le i \le \varrho\) the pair gluing \(\mathrm{Gl}_i\), for the double lift \(X_{2i} \to X_{2i+1} \to X_{2i+2}\), sums over the vectors \(y\) of residues modulo \(N\), each represented in \(\{0, \dots, N-1\}\), indexed by a basis of \(X_{2i+1}\) and the places \(w \mid \pi_N\): it translates the first factor by the last column \(-y/N\) supported at these places, and the second by the conjugate last row supported at their conjugates. Define the glued vacuum tensor of \(\mathfrak t \in \mathcal{T}\) by \(\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t) := \mathrm{gl}_{\varrho+1}(\mathfrak t)\), where \[\mathrm{gl}_0(\mathfrak t) := 1 \in \mathbb{C}, \qquad \mathrm{gl}_{i+1}(\mathfrak t) := \mathrm{Gl}_i\bigl(v_{2i}(\mathfrak t)\otimes v_{2i+1}(\mathfrak t)\bigr) \otimes \mathrm{gl}_i(\mathfrak t) \quad (0\le i\le\varrho),\] and for a finitely supported \(\Psi \colon \mathcal{T}\to \mathbb{C}\) the glued tensor is \(\Psi^{\mathrm{gl}} := \sum_{\mathfrak t} \Psi(\mathfrak t)\,\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\). Choose an integral branching map as in The size of the composite lift. The restriction \(\mathrm{res}\) takes an algebraic form on \(X_m\) to one on \(Y_m\) by evaluating at \(\operatorname{diag}(h,1)\), applying that map and multiplying by the scalar \(b_N\) below. The weight calculation in the proof gives \[b_N = \prod_\tau \tau(\bar\pi_N)^{m\Delta/2 - a_\tau},\] where \(\tau\) is read as the embedding of the CM type and \(a_\tau = a\) for \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), \(a_\tau = 0\) otherwise. The same letter \(\mathrm{res}\) is used for the corresponding map of automorphic forms. For every tame character \(\xi\) we define the composite lift by \[B_{\xi,\Psi}(\varphi) := \mathrm{res}\;\Theta^X_\xi\bigl(\Psi^{\mathrm{gl}}\bigr)\varphi \qquad (\varphi \in S).\]

Used in: Chapter 57: Definition 57.3.3; Chapter 65

Definition 52.4.4 (chain data). A finitely supported \(\Psi \colon \mathcal{T}\to \mathbb{C}\) is a chain datum for the twist \(\vartheta\) if: (i) for \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of \(\Psi\), every entry of every \(\mathfrak t_j\) becomes integral, after multiplication by \(\bar\pi_M\), at every finite place \(w\) not above a prime dividing \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and vanishes at every \(w\) not above a prime dividing \(M\,\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\); (ii) every value \(\Psi(\mathfrak t)\) lies in the ring \(\mathcal{O}^{[\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta]}\) of numbers integral away from \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\) (Chapter 37); (iii) if \(Q_\vartheta = \emptyset\), then \(\sum_{\mathfrak t} \lvert\sigma(\Psi(\mathfrak t))\rvert \le M^{3fmn_m^2}\) for every field automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\mathbb{C}\); (iv) \(\Psi^{\mathrm{gl}}\) is fixed by \(\bigotimes_j \omega^X_j(b_j, b_{j+1})\), formed for the twist \(\vartheta\), for all \(b_j \in K^X_j\) \((j < m)\) and \(b_m = 1\).

Used in: Chapter 65; Chapter Overview

Theorem 52.4.1 (the seesaw identity). Let \(D\) be admissible data, \(\lambda\) a linear form on the values of the source forms, and \(g\) a point of the archimedean unitary group of \(Y_0\). There is \(c \in \mathbb{C}^\times\) such that for every twist \(\vartheta\) there is a finitely supported \(\Psi \colon \mathcal{T}\to \mathbb{C}\) which is a chain datum for \(\vartheta\) and satisfies \[\Theta_\vartheta\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi\bigr) = c\, B_{\vartheta,\Psi}(\varphi) \qquad\text{for every } \varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}} .\]

For fixed \(D\), \(\lambda\), \(g\), one scalar \(c\) works for all twists, and for each twist one \(\Psi\) works for all \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\); the equality is one of algebraic forms on \(Y_m\). For the pair \((g,\lambda)\) that defines \(\mathcal{P}\) (Definition 39.2.1) the proof fixes one such scalar, the seesaw constant \(c_{\mathrm{ss}}\); it enters the normalized translates \(A_s\) of Definition 49.3.1.

For the chosen \(\mathcal{P}\) and a ramified source form \(\varphi\), the two routes are \[\begin{array}{ccccc} \varphi & \xrightarrow{\mathcal{P}} & \mathcal{P}\varphi & \xrightarrow{\Theta_\vartheta} & \Theta_\vartheta(\mathcal{P}\varphi) \\ \Vert &&&& \Vert \\ \varphi & \xrightarrow{\Theta^X_\vartheta(\Psi^{\mathrm{gl}})} & \Theta^X_\vartheta(\Psi^{\mathrm{gl}})\varphi & \xrightarrow{c_{\mathrm{ss}}\mathrm{res}} & c_{\mathrm{ss}}B_{\vartheta,\Psi}(\varphi). \end{array}\]

Chapters 53–56 develop the proof of Theorem 52.4.1, along the lines of Section 52.2, from statements read from the proof that each of them lists. Both sides are paired with a form \(x\) on \(Y_m\). Under the integral, the transposed lifts act successively on \(\bar x\), of conjugate type, as suggested by Problem 52.2.4. Theorem 53.4.1 transfers the resulting Bessel period through one lift. Theorem 54.4.1 takes the transfers two at a time: after each pair, the function left on the definite group is a coefficient of the definite chain with evaluated pair data. Theorem 55.4.1 sums the finite variables over the cuts of Definition 55.3.2, with weights as in Problem 52.3.2: there is \(c' \in \mathbb{C}\), possibly zero, such that for every tame character \(\xi\) and every \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) \[\Theta_{\xi,m}\cdots\Theta_{\xi,1}\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi = c'\,\mathrm{res}\,\Theta^X_\xi(\mathrm{ss}_\xi)\varphi ,\] an equality of automorphic forms on \(Y_m\), with the seesaw tensor \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\) of Definition 55.4.3. Write \(c_{\mathrm{res}} := M^{fn_m^2}\). By Theorem 56.5.1 there is \(c_{\mathrm{gl}} \in \mathbb{C}\), depending only on the data, such that for every tame \(\xi\) one has \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi = c_{\mathrm{gl}}\sum_{\mathfrak t} r_\xi(\mathfrak t)\,\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\). Only finitely many coefficients are nonzero. On their support every \(\bar\pi_M\mathfrak t_j\) is integral away from \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi\), and every \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m} r_\xi(\mathfrak t)\) lies in \(\mathcal{O}^{[\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi]}\). If \(Q_\xi = \emptyset\), then \(\sum_{\mathfrak t} \lvert\sigma(r_\xi(\mathfrak t))\rvert \le c_{\mathrm{res}}^m\) for every field automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\mathbb{C}\). Take \(u\) as in Problem 52.5.5 for \(c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}/c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}\). For a twist \(\vartheta\), the support reduction and joint invariance used in Problem 56.6.3 give a chain datum \(\Psi\) with \(\Psi^{\mathrm{gl}} = u c'\mathrm{ss}_\vartheta\). Passage to algebraic forms gives the theorem with \(c = u^{-1}\).

§52.5. Ramified forms, and what is not claimed

Where does the proof use the hypothesis that \(\varphi\) is ramified?

Problem 52.5.1. Let \(n \ge 1\) be an integer and let \(F\) be a linear endomorphism of a finite-dimensional complex vector space \(V\) with \(F^n = 1\). Then \(\ker\bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} F^i\bigr) = \operatorname{im}(1 - F)\), the sum of the eigenspaces of \(F\) for eigenvalues \(\ne 1\). For the cyclic shift on \(\mathbb{C}^n\) this space has dimension \(n - 1\).

Used in: Chapter 65

Problem 52.5.2. Let \(n \ge 1\) be an integer, and let \(F_1, \dots, F_l\) be commuting linear endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional complex vector space \(V\) with \(F_k^n = 1\) for every \(k\). Then \(\bigcap_k \ker\bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} F_k^i\bigr) = \operatorname{im}\prod_k (1 - F_k)\).

Used in: Problem 52.5.4; Chapter 55; Chapter 65

Problem 52.5.3. \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) is the image of \(\prod_{w \mid \pi_N}(1 - T_w)\) acting on \(S\).

Used in: Chapter 49; Chapter 51; Chapter 65: Problem 65.1.1

Problem 52.5.4. In the situation of Problem 52.5.2, let \(\Lambda \colon V \to V'\) be linear with \(\Lambda F_k = \Lambda\) for one \(k\). Then \(\Lambda\) vanishes on \(\bigcap_k \ker\bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} F_k^i\bigr)\).

Used in: Chapter 55

The proof uses Problem 52.5.4 for chains of lifts (Problem 55.2.14): if the Weil action of \((1, z_w)\) on one lift factor alone fixes the tensor of data of a chain, the chain annihilates \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\). With the initial finite point in \(K_M\), this vanishing gives the lattice bounds of Problem 55.2.16 at every stage \(1\le j<m\), for the graded translates specified there; at any point where the stage function is nonzero, the orbit of the finite component under the finite adelic Bessel subgroup therefore meets the cut (Definition 55.3.2(d)).

Theorem 52.4.1 asserts that \(c \ne 0\), but not that either side is nonzero, for any \(\varphi\) or any twist.

\(e_1^j e_2^{a-j}\) is the symmetrization of \(e_1^{\otimes j} \otimes e_2^{\otimes(a-j)}\), and \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(x)\) is the restriction of \(x^{\otimes a}\).

52.2.2

Problems 23.1.4 and 52.2.1; \(h(x,x) = h(x_1,x_1) + h(x_2,x_2)\), and \(\Gamma_{e_2} = \{\operatorname{diag}(u,1) : u \in \mu_4\}\) has the order of \(\Gamma_X\).

52.2.3

Substitute \(v \mapsto \gamma v\). For the values use Problems 23.2.3, 23.2.4 and 23.2.1, and \(c_k = 0\) for \(4 \nmid k\).

52.2.4

Problems 23.1.5 and 52.2.2.

§52.3

Apply the second lift to both sides of Problem 52.2.2; then Problems 52.2.3 and 23.2.4.

Orthogonality of the characters of the finite group \(\Lambda_0/\Lambda\). Characters of \(\Lambda_0\) extend to \(\mathbb{Q}_p^n\). For a general lattice \(\Lambda'\), use \(\Lambda_0 \cap \Lambda'\).
