Chapter 59 uses Theorem 17.5.1 to bound the jets of standard data along products of minors; its estimates for Schur entries and for the kernel then give the gauge bound in the first part of input (iii) of Section 57.3 of Chapter 57. At a non-split prime, Theorem 17.5.1 takes a series \(F\) in the normal form with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1)-\gamma_p\). That is, \(F(\varpi z) = W(z)\exp\bigl(\frac{\varpi^{p+1}}{p}\sum_{e \in I_p^\circ}\sum_{j \ge 0}\lambda_{ej}\Gamma_e^{p^j}\bigr)\), where \(W\) is integral and \(\Gamma_e = \sum_{\tau,i} z_{\tau i}^{p^e}z_{[-p^e]\tau,i}\). At a non-split prime, why do these couplings occur in the jet series of a standard datum of denominator \(1\) at scale \(c\,c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), \(\lvert\eta c\rvert \le 1\)? We see it first for one CM curve and one pair of places at \(p = 5\), and then state the theorem of the proof.

In this sheet \(\mathrm{th}\) is the theta function of a CM lattice (below), \(g := -\log \mathrm{th}\), and \(c_\varpi:= \varpi^{p+1}/p\) for \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\); \(\lambda_{ej}\) are always the coefficients of a normal form.

§60.1. One curve

Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be prime, \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), and \(\Lambda\), \(t\), \(a_1\) a CM lattice as in Section 19.3, of area \(A_\Lambda\). Write \(\sigma_\Lambda\) for the odd theta function with \(\sigma_\Lambda'(0) = 1\) for the form \(H_\Lambda(w, z) = (\pi/A_\Lambda)\bar w z\) and the multiplier equal to \(1\) on \(2\Lambda\) and \(-1\) elsewhere (it differs from Weierstrass’ sigma function by a factor \(e^{cz^2}\)), and put \(\mathrm{th}(z) := \exp(-H_\Lambda(t, z))\,\sigma_\Lambda(z+t)/\sigma_\Lambda(t)\). Read its Taylor series through a ring map \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\). We use four facts from the proof [GL]: \(\mathrm{th}\) is even; \(g'' = g''(0) + \wp(z+t) - \wp(t)\); \(\eta(\sqrt{-d})\, g''(0)\) is integral; and \(\wp(z+t) - \wp(t)\) lies in the ring \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[y]]\) of series \(G(y(z))\), \(G\) integral, which is closed under differentiation. Status: read from the proof.

Our curve is \(\mathcal C\colon y^2 + xy = x^3 - x^2 - 2x - 1\), of \(j\)-invariant \(-3375\), with complex multiplication by the integers of \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})\) [ATAEC, App. A, §3], and \(t\) is the point \((2,-1)\).

Problem 60.1.1. (i) \(\#\mathcal C(\mathbb{F}_5) = 6\), and \(t\) has order \(2\). (ii) For the lattice of the differential \(dx/(2y + x)\), \(\wp(t) = 7/4\) and \(g_2 = 35/4\); after scaling the lattice by \(7^{1/4}\), \(\wp(t) = \sqrt7/4\), \(g_2 = 5/4 = 12\wp(t)^2 - 4\) and \(a_1^2 = 3\sqrt7\). (iii) Neither \(\omega t\) nor \(\bar\omega t\) lies in the lattice, \(\omega = (1+\sqrt{-7})/2\).

Used in: Chapter 61

From now on, for \(\mathcal C\), the lattice is the one scaled by \(7^{1/4}\).

Problem 60.1.2. \(\wp_t(z) := \wp(z+t) - \wp(t)\) satisfies \(\wp_t'' = 6\wp_t^2 + a_1^2 \wp_t + 2\). For \(\mathcal C\), \(\wp_t = z^2 + \tfrac{\sqrt7}{4} z^4 + \tfrac38 z^6 + O(z^8)\), so \([z^4]g = 1/12\) and \([z^6]g = \sqrt7/120\).

Now let \(p\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\). The proof writes \(\mathrm{th}(\varpi z)\) as an integral series times one exponential.

One curve [GL]. There are \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\) with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\), integral series \(\widetilde W\), \(\mathcal{E}\) with \(\mathcal{E}(0) = 0\), and \(\lambda_{ej} \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\) with \[\mathrm{th}(\varpi z) = \widetilde W(z)\exp\bigl(c_\varpi\,\mathcal{E}(z)\bigr), \qquad \mathcal{E}\equiv \sum_{e \in I_p^\circ}\sum_{j \ge 0} \lambda_{ej}\,(z^{p^e} z)^{p^j} \pmod{\pi_p};\] if \(\chi_K(p) = -1\), the \(\lambda_{e0}\), \(e\) odd, are units. Moreover \(G(\varpi' z)\) is integral for \(G \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[y]]\) and \(\operatorname{val}\varpi' \ge 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\). Status: read from the proof.

How the proof gets it (inert \(p\)). The image of the proof’s \(\mathcal{E}\) in \(\mathbb{C}_p[[z]]\) is \(-c_\varpi^{-1} g_\flat(\varpi z)\), with \(g_\flat\) the part of \(g\) in the degrees \(\equiv p+1 \pmod{p^2-1}\), and all \(\lambda_{ej}\) are equal. The proof chooses \(\varpi\) so that \(y_\varpi := \mathsf T(\varpi z)/\varpi\), where \(\mathsf T\) is the part of \(y\) in the degrees \(\equiv 1 \pmod{p^2-1}\), is integral and \(y_\varpi \equiv \sum_{j \ge 0} z^{p^{2j}} \pmod p\), and proves separately that \(\mathcal{E}\equiv \lambda_{10}\, y_\varpi^{p+1} \pmod p\). Status: read from the proof. The next problem determines the support modulo \(p\).

Problem 60.1.3. Let \(p\) be odd and \(y_\varpi \in z + z^2\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[z]]\) with \(y_\varpi \equiv \sum_{j \ge 0} z^{p^{2j}} \pmod p\). Then \(y_\varpi^{p+1} \equiv \sum_{e \text{ odd}} \sum_{j \ge 0} (z^{p^e}z)^{p^j} \pmod p\), each pair \((e, j)\) occurring once. For \(p = 5\) the degrees up to \(200\) are \(6, 30, 126, 150\).

Problem 60.1.4. Let \(\chi_K(p) = -1\). With the \(\mathcal{E}\) of the proof, \([z^{p+1}]\mathcal{E}= -p\,[z^{p+1}]g \equiv \lambda_{10} \pmod{\pi_p}\). For \(\mathcal C\) and \(p = 5\) this is \(-\sqrt7/24\), a unit.

Problem 60.1.5. If \(\operatorname{val}a \ge 1/(p-1)\) and \(G\) is integral in any number of variables with \(G(0) = 0\), then \(\exp(aG)\) is integral. Hence \(\exp(c_\varpi\pi_p G)\) is integral if \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\), and in the one-curve factorization one may replace \(\mathcal{E}\) by \(\mathcal{E}+ \pi_p G\) for every \(G \in z\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[z]]\).

In Section 60.2 let \(\mathrm{th}\) be the theta function of the scaled curve \(\mathcal C\).

§60.2. One pair of places

Problem 17.5.2(i) exhibited a two-place coupling after a change of variable in a pairing on a formal group of height two. Here they come from one variable and linear forms, through a congruence between the \(p^e\)-th power at a place and conjugation at its partner.

The model: \(p = 5\), \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})\) and \(E = K(\mathrm{i})\), with its two embeddings \(\tau_1, \tau_2\) extending the fixed embedding of \(K\), \(\tau_1(\mathrm{i}) = \mathrm{i}\), \(\tau_2(\mathrm{i}) = -\mathrm{i}\). As \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K) \simeq (\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^\times\) and \(-5^e \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the partner \([-5^e]\tau\) of each place is the other place. The model is an analogue, not an instance: the parameters require \(18480\mid q_0\) (Definition 36.1.1) and \(q_1=q_0T(s)\) (Section 36.2), so \(5 \mid q_1\); every prime factor of \(q_1\) splits in \(K\) (Section 17.5), so \(\chi_K(5) = 1\) for every \(K\) of the proof. Variables \(z_1 = z_{\tau_1}\), \(z_2 = z_{\tau_2}\); so \(\Gamma_e = z_1^{5^e} z_2 + z_2^{5^e} z_1\), as in the two-place model of Chapter 58. For \(u \in E\) let \(u^\dagger z := \eta(\overline{\tau_1(u)})\, z_1 + \eta(\overline{\tau_2(u)})\, z_2\), and let \(u_1 := (1+\mathrm{i})/2\), \(u_2 := (1-\mathrm{i})/2\), \(\mu_l := u_l^\dagger z\).

Problem 60.2.1. \(\sum_{l=1}^2 \tau(u_l)\overline{\sigma(u_l)} = [\tau = \sigma]\) for \(\tau, \sigma \in \{\tau_1, \tau_2\}\). Hence \(\mu_1 = \tfrac{1-\mathrm{i}}{2}z_1 + \tfrac{1+\mathrm{i}}{2}z_2\), \(\mu_2 = \tfrac{1+\mathrm{i}}{2}z_1 + \tfrac{1-\mathrm{i}}{2}z_2\) satisfy, as complex forms (without \(\eta\)), \(\lvert \mu_1\rvert^2 + \lvert \mu_2\rvert^2 = \lvert z_1\rvert^2 + \lvert z_2\rvert^2\) on \(\mathbb{C}^2\), and \(\sum_l \tau(u_l)\, \mu_l = z_\tau\) for both places.

For the complex forms \(\mu = (\mu_1, \mu_2)\), the product \(\mathrm{th}(\mu_1)\mathrm{th}(\mu_2)\) is therefore, by Problem 60.2.1 (Parseval), a theta function on the lattice \(\mu^{-1}(\Lambda^2)\) whose hermitian form is \((\pi/A_\Lambda)(\bar w_1 z_1 + \bar w_2 z_2)\). Section 60.3 factors the jet series of a standard datum into a power of a product of translated theta functions and a series in the parameters.

Problem 60.2.2. Let \(\mathcal{O}_{E,(5)} := \mathbb{Z}_{(5)}[\mathrm{i}, \omega]\), \(\omega = (1+\sqrt{-7})/2\), the ring of elements of \(E\) integral at the primes above \(5\). For \(\beta \in \mathcal{O}_{E,(5)}\) and odd \(e\), \(\overline{\tau_1(\beta)}^{\,5^e} - \tau_2(\beta)\) and \(\overline{\tau_2(\beta)}^{\,5^e} - \tau_1(\beta)\) lie in \(5\,\tau_2(\mathcal{O}_{E,(5)})\) and \(5\,\tau_1(\mathcal{O}_{E,(5)})\). For \(e = 2\) and \(\beta = \sqrt{-7}\) the first fails.

Problem 60.2.3. For odd \(e\), \(\mu_1^{5^e} \mu_1 + \mu_2^{5^e} \mu_2 \equiv \Gamma_e \pmod 5\). The congruence also holds for even \(e\). For \(e = 1\) the exact identity is \(\mu_1^6 + \mu_2^6 = -\tfrac32(z_1^5 z_2 + z_1 z_2^5) + 5 z_1^3 z_2^3\).

Problem 60.2.4. \(\mathcal{E}(\mu_1) + \mathcal{E}(\mu_2) \equiv \sum_{e \text{ odd}}\sum_{j \ge 0} \lambda_{ej}\,\Gamma_e^{5^j} \pmod 5\), with \(\mathcal{E}\), \(\lambda\) of the one-curve statement for \(p = 5\).

Problem 60.2.5. For every series \(H = G(y(\mu_1), y(\mu_2))\) with \(G\) integral, \(\mathrm{th}(\mu_1)\mathrm{th}(\mu_2)H\) has the normal form for the odd \(e\), the transposition of the two places, and the \(\varpi\), \(\lambda\) of the one-curve statement.

Let \(F := \mathrm{th}(\mu_1)\mathrm{th}(\mu_2)\).

Problem 60.2.6. The part of degree \(6\) of \(\log F\) is \(\tfrac{\sqrt7}{80}(z_1^5 z_2 + z_1 z_2^5) - \tfrac{\sqrt7}{24} z_1^3 z_2^3\). Moreover \(\partial_1^5\partial_2 F(0) = \tfrac32\sqrt7 - 5g''(0)\), a unit at \(5\), and \(\partial_1^6 F(0) = 0\). By machine, \([z_1^{28}]F\) is a rational number of valuation \(-1\).

How does the count turn this into divisibility? For jet orders \(a, w \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) (as in Chapter 58) write \[\mathcal{D}(a, w) = \sum_{e \in I_p}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{a}{p^e} - w\Bigr)_+ + \Bigl(\frac{w}{p^e} - a\Bigr)_+\Bigr].\]

Problem 60.2.7. For \(F = \mathrm{th}(\mu_1)\mathrm{th}(\mu_2)\), \(p = 5\) and \(a + w \ge 1\), \(\operatorname{val}(\partial_1^a\partial_2^w F(0)) \ge \mathcal{D}(a, w) - 2(1 + \log_5(a+w))\). Moreover \(\mathcal{D}(126, 0) = 126\gamma_5 = 105/4\) and \(\mathcal{D}(125, 1) = 24\): moving one derivative to the partner variable lowers the predicted divisibility from \(105/4\) to \(24\), and the bound at \((125, 1)\) is about \(16\). By machine, \(\partial_1^{125}\partial_2 F(0)\) is a polynomial in \(g''(0)\) whose coefficients have valuations \(25\) and \(28\).

§60.3. The general statement

Now fix the parameters \(q_0\), \(d\) of Chapter 36, an integer \(s \ge 0\), a prime \(p\) and a ring map \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\). Let \(E\), \(\Phi\), \(q_1\) be those of Chapter 36 for \(s\), let \(\mathrm{th}\) be the theta function of the CM curve of \(K\) fixed in the proof (a CM lattice as in Section 19.3, with lattice the \(\Omega_0\mathcal{O}_K\) of Definition 59.4.3), \(\mathcal I\) a finite set, and the variables \(z_{\tau i}\), \((\tau, i) \in \Phi \times \mathcal I\). We write \(\tau_e := [-p^e]\tau\). In this sheet \(m_l\) are multiplicities (the number of lifts does not occur).

Definition 60.3.1. A resolution of the identity on \(E^{\mathcal I}\) is a finite family \((u_l, m_l)_{l \in \mathcal L}\) of vectors \(u_l \in E^{\mathcal I}\) and integers \(m_l \ge 0\) such that \(\sum_{l} m_l\,\tau(u_{l,i})\,\overline{\sigma(u_{l,i'})} = [\tau = \sigma][i = i']\) for all \(\tau, \sigma \in \Phi\) and \(i, i' \in \mathcal I\). For \(u \in E^{\mathcal I}\), \(u^\dagger z := \sum_{(\tau, i)} \eta(\overline{\tau(u_i)})\, z_{\tau i}\); and \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[y(u_l^\dagger z) : l \in \mathcal L]]\) is the set of series \(G\bigl((y(u_l^\dagger z))_{l \in \mathcal L}\bigr)\) with \(G\) integral.

Used in: Chapter 61: Definition 61.1.1

Standard data as theta products [GL]. Let \(V\) be a standard positive definite space over \(E\) with coordinates \(\mathcal I\) (Section 37.3); standard data and jet series on \(V\) are defined as in Definitions 59.4.1 and 59.4.3 with \(V\) in place of \(\mathrm{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\). Let \(\delta \in \mathcal{O}_K\), \(\delta \ne 0\), and \(\varphi\) standard of denominator \(\delta\). There are a resolution \((u_l, m_l)_{l \in \mathcal L}\) of the identity, with \(\alpha_0^{-1}u_l\) integral at the primes above \(p\) for the finite idele \(\alpha_0\) of the Weil data, and \(H \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[y(u_l^\dagger z) : l \in \mathcal L]]\), such that the jet series of \(\varphi\) at scale \(\bar\delta\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt d}/\Omega_0\), read through \(\eta\), is \(\bigl(\prod_l \mathrm{th}(u_l^\dagger z)^{m_l}\bigr)^{\delta\bar\delta} H\). The coefficients of \(\eta(c_{\mathrm{per}})\, u_l^\dagger z\) are integral; if \(p \nmid q_1\), the \(u_l\) are integral at the primes above \(p\) and \(\lvert\eta(c_{\mathrm{per}})\rvert = 1\). Status: read from the proof; it uses tight frames (families satisfying the weighted identity of Definition 60.3.1 up to a positive scalar) and finite Fourier inversion for theta functions [Mu].

Problem 60.3.1. Let \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(e \in I_p^\circ\), \(\tau \in \Phi\), and \(\beta \in E\) integral at the primes above \(p\). Then \(\eta(\overline{\tau(\beta)})^{p^e} \equiv \eta(\tau_e(\beta)) \pmod{\pi_p}\).

Problem 60.3.2. If \(F\) has the normal form for \((S, r, \varpi, \lambda)\) and \(c' \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\), then \(F(c' z)\) has the normal form for \((S, r, \varpi, \lambda')\) with \(\lambda'_{ej} = c'^{\,(p^e+1)p^j}\lambda_{ej}\).

For integrality at every prime, including \(p = d\), the scale carries a factor \(d\).

Problem 60.3.3. Let \(\mathfrak J\) be the subring of \(\mathbb{C}_p[[z]]\) generated by the series \(G(y(dz))\), \(G\) integral, and by \(\mathrm{th}(dz)\) and \(g'(dz)\), for any CM lattice and any prime \(p\). Then \(\mathfrak J\) is closed under differentiation, and its elements have integral constant terms.

Used in: Chapter 61

Problem 60.3.4. If a subring of a power series ring over \(\mathbb{C}_p\) in finitely many variables is closed under all partial derivatives and its elements have integral constant terms, then \(Q(\partial)F(0)\) is integral for each of its elements \(F\) and each polynomial \(Q\) over \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\). So \(d^n\mathrm{th}^{(n)}(0)\) is integral for all \(n\), for every CM lattice. For \(\mathcal C\) and \(p = 5\), \(\mathrm{th}^{(6)}(0) = -15g''(0)^3 + 30g''(0) - 6\sqrt7\) is integral, while \([z^6]\mathrm{th}\) has valuation \(-1\).

Theorem 60.3.2. Fix \(q_0\), \(d\), \(s\), a prime \(p\) and \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\) as above.

  1. If \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), there are \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\) with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\) and \(\lambda = (\lambda_{ej})_{e,j \ge 0}\) in \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}\), with \(\lambda_{e0}\) a unit for every odd \(e\) if \(\chi_K(p) = -1\), such that for every finite set \(\mathcal I\), every resolution of the identity \((u_l, m_l)_{l \in \mathcal L}\) on \(E^{\mathcal I}\) with all coordinates of all \(u_l\) integral at the primes above \(p\), and every \(H \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_p}[[y(u_l^\dagger z) : l \in \mathcal L]]\), the series \(\bigl(\prod_{l} \mathrm{th}(u_l^\dagger z)^{m_l}\bigr) H\) has the normal form for \((I_p^\circ, ([-p^e]\cdot)_e, \varpi, \lambda)\).

  2. If \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(V\) is a standard positive definite space over \(E\), \(\varphi\) a standard datum of denominator \(1\) on \(V\), and \(c \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\lvert\eta c\rvert \le 1\), then the jet series of \(\varphi\) at scale \(c\,c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), read through \(\eta\), has the normal form for \((I_p^\circ, ([-p^e]\cdot)_e, \varpi, \lambda)\) for some \(\varpi\) with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p-1) - \gamma_p\) and some integral \(\lambda\).

  3. For every standard positive definite space \(V\) over \(E\), if \(\delta \in \mathcal{O}_K\), \(\delta \ne 0\), and \(\varphi\) is standard of denominator \(\delta\) on \(V\), then every jet \(Q(\partial)F(0)\), \(Q\) a polynomial with coefficients in \(\mathcal{O}_\eta\), of the jet series \(F\) of \(\varphi\) at scale \(\bar\delta d\,c_{\mathrm{jet}}\) is integral at \(\eta\).
