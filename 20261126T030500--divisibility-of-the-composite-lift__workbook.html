For the application to Theorem A, we show that for every prime \(p\) and every ring map \(\eta\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}_p\) there is a submodule of the integral ramified source forms on which \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p)-\operatorname{err}(p)\), with the gain of Section 57.1, for every twist \(\vartheta\) with \(p\nmid\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\) and every chain datum \(\Psi\) for it. Its colength divides \(p^l\) for an integer \(0\le l\le\operatorname{err}(p)D_X\), where \(D_X=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S\). For every finite set \(A\) of natural numbers, \(\sum_{p\in A}\operatorname{err}(p)\log p\le U=fa\log^3 b\). We follow the four cases of the proof.

§57.1. The gain at a prime

We use \(q_0\), \(d\), \(K\), \(\chi_K\), \(q_1\), \(E\), \(\Phi\), \(f\), \([\nu]\) and the partner \([-n]\sigma\), \(\Phi_a\), \(\Phi_b\), the weight \(\kappa\) and \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}\) as in Chapter 36; recall that \(a/x_1 \le \kappa(\sigma) \le b \le a\) and that \(q_1\) is \(q_0\) times a product of powers of \(2\) and \(3\). For a natural number \(p\), let \(c_p = 1/2\) if \(p = d\) and \(c_p = 1\) otherwise, and let \(I_p\) be the set of integers \(e \ge 1\) such that \(p = d\), or \(\chi_K(p) = -1\) and \(e\) is odd. For \(y \in \mathbb R\) and \(\sigma \in \Phi\), with \(z_+ = \max(z, 0)\), define the local prime sum and the gain by \[s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(y, \sigma) = c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} \Bigl(\frac{y}{p^e} - \kappa([-p^e]\sigma)\Bigr)_+ , \qquad \operatorname{gain}(p) = m\Bigl(\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(a, \tau) + k \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi} s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\sigma), \sigma)\Bigr) .\] The first sum records the \(a\) columns of height one at each \(a\)-place; the second counts the local term at \(\kappa(\sigma)\) with multiplicity \(k\) at every place.

Problem 57.1.1. Every prime dividing \(q_1\) splits in \(K\). Hence a prime \(p \ne d\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) is prime to \(q_1\), and \([-p^e]\) is the place of the class of \(-p^e\).

Used in: Chapter 62; Chapter 78

Problem 57.1.2. Let \(p \ne d\) be inert in \(K\), \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), and suppose that \(\kappa([-p^e]\tau) = w\) for every positive odd integer \(e\). Then \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(a, \tau) = \sum_{e \ge 1,\ e\ \mathrm{odd}} (a/p^e - w)_+\), the first sum of Theorem 16.3.1 without the integer parts. Evaluated at \(p = 13\), \(a = 13^4\) and \(w = 1\), the sum is \(2208\).

Problem 57.1.3. \(\operatorname{gain}(p) = 0\) if \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) or \(p \ge x_1\).

Used in: Chapter 66

Problem 57.1.4. Let \(p\) be prime and \(y \le a\). The terms of \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(y, \sigma)\) with \(\nu\) a power of \(p\) add up to \(2\, s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(y, \sigma) \log p\).

Used in: Chapter 66; Chapter 79

Let \(\Pi\) be the set of primes \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\). By Problems 57.1.4 and 57.1.3, summing over primes gives \(2\sum_{p \in \Pi} \operatorname{gain}(p)\log p = m\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) + mk \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\tau), \tau)\); the contribution of the powers of \(p\) to the right-hand side is \(2\operatorname{gain}(p)\log p\).

§57.2. Collisions

In this section \(p\) is a prime. For a set \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), a collision outside \(\Phi'\) is a pair \((\tau, e)\) with \(e \in I_p\) and \(\tau, [-p^e]\tau \in \Phi_a \smallsetminus \Phi'\); define the collision sum by \[C_p(\Phi') = \sum_{(\tau, e)\ \text{collision outside}\ \Phi'} \frac{a}{p^e} .\] Let \(o_p\) be the order of the image of \([-p]\) in \(({\mathbb{Z}}/q_0)^\times\), and \(r_p\) the least integer \(h \ge 0\) with \(m \le p^h\). The prime \(p\) is exceptional if \(\chi_K(p) = -1\), \(p \not\equiv \pm 1 \pmod m\), and \(o_p\) is odd with \(o_p \le r_p\). Define the local loss allowance at \(p\) by \[L_p = f(\log b)^2\, \frac{a}{p} \cdot \begin{cases} m & \text{if } m \mid p - 1 \text{ or } m \mid p + 1,\\ 1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

Problem 57.2.1. Let \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) and let \((\tau, e)\) be a collision outside some \(\Phi'\). Then \(p \ne d\), \(e\) is odd, and \(o_p \mid e\); in particular \(o_p\) is odd.

Problem 57.2.2. Let \(q_0 = 18480\) and let \(p\) be a prime not dividing \(q_0\). If \(o_p\) is odd, then \(p \equiv -1 \pmod{240}\) and \(o_p \in \{1, 3, 5, 15\}\). For \(d = 138599\), the inert prime \(13\) has \(o_{13} = 20\). Among the primes inert in \({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-138599})\), the least with \(o_p\) odd is \(479\), with \(o_{479} = 15\), and the least with \(o_p = 1\) is \(55439\).

Problem 57.2.3. Let \(o \ge 0\) be an integer. If every collision \((\tau, e)\) outside \(\Phi'\) has \(e \ge o\), then \(C_p(\Phi') \le 2fa/p^o\).

From Chapter 36 we use \((\log b)^2 \ge 15\) and \(m \ge 2\).

Problem 57.2.4. Let \(p < x_1\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) that is not exceptional. Then \(m\, C_p(\varnothing) \le L_p\).

Problem 57.2.5. Let \(p\) be exceptional with \(o_p = 1\), and let \(h_0 = \#({\mathbb{Z}}/q_0)^\times\). Then \(\#\Phi_a = f/h_0\) and \(m\, C_p(\varnothing) \ge m f a/(h_0 p)\), which exceeds \(L_p\) when \(m > h_0(\log b)^2\).

Used in: Chapter 62

So at an exceptional prime the local loss allowance need not cover the collisions; Section 57.4 obtains the valuation bound there on a submodule.

§57.3. The chain of lifts

Fix a prime \(p\) and a ring map \(\eta\colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_p\); \(\mathcal O_\eta\) is the ring of Chapter 37.

Definition 57.3.1. The gauge \(\lVert\omega\rVert_g\) of a Weyl vector \(\omega\) at a point \(g\) of the finite adelic group, and the phrases “a form has valuation at least \(c\)” and “a form is integral”, at \(p\) and \(\eta\), are those of Definition 37.5.1.

Used in: Chapter 80

With \(S \supseteq S_\mathrm{ram}\) the source forms and the ramified ones (Chapter 37), let \(S_\eta \subseteq S\) be the \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-module of integral source forms and \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta} = S_\eta \cap S_\mathrm{ram}\).

The translations \(\mathfrak t = (\mathfrak t_j)_{j \ge 0} \in \mathcal T\) and the translated vacua \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) are those of Definition 52.4.2.

Definition 57.3.2. For a twist \(\vartheta\), a finitely supported \(\Psi\colon \mathcal T\to {\mathbb{C}}\) is a weak chain datum for \(\vartheta\) if: for every \(\mathfrak t\) in its support and every \(j\), the entries of \(\bar{\pi}_M\mathfrak t_j\) are integral at every finite place not dividing \(N \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\); every value \(\Psi(\mathfrak t)\) is integral under every ring map \({\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_\ell\) for every prime \(\ell \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\); if \(Q_\vartheta = \varnothing\), then \(\sum_{\mathfrak t} \lvert\sigma(\Psi(\mathfrak t))\rvert \le \exp(x^{60})\) for every automorphism \(\sigma\) of \({\mathbb{C}}\); and \(\sum_{\mathfrak t} \Psi(\mathfrak t) \bigotimes_{j < m} v_j(\mathfrak t)\) is fixed by the levels of \(X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}\).

Used in: Chapter 66; Chapter Overview

Definition 57.3.3. For a tame character \(\xi\) and a finitely supported \(\Psi\colon\mathcal T\to{\mathbb{C}}\), the restricted chain is \(R_{\xi,\Psi}:=\mathrm{res}\circ\bigl(\sum_{\mathfrak t}\Psi(\mathfrak t)\,\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_{m-1}\cdots\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_0\bigr)\) on \(S\), where \(\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_j\) is the definite lift from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\) of Definition 52.4.2, built from \(\xi\) with datum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\), and \(\mathrm{res}\) is the restriction to \(Y_m\) of Definition 52.4.3. We use this map for a twist \(\vartheta\) and a weak chain datum for \(\vartheta\). In this chapter and in Chapters 59, 62, 65 and 66, the lift from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\) carries its source index \(j\ge0\). The chain uses \(0\le j<m\), corresponding to lifts \(1,\dots,m\) of Chapter 52.

Used in: Chapter 65; Chapter 66; Chapter Overview

The proof supplies the following expansion [GL]. We use it to estimate valuations away from \(N\).

Theorem 57.3.4. For every twist \(\vartheta\) and every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) there is a weak chain datum \(\Psi'\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi = R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}\varphi\) for all \(\varphi \in S\).

Used in: Chapter 66

We use three inputs. In (i) and (ii), let \(\Psi'\) be a weak chain datum for \(\vartheta\) and suppose \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\).

  1. If \(p \ne N\) and \(p \ne d\), then \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}\) maps \(S_\eta\) to integral forms.

  2. Let \(p \ne N\). For every \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of \(\Psi'\), every \(j\ge0\) and every \(\delta\in\mathcal O_K\) such that \(\delta=\bar\pi_M\) or \(p\nmid M\), the datum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) is standard of denominator \(\delta\) (Definition 59.4.1). In particular, one may take \(\delta=1\) when \(p\nmid M\). If \(\rho_0, \rho_1, \dots\) are real numbers such that, for every \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of \(\Psi'\) and every \(j \ge 0\), the datum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) multiplies gauges by at most \(p^{-\rho_j}\) (Definition 59.4.2, for the lift \(j\) built from \(\vartheta\)), then \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}\) maps \(S_\eta\) to forms of valuation at least \(\sum_{j<m} \rho_j\).

  3. Let \(\mathcal D_j(\Phi')\), for \(j \ge 0\) and \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), be the stage bound of Definition 59.1.1. By Theorem 59.4.4: if \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), then for every twist, every \(j \ge 0\) and every standard datum \(\varphi\) of denominator \(1\) on \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\), \(\varphi\) multiplies gauges by at most \(p^{-\mathcal D_j(\varnothing)}\); and if \(p < x_1\) and \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), then for every \(0 \le j < m\) and every \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), \[\mathcal D_j(\Phi') \ge \frac{\operatorname{gain}(p)}{m} - C_p(\Phi') + c_p\, \#(\Phi_a \cap \Phi')\,\frac{a}{x_1} - \frac{fa(\log b)^2}{mp} .\]

Inputs (i) and (ii) are read from the proof. Chapter 59 proves the first part of (iii) from statements (II)–(IV) of Section 59.4 and the bound on jets along products of minors, Theorem 17.5.1, applied to the jet series of a standard datum. It proves the second part by comparing the predicted divisibility of the diagram of the lift with the prime sums that define \(\operatorname{gain}(p)\). The stage bound credits each erased box with \(\gamma_p := c_p\sum_{e \in I_p} p^{-e}\), which is \(p/(p^2 - 1)\) at an inert prime and \(1/(2d - 2)\) at \(p = d\) (Problem 58.1.1). A prime \(p < x_1\) divides neither \(M\) nor \(N\), since \(p_0, p_1 \ge b^{10}\) and \(N > M\) (Chapter 36).

Problem 57.3.1. Let \(\mathcal V_0, \dots, \mathcal V_m\) be finite-dimensional \({\mathbb{C}}_p\)-spaces with sup norms in fixed bases, let \(\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_j\colon \mathcal V_j \to \mathcal V_{j+1}\) satisfy \(\lVert\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_j u\rVert \le p^{-\rho_j}\lVert u\rVert\) for \(\mathfrak t\) in a finite set, and let \(\Psi(\mathfrak t) \in {\mathbb{C}}_p\) have \(\lvert\Psi(\mathfrak t)\rvert_p \le 1\). Then \(\bigl\lVert\sum_{\mathfrak t} \Psi(\mathfrak t)\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_{m-1}\cdots\Theta^{\mathfrak t}_0 u\bigr\rVert \le p^{-(\rho_0 + \dots + \rho_{m-1})}\lVert u\rVert\).

Used in: Chapter 62; Chapter 65

Problem 57.3.2. Let \(p < x_1\) be prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and \(\Psi\) a chain datum for \(\vartheta\). For \(\varphi \in S_\eta\), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - L_p - m\, C_p(\varnothing)\).

Used in: Problem 57.4.2; Chapter 59

Let \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\) and let \(\Psi\) be a chain datum for \(\vartheta\). If \(p < x_1\) is non-split and not exceptional, then by Problems 57.2.4 and 57.3.2, \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - 2L_p\) on \(S_\eta\). If \(p \ne N\) and either \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) or \(p \ge x_1\), then \(p \ne d\), as \(d < x_1\) by Chapter 36; by (i), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) maps \(S_\eta\) to integral forms, and \(\operatorname{gain}(p) = 0\).

§57.4. Exceptional primes

Let \(p\) be inert in \(K\). Each place \(\sigma\), represented by the embedding \(\Phi_\sigma\), gives through \(\eta\) a prime \(v_\sigma\) of \(E\) above \(p\); for every positive odd integer \(e\), \(v_{[-p^e]\sigma} = \bar v_\sigma\), the complex conjugate prime (Problem 62.1.1). Let \(p < x_1\) be exceptional. A mark is a set \(\mathfrak M\) of primes of \(E\) above \(p\), each of the form \(v_\tau\) with \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), such that \(\bar v \notin \mathfrak M\) for \(v \in \mathfrak M\). It is full if it contains \(v_\tau\) or \(\bar v_\tau\) for each \(\tau \in \Phi_a\); a full mark exists for every exceptional \(p < x_1\) (Problem 62.1.5). Let \(\Phi_{\mathfrak M} = \{\sigma \in \Phi : v_\sigma \in \mathfrak M\}\).

Problem 57.4.1. If \(\mathfrak M\) is a full mark, then \(C_p(\Phi_{\mathfrak M}) = 0\).

Used in: Chapter 59; Chapter 62

For \(v \in \mathfrak M\) let \(a_v = a \cdot \#\{\tau \in \Phi_a : v_\tau = v\}\), let \(T_v\) be the Hecke operator at the split pair \(v, \bar v\) of the double coset of \(\operatorname{diag}(p 1_{r_p}, 1)\) (\(p\) is a uniformizer at \(v\), as \(p\) is unramified in \(E\)), and let \(Y_v = p^{a_v} T_v\) on \(S\). Let \(Y_{\mathfrak M}\) be the product of the \(Y_v\), \(v \in \mathfrak M\), in a fixed order. By Theorem 62.4.1, for a full mark \(\mathfrak M\), a weak chain datum \(\Psi'\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and \(\varphi \in S_\eta\), the form \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\varphi)\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - 2L_p\). Let \(j_p = \lceil 2mfa/p \rceil\).

Problem 57.4.2. Under the hypotheses of Problem 57.3.2, \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}(p^{j_p}\varphi)\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - L_p\) for \(\varphi \in S_\eta\).

Used in: Chapter 59

Problem 57.4.3. Let \(p < x_1\) be exceptional, \(\mathfrak M\) a full mark, \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and \(\Psi\) a chain datum for \(\vartheta\). On \(S' = Y_{\mathfrak M}^m S_\eta + p^{j_p} S_\eta\), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - 2L_p\).

Used in: Chapter 62

How large is \(S'\) in \(S_\eta\)? We use the colength of Chapter 5 and the phrase “onto modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(c\)” as defined in Chapter 18. By (63.5.1), for a mark \(\mathfrak M\), \(v \in \mathfrak M\) and every integer \(u \ge 0\), \(Y_v\) is onto \(S_\eta\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(\bigl(12a_v/(mp) + u/(m^2 f)\bigr) D_X\). Chapter 63 deduces this from the theorem on elementary divisors, Theorem 63.4.1; the theorem and its application at a marked prime are read from the proof there.

Problem 57.4.4. Let \(\mathcal O\) be a commutative ring, \(p \in \mathcal O\), \(\mathcal L\) a submodule of an \(\mathcal O\)-module \(\mathcal W\), and \(Y_1\), \(Y_2\) \(\mathcal O\)-linear endomorphisms of \(\mathcal W\) that are onto \(\mathcal L\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colengths \(c_1\) and \(c_2\). Then \(Y_1Y_2\) is onto \(\mathcal L\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(c_1 + c_2\).

Used in: Chapter 63

Problem 57.4.5. For a mark \(\mathfrak M\), \(\sum_{v \in \mathfrak M} a_v \le af\) and \(\#\mathfrak M\le f\), and \[m \sum_{v \in \mathfrak M} \Bigl(\frac{12 a_v}{mp} + \frac{j_p}{m^2 f}\Bigr) \le \frac{14fa + p/m}{p} \le L_p .\]

Used in: Chapter 63

So \(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\) is onto \(S_\eta\) modulo \(p^{j_p}\) up to colength \(L_p D_X\): there is an integer \(0 \le l \le L_p D_X\) such that the colength of \(S'\) in \(S_\eta\) divides \(p^l\).

§57.5. The level prime and the sum of the errors

At the level prime \(N\), which is at least \(x_1\), the gain is \(0\). Let \(S_N\) be the module of Definition 65.3.1. By Theorem 65.4.1 there is an integer \(0 \le l \le mfb\, D_X\) such that the colength of \(S_N\) in \(S_\eta\) divides \(N^l\), and for every twist \(\vartheta\), every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) and every \(\varphi \in S_\mathrm{ram}\cap S_N\), the form \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(-2mfb\). The module \(S_N\) is defined in the space of forms on \(X_0\). At \(p=N\) the proof of Theorem 57.6.1 takes its inverse image under the inclusion of the source forms, so \(S'=S_N\cap S\).

Define the allowed loss by \[\operatorname{loss}(p) = \begin{cases} 2L_p & \text{if } p < x_1 \text{ is prime and } \chi_K(p) \ne 1,\\ 2mfb & \text{if } p = N,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}\]

Problem 57.5.1. Let \(m \ge 2\), \(p \ge 2\), and \(p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod m\). Then \(p = mc - 1\) or \(p = mc + 1\) for an integer \(c\) with \(1 \le c \le p\) and \(m \le 2p/c\).

Let \(T_0 = 4fa(\log b)^2\log x_1\).

Problem 57.5.2. For a prime \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) there is an integer \(1 \le c \le p\) with \(p \in \{mc - 1, c, mc + 1\}\) and \(\operatorname{loss}(p)\log p \le T_0/c\).

The term of \(N\) is also at most \(T_0\): the scale inequalities of Chapter 36 give \(2mfb\log N \le T_0\) (they give \(\log N \le 30\, m\log b\) and \(m \le 2\sqrt{x}\), and imply \((\log b)\log x_1 \ge 60\)). In sums over natural numbers we set \(\log 0 = 0\).

Problem 57.5.3. For every finite set \(A\) of natural numbers, \(\sum_{p \in A} \operatorname{loss}(p)\log p \le 4T_0(1 + \log x_1)\).

Problem 57.5.4. If \((\log x_1)^6 \le \log b\) and \(\log x_1 \ge 3\), then \(4T_0(1 + \log x_1) \le U\).

Both hypotheses of Problem 57.5.4 hold for the data of Chapter 36.

§57.6. The theorem

The four kinds of primes are: \(p \ne N\) split or at least \(x_1\), with \(S_\eta\) and \(l = 0\) (Section 57.3); \(p < x_1\) non-split and not exceptional, with \(S_\eta\) and \(l = 0\) (Section 57.3); \(p < x_1\) exceptional, with \(S'\) (Section 57.4); and \(p = N\), with \(S' = S_N \cap S\) and the integer \(l\) of Theorem 65.4.1 (Section 57.5). These four cases give the following theorem for ramified inputs.

Theorem 57.6.1. Let \(p\) be prime and \(\eta\colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_p\) a ring map. There are an integer \(l \ge 0\) with \(l \le \operatorname{loss}(p)\, D_X\) and an \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-submodule \(S' \subseteq S\) whose colength in \(S_\eta\) divides \(p^l\), such that, for every twist \(\vartheta\), every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and every \(\varphi \in S' \cap S_\mathrm{ram}\), the form \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - \operatorname{loss}(p)\).

Used in: Chapter 65

Intersecting \(S'\) with \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) keeps the bound on its colength, now in \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\). The definition of \(\operatorname{loss}\) gives its nonnegativity and vanishing, and Problems 57.5.3 and 57.5.4 bound its weighted sum. This gives the theorem of this chapter.

Theorem 57.6.2. There are real numbers \(\operatorname{err}(p)\), \(p \in \mathbb N\), with the following properties.

  1. \(\operatorname{err}(p) \ge 0\) for every \(p\).

  2. If \(p \ne N\), and \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) or \(x_1 \le p\), then \(\operatorname{err}(p) = 0\).

  3. \(\sum_{p \in A} \operatorname{err}(p)\log p \le U\) for every finite set \(A\) of natural numbers.

  4. For every prime \(p\) and every ring map \(\eta\colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_p\) there are an integer \(l \ge 0\) with \(l \le \operatorname{err}(p)\, D_X\) and an \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-submodule \(S' \subseteq S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) whose colength in \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) divides \(p^l\), such that for every twist \(\vartheta\), every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and every \(\varphi \in S'\), the form \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - \operatorname{err}(p)\).

Used in: Chapter 49

Its proof takes \(\operatorname{err}= \operatorname{loss}\). The dimension in (d) is that of all source forms, while the colength is measured in the integral ramified ones; the module \(S'\) depends on \(p\) and \(\eta\) and serves every twist of level prime to \(p\) and every chain datum for that twist.

Theorem 57.6.2 is the divisibility input to Theorem A: Chapter 49 combines (c) and (d) with the seesaw identity of Chapter 52 and further inputs to bound the index of finite families in the images \(A_s(S_\mathrm{ram}^{\mathrm{int}})\) of the integral ramified source forms under the normalized translates. Chapters 58 and 59 prove input (iii), Chapter 65 treats the level prime and Chapter 62 the exceptional primes; Chapter 66 bounds the size of the composite lift for the trivial twist, against which Theorem A sets this divisibility.
