Prerequisites.

Even unimodular lattices, \(E_8\), theta series and the Hecke operators \(T_n\) on modular forms of level one [SeA, Ch. V and VII]. Quadratic forms over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) and the number of their isotropic vectors (for split forms, Problem 22.2.2). Gaussian binomial coefficients \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{a}{b}_{Q}\), which count the \(b\)-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb{F}_Q^a\) [Andr, Ch. 3]. From Chapter 21, Masses and the count of forms: Section 21.1, in particular Problems 21.1.1, 21.1.2 and 21.2.3. From Chapter 22, The Siegel–Weil formula in the smallest case: Problems 22.1.2 and 22.3.7. From Chapter 23, Definite theta lifts and their norms: Section 23.1. For Section 24.4, maximal orders in a definite quaternion algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}\) [Gr], [Pi].

A theta lift is a sum over classes of lattices. A Hecke operator on a class set is also a sum over lattices, namely over the neighbours of a lattice. How do the two interact? Eichler found that a theta series summed over the neighbours of a lattice is a Hecke transform of the theta series of the lattice, up to an explicit multiple of the identity [Ei52], [Ei73]; see [An] for Siegel modular forms and [Ra] for the general theta correspondence. We prove this in degree one, and check it on class sets with two elements, where the Hecke operator is a \(2 \times 2\) matrix.

§24.1. Neighbours Tag 22NJ

How does one move from a lattice to the other lattices of its genus? Kneser’s answer is to change it at one prime [Kn].

Let \(L\) be a positive definite even unimodular lattice of positive rank \(n = 2k\), with \(8 \mid n\), and let \(p\) be a prime. A \(p\)-neighbour of \(L\) is an even unimodular lattice \(M \subset L \otimes \mathbb{Q}\) with \([L : L \cap M] = [M : L \cap M] = p\). Let \(\mathcal{N}_p(L)\) be the set of \(p\)-neighbours.

Tag 22NK

Problem 24.1.1. Let \(p\) be odd. For \(v \in L \setminus pL\) with \(v \cdot v \equiv 0 \pmod{2p^2}\), let \(L_v := \{x \in L : x \cdot v \equiv 0 \pmod p\}\). Then \(M_v := L_v + \mathbb{Z}\, v/p\) is a \(p\)-neighbour of \(L\), and \(M_v\) depends only on the line \(\mathbb{F}_p \bar v \subset L/pL\). Every \(p\)-neighbour arises in this way, from exactly one isotropic line of \(L/pL\).

Tag 22NL

Problem 24.1.2. For odd \(p\), the quadratic space \(L/pL\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) is split, so that \[\lvert \mathcal{N}_p(L) \rvert = \frac{(p^k - 1)(p^{k-1} + 1)}{p - 1} .\] For \(L = E_8\) and \(p = 3, 5\) this is \(1120\) and \(19656\).

For \(p = 2\) the same count holds, if \(v\) is chosen with \(v \cdot v \equiv 0 \pmod 8\) [CL]; for \(E_8\) it is \(135\). Since \(E_8\) is the only even unimodular lattice of rank \(8\), all its neighbours are copies of \(E_8\). In each even unimodular genus considered here, the graph on isometry classes defined by \(p\)-neighbours is connected for every prime \(p\), by Kneser’s theorem [Kn], [CL].

Let \(\mathrm{Cl}\) be the set of isometry classes in the genus of \(L\), and let \(\Gamma_L:=\mathrm{O}(L)\). Define the neighbour operator on functions \(\varphi \colon \mathrm{Cl}\to \mathbb{C}\) by \((T_p\varphi)(L) := \sum_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} \varphi(M)\), and call its matrix \(N_p(L, M) := \#\{M' \in \mathcal{N}_p(L) : M' \cong M\}\) the Brandt matrix of the class set at \(p\). Let \(\langle \varphi, \psi \rangle := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}} \varphi(L)\overline{\psi(L)}/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\), as in Chapter 21.

Tag 22NM

Problem 24.1.3. \(N_p(L, M)/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert = N_p(M, L)/\lvert\Gamma_M\rvert\). Hence \(T_p\) is self-adjoint for \(\langle\,,\rangle\), and the constant function is an eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\lvert\mathcal{N}_p(L)\rvert\).

In rank \(16\) the class set is \(\{E_8 \perp E_8,\, D_{16}^+\}\), with \(\lvert\Gamma\rvert = 2 \cdot 696729600^2\) and \(2^{15}\cdot 16!\) [CoS].

Tag 22NN

Problem 24.1.4. The Brandt matrix of the class set of rank \(16\) at \(p = 2\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} 14670 & 18225 \\ 12870 & 20025 \end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(32895\) and \(1800\).

Used in: Problem 24.2.5

The eigenvalue \(1800\) is \(15\,\tau(2) + (2^4 + 2^5 + \dots + 2^{10}) + 2^7\), where \(\tau(2) = -24\) is the coefficient of Ramanujan’s \(\Delta\). Chenevier and Lannes explain such identities by the Arthur parameters of the eigenvectors [CL]. In Section 24.2 we show that the theta series of degree one cannot see this eigenvector.

§24.2. Eichler’s commutation relation in degree one Tag 22NP

For \(L\) as in Section 24.1, let \(r_L(m) := \#\{x \in L : x \cdot x = 2m\}\), with \(r_L(m) := 0\) unless \(m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\), and let \(\theta_L := \sum_m r_L(m)\, q^m\). It is a modular form of weight \(k\) for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) [SeA, Ch. VII], and \(T_{p^2}\) acts on its coefficients by \[a(m) \mapsto a(p^2m) + p^{k-1} a(m)\,[p \mid m] + p^{2k-2} a(m/p^2) .\] For the remainder of the neighbour count, let \(p\) be odd.

Tag 22NQ

Problem 24.2.1. Let \(x \in L \setminus pL\) with \(x \cdot x = 2m\). The number of \(M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)\) with \(x \in M\) is \((p^{2k-2} - 1)/(p - 1)\) if \(p \nmid m\), and \(1 + p(p^{k-1} - 1)(p^{k-2} + 1)/(p - 1)\) if \(p \mid m\).

Tag 22NR

Problem 24.2.2. The vectors of \(\bigcup_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} M \setminus L\) are the \(y/p\) with \(y \in L \setminus pL\) and \(y \cdot y \equiv 0 \pmod{2p^2}\), and each lies in exactly one neighbour. Deduce Theorem 24.2.1.

Tag 22NS

Theorem 24.2.1 (Eichler). For odd \(p\), \(\displaystyle \sum_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} \theta_M = T_{p^2}\theta_L + \frac{p^{2k-2} - p}{p - 1}\,\theta_L\).

Used in: Problem 24.2.2, Problem 24.2.3; Chapter 75
Tag 22NT

Problem 24.2.3. The constant-term identity in Theorem 24.2.1 for \(L = E_8\) and \(p = 3\) reads \(1120 = 757 + 363\). The same identity of constant terms holds for \(p = 2\): \(135 = 73 + 62\).

Let \(\theta(\varphi) := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}} \varphi(L)\, \theta_L / \lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\) be the theta lift of a function on the class set. It is the analogue for these lattices of the lift of Chapter 23 to rank one in weight zero, with the vectors sorted by norm into a \(q\)-series instead of weighted by a Gaussian.

Tag 22NU

Problem 24.2.4. Let \(p\) be odd. Then \(\theta(T_p\varphi) = \bigl(T_{p^2} + \frac{p^{2k-2} - p}{p-1}\bigr)\theta(\varphi)\). If \(\theta(\varphi) \ne 0\) and \(T_p\varphi = \mu\varphi\), and \(\theta(\varphi)\) is an eigenform with \(T_p\theta(\varphi) = \lambda\theta(\varphi)\), then \(\mu = \lambda^2 - p^{k-1} + (p^{2k-2} - p)/(p-1)\).

Used in: Problem 24.2.5

For odd \(p\), the theta lift intertwines the neighbour operator with \(T_p^2-p^{k-1}+(p^{2k-2}-p)/(p-1)\) on modular forms.

In rank \(16\), \(\theta_{E_8 \perp E_8} = \theta_{D_{16}^+}\) by Problem 22.1.2 of Chapter 22.

Tag 22NV

Problem 24.2.5. The eigenvector of Problem 24.1.4 with eigenvalue \(1800\) lies in the kernel of \(\theta\). For the constant function, the eigenvalue \(32895\) agrees with the formula of Problem 24.2.4 read at \(p = 2\), where we have not proved it: \(129^2 - 2^7 + (2^{14} - 2) = 32895\).

The two lattices are distinguished by their theta series of degree \(4\), and not before [CL]. To detect this eigenspace by theta series one can pass to higher degree; for lift norms see Section 23.3.

§24.3. Hermitian lattices of rank five Tag 22NW

Let \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), let \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) be a prime, and let \(p = \pi\bar\pi\) in \(\mathcal{O}\). Let \(L\) be a unimodular Hermitian lattice of rank \(n\ge1\), as in Chapter 21, and let \(L^\sharp\) denote duals. Then \(L/\pi L\) is an \(n\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathcal{O}/\pi = \mathbb{F}_p\). For a hyperplane \(H \subset L/\pi L\) let \(L_H\) be its preimage in \(L\), and \[L'_H := L_H + \pi L_H^\sharp .\]

Tag 22NX

Problem 24.3.1. \(L'_H\) is unimodular and lies in the genus of \(L\), with \(L \cap L'_H = L_H\) of index \(p\) in both. Moreover \(L'_H \subset \bar\pi^{-1}L\), and \(L\) is obtained from \(L'_H\) by the same construction with \(\bar\pi\) in place of \(\pi\).

We call \(L'_H\) a \(\pi\)-neighbour of \(L\). There are \((p^n-1)/(p-1)\) of them. For \(L\) representing a class in \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\), the genus of \(I_n\) in Chapter 21, set \(T_\pi\varphi(L):=\sum_H\varphi(L'_H)\). For the mass inner product, the adjoint of \(T_\pi\) is \(T_{\bar\pi}\). Let \(r_L(m):=\#\{x\in L:h(x,x)=m\}\) for \(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}\) and \(r_L(m):=0\) otherwise, and let \(\theta_L := \sum_m r_L(m)\, q^m\), a modular form of weight \(n\) for \(\Gamma_0(4)\) with character \(\chi^n\), where \(\chi\) is the character modulo \(4\) [Iw, Ch. 10]. Since \(\chi(p) = 1\), \(T_p\) acts on such forms by \(a(m) \mapsto a(pm) + p^{n-1}a(m/p)\).

Tag 22NY

Problem 24.3.2. For \(x \in L\), the number of \(H\) with \(x \in L'_H\) is \((p^{n-1}-1)/(p-1)\) if \(x \notin \pi L\), and \((p^n - 1)/(p - 1)\) if \(x \in \pi L\). A vector \(x \notin L\) lies in some \(L'_H\) exactly when \(y := \bar\pi x \in L \setminus \bar\pi L\) and \(p \mid h(y,y)\), and then in exactly one.

Used in: Problem 24.5.2
Tag 22NZ

Problem 24.3.3. \(\displaystyle \sum_H \theta_{L'_H} = T_p\theta_L + \frac{p^{n-1} - p}{p - 1}\,\theta_L\). Hence, with \(\theta(\varphi) := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \varphi(L)\theta_L/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\), one has \(\theta(T_\pi\varphi) = \bigl(T_p + \frac{p^{n-1}-p}{p-1}\bigr)\theta(\varphi)\), and likewise for \(\bar\pi\).

Used in: Problem 24.3.6

Here the factor is linear in \(T_p\). At \(p\) the unitary group is \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)\). In the component corresponding to \(\pi\), \(L'_H\) and \(L\) form an edge of type one in the sense of Section 24.5.

Now take \(n = 5\), \(p = 5\) and \(\pi = 2 + \mathrm{i}\). By Problem 21.2.3, \(\mathrm{Cl}_5 = \{I_5, \Lambda \perp I_1\}\), where \(\Lambda\) is the Gaussian Hermitian lattice of Section 21.1 with underlying real lattice \(E_8\). By Problems 21.1.2 and 21.2.3 and \(\lvert\Gamma_\Lambda\rvert = 46080\) (loc. cit.), \(\lvert\Gamma\rvert = 122880\) and \(184320\), and \(r(1) = 20\) and \(4\).

Tag 22P2

Problem 24.3.4. The matrix of \(T_\pi\) on \(\mathrm{Cl}_5\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} 525 & 256 \\ 384 & 397 \end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(781\) and \(141\).

Let \(g\) be the cusp form called \(f\) in Chapter 22, \[g = \frac14\sum_{\alpha \in \mathcal{O}}\alpha^4 q^{\lvert\alpha\rvert^2} = \eta(\tau)^4\eta(2\tau)^2\eta(4\tau)^4 = q - 4q^2 + 16q^4 - 14q^5 - 64q^8 + 81q^9 + \cdots ;\] here \(\eta\) is Dedekind’s function. The form \(g\) spans the cusp forms of weight \(5\) for \(\Gamma_0(4)\) with character \(\chi\), and it has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\). By Problem 22.3.7, \(\theta_{I_5} - \theta_{\Lambda \perp I_1} = 16\,g\).

Tag 22P3

Problem 24.3.5. The \(141\)-eigenspace of \(T_\pi\) is spanned by \(\varphi = (2, -3)\), and \(\theta(\varphi) = g/3840\).

Tag 22P4

Problem 24.3.6. \(a_5(g) = -14 = 2\operatorname{Re}\bigl((2+\mathrm{i})^4\bigr)\), and \(141 = -14 + 155\), as Problem 24.3.3 predicts.

The lift identifies the two class functions supported on single classes, with value \(1\) there, with the basis \((\theta_{I_5}/122880,\theta_{\Lambda\perp I_1}/184320)\) of its image. In this basis the matrix of \(T_5+155\) is the matrix of \(T_\pi\) in Problem 24.3.4. Neither theta series is an eigenform; the eigenforms are \(g\) and the lift of the constant function, with \(T_5\)-eigenvalues \(-14\) and \(626 = 781 - 155\).

§24.4. Brandt matrices Tag 22P5

The original setting of Eichler’s relation is a quaternion algebra, where the theta series of the ideal lattices between ideal-class representatives give modular forms of weight two and prime level [Ei73], [Pi], [Gr]. We follow Gross [Gr] in the smallest case with two classes.

Let \(B = (-1, -11)/\mathbb{Q}\), with \(\mathrm{i}^2 = -1\), \(j^2 = -11\) and \(k = \mathrm{i}j\); it is ramified exactly at \(11\) and \(\infty\). Let \(\mathcal{O}_1 := \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\mathrm{i}+ \mathbb{Z}\frac{1+j}{2} + \mathbb{Z}\frac{\mathrm{i}+ k}{2}\). There is one other class of maximal orders, represented by an order \(\mathcal{O}_2\) with \(\Theta_{\mathcal{O}_2} := \sum_{x \in \mathcal{O}_2} q^{N(x)} = 1 + 6q + 6q^3 + 24q^4 + 18q^5 + \cdots\). Let \(w_i := \lvert\mathcal{O}_i^\times\rvert/2\). Gross defines the Brandt matrices \(B(m)\), \(m \ge 1\), whose entries \(B_{ij}(m)\) count the elements \(b \in M_{ij} := I_j^{-1} I_i\) with \(N(b) = m\, N(M_{ij})\), divided by \(2w_j\), where \(N(M_{ij})\) is the positive generator of the ideal generated by the reduced norms of elements of \(M_{ij}\), for left ideals \(I_i\) of a fixed maximal order with right orders \(\mathcal{O}_i\); he shows that \(B_{ii}(m) = r_{\mathcal{O}_i}(m)/2w_i\), that \(w_j B_{ij}(m) = w_i B_{ji}(m)\), and that \(\sum_j B_{ij}(m) = \sigma(m)\) for \(11 \nmid m\) [Gr]. The assignment \(T_m\mapsto B(m)\) identifies the Hecke action on \(M_2(\Gamma_0(11))\) with the Brandt action [Gr]. For a prime \(\ell \ne 11\), \(B_{ij}(\ell)\) counts the left ideals \(J \subset I_i\) of the fixed order with \([I_i : J] = \ell^2\) and \(J \cong I_j\); there are \(\ell + 1\) such \(J\) in all, and they play the role of the \(\ell\)-neighbours of Section 24.1.

Tag 22P6

Problem 24.4.1. \(\mathcal{O}_1\) is an order, its norm form is \((a^2 + ac + 3c^2) + (b^2 + bd + 3d^2)\), and \(\mathcal{O}_1^\times = \{\pm1, \pm\mathrm{i}\}\). Thus \(\Theta_{\mathcal{O}_1} = \bigl(\sum_{a,c} q^{a^2+ac+3c^2}\bigr)^2\).

Eichler’s mass formula \(\sum_i 1/w_i = (11 - 1)/12\) then gives \(w_2 = 3\) [Gr].

§24.5. A split place Tag 22P9

Consider a theta lift at a split place whose local kernel is the indicator of \(xL \subset L'\) for a matrix \(x\); the Hecke operators are sums over edges of lattices. The following subspace count includes the local count in Problem 24.3.2 when the source has rank one.

Let \(Q\) be a power of \(p\), and let \(W\) be the unramified extension of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) with residue field \(\mathbb{F}_Q\). For lattices \(L'' \subseteq L\) in a \(W[1/p]\)-vector space, \((L'', L)\) is an edge of type \(i\) if \(pL\subseteq L''\subseteq L\) and \(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_Q}(L/L'')=i\). Let \(L \subset W[1/p]^N\) and \(L' \subset W[1/p]^{N+g}\) be lattices, let \(x\) be an \((N+g) \times N\) matrix with \(xL \subset L'\), and let \(\rho\) be the rank of \(x\) modulo \(p\) as a map \(L/pL \to L'/pL'\). Here \(N\ge1\), \(g\ge0\), and \(i\ge0\) are integers, and \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{a}{b}_{Q}=0\) for \(b<0\) or \(b>a\).

Tag 22PA

Problem 24.5.1. The number of edges \((L_1, L')\) of type \(i\) with \(xL \subset L_1\) is \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{N+g-\rho}{i}_{Q}\). The number of edges \((L, A_1)\) of type \(i\) with \(xA_1 \subset L'\) is \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{N-\rho}{i}_{Q}\).

Tag 22PB

Problem 24.5.2. For every \((N+g)\times N\) matrix \(x\) over \(W[1/p]\), with \(\mathbf 1_{L,L'}(x):=[xL\subseteq L']\), one has \[\sum_{(L_1, L') \text{ of type } i} \mathbf 1_{L, L_1} = \sum_{l=0}^{i} Q^{(i-l)(g-l)} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q} \sum_{(L, A_1) \text{ of type } i-l} \mathbf 1_{A_1, L'} .\] For \(N=i=1\), \(g=n-1\) and \(Q=p\), this gives the count for \(x\in L\) in Problem 24.3.2, with \(\rho\) the rank of the vector reduced modulo \(\pi\).

Tag 22PC

Problem 24.5.3. For \(0\le l\le g\), the elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_l\) satisfies \[e_l\bigl((Q^{(g-1)/2-s})_{0\le s<g}\bigr) = Q^{-l(g-l)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q} .\] The family of arguments is empty if \(g=0\).

Used in: Chapter 25

Under the split-kernel hypotheses (S1)–(S3) of [Adr], write \(Q=z^2\) for the residue-field order, with \(z=p^{d/2}\) and \(d\) the even residue degree, \(N_j=N_0+jg\) with \(g\ge1\) for the ranks, and \(T^{(j)}_i\) for the raw edge sums. For \(0\le r\le N_0\) set \(\mathcal E^{(j)}_i=z^{-i(N_j-i)}T^{(j)}_i\) and \(E_j(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\mathcal E^{(j)}_i X^i\). For \(0\le j<m\), the lift \(K_j\) satisfies \(E_{j+1}(X)K_j=P_{j+1}(X)K_jE_j(u_{j+1}X)\) modulo \(X^{r+1}\). If \(a,a'\) and \(\mu\) are the length-weight parameters in (S3) for this lift, then \(u_{j+1}=(aa')^{-1}\) is a unit, \(m_{j+1}=ap^\mu\), and \(P_{j+1}(X)\) is the product of \(1+u_{j+1}m_{j+1}\alpha X\) over the arguments \(\alpha\) of Problem 24.5.3.

Tag 22PD

Problem 24.5.4. Suppose that \(E_{j+1}(X)\,K_j = K_j\, P_{j+1}(X)\, E_j(X)\) modulo \(X^{r+1}\) for \(0 \le j < m\), with \(P_{j+1}(X) = \prod_{a \in x_{j+1}}(1 + aX)\), and where \(E_j(X) = \sum_i \mathcal E^{(j)}_i X^i\), and let \(Y\) be the concatenation, with multiplicities, of the families \(x_1, \dots, x_m\) and \(\mathcal K := K_{m-1}\cdots K_0\). Then \(\mathcal K\, \mathcal E^{(0)}_r = \sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l h_l(Y)\, \mathcal E^{(m)}_{r-l}\, \mathcal K\), where \(h_l\) is the complete symmetric polynomial.

Used in: Chapter 25

The coefficient of \(\mathcal E^{(m)}_{r-l}\mathcal K\) is \((-1)^lh_l(Y)\). Problem 24.2.4 gives an analogous relation for a single orthogonal theta lift.
