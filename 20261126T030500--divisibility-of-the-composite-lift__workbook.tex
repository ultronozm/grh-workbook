\medskip
Theorem~A needs a valuation bound for the composite lift \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) at every prime \(p\).  The divisibility predicted at \(p\) is a number \(\gain(p)\).  It holds up to an error, on a submodule of colength at most the error times \(D_X = \dim_\C S\), and the errors, weighted by \(\log p\), add up to at most \(U = fa\log^3 b\).  We follow the four cases of the proof.
\begin{itemize}
\item Read from the proof: the expansion of a chain datum into a weak one and estimates~(i)--(iii) of Section~\ref{sec:chain}; the conjugate-prime identity and the existence of a full mark in Section~\ref{sec:exceptional}; the valuation bound at a full mark (Theorem~\ref{dc-thm:proof}) and the colength estimate for one marked prime (from Theorem~\ref{hn-thm:general}); the case of the level prime.
\item Derived here from these: Theorems~\ref{thm:one-prime} and~\ref{thm:divisibility}.
\end{itemize}

\section{The gain at a prime}\label{sec:gain}

We use \(q_0\), \(d\), \(K\), \(\chi_K\), \(q_1\), \(E\), \(\Phi\), \(f\), \([\nu]\), \(\Phi_a\), \(\Phi_b\), the weight \(\kappa\) and \(\sfin\) as in Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}; recall that \(a/x_1 \le \kappa(\sigma) \le b \le a\) and that \(q_1\) is \(q_0\) times a product of powers of \(2\) and \(3\).  For a natural number \(p\), let \(c_p = 1/2\) if \(p = d\) and \(c_p = 1\) otherwise, and let \(I_p\) be the set of integers \(e \ge 1\) such that \(p = d\), or \(\chi_K(p) = -1\) and \(e\) is odd.  For \(y \in \mathbb R\) and \(\sigma \in \Phi\), with \(z_+ = \max(z, 0)\), the \emph{local prime sum} and the \emph{gain} are
\[
  \sfp(y, \sigma) = c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} \Bigl(\frac{y}{p^e} - \kappa([-p^e]\sigma)\Bigr)_+ ,
  \qquad
  \gain(p) = m\Bigl(\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} \sfp(a, \tau) + k \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi} \sfp(\kappa(\sigma), \sigma)\Bigr) .
\]
The first sum records the \(a\) columns of height one at each \(a\)-place; the second counts the local term at \(\kappa(\sigma)\) with multiplicity \(k\) at every place.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:qone-split}
Every prime dividing \(q_1\) splits in \(K\).  Hence a prime \(p \ne d\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) is prime to \(q_1\), and \([-p^e]\) is the place of the class of \(-p^e\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:shape}
Let \(p \ne d\) be inert in \(K\), \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), and suppose that \(\kappa([-p^e]\tau) = w\) for every positive odd integer \(e\).  Then \(\sfp(a, \tau) = \sum_{e \ge 1,\ e\ \mathrm{odd}} (a/p^e - w)_+\), the first sum of Theorem~\ref{ip-thm:main} without the integer parts.  Evaluated at \(p = 13\), \(a = 13^4\) and \(w = 1\), the sum is \(2208\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gain-zero}
\(\gain(p) = 0\) if \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) or \(p \ge x_1\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:part-at-p}
Let \(p\) be prime and \(y \le a\).  The terms of \(\sfin(y, \sigma)\) with \(\nu\) a power of \(p\) add up to \(2\, \sfp(y, \sigma) \log p\).
\end{problem}

Summing over primes, \(2\sum_p \gain(p)\log p = m\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} \sfin(a, \tau) + mk \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \sfin(\kappa(\tau), \tau)\), the sum running over the primes \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\); the contribution of the powers of \(p\) to the right-hand side is \(2\gain(p)\log p\).

\section{Collisions}\label{sec:collisions}

In this section \(p\) is a prime.  For a set \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), a \emph{collision outside \(\Phi'\)} is a pair \((\tau, e)\) with \(e \in I_p\) and \(\tau, [-p^e]\tau \in \Phi_a \smallsetminus \Phi'\); the \emph{collision sum} is
\[
  C_p(\Phi') = \sum_{(\tau, e)\ \text{collision outside}\ \Phi'} \frac{a}{p^e} .
\]
Let \(o_p\) be the order of the image of \([-p]\) in \((\Z/q_0)^\times\), and \(r_p\) the least integer \(h \ge 0\) with \(m \le p^h\).  The prime \(p\) is \emph{exceptional} if \(\chi_K(p) = -1\), \(p \not\equiv \pm 1 \pmod m\), and \(o_p\) is odd with \(o_p \le r_p\).  The \emph{unit of loss} at \(p\) is
\[
  L_p = f(\log b)^2\, \frac{a}{p} \cdot \begin{cases} m & \text{if } m \mid p - 1 \text{ or } m \mid p + 1,\\ 1 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

\begin{problem}\label{pr:collision-odd}
Let \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) and let \((\tau, e)\) be a collision outside some \(\Phi'\).  Then \(p \ne d\), \(e\) is odd, and \(o_p \mid e\); in particular \(o_p\) is odd.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:o-example}
Let \(q_0 = 18480\) and let \(p\) be a prime not dividing \(q_0\).  If \(o_p\) is odd, then \(p \equiv -1 \pmod{240}\) and \(o_p \in \{1, 3, 5, 15\}\).  For \(d = 138599\), the inert prime \(13\) has \(o_{13} = 20\).  Among the primes inert in \(\Q(\sqrt{-138599})\), the least with \(o_p\) odd is \(479\), with \(o_{479} = 15\), and the least with \(o_p = 1\) is \(55439\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:geometric}
Let \(o \ge 0\) be an integer.  If every collision \((\tau, e)\) outside \(\Phi'\) has \(e \ge o\), then \(C_p(\Phi') \le 2fa/p^o\).
\end{problem}

From Chapter~\ref{c32-chap} we use \((\log b)^2 \ge 15\) and \(m \ge 2\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:non-exceptional}
Let \(p < x_1\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) that is not exceptional.  Then \(m\, C_p(\varnothing) \le L_p\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:why-mark}
Let \(p\) be exceptional with \(o_p = 1\), and let \(h_0 = \#(\Z/q_0)^\times\).  Then \(\#\Phi_a = f/h_0\) and \(m\, C_p(\varnothing) \ge m f a/(h_0 p)\), which exceeds \(L_p\) when \(m > h_0(\log b)^2\).
\end{problem}

So at an exceptional prime the unit of loss need not pay for the collisions; Section~\ref{sec:exceptional} obtains the valuation bound there on a submodule.

\section{The chain of lifts}\label{sec:chain}

Fix a prime \(p\) and a ring map \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\), and let \(\Oc_\eta = \{z \in \C : \lvert\eta z\rvert_p \le 1\}\).  At a point \(g\) of the finite adelic group, the gauge \(\lVert\omega\rVert_g\) of a Weyl vector \(\omega\) is the largest absolute value of a coordinate of \(g^{-1}\cdot\eta(\omega)\), where at \(\tau\) the adelic matrix acts in the Weyl representation through the extension to finite adeles of \(u \mapsto \eta(\Phi_\tau(\bar u))\), and \(\Phi_\tau\) is the embedding of \(E\) selected by the CM type.  A form \(\varphi\) \emph{has valuation at least \(\beta\)} if \(\lVert\varphi(g)\rVert_g \le p^{-\beta}\) for every \(g\); it is \emph{integral} if it has valuation at least \(0\).  With \(S \supseteq S_\ram\) the source forms and the ramified ones (Chapter~\ref{ob-chap}), let \(S_\eta \subseteq S\) be the \(\Oc_\eta\)-module of integral source forms and \(S_{\ram,\eta} = S_\eta \cap S_\ram\).

A \emph{translation} \(\lambda = (\lambda_j)_{j \ge 0}\) is a family of finite adelic matrices \(\lambda_j\) from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\); write \(\Tc\) for the set of translations.  For a twist \(\vartheta\), a finitely supported \(\Psi\colon \Tc \to \C\) is a \emph{weak chain datum} for \(\vartheta\) if: for every \(\lambda\) in its support and every \(j\), the entries of \(\alpha\lambda_j\) are integral at every finite place not dividing \(N \Nm Q_\vartheta\) (with \(\alpha\) as in Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}); every value \(\Psi(\lambda)\) is integral under every ring map \(\C \to \C_\ell\) for every prime \(\ell \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\); if \(Q_\vartheta = \varnothing\), then \(\sum_\lambda \lvert\sigma(\Psi(\lambda))\rvert \le \exp(x^{60})\) for every automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\C\); and \(\sum_\lambda \Psi(\lambda) \bigotimes_{j < m} v_j(\lambda)\) is fixed by the levels of \(X_0, \dots, X_{m-1}\), where \(v_j(\lambda)\) is the integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\) translated by the finite Heisenberg operator of \(\lambda_j\).  The \emph{restricted chain} \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) is \(\sum_\lambda \Psi(\lambda)\, \Theta^{\lambda}_{m-1} \cdots \Theta^{\lambda}_0\), followed by restriction to \(Y_m\), where the \(j\)th lift \(\Theta^\lambda_j\) has the datum \(v_j(\lambda)\).

Away from \(N\) the proof uses the following expansion: for every twist \(\vartheta\) and chain datum \(\Psi\) there is a weak chain datum \(\Psi'\) with \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi = R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}\varphi\) for all \(\varphi \in S\).  We use the following estimates read from the proof.  In~(i) and~(ii), let \(\Psi'\) be a weak chain datum for \(\vartheta\) and suppose \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] If \(p \ne N\) and \(p \ne d\), then \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}\) maps \(S_\eta\) to integral forms.
\item[(ii)] Let \(p < x_1\) and \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), and let \(\mathcal K^\lambda_j(g, h)\) be the kernel of the \(j\)th lift with the datum \(v_j(\lambda)\), from the Weyl space at \(g\) to the Weyl space at \(h\).  For every \(\lambda\) in the support of \(\Psi'\), every \(0 \le j < m\), all \(g\), \(h\) and every Weyl vector \(\omega\), \(\lVert \mathcal K^\lambda_j(g, h)\omega\rVert_h \le p^{-\beta_j(\varnothing)}\lVert\omega\rVert_g\).  The restricted chain \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}\) maps \(S_\eta\) to forms of valuation at least \(\sum_{j<m} \beta_j(\varnothing)\).
\item[(iii)] Here \(\beta_j(\Phi')\), for \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), is defined as follows.  Let \(n = n_{j+1}\).  Count only the columns of the diagram of the \(j\)th lift of height less than \(n\), and let \(n_\square\) be their number of boxes.  Delete the columns of height one at the places of \(\Phi'\), leaving \(n'_\square\) boxes, and let \(\Delta'\) be the predicted divisibility of the remaining diagram, in the notation of Theorem~\ref{pf-thm:count}.  Then \(\beta_j(\Phi') = \Delta' + \gamma_p(n_\square - n'_\square) - fn^3(1 + \log_p \max(n_\square, 1))\), where \(\gamma_p = 1/(p-1) - \rho(d, p)\) is \(p/(p^2 - 1)\) at an inert prime and \(1/(2d - 2)\) at \(p = d\).  For every prime \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), every \(0 \le j < m\) and every \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\),
\[
  \beta_j(\Phi') \ge \frac{\gain(p)}{m} - C_p(\Phi') + c_p\, \#(\Phi_a \cap \Phi')\,\frac{a}{x_1} - \frac{fa(\log b)^2}{mp} .
\]
\end{enumerate}
Estimates~(ii) and~(iii) are read from the proof, which obtains the kernel bound in~(ii) from the bound on jets along products of minors in Theorem~\ref{pf-thm:count}, applied to the theta series for a standard datum.  Inequality~(iii) compares the expression for \(\beta_j(\Phi')\), which uses the predicted divisibility of the remaining diagram, with the prime sums that define \(\gain(p)\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gauge-chain}
Let \(\mathcal V_0, \dots, \mathcal V_m\) be finite-dimensional \(\C_p\)-spaces with sup norms in fixed bases, let \(\Theta^\lambda_j\colon \mathcal V_j \to \mathcal V_{j+1}\) satisfy \(\lVert\Theta^\lambda_j u\rVert \le p^{\rho_j}\lVert u\rVert\) for \(\lambda\) in a finite set, and let \(\Psi(\lambda) \in \C_p\) have \(\lvert\Psi(\lambda)\rvert_p \le 1\).  Then \(\bigl\lVert\sum_\lambda \Psi(\lambda)\Theta^\lambda_{m-1}\cdots\Theta^\lambda_0 u\bigr\rVert \le p^{\rho_0 + \dots + \rho_{m-1}}\lVert u\rVert\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:collision-chain}
Let \(p < x_1\) be prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\), and \(\Psi\) a chain datum for \(\vartheta\).  For \(\varphi \in S_\eta\), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - L_p - m\, C_p(\varnothing)\).
\end{problem}

Let \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\) and let \(\Psi\) be a chain datum for \(\vartheta\).  If \(p < x_1\) is non-split and not exceptional, then by Problems~\ref{pr:non-exceptional} and~\ref{pr:collision-chain}, \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - 2L_p\) on \(S_\eta\).  If \(p \ne N\) and either \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) or \(p \ge x_1\), then \(p \ne d\), as \(d < x_1\) by Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}; by~(i), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) maps \(S_\eta\) to integral forms, and \(\gain(p) = 0\).

\section{Exceptional primes}\label{sec:exceptional}

Let \(p\) be inert in \(K\).  Each place \(\sigma\), represented by the embedding \(\Phi_\sigma\), gives through \(\eta\) a prime \(v_\sigma\) of \(E\) above \(p\); for every positive odd integer \(e\), \(v_{[-p^e]\sigma} = \bar v_\sigma\), the complex conjugate prime (read from the proof).  Let \(p < x_1\) be exceptional.  A \emph{mark} is a set \(\Mk\) of primes of \(E\) above \(p\), each of the form \(v_\tau\) with \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), such that \(\bar v \notin \Mk\) for \(v \in \Mk\).  It is \emph{full} if it contains \(v_\tau\) or \(\bar v_\tau\) for each \(\tau \in \Phi_a\); a full mark exists for every exceptional \(p < x_1\) (read from the proof).  Let \(\Phi_{\Mk} = \{\sigma \in \Phi : v_\sigma \in \Mk\}\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:full-mark}
If \(\Mk\) is a full mark, then \(C_p(\Phi_{\Mk}) = 0\).
\end{problem}

For \(v \in \Mk\) let \(a_v = a \cdot \#\{\tau \in \Phi_a : v_\tau = v\}\), let \(T_v\) be the Hecke operator at the split pair \(v, \bar v\) of the double coset of \(\operatorname{diag}(\varpi_v 1_{r_p}, 1)\), and let \(Y_v = p^{a_v} T_v\) on \(S\).  Let \(Y_{\Mk}\) be the product of the \(Y_v\), \(v \in \Mk\).  By Theorem~\ref{dc-thm:proof}, for a full mark \(\Mk\), a weak chain datum \(\Psi'\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\), and \(u \in S_\eta\), the form \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi'}(Y_{\Mk}^m u)\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - 2L_p\).  Let \(j_p = \lceil 2mfa/p \rceil\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:crude}
Under the hypotheses of Problem~\ref{pr:collision-chain}, \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}(p^{j_p}\varphi)\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - L_p\) for \(\varphi \in S_\eta\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:two-pieces}
Let \(p < x_1\) be exceptional, \(\Mk\) a full mark, \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\), and \(\Psi\) a chain datum for \(\vartheta\).  On \(S' = Y_{\Mk}^m S_\eta + p^{j_p} S_\eta\), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - 2L_p\).
\end{problem}

How large is \(S'\) in \(S_\eta\)?  We use the colength of Chapter~\ref{sm-chap} and the phrase ``onto modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(c\)'' as defined in Chapter~\ref{hn-chap}.  The proof applies Theorem~\ref{hn-thm:general} at the marked primes and obtains: for a mark \(\Mk\), \(v \in \Mk\) and every integer \(u \ge 0\), \(Y_v\) is onto \(S_\eta\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(\bigl(12a_v/(mp) + u/(m^2 f)\bigr) D_X\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:colength-add}
Let \(\Oc\) be a commutative ring, \(p \in \Oc\), \(\mathcal L\) a submodule of an \(\Oc\)-module \(\mathcal W\), and \(Y_1\), \(Y_2\) \(\Oc\)-linear endomorphisms of \(\mathcal W\) that are onto \(\mathcal L\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colengths \(c_1\) and \(c_2\).  Then \(Y_1Y_2\) is onto \(\mathcal L\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(c_1 + c_2\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:budget}
For a mark \(\Mk\), \(\sum_{v \in \Mk} a_v \le af\) and \(\#\Mk \le f\), and
\[
  m \sum_{v \in \Mk} \Bigl(\frac{12 a_v}{mp} + \frac{j_p}{m^2 f}\Bigr) \le \frac{14fa + p/m}{p} \le L_p .
\]
\end{problem}

So \(Y_{\Mk}^m\) is onto \(S_\eta\) modulo \(p^{j_p}\) up to colength \(L_p D_X\): there is an integer \(0 \le l \le L_p D_X\) such that the colength of \(S'\) in \(S_\eta\) divides \(p^l\).

\section{The level prime and the sum of the errors}\label{sec:sum}

At the level prime \(N\), which is at least \(x_1\), the gain is \(0\).  The following is read from the proof: \emph{at \(p = N\) there are an integer \(0 \le l \le mfb\, D_X\) and an \(\Oc_\eta\)-module \(S_N\) of source forms, whose colength in \(S_\eta\) divides \(N^l\), such that for every chain datum \(\Psi\) for a twist \(\vartheta\) and every ramified \(\varphi \in S_N\), \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(-2mfb\).}  Here \(S_N\) consists of the source forms \(\varphi\) for which \(\psi = \mathrm{Up}^{m/2}\varphi\) satisfies \(\lVert\psi(g)\rVert_{g\delta_0} \le 1\) for every \(g\), where \(\mathrm{Up}\) is an operator at \(N\) on the forms of stage zero and \(\delta_0\) an adelic point at \(N\), both fixed in the proof.

The \emph{allowed loss} is
\[
  \loss(p) = \begin{cases} 2L_p & \text{if } p < x_1 \text{ is prime and } \chi_K(p) \ne 1,\\ 2mfb & \text{if } p = N,\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
\]

\begin{problem}\label{pr:pm-one}
Let \(m \ge 2\), \(p \ge 2\), and \(p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod m\).  Then \(p = mc - 1\) or \(p = mc + 1\) for an integer \(c\) with \(1 \le c \le p\) and \(m \le 2p/c\).
\end{problem}

Let \(T_0 = 4fa(\log b)^2\log x_1\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:term}
For a prime \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) there is an integer \(1 \le c \le p\) with \(p \in \{mc - 1, c, mc + 1\}\) and \(\loss(p)\log p \le T_0/c\).
\end{problem}

The term of \(N\) is also at most \(T_0\): the scale inequalities of Chapter~\ref{c32-chap} give \(2mfb\log N \le T_0\) (they give \(\log N \le 30\, m\log b\) and \(m \le 2\sqrt{x}\), and imply \((\log b)\log x_1 \ge 60\)).  In sums over natural numbers we set \(\log 0 = 0\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:harmonic}
For every finite set \(A\) of natural numbers, \(\sum_{p \in A} \loss(p)\log p \le 4T_0(1 + \log x_1)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:final}
If \((\log x_1)^6 \le \log b\) and \(\log x_1 \ge 3\), then \(4T_0(1 + \log x_1) \le U\).
\end{problem}

Both hypotheses of Problem~\ref{pr:final} hold for the data of Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}.

\section{The theorem}\label{sec:theorem}

The four kinds of primes are: \(p \ne N\) split or at least \(x_1\), with \(S_\eta\) and \(l = 0\) (Section~\ref{sec:chain}); \(p < x_1\) non-split and not exceptional, with \(S_\eta\) and \(l = 0\) (Section~\ref{sec:chain}); \(p < x_1\) exceptional, with \(S'\) (Section~\ref{sec:exceptional}); and \(p = N\), with \(S_N\).  These four cases give the following theorem for ramified inputs.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:one-prime}
Let \(p\) be prime and \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\) a ring map.  There are an integer \(l \ge 0\) with \(l \le \loss(p)\, D_X\) and an \(\Oc_\eta\)-submodule \(S' \subseteq S\) whose colength in \(S_\eta\) divides \(p^l\), such that, for every twist \(\vartheta\), every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\), and every \(\varphi \in S' \cap S_\ram\), the form \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - \loss(p)\).
\end{theorem}

Intersecting \(S'\) with \(S_{\ram,\eta}\) keeps the bound on its colength, now in \(S_{\ram,\eta}\).  The definition of \(\loss\) gives its nonnegativity and vanishing, and Problems~\ref{pr:harmonic} and~\ref{pr:final} bound its weighted sum.  This gives the theorem of this chapter.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:divisibility}
There are real numbers \(\err(p)\), \(p \in \mathbb N\), with the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(\err(p) \ge 0\) for every \(p\).
\item[(b)] If \(p \ne N\), and \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) or \(x_1 \le p\), then \(\err(p) = 0\).
\item[(c)] \(\sum_{p \in A} \err(p)\log p \le U\) for every finite set \(A\) of natural numbers.
\item[(d)] For every prime \(p\) and every ring map \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\) there are an integer \(l \ge 0\) with \(l \le \err(p)\, D_X\) and an \(\Oc_\eta\)-submodule \(S' \subseteq S_{\ram,\eta}\) whose colength in \(S_{\ram,\eta}\) divides \(p^l\), such that for every twist \(\vartheta\), every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\), and every \(\varphi \in S'\), the form \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - \err(p)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Its proof takes \(\err = \loss\).  The dimension in~(d) is that of all source forms, while the colength is measured in the integral ramified ones; the module \(S'\) depends on \(p\) and \(\eta\) and serves every twist of level prime to \(p\) and every chain datum for that twist.

Theorem~\ref{thm:divisibility} is the divisibility input to Theorem~A: Chapter~\ref{c36-chap} combines~(c) and~(d) with the seesaw identity of Chapter~\ref{c34-chap} and further inputs to bound the index of finite families in the images \(A_s(S_\ram^{\mathrm{int}})\) of the integral ramified source forms under the normalized translates.

\section*{Hints}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:gain}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:qone-split}] Show \(d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \pmod{q_0}\), so \(d \equiv -1\) modulo \(8\) and modulo each odd prime dividing \(q_0\).  Then quadratic reciprocity.
\item[\ref{pr:shape}] \(I_p\) is the set of odd \(e \ge 1\), and \(c_p = 1\).
\item[\ref{pr:gain-zero}] If \(\chi_K(p) = 1\), \(I_p\) is empty.  If \(p \ge x_1\), compare \(y/p^e\) with \(a/x_1\).
\item[\ref{pr:part-at-p}] Compute \(1 - \chi_K(p^e)\) in the three cases inert, ramified, split.  For \(p^e > a\) the term of \(\sfp\) vanishes.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:collisions}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:collision-odd}] \(\tau\) and \([-p^e]\tau\) have class \(1\) modulo \(q_0\).  For \(p = d\), compute \(-d^e\) modulo \(q_0\) separately for even and odd \(e\), using \(4 \mid q_0\) and the congruence for \(d\) in the hint to Problem~\ref{pr:qone-split}.
\item[\ref{pr:o-example}] Decompose \((\Z/18480)^\times\) by the Chinese remainder theorem and find its elements of odd order.  Use a prime search for the two minima.
\item[\ref{pr:geometric}] At each of the \(f\) places, a geometric series of ratio at most \(1/2\).
\item[\ref{pr:non-exceptional}] Two cases, according as \(p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod m\) (Problem~\ref{pr:geometric} with \(o = 1\)) or not; in the second, Problem~\ref{pr:collision-odd} and \(m \le p^{r_p}\).
\item[\ref{pr:why-mark}] Every \((\tau, 1)\) with \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) is a collision.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:chain}}

\item[\ref{pr:gauge-chain}] The sup norm is ultrametric.

\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:exceptional}}
