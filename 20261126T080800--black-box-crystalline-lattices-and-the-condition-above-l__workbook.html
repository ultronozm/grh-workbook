Chapter 32, Black box: Galois representations of eigenforms, introduces the representations attached to eigenforms. Here we consider crystalline cocycles of lattices at places above \(\ell\). This chapter gives the rank of the cocycle module and a bound on the index of its image after reduction. Tate twists illustrate both facts.

Prerequisites.

Local fields, the cyclotomic character and continuous Galois cohomology, including cup products and Kummer theory. Use the finite local duality and Euler characteristic of Chapter 33, Theorem 33.1.1. The module construction uses Witt vectors.

§34.1. Crystalline cocycles Tag 2397

We define the Hodge–Tate jumps and the crystalline cocycles of a lattice, then use the jumps to state the rank of the cocycle module.

Fix a prime \(\ell\) and a completion \(F_v\) of a number field \(F\) at \(v\mid\ell\), embedded in \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\). Write \(G_{F_v}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell/F_v)\) and \(\mathbb C_\ell=\widehat{\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell}\). A lattice \(L\) is finite free over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) with continuous \(G_{F_v}\)-action. Put \(V=L\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell\). For \(e\in\mathbb Z\), write \(\mathbb Z_\ell(e)\) for the rank-one lattice with action through \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^e\), where \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is the cyclotomic character. Write \(L^*(1)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(L,\mathbb Z_\ell(1))\) with the induced action. Tensors with period rings are over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) and carry the diagonal Galois action.

Tag 2398

Definition 34.1.1. A lattice \(L\) is crystalline if the fixed vectors of \(G_{F_v}\) in \(B_{\max}\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}L\) span over \(B_{\max}\). It is potentially crystalline if the restriction to the Galois group of a finite extension of \(F_v\) is crystalline. Here \(B_{\max}\) is Colmez’s crystalline period ring. Fontaine’s ring \(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\) gives the same conditions, using \(\varphi(B_{\max})\subseteq B_{\mathrm{cris}}\subseteq B_{\max}\) for the Galois-equivariant Frobenius \(\varphi\) [Fo; Co].

Used in: Chapter 35
Tag 2399

Definition 34.1.2. A lattice is Hodge–Tate if \(\mathbb C_\ell\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell} L\) is a direct sum of twists \(\mathbb C_\ell(e)=\mathbb C_\ell\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Z_\ell(e)\) as a semilinear representation. A summand \(\mathbb C_\ell(e)\) contributes one jump \(u=-e\). In particular \(\mathbb Z_\ell(e)\) has jump \(-e\).

Used in: Chapter 35

Potentially crystalline lattices are Hodge–Tate and remain potentially crystalline under Tate duality. The Tate twists are crystalline. A crystalline lattice with all jumps zero is unramified, so inertia acts trivially [Fo].

For a \(G_{F_v}\)-module write \(Z^1\) for the continuous cocycles and \(B^1\) for the coboundaries \(g\mapsto gy-y\). Write \(\mathrm{red}_n\) for pointwise reduction modulo \(\ell^n\).

Tag 239A

Definition 34.1.3. The module \(Z_f(L)\) consists of maps \(c\colon G_{F_v}\to L\) for which there is a \(y\in B_{\max}\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}L\) with \(1\otimes c(g)=gy-y\) for every \(g\in G_{F_v}\).

Tag 239B

Theorem 34.1.1 (Rank of crystalline cocycles). Suppose \(L\) is potentially crystalline. Then \(Z_f(L)\) is a saturated submodule of \(Z^1(F_v,L)\) and is finite free over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\). If \(n^-\) is the number of negative jumps, counted with multiplicity, then \[\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Z_\ell} Z_f(L)=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Z_\ell} L+[F_v:\mathbb Q_\ell]n^- .\]

Used in: Chapter 82; Chapter 90

Rank and finite generation are read from the proof [GL]. Write \(H^1_f(F_v,L)=Z_f(L)/B^1(F_v,L)\). The classical rational local condition \(H^1_f(F_v,V)\) is the kernel of extension of scalars to \(B_{\mathrm{cris}}\) on \(H^1(F_v,V)\). The map from \(H^1(F_v,L)\) to \(H^1(F_v,V)\) pulls back the rational local condition to \(H^1_f(F_v,L)\) and induces \(H^1_f(F_v,L)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell\cong H^1_f(F_v,V)\). Bloch and Kato give the rational condition and its inverse image [BK]. The comparison of period rings in Definition 34.1.1 also gives the same kernel on cohomology [Co]. For the trivial representation the rational condition consists of unramified homomorphisms, which factor through \(G_{F_v}\) modulo inertia. For \(\mathbb Q_\ell(1)\) the rational condition is the image under the Kummer map of the units of \(F_v\) after \(\ell\)-adic completion and tensoring with \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) [BK, Example 3.9]. Over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), the completed unit group has no \(\ell\)-torsion when \(\ell\) is odd.

The first problems compare the cocycles of the Tate twists with their coboundaries.

Tag 239C

Problem 34.1.1. For \(L=\mathbb Z_\ell(e)\) the ranks of \(Z_f(L)\), for \(e<0\), \(e=0\) and \(e>0\), are \(1,1,1+[F_v:\mathbb Q_\ell]\), respectively. The corresponding dimensions of \(H^1_f(F_v,\mathbb Q_\ell(e))\) are \(0,1,[F_v:\mathbb Q_\ell]\).

Tag 239D

Problem 34.1.2. The group \(Z_f(\mathbb Z_\ell)\) consists of the unramified homomorphisms, with one free parameter given by the value on arithmetic Frobenius.

Tag 239E

Problem 34.1.3. Take \(F_v=\mathbb Q_\ell\), \(\ell\) odd and \(e<0\). Put \(n_0=0\) if \((\ell-1)\nmid e\) and \(n_0=1+\operatorname{val}_\ell(e)\) otherwise, with \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(\ell)=1\). In a basis of \(\mathbb Z_\ell(e)\) the function \[g\longmapsto \frac{\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^e-1}{\ell^{n_0}}\] generates \(Z_f(\mathbb Z_\ell(e))\). The coboundaries have index \(\ell^{n_0}\). For \((\ell,e)=(5,-4)\) and \((5,-20)\) the indices are \(5\) and \(25\).

Reduction modulo \(\ell^n\) lets us count the two crystalline subgroups for a lattice and its Tate dual.

Tag 239F

Problem 34.1.4. For potentially crystalline \(L\) and \(n\ge 1\), the kernel of \(\mathrm{red}_n\) on \(Z_f(L)\) is \(\ell^n Z_f(L)\). Consequently \(\#\mathrm{red}_n Z_f(L)=\ell^{n\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Z_\ell} Z_f(L)}\).

Tag 239G

Problem 34.1.5. Let \(L\) be potentially crystalline and \(n\ge 1\). A jump \(u\) of \(L\) corresponds to a jump \(-u-1\) of its Tate dual, with the same multiplicity. Exactly one of the two integers is negative, and \[\#\mathrm{red}_n Z_f(L)\,\#\mathrm{red}_n Z_f(L^*(1)) =\lvert L/\ell^nL\rvert^{\,2+[F_v:\mathbb Q_\ell]} .\] For a Tate twist over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\) the product is \(\ell^{3n}\) for every \(e\).

§34.2. Orthogonality Tag 239H

Local duality converts a restriction on cup products into a bound on an index of crystalline cocycles.

Tag 239J

Theorem 34.2.1 (Crystalline local duality). Let \(L\) be potentially crystalline, let \(L'=L^*(1)\), and put \(V'=L'\otimes_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathbb Q_\ell\). In the local Tate pairing of \(H^1(F_v,L)\) with \(H^1(F_v,V'/L')\), the subgroup \(H^1_f(F_v,L)\) and the image of \(H^1_f(F_v,V')\) are exact annihilators.

Used in: Chapter 82

The annihilator statement is the lattice form of the classical rational duality of Bloch and Kato [BK, Proposition 3.8].

For the index form keep \(L\) and \(L'\) as in Theorem 34.2.1 and let \(n\ge 0\) be an integer. Let \(\mathcal Q\) be an additive subgroup of maps \(G_{F_v}\to L'\), and \(\mathcal S\) an additive subgroup of \(Z^1(F_v,L/\ell^n L)\). Suppose the cup product of every \(s\in\mathcal S\cup\mathrm{red}_n Z_f(L)\) with \(\mathrm{red}_n c\) for every \(c\in\mathcal Q\), evaluated in the group \(\mu_{\ell^n}\) of \(\ell^n\)-th roots of unity, is the coboundary of a continuous \(1\)-cochain in the discrete \(G_{F_v}\)-module \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell^\times\). Then \[[\mathcal S:\mathcal S\cap \mathrm{red}_n Z_f(L)] \ \mid\ [Z_f(L'):Z_f(L')\cap\mathcal Q] .\] An index of an infinite quotient is recorded as zero. The index form is read from the proof [GL].

For the finite example take \(F_v=\mathbb Q_\ell\) and \(\ell\) odd. The two modules are \(M=\mathbb Z/\ell\) with trivial action, and its dual \(\mu_\ell\).

Tag 239K

Problem 34.2.1. Both first cohomology groups have order \(\ell^2\). The cocycle groups of \(M\) and \(\mu_\ell\) have orders \(\ell^2\) and \(\ell^3\), respectively. For \(\ell=5\) the cocycle orders are \(25\) and \(125\).

Tag 239L

Problem 34.2.2. The reductions modulo \(\ell\) of \(Z_f(\mathbb Z_\ell)\) and \(Z_f(\mathbb Z_\ell(1))\) are exact annihilators for the pairing of \(Z^1(\mathbb Q_\ell,M)\) and \(Z^1(\mathbb Q_\ell,\mu_\ell)\) induced by cup product and the local invariant. Their orders are \(\ell\) and \(\ell^2\), respectively, and their images in cohomology are the unramified classes and the Kummer classes of units. For \(\ell=5\) the two orders are \(5\) and \(25\).

§34.3. Modules over the Kisin ring Tag 239M

The classical module has a Frobenius isomorphism after localization. A basis over the chart ring, described below, gives an integral inclusion after multiplication by a power of an Eisenstein element.

Write \(\mathbb C_\ell^\flat\) for the tilt, whose ring of integers is \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_\ell^\flat}=\varprojlim_{y\mapsto y^\ell} \mathcal O_{\mathbb C_\ell}/\ell\), and put \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}=W(\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_\ell^\flat})\). Brackets denote Teichmüller lifts.

Tag 239N

Theorem 34.3.1 (Breuil–Kisin modules). Choose a uniformizer \(\varpi_v\) of \(F_v\) with compatible \(\ell\)-power roots. Put \(\mathfrak S=W(k)[[z]]\) for the residue field \(k\) of \(F_v\), let \(\mathrm{Eis}\) be the Eisenstein polynomial of \(\varpi_v\), and let \(\varphi\) be Witt-vector Frobenius extended by \(\varphi(z)=z^\ell\). There is a fully faithful tensor functor from crystalline lattices \(L\) to finite free \(\mathfrak S\)-modules \(\mathfrak M(L)\) with a Frobenius isomorphism \(\varphi^*\mathfrak M(L)[1/\mathrm{Eis}]\cong \mathfrak M(L)[1/\mathrm{Eis}]\). Extend scalars via the map sending \(z\) to the Teichmüller lift of the chosen system of roots and then applying Witt Frobenius to the resulting scalar map. Over \(W(\mathbb C_\ell^\flat)\) the module is identified with \(L\otimes W(\mathbb C_\ell^\flat)\) compatibly with Frobenius and the Galois group fixing the chosen roots.

The functor is classical [Ki][BMS, Theorem 4.4]. Its value on \(\mathbb Z_\ell(1)\) is \(\mathfrak S\{1\}\), free of rank one with Frobenius given in coordinates by a unit multiple of \(y\mapsto \varphi(y)/\mathrm{Eis}\) [BMS, Example 4.2 and Corollary 4.33].

For the chart form, choose a compatible sequence of primitive \(\ell^j\)-th roots of unity and write \(\epsilon=(1,\zeta_\ell,\zeta_{\ell^2},\ldots)\) in the tilt. Put \(\tau=[\epsilon^{1/\ell}]-1\). Fontaine’s map \(\theta\colon A_{\mathrm{inf}}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_\ell}\) sends \([\epsilon]\) to \(1\) and \([\epsilon^{1/\ell}]\) to \(\zeta_\ell\). Its kernel is generated by \(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}[\epsilon^{1/\ell}]^i\) [BMS, Example 3.16]. In the classical description, \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\) is the completion of \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\ell]\) at the kernel of the extension of \(\theta\), and \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}\) is its fraction field [Co].

A Kummer chart has a ring \(\mathfrak S=W(k)[[z]]\subset A_{\mathrm{inf}}\), with \(k\) a finite field of characteristic \(\ell\) and \(z\) a nonunit Teichmüller lift. Its Eisenstein element \(\mathrm{Eis}\) is \(z^d\) times a unit plus \(\ell\), with \(d\ge 1\), and generates \(\ker\theta\). The subgroup \(\Gamma'\) of \(G_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\) fixing \(W(k)\) pointwise and fixing \(\theta(z)\) lies in \(G_{F_v}\), has finite index prime to \(\ell\) there, and contains a neighborhood of the identity in \(G_{F_v}\). For every integer \(m\ge 0\) some \(g\in\Gamma'\) has \(g(z)=[\epsilon]^m z\). Take \(L\) crystalline on \(\Gamma'\) and let \(h\) be the least nonnegative bound for the absolute values of its jumps on \(\Gamma'\). Write \(N_A\) for the submodule of \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}[1/\tau]\otimes L\) consisting of vectors \(y\) with \(f(\varphi^j y)\in B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\) for every \(j\ge 1\) and every \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}\)-linear functional \(f\colon W(\mathbb C_\ell^\flat)\otimes L\to B_{\mathrm{dR}}\) whose restriction to \(1\otimes L\) is \(\Gamma'\)-equivariant. Frobenius acts on the Witt vectors and fixes the lattice factor.

A chart frame is a basis over \(\mathfrak S\) of a submodule \(N\subseteq N_A\) that is also a basis of \(N_A\) over \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}\). The chart frame theorem [GL] gives such an \(N\) of rank \(\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}L\). In \(W(\mathbb C_\ell^\flat)\otimes L\) the inclusions are \[\tau^h N_A\subseteq A_{\mathrm{inf}}\otimes L,\qquad \tau^h(A_{\mathrm{inf}}\otimes L)\subseteq N_A,\qquad \mathrm{Eis}^h\varphi N\subseteq N .\] The chart description and the frame consequence are taken as given.

§34.4. Limits with bounded jumps Tag 239P

The next two statements give conditions under which a limit is crystalline.

Tag 239Q

Theorem 34.4.1 (Crystallinity from finite quotients). Let \(r\) be a positive integer. A lattice \(L\) is crystalline with jumps in \([-r,0]\) if, for every \(n\ge 1\), \(L/\ell^n L\) is isomorphic as a \(G_{F_v}\)-module to a quotient of two stable lattices in a crystalline representation with jumps in \([-r,0]\).

The lattice theorem is Liu’s [Liu], with the unramified base case due to Berger [Be]. The jumps use the sign of Definition 34.1.2.

For the trace form, fix a valuation ring \(A\) above \(\ell\) on the algebraic closure \(\overline F\), with stabilizer \(D_A\) in \(G_F=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline F/F)\). Fix \(\iota_\ell\colon\mathbb C\simeq\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\). A local datum is a field embedding \(j\colon\overline F\hookrightarrow \overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) pulling back the valuation ring of \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) to \(A\), and a homomorphism \(j_A\colon D_A\to G_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\) giving a topological isomorphism onto the pointwise fixer of \(j(F)\), with \(j_A(s)(j(y))=j(sy)\) for \(s\in D_A\) and \(y\in\overline F\).

Let \(r\) and \(\rho_{\eta i}\) be continuous finite-dimensional representations of \(G_F\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\). Here \(I\) is an index set and \(i\) ranges over a finite set \(I_\eta\) for each \(\eta\in I\). Fix \(a_{\eta i}\in\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\). Suppose \(\sum_{i\in I_\eta}a_{\eta i}\operatorname{tr}\rho_{\eta i}\) converges uniformly on \(G_F\) to \(\operatorname{tr}r\) along a nontrivial filter on \(I\), and \((y,y')\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(yy')\) is nondegenerate on the \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\)-span of the matrices of \(r\). For a local representation of \(D_A\) on a space \(V_{\mathrm{loc}}\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\), use Definition 34.1.1 with \(V_{\mathrm{loc}}\) in place of \(L\) and \(D_A\) acting on \(B_{\max}\) through \(j_A\). Assume each \(\rho_{\eta i}|_{D_A}\) is crystalline for every local datum.

For \(g\in G_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\), let \(\gamma\) be \(g\) followed by inclusion into \(\mathbb C_\ell\). The multiplicity of \(u\in\mathbb Z\) at \(\gamma\) is the dimension over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) of the space of \(\gamma\)-semilinear maps \(f\colon V_{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb C_\ell\) satisfying \[f(sy)=\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(j_A(s))^{-u}j_A(s)(f(y))\] for each \(y\in V_{\mathrm{loc}}\) and all \(s\) sufficiently near the identity for that \(y\). The multiplicity is recorded as zero if the dimension is infinite.

Assume either that \(I\) is finite or that there is one family of finite multisets \(\mathcal J_\sigma\), indexed by embeddings \(\sigma\colon F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\), with the following property. For every \(\eta\in I\), \(i\in I_\eta\), local datum with embedding \(j\), and \(g\in G_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\), the jump multiplicities of \(\rho_{\eta i}|_{D_A}\) at \(\gamma\) are those of \(\mathcal J_{\iota_\ell^{-1}\circ g^{-1}\circ j|_F}\). Then \(r|_{D_A}\) is crystalline for every local datum. The trace criterion is read from the proof [GL].

A sequence of Tate twists shows the need for control of jumps. For odd \(\ell\), the units of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) are the product \(\mu_{\ell-1}\times(1+\ell\mathbb Z_\ell)\), with \(\mu_{\ell-1}\) the \((\ell-1)\)-st roots of unity. A continuous character factoring through \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) whose principal-unit part is \(y\mapsto y^s=\exp(s\log y)\), for \(s\in\mathbb Z_\ell\), can be Hodge–Tate only if \(s\in\mathbb Z\), by the rank-one Sen weight criterion [Sen].

Tag 239S

Problem 34.4.2. Take \(\ell\) odd and \(k_n\) as in Problem 34.4.1. §34.5. A chart for a Tate twist Tag 239T

The rank-one calculation compares the chart module with \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}\otimes L\). In \(B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+\) the classical cyclotomic period \(t_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\log[\epsilon]\) is a uniformizer with \(g(t_{\mathrm{cyc}})=\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)t_{\mathrm{cyc}}\), and \((B_{\mathrm{dR}})^{G_{F_v}}=F_v\) [Fo; Co].
