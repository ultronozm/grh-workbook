\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
Discrete valuation rings and lengths of modules; the elementary divisor theorem.  The Chebotarev density theorem and the Brauer--Nesbitt theorem \cite[(30.16)]{CR}.  From Chapter~\ref{cg-chap}, \emph{Congruence modules}: the congruence module \(\Cg(\,\cdot\,;\,\cdot\,)\) and Problems~\ref{cg-pr:symmetry} and~\ref{cg-pr:denominator}.  From Chapter~\ref{ec-chap}, \emph{Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes}: Problem~\ref{ec-pr:diag} and Ribet's lemma, Theorem~\ref{ec-thm:ribet-lemma}.  From Chapter~\ref{hk-chap}, \emph{Hecke operators and theta lifts}: Section~\ref{hk-sec:brandt}, with Problems~\ref{hk-pr:brandt} and~\ref{hk-pr:basis}.  From Chapter~\ref{gr-chap}: Section~\ref{gr-sec:es} on \(X_0(11)\).  For Section~\ref{sec:classes}, Chapters~\ref{tt-chap} and~\ref{gs-chap} on Tate twists and odd classes; Chapter~\ref{pc-chap} is background.  For Section~\ref{sec:theorem}, the data, the single lifts \(u_{\vartheta,j}\), the good forms and the twists of Chapter~\ref{da-chap}, Section~\ref{da-sec:objects}, whose notation we use.

To compare lifts at the trivial twist with lifts at a twist chosen for each \(\iota \mid \ell\), one needs to control the denominators of the orthogonal projections of integral test forms onto spaces of lifts.  How large can they be?  In Chapter~\ref{cg-chap} the exponent of a congruence module gave the scalar denominator of a projection onto an eigenspace, and in Chapter~\ref{ec-chap} a congruence produced a Galois cohomology class.  Here we measure a denominator by the determinant of an operator that clears it.  Sections~\ref{sec:clearing} and~\ref{sec:eleven} are models, the second the smallest example, a class set with two elements; Section~\ref{sec:classes} describes the Galois side; Theorem~\ref{thm:denominators} is read from the rewritten Lean proof \cite{GL} of Alpöge's theorem.

\section{Clearing a projection}\label{sec:clearing}

Let \(\Oc\) be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\) and valuation \(v\), normalized by \(v(\varpi) = 1\) for a uniformizer \(\varpi\); we use \(v(0) = +\infty\).  Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional \(K\)-vector space with a lattice \(L\), and let \(V = Z \oplus Z'\) with projection \(\pr \colon V \to Z\) along \(Z'\).  Write \(\Cg(L; Z) := \pr(L)/(L \cap Z)\), as in Chapter~\ref{cg-chap}.  An operator \(C \in \mathrm{End}_K(Z)\) \emph{clears} \(\pr\) on a subset \(Y \subseteq L\) if \(C(\pr(y)) \in L\) for every \(y \in Y\).  A scalar \(c\) clears \(\pr\) on \(L\) when \(c\,\pr(L) \subseteq L\); an operator may clear different directions of \(Z\) by different amounts, and we measure it by \(v(\det C)\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:clear-det}
\(C\) clears \(\pr\) on \(L\) if and only if \(C(\pr(L)) \subseteq L \cap Z\).  The least value of \(v(\det C)\) over such \(C\) is \(\len_\Oc \Cg(L; Z)\).
\end{problem}

The operators that clear \(\pr\) on a given \(Y\) form an \(\Oc\)-module; the definitions make sense for any valuation ring \(\Oc\) and any \(\Oc\)-submodule \(L\) of \(V\).  The next problem combines clearing operators that are diagonal in one basis.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:diagonal}
Let \(\Oc\) be a valuation ring with infinite residue field and valuation \(v\), such as the ring of integers of \(\bar\Q_\ell\) with \(v(\ell) = 1\), and let \(b_1, \dots, b_n\) be a basis of \(Z\).  Let \(C^{(1)}, \dots, C^{(n)}\) clear \(\pr\) on \(Y\), with \(C^{(j)} b_i = \lambda_{ji} b_i\).  Then some \(C = \sum_j u_j C^{(j)}\) with \(u_j \in \Oc\) has \(C b_i = \mu_i b_i\) with \(v(\mu_i) \le v(\lambda_{ii})\) for every \(i\); it clears \(\pr\) on \(Y\).
\end{problem}

Now let \(K\) be a subfield of \(\C\) stable under complex conjugation, and \(\Oc\) a discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\).  Let \(V\) carry a positive definite hermitian form \(\langle\,,\rangle\), linear in the first variable, let \(Z' = Z^\perp\), and let \(T\) be a self-adjoint operator on \(V\) with \(T(L) \subseteq L\), diagonalizable with eigenvalues in \(\Oc\), such that \(Z \subseteq \ker(T - \lambda)\).  The vectors of \(Z^\perp\) with the eigenvalue of \(Z\),
\[
  F := Z^\perp \cap \ker(T - \lambda) ,
\]
are the \emph{false lifts} for \(T\).  Let \(\Pi(X) := \prod_\mu (X - \mu)\), the product over the eigenvalues \(\mu \ne \lambda\) of \(T\), each taken once.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:hecke-clear}
For \(y \in L\) orthogonal to \(F\), \(\Pi(T)\,y = \Pi(\lambda)\,\pr(y)\).  Hence the scalar \(\Pi(\lambda)\) clears \(\pr\) on the \(y \in L\) orthogonal to \(F\), and the determinant of the scalar \(\Pi(\lambda)\) acting on \(Z\) has valuation \(\dim Z \cdot v(\Pi(\lambda))\).
\end{problem}

The cost \(v(\Pi(\lambda)) = \sum_\mu v(\lambda - \mu)\) counts congruences between the eigenvalue of \(Z\) and the other eigenvalues.  The restriction to vectors orthogonal to the false lifts cannot be dropped.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:false-needed}
Let \(V = K^2\) with \(\langle x, y \rangle = x_1\bar y_1 + x_2\bar y_2\), \(L = \Oc^2\), \(T = \lambda\cdot 1\), \(Z = K(1,1)\), and suppose \(v(2) > 0\).  Then \(F = K(1,-1)\), no unit \(c \in \Oc^\times\) clears \(\pr\) on \(L\), and \(c = 1\) clears it on the \(y \in L\) orthogonal to \(F\).
\end{problem}

\section{Two classes}\label{sec:eleven}

We take the class set of Section~\ref{hk-sec:brandt} of Chapter~\ref{hk-chap}: the two left ideal classes of a maximal order in the quaternion algebra over \(\Q\) ramified at \(11\) and \(\infty\), indexed by their right orders, with \(w_1 = 2\) and \(w_2 = 3\).  Functions on the two classes are vectors \(\varphi = (\varphi(1), \varphi(2))\), with
\[
  \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle := \frac{\varphi(1)\overline{\varphi'(1)}}{w_1} + \frac{\varphi(2)\overline{\varphi'(2)}}{w_2} ,
\]
and the Brandt matrix \(B(\ell)\), for a prime \(\ell \ne 11\), acts by \((B(\ell)\varphi)(i) = \sum_j B_{ij}(\ell)\varphi(j)\).  Recall from that section that \(B(\ell)\) has integer entries, that \(w_jB_{ij}(\ell) = w_iB_{ji}(\ell)\) and \(\sum_j B_{ij}(\ell) = \ell + 1\), and that \(B(\ell)\) is the action of \(T_\ell\) on \(M_2(\Gamma_0(11))\), which is spanned by an Eisenstein series of \(T_\ell\)-eigenvalue \(\ell + 1\) and by the form called \(f\) there; we write
\[
  f_{11} := q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1-q^n)^2(1-q^{11n})^2 = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n .
\]
By Problem~\ref{hk-pr:brandt}, \(B(2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(B(3) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\).  The constant function \(1\) plays the role of the lifts: \(Z\) is the line of constant functions, and \(\pr\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(Z\).  The integral functions are \(L := \Z^2\).  Apply Section~\ref{sec:clearing} over \(K = \Q\) with \(\Oc = \Z_{(\ell)}\); the two orthogonal lines and the projection extend by scalars to \(\Q_\ell\), where we use the lattice \(\Z_\ell^2\) and the same formula without the bar.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:eleven-adjoint}
\(B(\ell)\) is self-adjoint for \(\langle\,,\rangle\), \(B(\ell)1 = (\ell + 1)1\), and \(\varphi_{11} := (2, -3)\) spans \(Z^\perp\) and satisfies \(B(\ell)\varphi_{11} = a_\ell\varphi_{11}\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:eleven-pr}
\(\pr(\varphi) = \frac15\bigl(3\varphi(1) + 2\varphi(2)\bigr)\cdot 1\), and \(5\,\pr = B(2) + 2 = B(3) + 1\).  Hence \(\pr(L) = \frac15\Z\cdot 1\) and \(\Cg(L; Z) \cong \Z/5\Z\).  The least value of \(v_5(\det C)\) for \(C\) clearing \(\pr\) on \(\Z_5^2\) is \(1\); for \(\ell \ne 5\) the identity clears \(\pr\) on \(\Z_\ell^2\).  The scalar \(\Pi(\lambda)\) of Problem~\ref{pr:hecke-clear} is \(5\) both for \(T = B(2)\) and for \(T = B(3)\).
\end{problem}

Read backwards, the denominator forces a congruence modulo \(5\) at every prime \(\ell \ne 11\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:eleven-cong}
\(B(\ell) - a_\ell = (\ell + 1 - a_\ell)\pr\).  Hence \(a_\ell \equiv 1 + \ell \pmod 5\) for every prime \(\ell \ne 11\); for example \(a_{13} = 4\) (Problem~\ref{gr-pr:coeffs}).
\end{problem}

The denominator \(5\) is the numerator of the mass \(1/w_1 + 1/w_2 = (11 - 1)/12 = 5/6\).  The next problem gives the denominator at primes dividing none of the weights.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:mass}
Let \(I\) be a nonempty finite set with positive integers \(w_i\), let \(\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle := \sum_i \varphi(i)\varphi'(i)/w_i\) on \(\Q_\ell^I\), let \(Z\) be the line of constant functions, and let \(\ell\) be a prime dividing no \(w_i\).  Then \(\mathfrak m := \sum_i 1/w_i\) has \(v_\ell(\mathfrak m) \ge 0\), and \(\Cg(\Z_\ell^I; Z) \cong \Z/\ell^{v_\ell(\mathfrak m)}\Z\).
\end{problem}

For the quaternion algebra ramified at a prime \(p\) and \(\infty\) the mass is \((p-1)/12\), and Mazur showed that the index of his Eisenstein ideal is the numerator of \((p-1)/12\) \cite{Ma}.  There is also an integrality question for the pairing of a cleared form with an integral form.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:pairing}
In the setting of Problem~\ref{pr:mass}, drop the condition on \(\ell\), and let \(n_0\) be a common multiple of the \(w_i\).  If \(C\) clears \(\pr\) on \(Y \subseteq \Z_\ell^I\), then so does \(n_0C\), and \(\langle n_0C\pr(y), \varphi' \rangle \in \Z_\ell\) for \(y \in Y\) and \(\varphi' \in \Z_\ell^I\).  In the class set with two elements, \(6\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \in \Z\) for \(\varphi, \varphi' \in \Z^2\).
\end{problem}

\section{Lifts and the classes they produce}\label{sec:classes}

Where does the congruence of Problem~\ref{pr:eleven-cong} come from?  The constant function carries the Galois representation \(1 \oplus \cyc\), where \(\cyc\) is the \(5\)-adic cyclotomic character; its trace at an arithmetic Frobenius \(\Frob_\ell\) is \(1 + \ell\) for \(\ell \ne 5\).  The form \(f_{11}\) carries the representation \(\rho_{11}\) of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/\Q)\) on the rational \(5\)-adic Tate module \(T_5(X_0(11)) \otimes \Q_5\), whose trace at \(\Frob_\ell\) is \(a_\ell\) for \(\ell \nmid 55\) (Section~\ref{gr-sec:es} of Chapter~\ref{gr-chap}).  Let \(\omega\) be the reduction of \(\cyc\) modulo \(5\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:residual}
The semisimplification of the reduction of \(\rho_{11}\) modulo \(5\) is \(1 \oplus \omega\), and \(\omega(c) = -1\) for every complex conjugation \(c\).
\end{problem}

The representation \(\rho_{11}\) is irreducible: it is semisimple and its commutant is \(\Q_5\), by Faltings' theorem \cite{Fa}, since \(X_0(11)\) has no complex multiplication (its \(j\)-invariant \(-122023936/161051\) is not an integer).  Ribet's lemma, Theorem~\ref{ec-thm:ribet-lemma}, then gives a nonzero class with values in \(\F_5(\omega)\) or \(\F_5(\omega^{-1})\), an odd character.

In the general setting a single theta lift takes values in forms on a standard definite unitary space for the CM extension \(E/E^+\).  For a main summand \(R\), the trace identity in \texttt{Selmer.IsMainSummand} of the rewritten Lean proof \cite{GL} concerns the normalized representation \(S\) attached to a compatible twisted lift eigenform.  If the tame twist is \(\xi\), \(\psi\) is the \(\ell\)-adic avatar of the base change of \(\xi^{-1}\) to \(E\), and \(t'\) is the rank difference, then \(\mathrm{tr}\,S(g)=\psi(g)\,\mathrm{tr}\,R(g)+\sum_{0\le e<t'}\cyc(g)^{-e}\) for every \(g\in\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E)\), where \(\cyc\) is the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character.  The constant function above has this shape, with no \(R\) and two characters, after a twist by the inverse \(5\)-adic cyclotomic character.  Ribet's method deals with extensions between such summands: for an extension of \(\cyc^{-e'}\) by \(\cyc^{-e}\) modulo \(\ell^\nu\), the extension cocycle takes values in \((\Oc/\ell^\nu)(-a)\), where \(a = e - e'\).  On cocycles \(z\) of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E)\) a complex conjugation \(c\) acts by \(z^c(g) := z(cgc)\); call \(z\) \emph{odd} if \(z^c = -z\) and \emph{even} if \(z^c = z\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:parity}
Let \(\ell\) be odd and \(\Oc\) the ring of integers of a finite extension of \(\Q_\ell\).  Every cocycle of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E^+)\) with values in \((\Oc/\ell^\nu)(-a)\) differs by a coboundary from one with \(z(c) = 0\).  For such \(z\), the restriction to \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E)\) satisfies \(z^c = (-1)^a z\).
\end{problem}

For odd \(\ell\) and odd \(a\), restriction from \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E^+)\) to \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E)\) therefore gives classes with odd representatives.  For the CM field \(E\subseteq\Q(\mu_{q_1d})\) of Chapter~\ref{da-chap}, Theorem~\ref{tt-thm:oddsha}, proved using Gauss sums as in Chapter~\ref{gs-chap}, gives one positive integer \(X\) for every prime \(\ell\), every coefficient ring \(\Oc\) allowed there, every \(0<a<t\) and every precision \(\nu\ge0\).  The \(\Oc\)-length of the module of continuous odd cocycles with values in \((\Oc/\ell^\nu)(-a)\), unramified off \(\ell\) and zero on the kernel of \(\cyc\) in every decomposition group above \(\ell\), is at most \(\operatorname{ord}_{\Oc}(X)\), with a uniformizer of order \(1\).  For \(a=0\) the same bound holds for the everywhere unramified homomorphisms with values in \(\Oc/\ell^\nu\).  This is \texttt{New.exists\_length\_shaOdd\_le}.  For the even cocycles occurring in the proof, \texttt{Selmer.exists\_isPrimeTriangle} supplies Taylor--Wiles places.  No order of a group of even classes occurs in Definition~\ref{def:clearing-bound}.  Detection of a class by a Frobenius element is the point; here it is for a Kummer class.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:kummer}
Let \(\ell\) be an odd prime, \(u \in \Q^\times\) not an \(\ell\)-th power, and \(\mathrm{kum}_u\) the Kummer class of \(u\) in \(H^1(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/\Q), \mu_\ell)\).  For a prime \(q \equiv 1 \pmod \ell\) with \(v_q(u) = 0\), \(\mathrm{kum}_u\) is unramified at \(q\), and \(\mathrm{kum}_u(\Frob_q) = 0\), a condition independent of the choice of \(\Frob_q\), if and only if \(u^{(q-1)/\ell} \equiv 1 \pmod q\).  There are infinitely many such \(q\) with \(\mathrm{kum}_u(\Frob_q) \ne 0\).  For \(\ell = 3\) and \(u = 2\), \(q = 7\) detects \(\mathrm{kum}_2\) and \(q = 31\) does not.
\end{problem}

Ribet's lemma needs an element on which the two characters differ (Problem~\ref{ec-pr:diag}).  For the characters \(\cyc^{-e}\), \(0 \le e < t\), this costs the valuations of the differences \(\cyc(\gamma)^{a} - 1\), \(0 < a < t\): for large \(\ell\) an element of inertia at \(\ell\) costs nothing, by Problem~\ref{pr:generator}, and otherwise a Frobenius element at a chosen place above a level prime costs at most \(v_\ell(\mathcal N)\), by Problem~\ref{pr:gl-order} with \(n = rt\), for the integer \(\mathcal N\) of Definition~\ref{def:clearing-bound}.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:generator}
Let \(g \in \Z_\ell^\times\) reduce to a generator of \(\F_\ell^\times\).  Then \(g^a - 1 \in \Z_\ell^\times\) for \(0 < a < \ell - 1\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gl-order}
Let \(q\) be a prime power and \(1 \le a \le n\).  Then \(q^a - 1\) divides \(\#\GL_n(\F_q) = q^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^n (q^i - 1)\).
\end{problem}

\section{The clearing bound}\label{sec:theorem}

We use the data of Chapter~\ref{da-chap}: the CM field \(E\) with \(f\) infinite places, the rank \(r\), \(t = 5r\), the gap \(\Delta = 2r^8\), the level primes \(p_0, p_1\), the level \(N\), the moduli \(q_1\) and \(d\), the chosen places \(\mathcal M\), the good idempotent \(e_0\), the single lifts \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) on good forms, and the twists \(\vartheta\) with modulus \(Q_\vartheta\) (Section~\ref{da-sec:objects}).  Fix a prime \(\ell\) and a ring isomorphism \(\iota \colon \C \simeq \bar\Q_\ell\), and write \(v\) for the valuation of \(\bar\Q_\ell\) with \(v(\ell) = 1\).  A form is \emph{integral at \(\iota\)} if its values, read through \(\iota\) in the \(\ell\)-adic gauge of its weight, have norm at most \(1\).

\begin{definition}\label{def:clearing-bound}
Let \(\mathcal N := \prod_{w \in \mathcal M} \#\GL_{rt}(k_w)\), \(n_\ell := v_\ell(\mathcal N)\), and \(o_\ell := v_\ell(X)\), where \(X\) is the integer \texttt{Selmer.oddClassBound} of the rewritten Lean proof, chosen for the length bound of Section~\ref{sec:classes}, with \(0 < X \le 2^{4(q_1d + t + 3)^{27}} h_E\).  Let \(\mathrm{loc}_\ell := 4f\,\mathrm{defect}_\ell\), with \(\mathrm{defect}_\ell\) an explicit number of the rewritten Lean proof depending only on the data and \(\ell\), not stated here, and put
\[
  \mathrm{tw}_\ell := (4f + 3)\,r\,n_\ell + 2^{-\ell}, \qquad \varepsilon_\ell := t\,o_\ell + 8ft\,n_\ell + 2n_\ell + \bigl((4t + 1)(\Delta/2)^{22} + \Delta/2\bigr)n_\ell .
\]
The \emph{clearing bound} is \(\beta_\ell := \mathrm{loc}_\ell + (1 + 2t)\,\mathrm{tw}_\ell + \varepsilon_\ell + n_\ell\).
\end{definition}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:budget}
Let \(r', t'\) be integers with \(1 \le r' \le r\) and \(0 \le t' \le t\), and let \(0 \le \mathrm{sel}, \mathrm{ram} \le \mathrm{tw}_\ell\).  Then
\[
  \mathrm{sel} + 2t'\,\mathrm{ram} + r'\,\mathrm{loc}_\ell + \varepsilon_\ell + n_\ell \le r'\beta_\ell .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:det-bound}
Let \(Z\) be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, \(n_0 \ge 0\) an integer, \(u_0 \ge 0\), and \(C \in \mathrm{End}_\C(Z)\) with \(v(n_0\iota(\mu)) \le u_0\) for every eigenvalue \(\mu\) of \(C\).  Then, with \(\lvert \ell \rvert_\ell = \ell^{-1}\), \(\lvert \iota(\det(n_0C)) \rvert_\ell \ge \exp(-\dim Z \cdot u_0 \log \ell)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:negligible}
The sum of \(2^{-\ell}\log \ell\) over all primes \(\ell\) is \(0.3776\ldots\), and for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0} \mathrm{tw}_\ell \log \ell \le (4f + 3)\,r \log \mathcal N + 0.378\).
\end{problem}

\begin{definition}\label{def:descent-pair}
A \emph{descent pair} \(\DP\) has two finite indexed families of places of \(E\), chosen places and twist places; all their entries are distinct, and none is the conjugate of any entry.  It has a hermitian space \(W\) of rank \(r'\) with signs on its basis indices, a natural number assigned to each index, an injection of those indices into the basis indices of the standard definite space \(V = I_{n'}\) with \(r' < n'\), column multiplicities \(k^\pm(\tau,i)\) at each infinite place \(\tau\) and height \(i \ge 0\), and a continuous unitary character \(\mu\) of \(U(1)(\mathbb A)\), trivial on rational points, with specified integral weight at each infinite place and specified tame characters at all finite places, trivial off the two families.  Its forms \(\mathcal A(\DP)\) are the algebraic forms on \(V\) of the weight, level and level character of \(\DP\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:false-lift}
Let \(\varepsilon\) be an element of the group algebra of \(\prod_w \GL_{r'}(k_w)\) over the chosen places of \(\DP\), and \(A \subseteq \mathcal A(\DP)\) a subspace.  For a split place \(w\) such that neither \(w\) nor \(\bar w\) is a place of \(\DP\), the \emph{false lifts} at \(w\) are the forms in \(\mathrm{im}(\varepsilon) \cap A\) killed by the Hecke polynomial operator of \(\DP\) at \(w\) at the value \(\bigl(\nu_w q_w^{n'-r'-1}\bigr)^{-1}\) expected of a lift, where \(q_w = \#k_w\) and \(\nu_w\) is the rank-\(r'\) Frobenius value of the character of the source space \(W\).  A form is \emph{orthogonal to the false lifts} for \((\DP, \varepsilon, A)\) if it is orthogonal to the false lifts at every such \(w\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:denominator}
Let \(\varepsilon\) be as in Definition~\ref{def:false-lift}, \(Z \subseteq \mathcal A(\DP)\), with orthogonal projection \(\pr\), and \(\beta \in \mathbb R\).  The projection onto \(Z\) has \emph{denominator at most \(\beta\)} at \(\iota\) if some operator \(C_\ell\) on \(Z\) has \(\lvert \iota(\det C_\ell) \rvert_\ell \ge \exp(-\dim Z \cdot r' \cdot \beta)\) and, for every form \(y\) integral at \(\iota\) and orthogonal to the false lifts for \((\DP, \varepsilon, Z^\perp)\), \(C_\ell(\pr(y))\) is integral at \(\iota\) and \(\lvert \iota\langle C_\ell(\pr(y)), y' \rangle \rvert_\ell \le 1\) for every form \(y'\) integral at every ring map \(\C \to \C_\ell\).  Here \(\langle\,,\rangle\) is the class sum of the pointwise inner products, linear in the first variable.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:clears}
For a twist \(\vartheta\) and an integer \(j\ge0\), let \(Z\) be the range of the single lift restricted to good forms at \((\vartheta,j)\).  This map is \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) for \(1\le j\le m\) and is zero otherwise.  For \(\beta \in \mathbb R\), the projection onto \(Z\) \emph{can be cleared with exponent \(\beta\)} at \(\iota\) if there is a complex linear operator \(C_\ell\) on \(Z\) with \(\lvert\iota(\det C_\ell)\rvert_\ell \ge \exp(-\dim Z \cdot r \cdot \beta)\) such that \(C_\ell(\pr(y))\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(y\) integral at \(\iota\) and orthogonal to the false lifts for the \(j\)-th descent pair of \(\vartheta\), \(e_0\) and \(Z^\perp\).  Such an operator \emph{clears the projection} with exponent \(\beta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:main-summand}
Let \(0 \le t' \le t\) be an integer, let \(J\) assign a multiset of integers to each complex embedding of \(E\), and let \(R\) be a continuous \(\ell\)-adic representation of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar\Q/E)\) of rank \(1 \le r' \le r\).  It is \emph{main-summand-like} for \((t',J)\) if the following conditions hold.  The trace pairing on the span of its image is nondegenerate, and \(\mathrm{tr}\,R(cgc) = \cyc(g)^{-\max(t'-1,0)}\mathrm{tr}\,R(g^{-1})\) for every \(g\).  At every prolongation of every finite place of \(E\), \(R\) has finite inertia image away from \(\ell\) and is potentially crystalline above \(\ell\), with jumps \(J\) at the embeddings corresponding to each \(\ell\)-adic local datum.  Away from \(p_0p_1\) it is unramified off \(\ell\) and crystalline above \(\ell\).  The jumps satisfy \texttt{Selmer.IsMainSummandWeights} for the data and \((r',t',J)\).  For every \(w \mid p_0p_1\) away from \(\ell\) and every place above \(w\) in \(\bar\Q\), there are an inertia element \(\gamma\) and a natural number \(N_w\) prime to the residue characteristic such that \(R(s^{N_w}) = 1\) for every inertia element \(s\) there; at a chosen \(w\), \(\gamma\) is the distinguished tame generator at the specified place above it; and both \(\det(R(\gamma)-1)\) and \(\det(R((c\gamma c)^{-1})-1)\) have valuation at most \(r'n_\ell\).  For every sufficiently large coefficient ring \(\Oc\), the image of every \(\Oc\)-lattice model spans all \(r'\)-by-\(r'\) matrices over \(\Oc\), and for each such model some \(g\) acts by a scalar and satisfies \(\cyc(g) = p_i^{r'}\) for some \(i \in \{0,1\}\).  Finally some inertia element above \(p_0p_1\) away from \(\ell\) acts by a scalar \(y\) with \(\lvert y^{\ell^a j} - 1\rvert_\ell = 1\) for every integer \(a \ge 0\) and \(1 \le j \le 4r' + 2\).  A representation \(R\) is a \emph{main summand} for a descent pair \(\DP_0\) of ranks \(r'<n'\) and a family \(z=(z(w,i))\) of complex numbers indexed by finite places \(w\) and integers \(i\ge0\) if it is main-summand-like for \((n'-r',J)\) for some \(J\), and the trace identity of Section~\ref{sec:classes} holds for every tame character \(\xi\), every pair \(\DP\) on the same index sets whose character is that of \(\DP_0\) times \(\xi\), every eigenform of \(\DP\) satisfying the lift Hecke polynomial identity for \(z\), and every representation attached to its Hecke polynomials, after normalization for \(\DP\).  This is \texttt{Selmer.IsMainSummand}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:lift-setting}
A descent pair \(\DP\) with a tuple of polynomials over the residue fields of its chosen places is in \emph{lift setting} for \(\vartheta\) if its indexed chosen and twist places agree with those of the data and of \(\vartheta\); its tame part is the \(j\)-th power of the level character of the data times that of \(\vartheta\), for some \(1 \le j \le m\); \(r'\) and \(n' - r'\) are odd, with \(r' \le r\) and \(n' - r' \le t\); each polynomial is monic and squarefree of degree \(r'\) with nonzero constant term, and none of its roots becomes \(1\) on multiplication by the element of \(k_w^\times\) naming the inverse tame character of \(\DP\) at its place \(w\); and above each \(p_i\) one polynomial is primitive with \(r' = r\), or equal to \(X - 1\) with \(r' = 1\).
\end{definition}

A twist \(\vartheta\) is \emph{auxiliary with ramification at most \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\)} if it satisfies the condition of the rewritten Lean proof \texttt{Selmer.IsAuxTwist} for the places dividing \(\ell p_0p_1N\), its modulus \(Q_\vartheta\), the avatar through \(\iota\) of the base change of \(\vartheta^{-1}\) to \(E\), and the bound \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\).  A \emph{lift generator} for \((\vartheta, \DP, \varepsilon, Z)\) with Selmer bound \(s\) is a form \(y\) satisfying \texttt{Selmer.IsLiftGenerator} for \(\vartheta\), \(\DP\), \(\varepsilon\), \(Z\) and \(s\); it lies in \(Z\) and in the range of the idempotent of a tuple in lift setting (Definition~\ref{def:lift-setting}), with the entire range of this idempotent contained in \(\mathrm{im}(\varepsilon)\).  The remaining conditions relate a lift Hecke polynomial identity for \(y\) to a main summand for a second pair whose character, multiplied by \(\vartheta\), is the character of \(\DP\), and impose a Selmer bound on its twisted main summand.  We use the full predicate \texttt{Selmer.IsLiftGenerator}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:denominators}
Let \(\ell\) and \(\iota\) be as above, and let \(\vartheta\) be a twist that is auxiliary with ramification at most \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\).  Let \(\DP\) be a descent pair of ranks \(r' < n'\), \(\varepsilon\) an element of the group algebra of \(\prod_w \GL_{r'}(k_w)\) over its chosen places, and \(Z \subseteq \mathcal A(\DP)\) a subspace contained in the span of the lift generators for \((\vartheta, \DP, \varepsilon, Z)\) with Selmer bound \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\).  Then the projection onto \(Z\) has denominator at most \(\beta_\ell \log \ell\) at \(\iota\), in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:denominator}.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Sketch.}  For \(Z = 0\) the identity serves.  Otherwise write \(t'=n'-r'\) and choose a basis of \(Z\) among the lift generators.  For each basis vector Ribet's method gives a clearing operator diagonal in this basis whose eigenvalue on that vector has valuation at most \((1+2t')\mathrm{tw}_\ell+r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell+\varepsilon_\ell\) through \(\iota\).  Problem~\ref{pr:diagonal} gives one integral combination with this bound on every eigenvalue.  Multiplication by \(\mathcal N\) clears the pairings as in Problem~\ref{pr:pairing}, and Problems~\ref{pr:budget} and~\ref{pr:det-bound} give the determinant bound.  The corresponding statement of the rewritten Lean proof is \texttt{Selmer.exists\_operator\_det\_clearingBound}.

The clearing operator bounds the \(\ell\)-adic norm of the determinant of the comparison operator after projection onto the twisted lift space.  For \(1 \le j \le m\), \(\varepsilon = e_0\) and \(Z\) the image of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\), so that \(r' = r\), Theorem~\ref{thm:denominators} gives an operator that clears the projection in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:clears} with every exponent \(\beta \ge \beta_\ell\log\ell\), provided \(\vartheta\) is auxiliary with ramification at most \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\) and every eigensystem occurring in a nonzero good form on \(W_m\) has a main summand, for the \(j\)-th descent pair of the trivial twist, whose twist by the avatar of \(\vartheta^{-1}\) satisfies the Selmer bound of a lift generator with \(s = \mathrm{tw}_\ell\).  Chapter~\ref{tw-chap} chooses an \(m\)-fold twist \(\tau\) for each \(\iota \mid \ell\), and at index \(j\) one takes \(\vartheta = \tau^j\).

\section*{Hints}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:clearing}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:clear-det}] \(C\) maps \(Z\) to \(Z\).  For the minimum, \(\pr(L)\) and \(L \cap Z\) are lattices in \(Z\); use elementary divisors.
\item[\ref{pr:diagonal}] Choose the \(u_j\) generically modulo the maximal ideal.
\item[\ref{pr:hecke-clear}] Decompose \(y\) into eigenvectors of \(T\); \(\ker(T - \lambda) = Z \oplus F\) is orthogonal to the other eigenspaces.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:eleven}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:eleven-adjoint}] Apply the balance and row-sum identities to the two orthogonal lines.
\item[\ref{pr:eleven-pr}] Apply the projection onto constants to the coordinate functions; then Problem~\ref{pr:clear-det}.
\item[\ref{pr:eleven-cong}] Compare both sides on a basis.
\item[\ref{pr:mass}] Compute \(\pr\) on the indicator functions.
\item[\ref{pr:pairing}] Inspect each weight in the class sum.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:classes}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:residual}] The characteristic polynomial of \(\rho_{11}(\Frob_\ell)\) is \(X^2 - a_\ell X + \ell\) for \(\ell \nmid 55\) (Section~\ref{gr-sec:es} of Chapter~\ref{gr-chap}).  Reduce on the lattice \(T_5(X_0(11))\); \(\rho_{11}\) is unramified outside \(55\).  Use Problem~\ref{pr:eleven-cong}, the Chebotarev density theorem and the Brauer--Nesbitt theorem for characteristic polynomials.
\item[\ref{pr:parity}] Evaluate the cocycle relation on \(c \cdot c = 1\) and on \(c \cdot g \cdot c\), using \(\cyc(c) = -1\).
\item[\ref{pr:kummer}] \(\mathrm{kum}_u(\Frob_q)\) is read off from the action of \(\Frob_q\) on \(u^{1/\ell}\) modulo a prime above \(q\).  Since \(u\) is not an \(\ell\)-th power, \([\Q(u^{1/\ell}):\Q] = \ell\), so \(\Q(\mu_\ell, u^{1/\ell})/\Q(\mu_\ell)\) has degree \(\ell\), as \(\gcd(\ell, \ell - 1) = 1\); apply the Chebotarev density theorem to its nontrivial elements.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:theorem}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:budget}] Use \(r' \ge 1\) and \(t' \le t\).
\item[\ref{pr:negligible}] Use the factorization of \(\mathcal N\).  For the prime sum, sum through \(31\) and bound the rest by \(\sum_{k > 31} k\,2^{-k}\).
\end{description}
