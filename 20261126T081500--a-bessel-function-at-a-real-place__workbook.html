The real Bessel functions in Theorem 40.3.1 supply the local factors in the Gram operators for Theorem 39.4.2. The proof averages a matrix coefficient over the Bessel subgroup, using a position at which the average is large enough to normalize. The half-plane and two unitary groups give models for choosing the position. The period determines the squared norm and the correlation of the average.

Prerequisites.

Use Chapter 10 for the half-plane and Chapters 36, 37 and 39 for the data, weights and Bessel subgroup, including the Bessel tuple \((v_1,\dots,v_\varrho,z)\) of Section 39.1. Assume the discrete-series facts of Chapter 41 and Schur’s lemma [Se]. Inner products are linear in the first variable, as in Chapter 40.

§42.1. The half-plane Tag 23D8

The first model recovers the seed of a holomorphic Poincaré series from a matrix coefficient. Use integers \(k\ge2\) and \(m\ge1\) for the weight and index in this section. On the upper half-plane let \(e(z)=\exp(2\pi\mathrm iz)\) and \(j(g,z)=g_{21}z+g_{22}\) for \(g\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\) acting by fractional linear transformations. Write \[n(w)=\begin{pmatrix}1&w\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad a(y)=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt y,1/\sqrt y),\qquad N_{\mathbb R}=\{n(w):w\in\mathbb R\}.\] The representation \((\pi_k(g)f)(z)=j(g^{-1},z)^{-k}f(g^{-1}z)\) and its unit kernel vector \(v_{\mathrm i}\) are as in Chapter 10, with the slash action on \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\). Use the conjugate coefficient \(\mathsf c(g)=\langle v_{\mathrm i},\pi_k(g)v_{\mathrm i}\rangle\) and formal degree \(d=(k-1)/(4\pi)\) for \(dw\,dy/y^2\) times stabilizer probability (Problem 10.5.5).

Tag 23D9

Problem 42.1.1. For \(w\in\mathbb R\) and \(y>0\), \[\mathsf c(n(w)a(y))=y^{k/2}\bigl((1+y-\mathrm iw)/2\bigr)^{-k}.\] Also \(\mathsf c(1)=1\) and \(\mathsf c(g^{-1})=\overline{\mathsf c(g)}\).

Fix \(y_0>0\) and use \(y_*=a(y_0)\) as the position. Average against the inverse of the character \(n(w)\mapsto e(mw)\) of \(N_{\mathbb R}\), writing \(n(-w)\) as the integration variable. \[W_{y_*}(g)=\int_{\mathbb R}\mathsf c(y_*^{-1}n(-w)g)e(mw)\,dw .\]

Tag 23DA

Problem 42.1.2. At \(g=a(y)\) the average is \[\frac{(4\pi)^k(yy_0)^{k/2}m^{k-1}}{(k-1)!} e^{-2\pi m(y+y_0)} .\] Check by machine that at \((k,m,y_0,y)=(4,1,0.3,0.5)\) the value is \(0.613576\) to six decimal places.

Tag 23DB

Problem 42.1.3. For arbitrary \(g\), the average is \(e(m\,g\mathrm i)j(g,\mathrm i)^{-k}\) times \((4\pi)^k y_0^{k/2}m^{k-1}e^{-2\pi my_0}/(k-1)!\).

Write \(P(s)=W_{y_*}(y_*)/y_0\), where \(s=4\pi my_0\). The next problem relates the period to the squared norm on \(N_{\mathbb R}\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)\).

Tag 23DC

Problem 42.1.4. One has \(d\int_0^\infty |W_{y_*}(a(y))|^2\,dy/y^2=W_{y_*}(y_*)\), with common value about \(0.776107\) when \((k,m,y_0)=(4,1,0.3)\). The set \(\{w: |w|/y_0\le1\}\) has volume \(2y_0\), and \[P(s)=\frac{4\pi s^{k-1}e^{-s}}{(k-1)!}>0.\] At \(s=1\) check \(P(1)\approx1.16\cdot10^{-7}\) for \(k=12\) and \(4.95\cdot10^{-156}\) for \(k=100\).

The next problems estimate the period at a position where the phase is stationary.

Tag 23DD

Problem 42.1.5. At \(g=y_*\), the integrand in the coordinate \(u=w/(2y_0)\) is \(Z(u)=(1+\mathrm iu)^{-k}e^{\mathrm is u}\). Its continuous argument vanishing at \(u=0\) has derivative zero there exactly when \(s=k\). At that position, \[|Z(u)|=(1+u^2)^{-k/2},\qquad |\arg Z(u)|\le k|u|^3/3 .\]

Tag 23DE

Problem 42.1.6. At \(s=k\) one has \(P(k)=4\pi k^k e^{-k}/k!\) and \(2y_0\le\sqrt{k}\,W_{y_*}(y_*)\). Check that \(P(k)/\sqrt{8\pi/k}\) is approximately \(0.9595,0.9794,0.99308,0.999167\) for \(k=2,4,12,100\), respectively.

Tag 23DF

Problem 42.1.7. At \(s=k\), write \(d_k=k^{-1/3}\). On \(|u|\le d_k\) one has \(|Z(u)|\le2\operatorname{Re}Z(u)\), and \(Z(-u)=\overline{Z(u)}\). Deduce \[\int_{\mathbb R} Z(u)\,du\ \ge\ \tfrac12\int_{|u|\le d_k}|Z(u)|\,du-\int_{|u|>d_k}|Z(u)|\,du .\] Check both sides by machine. For \(k=12\) they are about \(0.71859\) and \(0.18324\), and for \(k=100\) they are \(0.25045\) and \(0.11321\). For \(k=4\) the right side is about \(-0.0511\).

Tag 23DG

Problem 42.1.8. The coefficient has \(|\mathsf c(a(e^{2t}))|=\cosh(t)^{-k}\), about \(0.60703\) at \(k=100,t=0.1\), while \(e^{-kt^2/2}\approx0.60653\). The measure \(|W_{y_*}(a(y))|^2\,dy/y^2\) has density proportional to \(y^{k-2}e^{-4\pi my}\). At \(s=k\) and \(k=12\), the shares of its mass on \(y>4y_0\) and \(y<y_0/4\) are about \(3.2\cdot10^{-11}\) and \(2.9\cdot10^{-4}\).

§42.2. A circle and a flag Tag 23DH

On the half-plane changing a height matches the phase derivatives at the identity. For \(G=U(1,1)\) preserving \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), the change is a conjugation of the circle \(R=\{\operatorname{diag}(w,1):|w|=1\}\). Use probability measure \(dw\) on the circle. In this model \(\mathsf a\ge1\) and \(\mathsf N\ge2\) are integers, and the coefficient formula of Theorem 41.3 gives \(\mathsf c(g)=\det(g)^{-\mathsf a}g_{22}^{-\mathsf N}\) for lowest \(K\)-type \((-\mathsf a,-\mathsf a-\mathsf N)\). Pair the coefficient against \(w^{t_0}\) with \(t_0\) a nonnegative integer. For columns write \(I^\dagger=I^*J\).

Tag 23DJ

Problem 42.2.1. The period at the identity vanishes. For a real \(\mathsf b\ge0\), let \(I_0=(\sqrt{1+\mathsf b},\sqrt{\mathsf b})^T\) and use the inverse of the real boost with first column \(I_0\) as the position. The conjugated circle is \(g(w)=1+(w-1)I_0I_0^\dagger\), with \(\det g(w)=w\) and \(g(w)_{22}=1+\mathsf b-\mathsf b w\). For \(j=\mathsf a+t_0\), \[\int_{|w|=1}\mathsf c(g(w))w^{-t_0}\,dw =\binom{\mathsf N+j-1}{j}\frac{\mathsf b^j}{(1+\mathsf b)^{\mathsf N+j}} .\] For \((\mathsf a,\mathsf N,t_0)=(2,3,0)\) the periods at \(\mathsf b=1/3,2/3,1\) are \(81/512,648/3125,3/16\); for \((2,3,1)\) the last two are \(432/3125,5/32\).

Tag 23DK

Problem 42.2.2. The period is largest at \(\mathsf b=j/\mathsf N\), equivalently when \(I_0^\dagger D I_0=t_0\) for \(D=\operatorname{diag}(-\mathsf a,-\mathsf a-\mathsf N)\). The same equation says that the argument of \(\mathsf c(g(e^{\mathrm i\theta}))e^{-\mathrm it_0\theta}\) is stationary at \(\theta=0\). As \(j,\mathsf N\) tend to infinity in fixed ratio the maximum is asymptotic to \((2\pi j(j+\mathsf N)/\mathsf N)^{-1/2}\). For \((j,\mathsf N)=(2,3),(20,30),(200,300)\) the ratios to the asymptotic value are \(0.94897,0.99474,0.99947\) to five decimal places.

The compression \(I_0^\dagger D I_0\) is the weight seen on the embedded line. For the role of such weight restrictions in the orbit method, see Nelson and Venkatesh [NV].

In \(U(2,2)\) the position also involves an isotropic line. Use \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,1,-1)\) and the same adjoint notation. In this model let \((\mu_i)=(-1,-2,-3,-4)\), \(p(z)=\prod_i(z-\mu_i)\), \(q(z)=z\) (the smaller weight is \(0\)), and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\mu_i)\). For a polynomial \(F\) write \([F]_i=F(\mu_i)|p'(\mu_i)q(\mu_i)|^{-1/2}\).

Tag 23DL

Problem 42.2.3. One has \((p'(\mu_i))=(6,-2,2,-6)\). For \(v_0=[z]\), \(v_1=[z^2]\) and \(I_0=\sqrt{24}[1]\), the matrix of pairings in that order is \(\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,1)\), and \[Dv_0=v_1,\qquad DI_0=\sqrt{24}v_0,\qquad I_0^\dagger DI_0=0 .\]

Tag 23DM

Problem 42.2.4. Let \(Y=-\mathrm i(v_0v_1^\dagger+v_1v_0^\dagger)\). Then \(Y^3=0\) and \(g(t)=1+tY+t^2Y^2/2\), \(t\in\mathbb R\), is a one-parameter subgroup of \(U(2,2)\) fixing \(v_0\) and \(I_0\). It has \(g(t)v_1=v_1+\mathrm it v_0\), \(\operatorname{tr}(g(t)-1)=0\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(D(g(t)-1))=2\mathrm it\), giving \(0.2\mathrm i\) at \(t=0.1\).

§42.3. The period bound for the data Tag 23DN

The general period bound gives a position at a real place of the data. The model calculations illustrate phase cancellation at small weights. The bound for the data uses much larger gaps.

Fix a place \(\tau\). Write \(G=G_\tau=U(Y_0)_\tau\), \(H=U(X_0)_\tau\), and \(R=\mathcal U_\tau\rtimes H\) as in Section 39.1. The sign basis is obtained from the rescaled basis by a Euclidean-unitary change that diagonalizes the form in the order of the signs for the parameter \(2\varsigma_\tau\). Use \(ds=du\,dh\) with \(dh\) Haar probability. In the rescaled basis, \(du\) is Lebesgue measure on the entries in row \(v_a\) and columns \(v_b,v'_b\) for \(b>a\), and on the entries in that row in the \(X_0\) columns and column \(z\), together with the imaginary part of the entry in column \(v'_a\). The measure \(du\) is used in the covolume of Definition 39.4.1. Use \(dg\) as in Theorem 41.2 and \(dy\) on \(\mathcal U_\tau\backslash G\) determined by \(du,dg\).

The character from Section 39.2 is \(\exp(\sigma_R-\overline{\sigma_R})\) at \(\tau\), where \(\sigma_R\) is the sum of the entries just above the diagonal on the rescaled Bessel tuple. With the chosen complex embedding at \(\tau\), the \(j\)-th member of the Bessel tuple is multiplied by \(\bigl(M\tau(\delta_E)/(2\pi\mathrm i)\bigr)^{\varrho+1-j}/\sqrt N\). The character therefore uses \(\sigma_R=2\pi\mathrm i\lambda(u)/(M\tau(\delta_E))\), with \(\lambda(u)\) the lattice-basis sum of Section 39.1 evaluated at the real place.

Let \(\pi_l\) have doubled Harish-Chandra parameter \(l=2\varsigma_\tau\) in the sign basis. For a unit highest-weight vector \(u_\tau\) in the lowest \(K\)-type, take \(\mathsf c_\tau(y)=\langle\pi_l(y)u_\tau,u_\tau\rangle\) in that basis, with formal degree \(d_\tau\) (Theorems 41.1 and 41.2). Take the irreducible unitary representation \(\sigma_\tau\) of \(H\) on \(\mathbb C^{I_\tau}\) with the source highest weight given by the diagram of Definition 37.2.1, and let \(v\) be a unit highest-weight vector. Write \(\pi(uh)=\psi_{\mathcal U}(u)\sigma_\tau(h)\).

Tag 23DP

Definition 42.3.1. For a position \(y_*\in G\), the Bessel average at \(v\) is \[W_{y_*,v}(y)=\int_R\mathsf c_\tau(y_*^{-1}sy)\,\pi(s)^{-1}v\,ds .\] The integral converges absolutely for the data, uniformly in the sense that the integral of the norm is bounded as \(y\) varies. This convergence estimate is read from the proof.

Tag 23DQ

Definition 42.3.2. The period at \(y_*\) is \(\mathrm{Per}(y_*)=\langle W_{y_*,v}(y_*),v\rangle\), the integral of \(\mathsf c_\tau(y_*^{-1}sy_*)\overline{\langle\pi(s)v,v\rangle}\) over \(R\). Write \[V_1(y_*)=\operatorname{vol}_R\{s:\|y_*^{-1}sy_*-1\|\le1\},\] using the operator norm in the sign basis. The Bessel length of \(y\) is \(\inf_{s\in R}(\|sy\|+1)\), with the operator norm in the rescaled basis.

Tag 23DR

Theorem 42.3.1 (Lower bound for the period). For the data, at each \(\tau\) there is \(y_*\in G_\tau\) of Bessel length at most \(b^{5n}\), \(n=\varrho+1\), with \(V_1(y_*)\le b^{60(r+k)^2}|\mathrm{Per}(y_*)|\). Here \(r,k\) are the ranks of \(Y_0,X_0\).

Used in: Problem 42.4.3; Chapter 40; Chapter 41

Status. Read from the proof; not proved here. At the position used in the proof, \(g=y_*^{-1}sy_*\), for \(s\in R\), sends each member of the translated Bessel tuple \((y_*^{-1}v_1,\dots,y_*^{-1}v_\varrho,y_*^{-1}z)\) to itself plus a combination of earlier vectors. Let \(h\) be the compact projection of \(s\). Let \(D_l\) be the diagonal matrix of the highest weight of the lowest \(K\)-type of \(\pi_l\) in the sign basis, and \(D_u\) that of the highest weight of \(\sigma_\tau\) in the standard basis of \(X_0\). In these coordinates, \(\operatorname{tr}(D_l(g-1))-\operatorname{tr}(D_u(h-1))=\sigma_R-\overline{\sigma_R}\), where the sum \(\sigma_R\) is additive under multiplication in \(R\). Write \(F(s)\) for the period integrand of Definition 42.3.2. The counterpart of Problem 42.1.7 is \[|F(s)|\le2\operatorname{Re}F(s)+ 6b^{-150(r+k)^2}(2/\|g\oplus h\|)^{9(r+k)^2},\] with the last power integrable on \(R\). The norm of \(g\oplus h\) is the operator norm on the direct sum.

§42.4. Norm and correlation Tag 23DS

The following calculations relate the period to the norm and correlation. Take a locally compact unimodular group \(G\) and a closed unimodular subgroup \(R=\mathcal U\rtimes H\), with \(\mathcal U\) closed and unimodular and \(H\) compact. Use Haar measures \(du,dg\), \(ds=du\,dh\) with \(dh\) of mass one, and the quotient measure \(dy\) on \(\mathcal U\backslash G\) determined by \(du,dg\). In the average use a continuous unitary representation \(\pi\) of \(R\) on a finite-dimensional space \(V\), with \(\mathcal U\) acting by scalars, and a unit vector \(v\in V\). Replace \(\mathsf c_\tau\) by a normalized unit-vector coefficient \(\mathsf c\) in \(L^1(G)\) of a square-integrable irreducible representation of formal degree \(d_\tau\). Schur orthogonality, as in Theorem 41.2 for the real unitary case, gives \[d_\tau\int_G \mathsf c(g_0 a g)\overline{\mathsf c(a)}\,da=\mathsf c(g_0)\mathsf c(g).\] Assume \(\sup_y\int_R|\mathsf c(y_*^{-1}sy)|\,ds<\infty\). For the data the proof supplies this bound and \(\mathsf c_\tau\in L^1(G)\), using the coefficient decay of Theorem 41.4. Write \(W=W_{y_*,v}\), with norms on \(\mathcal U\backslash G\).

Tag 23DT

Problem 42.4.1. For \(s\in R\), \(W(sy)=\pi(s)W(y)\). Also \(W\) is bounded and in \(L^1\cap L^2\) on the quotient, in the sense that its norm is bounded and belongs to both spaces.

Tag 23DU

Problem 42.4.2. One has \(d_\tau\int_G\mathsf c(a^{-1})W(ya)\,da=W(y)\), and \(W\) is continuous.

Tag 23DV

Problem 42.4.3. One has \(d_\tau\|W\|_2^2=\mathrm{Per}(y_*)\). In particular the period is real and nonnegative, and \(W\ne0\) at the position of Theorem 42.3.1.

Tag 23DW

Problem 42.4.4. The correlation \(g\mapsto\int_{\mathcal U\backslash G}\langle W(y),W(yg)\rangle\,dy\) equals \(\overline{\mathsf c(g)}\|W\|_2^2\), and its squared \(L^2(G)\) norm is \(\|W\|_2^4/d_\tau\). If \(H\) acts irreducibly on \(V\), the coordinate products in an orthonormal basis satisfy \(\int_{\mathcal U\backslash G}W_i\overline{W_j}\,dy=\delta_{ij}\|W\|_2^2/\dim V\). Check that the half-plane correlation for \(\|W\|_2=1,\ k=4,\ m=1,\ g=a(2)\) has modulus \(64/81\).

Scale the average to \(\|W_\tau\|_2^2=\#I_\tau\). The problems give the transformation law, orthonormality and squared correlation of Theorem 40.3.1(a)–(c) for \(W_\tau\). The Bessel truncation \(W_\tau^\flat\) is the product of \(W_\tau\) and the cutoff in Bessel length that is \(1\) up to half the radius \(b^{6n+2}\), decreases linearly to \(0\) at the radius and is \(0\) thereafter. The length is left \(R\)-invariant.

We used the discrete-series facts of Chapter 41, absolute convergence, the period bound and the phase and cone estimates. The Bessel truncation and its tail bound in Theorem 40.3.1(d) and the common decay majorant for the Gram-operator estimates are read from the proof.

For the data, the average, convergence and reproduction are DiscreteSeries.besselAverage, integrable_besselIntegrand and convolve_besselAverage. The data’s norm identity is formalDegree_mul_integral_norm_sq_besselAverage with inner-product slots reversed. The data’s squared correlation and normalized coordinate pairings are IsBesselFunction.lintegral_enorm_sq_integral_inner_translate and BesselSystem.orthonormal.
