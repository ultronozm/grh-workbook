\medskip
How does the determinant \(Z(\vartheta)\) of the chain change with the \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\)?  By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, its quotient by \(Z(1)\) is the product of the \(m\) quotients \(Z_j(\vartheta)/Z_j(1)\) for single lifts, times a correction factor: a product of pairwise ratios \(\rho_{ij}(\vartheta)\) of Hecke \(L\)-values, multiplied by \((\Nm Q_\vartheta)^{e_Z/2}\) with an integer \(e_Z\) independent of \(\vartheta\), and raised to the power \(d_T\).  The following statements are read from the proof: Theorems~\ref{thm:equal}, \ref{thm:rallis}, \ref{thm:one-lift}, \ref{thm:exchange}, \ref{thm:chain-gram}, \ref{thm:aux} and~\ref{thm:small-ratios}.  The problems derive Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from the Gram identities, the comparison of determinants and the injectivity of the lifts and the chain.  The proof also states this theorem.  Sections~\ref{sec:volumes} and~\ref{sec:multiplicities} treat the linear algebra in its simplest setting; Sections~\ref{sec:one-lift}--\ref{sec:factor} assemble the theorem, and Section~\ref{sec:aux} gives two further results used in the proof of Theorem~C.

\section{Volumes of maps}\label{sec:volumes}

For a linear map \(u \colon U \to V\) of finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces let
\[
  Z(u) := \det\nolimits_U (u^* u) ,
\]
the determinant on the domain \(U\); we call it the \emph{volume} of \(u\).  Dimensions are complex dimensions, the determinant on the zero space is \(1\), and inner products are conjugate-linear in the first argument and linear in the second.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gram}
For every orthonormal basis \(e_1, \dots, e_d\) of \(U\), \(Z(u) = \det\bigl(\langle u e_a, u e_b \rangle\bigr)_{a,b}\).  The number \(Z(u)\) is real and \(\ge 0\), and \(Z(u) > 0\) if and only if \(u\) is injective.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:scalar-gram}
Let \(S\) be an endomorphism of \(U\) and \(a \in \C\) with \(\langle ux, uy\rangle = a \langle x, Sy\rangle\) for all \(x, y \in U\).  Then \(Z(u) = a^{\dim U} \det S\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:square-chain}
If \(\theta_j \colon V_{j-1} \to V_j\), for \(1 \le j \le m\), are linear maps between spaces of one and the same dimension, then \(Z(\theta_m \circ \dots \circ \theta_1) = \prod_{j=1}^m Z(\theta_j)\).
\end{problem}

When the dimension grows along the chain, equality can fail.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:position}
Let \(\theta_2 \colon \C^2 \to \C^2\) be \(\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)\), and let \(\theta_1, \theta_1' \colon \C \to \C^2\) be \(x \mapsto (x, 0)\) and \(x \mapsto (0, x)\).  Then \(Z(\theta_1) = Z(\theta_1') = 1\) and \(Z(\theta_2) = 4\), while \(Z(\theta_2\theta_1) = 1\) and \(Z(\theta_2\theta_1') = 4\).
\end{problem}

What replaces Problem~\ref{pr:square-chain} is an identity for \(\Theta^*\Theta\) on the first space itself.  Here is a model of how such an identity can be propagated along a chain.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:push-down}
Let \(\Theta_j := \theta_j \circ \dots \circ \theta_1 \colon V_0 \to V_j\).  Suppose that \(\theta_1^*\theta_1 = a_1 R_1\) and that \(\theta_j^*\theta_j\,\Theta_{j-1} = a_j\,\Theta_{j-1} R_j\) for \(2 \le j \le m\), with scalars \(a_j\) and endomorphisms \(R_j\) of \(V_0\).  Then \(\Theta_m^*\Theta_m = a_1 \cdots a_m\, R_1 R_2 \cdots R_m\), and \(Z(\Theta_m) = (a_1 \cdots a_m)^{\dim V_0} \prod_j \det R_j\).
\end{problem}

For \(a \ge 0\) divisible by \(4\), the two lifts from \(\mathrm U(1)\) of Chapter~\ref{c22-chap} used here have a one-dimensional source.  The lift \(\Theta_{(a)}\) from \(\mathrm U(1)\) to \(\mathrm U(1)\) is multiplication by \(c_a/4\) (Problem~\ref{c22-pr:U1U1}), and by Problem~\ref{c22-pr:rallis} the lift \(\Theta_{(a)}\) from \(\mathrm U(1)\) to \(\mathrm U(2)\) satisfies
\[
  \lVert \Theta_{(a)}\varphi \rVert^2 = \lambda_a \lVert \varphi \rVert^2 , \qquad
  \lambda_a := \frac{1}{128} \sum_j \binom{a}{j} c_j^2 c_{a-j}^2 .
\]
This finite calculation illustrates the inner product of a lift with itself; the strict rank inequality in Theorem~\ref{thm:rallis} below first allows a source of rank \(1\) when the target has rank \(3\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:smallest}
For \(4 \mid a\), the composite of the lift \(\mathrm U(1) \to \mathrm U(1)\) with the lift \(\mathrm U(1) \to \mathrm U(2)\) of Chapter~\ref{c22-chap}, in degree \(a\), has volume \(\lambda_a c_a^2/16\), the product of the two volumes.
\end{problem}

\section{Equal multiplicities}\label{sec:multiplicities}

The single lifts of Section~\ref{sec:one-lift} start from spaces \(W_j\) that differ from the source \(Y_0\) of the chain.  Their volumes are compared with that of the chain through Hecke operators that act on both, with the same traces.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:trace-charpoly}
Let a set \(\mathcal H\), closed under products and containing \(1\), act linearly, with \(1\) acting as the identity and products as composites, on finite-dimensional complex spaces \(U\) and \(U'\), and suppose \(\operatorname{tr}_U h = \operatorname{tr}_{U'} h\) for every \(h \in \mathcal H\).  Then \(\dim U = \dim U'\), and every \(h \in \mathcal H\) has the same characteristic polynomial, hence the same determinant, on \(U\) and on \(U'\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:trace-check}
The matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)\) and \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 1 & 3 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) both have traces \(3, 5, 9\) in their first three powers, and \(\det(h + 1) = 6\) for both.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:trace-mult}
In Problem~\ref{pr:trace-charpoly}, suppose moreover that \(\mathcal H\) is commutative and acts diagonalizably on \(U\) and on \(U'\).  Then for every function \(p \colon \mathcal H \to \C\) the joint eigenspaces \(\{y : hy = p(h)y \text{ for all } h \in \mathcal H\}\) in \(U\) and in \(U'\) have the same dimension.
\end{problem}

We use the objects of Section~\ref{c32-sec:objects} of Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}.  We use pointed Hermitian spaces \(V\) of rank \(r\) with signs \(s\), such as \(Y_0\) and \(W_j\).  The point identifies the adelic Hermitian form with \(\operatorname{diag}(s)\) at infinity and the identity at the finite places.  Give \(H(V)\) and its subspaces the Petersson inner product.  For such a \(V\), the space \(H(V)\) is finite-dimensional under the following \emph{gap hypothesis} at every infinite place \(\tau\): the entries of \(2\varsigma_\tau\) are distinct, have the parity of \(r - 1\), and satisfy \(\lvert 2\varsigma_\tau(i) - 2\varsigma_\tau(i')\rvert \ge 2\Delta\) whenever \(s(i) \ne s(i')\).  It holds for \(Y_0\) and for every \(W_j\).  The \(W_j\) and \(Y_0\) have rank \(r\) and can have different signatures (for \(1 \le j \le m\), \(W_j\) has \(m - j\) negative signs, Problem~\ref{c32-pr:signs-W}), while the rank \(r + (j-1)t\) of \(Y_{j-1}\) exceeds \(r\) for \(j > 1\).

The proof shows that a spherical Hecke operator away from \(\mathcal M\) has the same trace on \(H_g(V)\) and on \(H_g(V')\) when the gap hypothesis holds for both sign systems.  For each tame character \(\xi\), each \(j \in \mathbb N\) and each such \(V\) it defines an operator \(\Rc_{V,j}(\xi)\) on \(H(V)\), the series of Rallis's formula, and shows that one spherical Hecke operator acts as \(\Rc_{V,j}(\xi)\) on \(H(V)\) and as \(\Rc_{V',j}(\xi)\) on \(H(V')\); in particular \(\Rc_{V,j}(\xi)\) preserves \(H_g(V)\).  Write \(R_j(\xi)\) for the restriction of \(\Rc_{Y_0,j}(\xi)\) to \(T\), and \(R^W_j(\xi)\) for that of \(\Rc_{W_j,j}(\xi)\) to \(H_g(W_j)\).  For a finite place \(w\) and \(i \in \mathbb N\) let \(h_{w,i}\) be the principal spherical split Hecke operator of index \(i\) at \(w\) if \(w \ne \bar w\) and \(w, \bar w \notin \mathcal M\), and \(h_{w,i} = 0\) otherwise.  Problems~\ref{pr:trace-charpoly} and~\ref{pr:trace-mult} model the argument; the proof establishes the following three assertions.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:equal}
For \(1 \le j \le m\): \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T\); for every family \(p = (p_{w,i})\) of complex numbers indexed by finite places \(w\) and \(i \in \mathbb N\), the simultaneous \(p_{w,i}\)-eigenspaces of the \(h_{w,i}\) in \(T\) and in \(H_g(W_j)\) have the same dimension; and \(\det R^W_j(\xi) = \det R_j(\xi)\) for every tame character \(\xi\).
\end{theorem}

\section{One lift}\label{sec:one-lift}

The norm of a theta lift is computed by the Rallis inner product formula, which the proof establishes in Weil's range.  We state it with the definitions of its objects abbreviated.  For this formula and the exchange identity, fix a CM field with a CM type and Weil data as in the proof (the field \(E\) of our data has this structure), and use pointed Hermitian spaces over it.  Let \(U\) and \(U'\) be such spaces on finite coordinate sets \(\mathcal I\) and \(\mathcal J\), with adelic unitary groups \(G\) and \(G'\), and suppose \(2\#\mathcal I < \#\mathcal J\).  Let \(\eta, \eta'\) be automorphic characters of the norm-one ideles, let \(f\) and \(f'\) be vectors of the adelic Weil representation of \(\operatorname{Hom}(U, U')\), with \(f\) satisfying a condition called cleanness (which holds whenever all signs of \(U\) agree), and let \(y, y'\) be automorphic functions on \(G\) of rapid decay.  Let \(L_f y\) be the theta lift of \(y\) to \(G'\) with datum \(f\) and characters \((\eta, \eta')\), and let \(K(g) := \langle f', \omega(g) f\rangle\, \langle y', y(\cdot\, g)\rangle_U\), where \(\omega(g)\) is the pair Weil action at \((g, 1)\) with characters \((1, \eta')\), whose character factor is \(\eta'(\det g)^{-1}\), and \(\langle\ ,\ \rangle_U\) is the Petersson product.

\begin{theorem}[Rallis inner product formula]\label{thm:rallis}
\(K\) is integrable on all of \(G\), and \(\langle L_{f'} y', L_f y\rangle_{U'} = \vol[U'] \int_G K(g)\, dg\), where \(\vol[U']\) is the total mass of the quotient measure of \(U'\).
\end{theorem}

The \(j\)th single lift goes from \(W_j\), of rank \(r\), to \(V_j\), of rank \(r + t\), and \(2r < r + t\).  For \(1 \le j \le m\) and a tame character \(\xi\), let \(\hat u_{\xi,j} \colon H(W_j) \to \mathcal F_{\xi,j}(V_j)\) be this lift on all principal forms; its target is the space of algebraic level forms with the weight, level and character of the lift for \((\xi, j)\), with its class-sum inner product, and its restriction to \(H_g(W_j)\) is \(u_{\xi,j}\).  The proof shows that \(\hat u_{\xi,j}\) is injective, and, using a specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:rallis} to a sharp datum and the normalization of the single lift, the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:one-lift}
For every \(1 \le j \le m\) there is \(a_j \in \C\), \(a_j \ne 0\), such that for every tame character \(\xi\), \(\hat u_{\xi,j}^* \hat u_{\xi,j} = a_j c_{Q_\xi}\, \Rc_{W_j,j}(\xi)\) on \(H(W_j)\), where for a finite set \(Q\) of finite places
\[
  c_Q := (\Nm Q)^{rt} \prod_{w \in Q} \prod_{i = t+1}^{t+r} \bigl(1 - q_w^{-i}\bigr), \qquad \Nm Q := \prod_{w \in Q} q_w ,
\]
and \(q_w\) is the order of the residue field \(k_w\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:cQ}
\(c_Q\) depends only on \(Q\), \(r\) and \(t\).  Evaluating the displayed expression at \(Q = \{w\}\), \(q_w = 5\), \(r = 1\), \(t = 5\) gives \(c_Q = 15624/5\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:one-lift}
For \(1 \le j \le m\) and every tame character \(\xi\), \(Z(u_{\xi,j}) = (a_j c_{Q_\xi})^{d_T} \det R_j(\xi)\), the determinant being taken on \(T\).  Moreover \(Z(u_{\xi,j}) > 0\).
\end{problem}

\section{Twists and the exchange identity}\label{sec:twists}

Tame characters \(\xi\), their sets \(Q_\xi\) and powers \(\xi^n\), twists and \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\) are as in Section~\ref{c32-sec:objects} of Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}; the power \(\xi^n\) has the same set \(Q_\xi\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:powers}
If \(\vartheta\) is an \(m\)-fold twist and \(1 \le n \le m\), then \(\vartheta^n\) is a twist with the same set \(Q_\vartheta\), so \(c_{Q_{\vartheta^n}} = c_{Q_\vartheta}\).
\end{problem}

For an automorphic character \(\nu\) of the norm-one ideles write \(\widetilde\nu(x) = \nu(x/\bar x)\).  For a set \(S\) of finite places let \(L^S(z, \nu)\) be the function of a real variable \(z\) used in the proof: for \(z > 1\) it is the Euler product with coefficients \(\widetilde\nu_f(\varpi_w)\) at chosen uniformizers outside \(S \cup \bar S\) and \(0\) on \(S \cup \bar S\), which is the usual partial Hecke \(L\)-function when \(\widetilde\nu\) is unramified outside \(S \cup \bar S\); at \(z = 1\) its value agrees with the limit from the right whenever that limit exists in \(\C\).  For finite \(S\) and \(h \in \mathbb N\) let
\[
  B(S, \nu, h) := \Bigl(\prod_{w \in S} q_w\Bigr)^{h^2} \prod_{z=1}^{h} \lvert L^S(z, \nu) \rvert^2 .
\]
The pairwise \(L\)-value factors in the modulus of the chain coefficient of Section~\ref{sec:factor} come from the \emph{exchange identity} of the proof.  We state only its shape and the size of its constant; the definitions of the Fock models, of the data modulo \(\mathfrak m\), of matched functions and of the characters of the kernels are omitted.  Let \(U_0, U_1, U_2\) be pointed Hermitian spaces on finite coordinate sets \(\mathcal N, \mathcal A, \mathcal B\), with \(\mathcal B \sqcup \mathcal A \simeq \mathcal N\), such that the number \(n_-\) of negative signs of \(U_0\) is the sum of those of \(U_1\) and \(U_2\), \(\#\mathcal A = \#\mathcal B + 2h\) with \(h \in \mathbb N\), \(2n_- + 2 \le \#\mathcal N\), and \(\#\mathcal N \le 2\mu(\tau) + 2n_-\) for an integer-valued function \(\mu\) on the infinite places and every \(\tau\).  For a modulus \(\mathfrak m\) of \(E\) (a nonzero ideal of \(\mathcal O_E\) divisible by no prime together with its conjugate) let \(S_{\mathfrak m}\) be the set of its prime divisors.

\begin{theorem}[Exchange identity]\label{thm:exchange}
For every Schwartz vector \(v\) of the real Fock model of \((U_2, U_0)\) there are a real \(C > 0\) and a Schwartz vector \(x\) of the real Fock model of \((U_0, U_1)\) such that for every modulus \(\mathfrak m\), every automorphic character \(\nu\) of the norm-one ideles and every multiplicative character \(\chi\) of \(\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m\) for which \(\nu\) is tame of weight \(\mu\), with tame part nontrivial exactly at \(S_{\mathfrak m} \ne \varnothing\) and given by \(\chi\), there is \(c \in \C\) with
\[
  \lvert c \rvert^2 = C\, B(S_{\mathfrak m}, \nu, h) \ne 0
\]
with the following property.  On one side, integrate over \([U_1]\) the theta kernel of \((U_0, U_1)\) for the datum made of \(x\) and a function \(\Phi\) on matrices modulo \(\mathfrak m\); on the other, integrate over \([U_2]\) the theta kernel of \((U_2, U_0)\) for the datum made of \(v\) and a function \(\Psi\) matched with \(\Phi\) by \(\chi\).  For all characters of the kernels compatible with \(\nu\), all such \(\Phi, \Psi\) and every point \(g\) of the group of \(U_0\), the first integral at \(g\) is \(c\) times the second.
\end{theorem}

The vector \(x\) and the constant \(C\) are chosen before \(\mathfrak m, \nu, \chi\); the scalar \(c\) may depend on all three.  In the chain, lift \(j\) has the character \(\mu^0_j \vartheta^j\) (Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}).  For an automorphic character \(\nu\) of the norm-one ideles and a tame character \(\xi\) put
\[
  \Lambda_t(\nu, \xi) := \prod_{z=1}^{t} \frac{\lvert L^{\mathcal M \cup Q_\xi}\bigl(z, (\nu\xi)^{-1}\bigr)\rvert}{\lvert L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu^{-1})\rvert} .
\]
In this general definition a factor with zero denominator is assigned the value \(0\), as in the proof.
For \(1 \le i < j \le m\) and an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) put
\[
  \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) := \Lambda_t\bigl(\mu^0_j/\mu^0_i,\ \vartheta^{j-i}\bigr) ,
\]
a nonnegative real number.  The proof shows that the denominators \(L^{\mathcal M}(z, (\mu^0_j/\mu^0_i)^{-1})\), \(1 \le z \le t\), are nonzero.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:B-ratio}
Let \(S\) and \(Q\) be disjoint finite sets of finite places, \(h \in \mathbb N\), and \(\nu, \nu'\) characters whose values \(L^S(z, \nu)\) and \(L^{S \cup Q}(z, \nu')\) are finite, with \(L^S(z, \nu) \ne 0\), for each integer \(1 \le z \le h\).  Then
\[
  B(S \cup Q, \nu', h) = B(S, \nu, h)\, (\Nm Q)^{h^2} \prod_{z=1}^{h} \frac{\lvert L^{S \cup Q}(z, \nu') \rvert^2}{\lvert L^S(z, \nu) \rvert^2} .
\]
\end{problem}

For \(1 \le i < j \le m\), \(n = j - i\), \(\nu = \mu^0_j/\mu^0_i\) and an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), the proof establishes
\[
  B\bigl(\mathcal M \cup Q_\vartheta, (\nu\vartheta^n)^{-1}, t\bigr) = B(\mathcal M, \nu, t)\, (\Nm Q_\vartheta)^{t^2} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta)^2 ,
\]
using \(L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu^{-1}) = L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu)\) for this pair; this is how \(\Nm Q_\vartheta\) and the ratios \(\rho_{ij}\) enter.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:rho-one}
\(\rho_{ij}(1) = 1\) for \(1 \le i < j \le m\).
\end{problem}

\section{The factorization}\label{sec:factor}

For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), the lower chain \(\Theta_\vartheta\) of Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}, restricted to \(T\), takes values in a space of algebraic level forms on \(Y_m\), with weight fixed by \(D\), level and level character determined by \(\vartheta\), and the class-sum inner product; the proof shows that it is injective.  In the notation of Section~\ref{sec:volumes}, \(Z(\vartheta) = Z(\Theta_\vartheta|_T)\) and \(Z_j(\vartheta) = Z(u_{\vartheta^j, j})\).  Let \(R_\vartheta := R_1(\vartheta^1) R_2(\vartheta^2) \cdots R_m(\vartheta^m)\), an endomorphism of \(T\).
The proof computes the Gram form of the chain by induction over a triangle of spaces of forms, each of whose squares satisfies a ``one-square'' identity obtained from Theorem~\ref{thm:exchange}.  The result is the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:chain-gram}
There are a function \(c\) from \(m\)-fold twists to \(\C\), a real number \(C'\) and an integer \(e'\) such that for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\),
\[
  \lvert c(\vartheta) \rvert^2 = C'\, (\Nm Q_\vartheta)^{e'} \Bigl( \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) \Bigr)^2 ,
\]
and \(\langle \Theta_\vartheta x, \Theta_\vartheta y \rangle = c(\vartheta)\, c_{Q_\vartheta}^m\, \langle x, R_\vartheta y \rangle\) for all \(x, y \in T\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:abs-ratio}
Let \(X\) be a set with a point \(1\), let \(N \colon X \to \R_{>0}\) and \(f \colon X \to \R_{\ge 0}\) with \(N(1) = f(1) = 1\), and let \(c \colon X \to \C\), \(C' \in \R\) and \(e' \in \Z\) satisfy \(\lvert c(x) \rvert^2 = C' N(x)^{e'} f(x)^2\) for all \(x\).  If \(c(1) \ne 0\), then \(C' = \lvert c(1)\rvert^2\) and \(\lvert c(x)/c(1) \rvert = N(x)^{e'/2} f(x)\) for all \(x\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:chain-det}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-gram}, if \(c(1) = 0\) then \(T = 0\).  For every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\),
\[
  Z(\vartheta) = \Bigl( c(\vartheta) \Big/ \prod_{j=1}^m a_j \Bigr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m Z_j(\vartheta) ,
\]
and \(Z(\vartheta) > 0\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:quotient}
If \(T \ne 0\), then for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\),
\[
  \frac{Z(\vartheta)}{Z(1)} = \Bigl( (\Nm Q_\vartheta)^{e'/2} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) \Bigr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{Z_j(\vartheta)}{Z_j(1)} .
\]
If \(T = 0\), both sides equal \(1\) (a real number to the power \(0\) is \(1\)).
\end{problem}

The proof states the normalized determinant identity as follows.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(P\) be parameters and \(D\) admissible data for \(P\).  There is an integer \(e_Z\) such that for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \(0 < Z(\vartheta)\);
\item[(b)] \(0 < Z_j(\vartheta)\) for every integer \(j\) with \(1 \le j \le m\);
\item[(c)] in \(\C\),
\[
  \frac{Z(\vartheta)}{Z(1)} = \biggl( (\Nm Q_\vartheta)^{e_Z/2} \prod_{i=1}^{m} \prod_{j=i+1}^{m} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) \biggr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{Z_j(\vartheta)}{Z_j(1)} .
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Here \(Z(\vartheta)\) and \(Z_j(\vartheta)\) are complex numbers by definition, and (a), (b) say that they are real and positive; the base of the power \(d_T\) in (c) is a real number, with \(e_Z/2\) a real exponent, and \(e_Z\) does not depend on \(\vartheta\).  Problems~\ref{pr:one-lift} and~\ref{pr:abs-ratio}--\ref{pr:quotient}, with Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-gram}, prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; one may take \(e_Z = e'\) when \(T \ne 0\).  Thus the volume of the chain at a twist, relative to the trivial twist, is expressed through \(m\) volumes of single lifts, a power of \(\Nm Q_\vartheta\), and \(m(m-1)/2\) ratios \(\rho_{ij}\) of \(L\)-values.

\section{Auxiliary forms}\label{sec:aux}

The proof of Theorem~C extends a linearly independent family of target forms to a basis.  For each \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), a common nonzero scalar makes the \(\vartheta\)-lifts of the added forms integral at every \(\iota\mid\ell\) with \(\ell\nmid\Nm Q_\vartheta\).  Both statements of this section are read from the proof.  The \emph{turn ratios} are
\[
  \rho'_{ij}(\vartheta) := \Lambda_t\bigl(\mu_{i+1}^0\mu_{j+1}^0,\ \vartheta^{j+1}\bigr), \qquad 0 \le i, j < m .
\]
For an automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\C\) write \(\lVert y \rVert_\sigma^2\) for the squared norm of the conjugate of a form \(y\) by \(\sigma\).

\begin{theorem}[auxiliary forms]\label{thm:aux}
There are an integer \(e_\gamma\) and numbers \(\gamma(\vartheta) \in \C^\times\), one for each \(m\)-fold twist, with \(\gamma(1) = 1\) and
\[
  \lvert\gamma(\vartheta)\rvert = (\Nm Q_\vartheta)^{e_\gamma/2} \prod_{0 \le i, j < m} \rho'_{ij}(\vartheta) ,
\]
such that \(T\) is spanned by the forms \(y\) with the two properties: \(\gamma(\vartheta)\Theta_\vartheta y\) is integral at every \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell \nmid \Nm Q_\vartheta\), for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\); and \(\lVert\Theta_1 y\rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp(2x_1^2b^2)\) for every automorphism \(\sigma\) of \(\C\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[small ratios]\label{thm:small-ratios}
Let \(\theta\) be a family of \(m\)-fold twists with small ratios (Definition~\ref{c38-def:small-ratios}).  For every integer \(e\), the families
\[
  \iota \mapsto \Bigl((\Nm Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\Bigr)^{-1}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \iota \mapsto \Bigl((\Nm Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\Bigr)^{-1}
\]
have height at most \(m^2tx_1\).
\end{theorem}

In the proof each \(\rho_{ij}\) and each \(\rho'_{ij}\) is a product of \(t\) ratios \(R_P(\vartheta^n, 1)\), with \(1 \le n \le m\) and \(P\) a ramified critical pair.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:count-ratios}
Let \(\theta\) be a family of \(m\)-fold twists such that, for every \(1 \le n \le m\) and every ramified critical pair \(P\), the ratios \(R_P(\theta_\iota^n, 1)\) are nonzero and the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^n, 1)^{-1}\) has height at most \(x_1\).  Then the families \(\iota \mapsto \bigl(\prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\bigr)^{-1}\) and \(\iota \mapsto \bigl(\prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\bigr)^{-1}\) have height at most \(m^2tx_1\); the first is a product of \(m(m-1)/2\) inverse ratios \(\rho_{ij}^{-1}\), the second of \(m^2\).
\end{problem}

\section*{Hints}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:volumes}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:gram}] \(\langle u e_a, u e_b\rangle = \langle e_a, u^*u\, e_b\rangle\), and \(u^*u\) is positive semidefinite with kernel \(\ker u\).
\item[\ref{pr:scalar-gram}] Rewrite the hypothesis as an identity of endomorphisms of \(U\).
\item[\ref{pr:square-chain}] In orthonormal bases of spaces of equal dimension, \(Z(u) = \lvert\det u\rvert^2\).
\item[\ref{pr:position}] Problem~\ref{pr:gram} with \(U = \C\).
\item[\ref{pr:push-down}] Induction on \(m\).
\item[\ref{pr:smallest}] Problem~\ref{pr:gram} on a line.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:multiplicities}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:trace-charpoly}] Take \(h = 1\), then the traces of the powers of \(h\), and Newton's identities.
\item[\ref{pr:trace-mult}] Only finitely many \(p\) occur on \(U \oplus U'\); a polynomial in finitely many elements of \(\mathcal H\) acts as the projection onto one joint eigenspace.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:one-lift}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:one-lift}] \(\Rc_{W_j,j}(\xi)\) preserves \(H_g(W_j)\); Problem~\ref{pr:scalar-gram} on \(H_g(W_j)\), then Theorem~\ref{thm:equal}.  For positivity, Problem~\ref{pr:gram}.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:twists}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:powers}] The definition of \(\vartheta^n\) in Chapter~\ref{c32-chap}, and Problem~\ref{pr:cQ}.
\item[\ref{pr:B-ratio}] Split the product over \(S \cup Q\).
\item[\ref{pr:rho-one}] At the trivial twist \(Q_1 = \varnothing\) and \(1^{j-i} = 1\).
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:factor}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:abs-ratio}] Evaluate at \(1\) first.
\item[\ref{pr:chain-det}] For the first statement, Theorem~\ref{thm:chain-gram} at \(\vartheta = 1\), and injectivity.  For the formula, Problems~\ref{pr:scalar-gram}, \ref{pr:powers} and~\ref{pr:one-lift}; compare the factors \(c_{Q}\) for \(\vartheta^j\) and \(\vartheta\).
\item[\ref{pr:quotient}] Take absolute values in Problem~\ref{pr:chain-det} and divide by the case \(\vartheta = 1\), using Problems~\ref{pr:abs-ratio} and~\ref{pr:rho-one}.  For \(T = 0\), Theorem~\ref{thm:equal}.
\end{description}
