Prerequisites.

Linear algebra over \({\mathbb{C}}\): adjoints, orthogonal projections, determinants as products of eigenvalues. The fields \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) and \({\mathbb{C}}_\ell\), with \(\lvert\ell\rvert_\ell = 1/\ell\). Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first variable. From Chapter 29: congruence modules, Problems 29.1.1 and 29.1.2. From Chapter 23: theta lifts between definite spaces and their norms (Theorem 23.3.1). From Chapter 5: isomorphisms \(\iota \mid \ell\) and heights of families (Section 5.3); from Chapter 6, Theorem 6.2.1, which needs a height bound for \(Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\). For Section 75.3 only: the data (Chapter 36) and the spaces, forms, twists and single lifts of Chapter 37; the gauge and forms integral at a ring map \(\eta\) (Definition 37.5.1); the Hecke operators \(T_{w,i}\) of Section 32.4; the exchange factors of Chapter 71; Chapters 69 and 70 for two facts recalled there; and Theorem 24.2.1 as a model of a Hecke relation.

This sheet serves Theorem 74.2.1 of Chapter 74, The cost of one lift: for a regular family \((\theta_\iota)\) of \(m\)-fold twists (Definition 79.1.3), a bound for the height of \(\iota \mapsto Z_j(1)/Z_j(\theta_\iota)\), where \(Z_j(\theta_\iota)\) is the Gram determinant of the \(j\)-th single lift at the twist \(\theta_\iota^j\). How can the ratio be bounded without evaluating either determinant? The Gram determinant at a twist, times the determinant of the compression of a second operator to the image of the lift, is a fixed nonzero number times a power of a ratio of Hecke values (Theorem 75.3.1). The compression determinant is bounded under \(\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\) at the trivial twist, and at \(\ell\) at a twist prime to \(\ell\) at which the projection is cleared. The second bound projects integral forms orthogonal to the false lifts: forms that satisfy the equations of a lift and are orthogonal to the lifts (Definition 75.3.2). We model this on lines in \({\mathbb{C}}^3\), then state the theorem of the proof.

§75.1. Compressions

Let \(A\) and \(V\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces and \(u \colon A \to V\) an injective linear map, with image \(R\). Write \(\operatorname{pr}_R\) for the orthogonal projection of \(V\) onto \(R\).

Definition 75.1.1. For \(G \in \operatorname{End}V\), call \(\operatorname{pr}_R G|_R \in \operatorname{End}R\) the compression of \(G\) to \(R\).

Problem 75.1.1. For every \(G \in \operatorname{End}V\), \[\det(u^*u)\,\det(\operatorname{pr}_R G|_R) = \det(u^* G u) .\]

Now let \(\vartheta\) run over a set with an element \(1\), and suppose given for each \(\vartheta\) an injective \(u_\vartheta \colon A \to V_\vartheta\) with image \(R_\vartheta\), an operator \(\Upsilon_\vartheta \in \operatorname{End}V_\vartheta\), and a complex number \(\rho_\vartheta \ne 0\). In this section and the next, \(Z(\vartheta) := \det(u_\vartheta^* u_\vartheta)\), \(D_\vartheta := \det(\operatorname{pr}_{R_\vartheta} \Upsilon_\vartheta|_{R_\vartheta})\) and \(\rho_\vartheta\) are local notations, models of the objects of Section 75.3.

Problem 75.1.2. Suppose that some \(\Upsilon^\circ \in \operatorname{End}A\) has \(\langle y, \Upsilon^\circ y\rangle > 0\) for every \(y \ne 0\) and that \(\langle u_\vartheta y', \Upsilon_\vartheta u_\vartheta y\rangle = \rho_\vartheta \langle y', \Upsilon^\circ y\rangle\) for all \(\vartheta\) and \(y', y \in A\). Then \(\det \Upsilon^\circ \ne 0\), every \(D_\vartheta\) is nonzero, and \[Z(\vartheta)\,D_\vartheta = \det(\Upsilon^\circ)\, \rho_\vartheta^{\dim A} \qquad\text{for every } \vartheta .\] If moreover \(\rho_1 = 1\), then \(Z(1)/Z(\vartheta) = (D_\vartheta/D_1)\,\rho_\vartheta^{-\dim A}\).

Used in: Problem 75.1.3, Problem 75.1.5; Chapter 76: Problem 76.1.1

So \(Z(\vartheta)\) is traded for \(\rho_\vartheta\) and \(D_\vartheta\). The simplest operators have rank one.

Problem 75.1.3. Let \(A = {\mathbb{C}}\), \(u_\vartheta(1) = v_\vartheta\), and \(\Upsilon_\vartheta = g_\vartheta g_\vartheta^*\) with \(g_\vartheta \colon {\mathbb{C}}\to V_\vartheta\), \(g_\vartheta(1) = w_\vartheta\). Then \(Z(\vartheta) = \lVert v_\vartheta\rVert^2\) and \(D_\vartheta = \lvert\langle w_\vartheta, v_\vartheta\rangle\rvert^2/\lVert v_\vartheta\rVert^2\). If \(\langle w_\vartheta, v_\vartheta\rangle = c_\vartheta^{-1} \mathrm{per}\) with numbers \(c_\vartheta \ne 0\) and \(\mathrm{per} \ne 0\), \(\mathrm{per}\) independent of \(\vartheta\), the hypothesis of Problem 75.1.2 holds with \(\rho_\vartheta = \lvert c_1\rvert^2/\lvert c_\vartheta\rvert^2\) and \(\Upsilon^\circ = \lvert \mathrm{per}\rvert^2/\lvert c_1\rvert^2\).

The toy is the following pair of lines in \({\mathbb{C}}^3\), with the standard inner product, and the set \(\{1, \vartheta\}\): \[v_1 = (1,-1,0),\quad v_\vartheta = (1,1,-2),\qquad w_1 = (2,0,1),\quad w_\vartheta = (1,1,0),\] with \(u_1(z) = zv_1\), \(u_\vartheta(z) = zv_\vartheta\), \(\Upsilon_1 = w_1w_1^*\) and \(\Upsilon_\vartheta = w_\vartheta w_\vartheta^*\).

Problem 75.1.4. In the toy \(Z(1) = 2\) and \(Z(\vartheta) = 6\), \(D_1 = 2\) and \(D_\vartheta = 2/3\), and \(Z(1)D_1 = Z(\vartheta)D_\vartheta = 4\).

Problem 75.1.5. Replace \(w_\vartheta\) by \(3w_\vartheta\). Then \(D_\vartheta = 6\), the hypothesis of Problem 75.1.2 holds with \(\Upsilon^\circ = 4\), \(\rho_1 = 1\) and \(\rho_\vartheta = 9\), and \((D_\vartheta/D_1)\,\rho_\vartheta^{-1} = 1/3 = Z(1)/Z(\vartheta)\).

Determinants are bounded through eigenvalues.

Problem 75.1.6. Let \(Y\) be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, \(G \in \operatorname{End}Y\), \(f_0 \colon {\mathbb{C}}\to F_0\) a ring homomorphism into a field with an absolute value, and \(c \in {\mathbb{R}}\). If \(\lvert f_0(\alpha)\rvert \le c\) for every eigenvalue \(\alpha\) of \(G\), then \(\lvert f_0(\det G)\rvert \le c^{\dim Y}\).

§75.2. False lifts in \({\mathbb{C}}^3\)

Where do bounds for \(D_\vartheta\) come from? Let \(V = {\mathbb{C}}^n\) with \(\langle y, z\rangle = \sum \bar y_i z_i\). An automorphism \(\sigma\) of \({\mathbb{C}}\) acts on \(V\) coordinatewise; put \(\langle y, z\rangle_\sigma := \langle\sigma y, \sigma z\rangle\), so that \(\langle y, y\rangle_\sigma = \lVert\sigma y\rVert^2\). For \(\iota \mid \ell\) let \({\mathcal{L}}_\iota := \{y \in V : \lvert\iota(y_i)\rvert_\ell \le 1 \text{ for all } i\}\), the vectors integral at \(\iota\).

In this section let \(X \subseteq V\) be a subspace, the space cut out by the equations that every lift satisfies, and \(R \subseteq X\) a subspace, the lifts. Put \(F := X \cap R^\perp\), the false lifts: they satisfy the equations that define \(X\) but are orthogonal to the lifts. Let \(\Upsilon \in \operatorname{End}V\) with \(\Upsilon(R) \perp F\), and put \(D := \det(\operatorname{pr}_R \Upsilon|_R)\). In Section 75.3, \(X\) is the intersection of the image of an idempotent with the kernel of a Hecke operator.

Problem 75.2.1. If \(y \perp F\), then \(\operatorname{pr}_R y = \operatorname{pr}_X y\).

Problem 75.2.2. Let \(e, {\mathbf{H}}\in \operatorname{End}V\) with \(e = e^* = e^2\), and \(X = \operatorname{im}e \cap \ker {\mathbf{H}}\). Then \(F^\perp = R + \ker e + \operatorname{im}{\mathbf{H}}^*\). In the toy, with \(e\) the identity and \({\mathbf{H}}\) the matrix with all entries \(1\), \(X = \{y : y_1 + y_2 + y_3 = 0\}\) contains \(R_1 = {\mathbb{C}}v_1\) and \(R_\vartheta = {\mathbb{C}}v_\vartheta\); the false lifts are \(F_1 = {\mathbb{C}}v_\vartheta\) and \(F_\vartheta = {\mathbb{C}}v_1\), and \(F_1^\perp = {\mathbb{C}}v_1 + {\mathbb{C}}(1,1,1)\).

Used in: Problem 75.2.4

The projection to \(R\) can fail to commute with \(\sigma\). The next two problems give sufficient conditions for \(\lVert\sigma(\operatorname{pr}_R y)\rVert \le \lVert\sigma y\rVert\) when \(y \perp F\).

Problem 75.2.3. Let \(K_0 \subset {\mathbb{C}}\) be a subfield, Galois over \({\mathbb{Q}}\) with abelian Galois group, such as \({\mathbb{Q}}\) or \({\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{i})\), and suppose that \(X\) is spanned by vectors with coordinates in \(K_0\). For \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\) and \(y \in V\), \(\sigma(\operatorname{pr}_X y) = \operatorname{pr}_{\sigma X}(\sigma y)\), where \(\sigma X\) is spanned by the images under \(\sigma\) of such spanning vectors. Hence \(\lVert\sigma(\operatorname{pr}_R y)\rVert \le \lVert\sigma y\rVert\) whenever \(y \perp F\).

The next problem models, without rationality, the adjoint identities stated after Definition 75.3.2.

Problem 75.2.4. Let \(e, {\mathbf{H}}\) and \(X\) be as in Problem 75.2.2, and suppose that for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\): \(\langle e y, z\rangle_\sigma = \langle y, e z\rangle_\sigma\) for all \(y, z\), and \(\langle {\mathbf{H}}y, z\rangle_\sigma = 0\) whenever \({\mathbf{H}}z = 0\). Then \(\operatorname{im}{\mathbf{H}}^*=\operatorname{im}{\mathbf{H}}\). For every \(y\perp F\) there are \(z\in R\) and \(x\in V\) such that \(y=z+x\) and \(\langle x,z'\rangle_\sigma=0\) for every \(z'\in R\) and every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\). For each such \(y\), \(\lVert\sigma(\operatorname{pr}_R y)\rVert\le\lVert\sigma y\rVert\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\).

Problem 75.2.5. Suppose that \(\lVert\sigma(\operatorname{pr}_R y)\rVert \le \lVert\sigma y\rVert\) for every \(\sigma\) and every \(y \perp F\), and that \(\lVert\sigma(\Upsilon y)\rVert \le \exp(c)\,\lVert\sigma y\rVert\) for all \(y \in V\) and \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\). Then \(\lvert\sigma(D)\rvert \le \exp(c\dim R)\) for all \(\sigma\).

Used in: Problem 75.2.8

Problem 75.2.6. Suppose that \(\Upsilon({\mathcal{L}}_\iota) \subseteq {\mathcal{L}}_\iota\), and that \(\Lambda \in \operatorname{End}R\) satisfies \(\Lambda(\operatorname{pr}_R y) \in {\mathcal{L}}_\iota\) for every \(y \in {\mathcal{L}}_\iota\) with \(y \perp F\). Then \(\lvert\iota(\det\Lambda \cdot D)\rvert_\ell \le 1\).

Used in: Problem 75.2.7, Problem 75.2.8

For \(X\) spanned by vectors in \({\mathbb{Q}}^n\), the least positive integer \(c\) with \(c\operatorname{pr}_X({\mathbb{Z}}^n)\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^n\) is the exponent of the congruence module of \(X\) for \({\mathbb{Q}}^n=(X\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^n)\oplus(X^\perp\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^n)\) (Problem 29.1.2). We return to the toy, with \(w_\vartheta=(1,1,0)\) and \(X\) as in Problem 75.2.2.

Problem 75.2.7. For the decomposition \({\mathbb{Q}}^3 = (X \cap {\mathbb{Q}}^3) \oplus {\mathbb{Q}}(1,1,1)\), the congruence module of \(X\) in \({\mathbb{Z}}^3\) is \({\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}\), and \(3\operatorname{pr}_X({\mathbb{Z}}^3) \subseteq {\mathbb{Z}}^3\). Hence, for \(R = R_1\) and for \(R = R_\vartheta\) and at every \(\iota\), \(\Lambda = 3\) satisfies the hypothesis on \(\Lambda\) in Problem 75.2.6. For \(R = R_1\) already \(\Lambda = 1\) does.

Problem 75.2.8. In the toy, \(\Upsilon_1(R_1) \perp F_1\) and \(\Upsilon_\vartheta(R_\vartheta) \perp F_\vartheta\). The hypotheses of Problem 75.2.5 hold for \(\Upsilon_1\) with \(\exp(c) = 5\), so \(\lvert\sigma(D_1)\rvert \le 5\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\); those of Problem 75.2.6 hold for \(\Upsilon_\vartheta\) at \(\iota \mid 3\) with \(\Lambda = 3\), so \(\lvert D_\vartheta\rvert_3 \le 3\), and equality holds.

So for rational \(X\): if \(c\operatorname{pr}_X({\mathbb{Z}}^n)\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^n\) and \(\Upsilon\) preserves \({\mathcal{L}}_\iota\), then \(c^{\dim R}D\) is integral at \(\iota\); and under the hypotheses of Problem 75.2.5, \(\lvert\sigma(D)\rvert\le\exp(c'\dim R)\), where \(\exp(c')\) is the operator bound there. Neither bound uses \(\det(u^*u)\).

§75.3. The general statement

We use the data of Chapter 36 and the objects of Chapter 37: the chosen places \({\mathcal{M}}\) and their residue fields \(k_w\), with \(q_w := \#k_w\); the group \(\bar G\) and the idempotent \(e_0\); the good forms \(H_g(W_j)\); twists \(\vartheta\), with modulus \(Q_\vartheta\) and \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\), and \(m\)-fold twists; the characters \(\mu_j^0\), \(\upsilon\) and \(\chi_M\); the single lifts \(u_{\xi,j}\) and \(Z_j(\vartheta)\). Fix \(1 \le j \le m\). For a twist \(\vartheta\), the single lift \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) maps \(H_g(W_j)\) to the space of algebraic forms on \(V_j\) with the weight, level and level character prescribed by the lift datum. Write \(R_{\vartheta,j}\) for its image and \(\operatorname{pr}_{\vartheta,j}\) for the orthogonal projection onto that image. We use two facts from Chapters 69 and 70: \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) is injective for every tame character \(\vartheta\) (Problem 69.4.2 with Theorem 69.4.1), and \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T\) (Corollary 70.4.1).

For \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\) and a form \(y\) on \(V_j\), in this sheet \(N_\sigma(y)\) is the class sum, weighted by the reciprocal orders of the stabilizers, of the squared Gram norms of the vectors \(\sigma(y(g))\); \(N_1(y) = \lVert y\rVert^2\). For a ring homomorphism \(\eta \colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_\ell\), a form is integral at \(\eta\) as in Definition 37.5.1; integral at \(\iota \mid \ell\) means integral at \(\iota\) followed by \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell \subset {\mathbb{C}}_\ell\).

Definition 75.3.1. Put \(z_\varnothing := (7m + k)/2\), \(z_+ := m\), \(z_- := m/2\), integers, as is \((t - 11m - 1)/2\), since \(m = 2^e\) and \(r = 3^{e'}\) (Definition 36.3.1); \(\epsilon_\varnothing = \epsilon_+ = 1\) and \(\epsilon_- = -1\); \(\nu_\varnothing := \mu_j^0\,\upsilon^{\,j + (t - 11m - 1)/2}\) and \(\nu_\pm := \upsilon^{-z_\pm}\chi_M^{\pm j}\). Define the Hecke value product of lift \(j\) at a twist \(\vartheta\) by \[\begin{align*} {\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta) &:= \prod_{i \in \{\varnothing, +, -\}} \mathrm{ex}_{z_i}\bigl({\mathcal{M}}\cup Q_\vartheta,\, (\nu_i\vartheta^{\epsilon_i})^{-1}\bigr) \\ &= \prod_{i} \biggl[\Bigl(\prod_{w \in {\mathcal{M}}\cup Q_\vartheta} q_w\Bigr)^{z_i^2} \prod_{n=1}^{z_i} \bigl\lvert L^{{\mathcal{M}}\cup Q_\vartheta}\bigl(n, (\nu_i\vartheta^{\epsilon_i})^{-1}\bigr)\bigr\rvert^2\biggr] , \end{align*}\] with the exchange factors of Definition 71.2.3 and the partial Hecke values \(L^S(z,\nu)\) of Definition 37.10.1.

Used in: Chapter 74; Chapter 76; Chapter 77

Let \(w\) be a finite place of \(E\) with \(\bar w \ne w\) and \(w, \bar w \notin {\mathcal{M}}\cup Q_\vartheta\). Let \({\mathbf{H}}_w\) be the Hecke polynomial of lift \(j\) at \(w\) at its test value: the operator \[{\mathbf{H}}_w := \sum_{i=0}^{r+t} (-c_w)^i\, q_w^{i(i-1)/2}\, T_{w,i}\] on the forms on \(V_j\), where the \(T_{w,i}\) are the Hecke operators at \(w\) (Section 32.4) and the test value is \(c_w := (\nu_w q_w^{t-1})^{-1}\), with \(\nu_w\) the Frobenius value at \(w\) of Definition 80.1.3 for the descent pair of lift \(j\), of ranks \(r < r + t\). The group \(\bar G\) acts on these forms by right translation on the block of rank \(r\) of \(V_j\), twisted by the tame part of the character of the lift, and \(e_0\) acts through this action.

Definition 75.3.2. Call the forms in \(\operatorname{im}e_0 \cap \ker {\mathbf{H}}_w\) orthogonal to \(R_{\vartheta,j}\) the false lifts at \(w\). A form is orthogonal to the false lifts if it is orthogonal to the false lifts at every such \(w\).

Used in: Chapter 76; Chapter 79: Definition 79.1.1; Chapter 80; Chapter Overview

For every such \(w\), the image \(R_{\vartheta,j}\) is contained in \(\operatorname{im}e_0 \cap \ker {\mathbf{H}}_w\): this is the unramified Hecke relation of a theta lift, with the equivariance of the lift under \(\bar G\); for its background see [Ra82], and Theorem 24.2.1 is a lattice-theta analogue. Status: the inclusion, and the estimate below, are read from the proof; not proved here. So \(\operatorname{im}e_0 \cap \ker{\mathbf{H}}_w\) plays the part of \(X\) in Section 75.2, at each \(w\). For \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\) let \(\mathfrak b_\sigma\) be the pairing on forms that is \(\sigma^{-1}\) of the class sum of the Gram pairings of the values after \(\sigma\), so that \(N_\sigma(y)\) is the real part of \(\sigma(\mathfrak b_\sigma(y,y))\). The projection estimate is \(N_\sigma(\operatorname{pr}_{\vartheta,j}y) \le N_\sigma(y)\) for every \(\sigma\) and every \(y\) orthogonal to the false lifts; its proof is that of Problem 75.2.4 at one place \(w\), using that for each \(\sigma\), \(e_0\) is self-adjoint for \(\mathfrak b_\sigma\) and \({\mathbf{H}}_w\) has an adjoint for \(\mathfrak b_\sigma\) that commutes with \({\mathbf{H}}_w\).

Definition 75.3.3. A family of operators \(\Upsilon_\vartheta\) on the target space of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\), one for every twist \(\vartheta\), is a comparison operator if: (1) the ratio \({\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta)/{\mathcal{H}}_j(1)\) is defined and nonzero for every twist \(\vartheta\); (2) some \(\Upsilon^\circ \in \operatorname{End}H_g(W_j)\) has \(\langle y, \Upsilon^\circ y\rangle > 0\) for \(y \ne 0\) and \(\langle u_{\vartheta,j}y', \Upsilon_\vartheta u_{\vartheta,j}y\rangle = ({\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta)/{\mathcal{H}}_j(1))\langle y', \Upsilon^\circ y\rangle\) for every twist \(\vartheta\) and all \(y', y \in H_g(W_j)\); (3) for every twist \(\vartheta\) and \(z \in R_{\vartheta,j}\), \(\Upsilon_\vartheta z\) is orthogonal to the false lifts; (4) for every twist \(\vartheta\), every prime \(\ell\) prime to \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\), and every \(\eta \colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_\ell\), \(\Upsilon_\vartheta\) maps forms integral at \(\eta\) to forms integral at \(\eta\); (5) \(N_\sigma(\Upsilon_1 y) \le \exp(3fmb\log^2 b)^2\, N_\sigma(y)\) for every form \(y\) at the trivial twist and every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\).

Used in: Chapter 74; Chapter 76: Theorem 76.5.1

For a comparison operator put \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:D}\tag{1} D_\vartheta := \det\bigl(\operatorname{pr}_{\vartheta,j}\Upsilon_\vartheta|_{R_{\vartheta,j}}\bigr) . \end{equation}\]

Definition 75.3.4. For \(\iota \mid \ell\), a twist \(\vartheta\) and a real number \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\), say that \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) clears the projection at \((\iota,\vartheta,j)\) if some \(\Lambda \in \operatorname{End}R_{\vartheta,j}\) has \(\lvert\iota(\det\Lambda)\rvert_\ell \ge e^{-(\dim R_{\vartheta,j})\,r\,\mathrm{cl}(\ell)}\) and \(\Lambda(\operatorname{pr}_{\vartheta,j}y)\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(y\) that is integral at \(\iota\) and orthogonal to the false lifts.

Used in: Chapter 74; Chapter 79: Definition 79.1.3; Chapter 80

Every class of the finite adelic group modulo the rational points and the level has a representative whose components above \(\ell\) are integral, by weak approximation.

Problem 75.3.1. Let \(y \ne 0\) be a form in the target space of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\), let \(\eta \colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_\ell\), and fix class representatives integral above \(\ell\). At \(g = g_0 s g_1\), with \(g_0\) rational, \(s\) a representative and \(g_1\) in the level, the gauge of \(y(g)\) is the largest absolute value of a coordinate of \(\eta(y(s))\). Hence there is \(c \in {\mathbb{C}}^\times\) such that \(cy\) is integral at \(\eta\) and \(\alpha c y\) is integral at \(\eta\) only if \(\lvert\eta(\alpha)\rvert_\ell \le 1\).

Problems 75.1.1, 75.1.2, 75.1.6, 75.2.5, 75.2.6 and 75.3.1, carried over to forms with the projection estimate, the facts that \(N_\sigma(y) > 0\) for \(y \ne 0\) and \(N_\sigma(ay) = \lvert\sigma(a)\rvert^2 N_\sigma(y)\), the injectivity of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) and \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T\) (so that \(\dim R_{\vartheta,j} = d_T\)), prove the following theorem.

Theorem 75.3.1. Let \(1 \le j \le m\) and let \((\Upsilon_\vartheta)\) be a comparison operator. (a) The number \(\det(u_{\vartheta,j}^*u_{\vartheta,j})\,D_\vartheta\,({\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta)/{\mathcal{H}}_j(1))^{-d_T}\) is nonzero and does not depend on the twist \(\vartheta\). (b) For every twist \(\vartheta\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell\) prime to \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\), and every real \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) that clears the projection at \((\iota,\vartheta,j)\), \(\lvert\iota(D_\vartheta)\rvert_\ell \le e^{d_T r\,\mathrm{cl}(\ell)}\). (c) \(\lvert\sigma(D_1)\rvert \le e^{3d_T fmb\log^2 b}\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\).

Used in: Chapter 74; Chapter 80

In particular every \(D_\vartheta\) is nonzero. For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) the power \(\vartheta^j\) is a twist (Section 37.6), and \(1^j = 1\).

Problem 75.3.2. For every comparison operator and every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \[\frac{Z_j(1)}{Z_j(\vartheta)} = \frac{D_{\vartheta^j}}{D_1}\Bigl(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_j(\vartheta^j)}{{\mathcal{H}}_j(1)}\Bigr)^{-d_T} .\]
