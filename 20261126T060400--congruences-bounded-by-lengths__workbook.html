Theorem 80.1.1 bounds the denominator of the projection onto a space of lifts at \(\iota\) by \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell\log\ell\). This sheet serves that step: it states the bound for the eigenvalues of one clearing operator, Theorem 83.4.1, and deduces the bound for the projection from it (Problem 83.4.5). In Problem 31.3.3 a congruence of exponent \(e\) gave a cyclic module of classes of length \(e\). Here bounds for the lengths of modules of cocycles enter, through a Fitting ideal, an upper bound for the order at the lift of a coefficient of a Hecke element. The element multiplies certain determinant families, which vanish on the lifts, into a module of traces; in the two-system model it can be chosen to vanish at the other system. How small can its order at the lift be? We work this out on \(S_3\) at \(3\), where the congruence exponent and the length of the normalized cocycles both equal \(1\), and then count the costs of separating characters and of tame ramification in the setting of Chapter 80.

§83.1. The smallest example

Let \(G = S_3\) and \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}_3\). Let \(U_1(g) := \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{sgn}(g), 1)\), and let \(U_2(g)\) be the matrix of \(g\) on the lattice \(\{(a,b,c) \in \mathbb{Z}_3^3 : a+b+c = 0\}\), permuting coordinates, in the basis \(e_1 - e_2\), \(e_2 - e_3\). Think of \(U_1\) as a lift (a block \(\mathrm{sgn}\) plus the trivial character) and of \(U_2\) as another system. Let \(\mathbb{T}\subseteq \mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3\) be the \(\mathbb{Z}_3\)-algebra generated by the pairs of coefficients of the characteristic polynomials of \(U_1(g)\) and \(U_2(g)\), \(g \in G\): a toy Hecke algebra (in this sheet \(\mathbb{T}\) is this algebra, not \(\mathbb T^{\mathcal M}\)).

Problem 83.1.1. The pairs \((\operatorname{tr}U_1(g), \operatorname{tr}U_2(g))\) are \((2,2)\), \((2,-1)\), \((0,0)\) for \(g = 1\), a \(3\)-cycle, a transposition. The characteristic polynomials of \(U_1(g)\) and \(U_2(g)\) agree modulo \(3\) for every \(g\), and \(\mathbb{T}= \{(a,b) : a \equiv b \pmod 3\}\).

Problem 83.1.2. The elements of \(\mathbb{T}\) that vanish at \(U_2\) are the \((3u, 0)\), \(u \in \mathbb{Z}_3\); so the congruence between \(U_1\) and \(U_2\) has exponent \(1\). The representation \(U_2 \otimes \mathbb{Q}_3\) is irreducible, and the reduction of \(U_2\) modulo \(3\) has semisimplification \(1 \oplus \mathrm{sgn}\).

§83.2. Ribet cocycles

Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be a commutative ring, \(G\) a group, and \(U_1, \dots, U_n \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_q(\mathcal{O})\) homomorphisms; write \(U(g) - 1 := (U_i(g) - 1)_i \in M_q(\mathcal{O})^n\), and let \(\mathcal{L}\) be the \(\mathcal{O}\)-span of the \(U(g) - 1\). In this sheet the sans-serif letters \(\mathsf B, \mathsf C, \mathsf K, \mathsf M, \mathsf N, \mathsf R\) denote modules, rings and scalars of commutative algebra, distinct from the reserved \(B, K, M, N\).

Definition 83.2.1. Let \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_s(\mathcal{O})\) be a homomorphism (a block) and \(\mathsf B\) an \(\mathcal{O}\)-module. A Ribet cocycle for \(\rho\) with values in \(\mathsf B^s\) is a map \(z \colon G \to \mathsf B^s\) with \(z(gh) = z(g) + \rho(g)z(h)\) for all \(g, h\), that obeys the relations among the \(U(g) - 1\): for every finitely supported \(\theta \colon G \to \mathcal{O}\) with \(\sum_g \theta(g)(U(g) - 1) = 0\), also \(\sum_g \theta(g) z(g) = 0\). Call \(z\) normalized at \(g_* \in G\) if \(z(g_*) = 0\).

Used in: Chapter 84

For \(\mathsf B = \Lambda/\alpha\) these are the cocycles of Section 31.4.

Problem 83.2.1. Suppose \(\rho(g_*) = y \cdot 1\) with \(y - 1 \in \mathcal{O}^\times\). Then a coboundary \(g \mapsto \rho(g)m - m\) that vanishes at \(g_*\) is \(0\), and every Ribet cocycle for \(\rho\) differs by exactly one coboundary from a Ribet cocycle normalized at \(g_*\).

So normalized cocycles stand for classes. Return to the model of Section 83.1, with \(U = (U_1, U_2)\), the block \(\rho = \mathrm{sgn}\), and \(g_* = \tau := (1\,2)\), where \(\mathrm{sgn}(\tau) - 1 = -2\) is a unit.

Problem 83.2.2. By machine: \(\mathcal{L}\subseteq M_2(\mathbb{Z}_3)^2\) is free of rank \(5\). Let \(\mathcal{L}'\) be the span of the elements \((U(g) - \mathrm{sgn}(g))(U(h) - 1)\), \(g, h \in G\), and of \(U(\tau) - 1\). Then \(\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}' \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\); without \(U(\tau) - 1\) the quotient is \(\mathbb{Z}_3 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\).

Problem 83.2.3. For every \(n \ge 1\), the Ribet cocycles for \(\mathrm{sgn}\) with values in \(\mathbb{Z}/3^n\) normalized at \(\tau\) form a group of order \(3\), and all Ribet cocycles for \(\mathrm{sgn}\) a group of order \(3^{n+1}\). With \(U = U_1\) alone, the normalized ones are \(0\).

With values in \(\mathbb{Q}_3/\mathbb{Z}_3\) the normalized cocycles therefore have length \(1\), the exponent of the congruence of Problem 83.1.2; with \(U_1\) alone the module is zero.

§83.3. Lengths bound Fitting ideals

From now on \(\mathcal{O}\) is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(\varpi\), fraction field \(\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O})\) and normalized valuation \(\operatorname{ord}\). For a commutative ring \(\mathsf R\) and an \(\mathsf R\)-submodule \(\mathsf N \subseteq \mathsf R^s\), \(\mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf R^s/\mathsf N)\) is the ideal generated by the determinants of the \(s \times s\) matrices whose rows lie in \(\mathsf N\).

Problem 83.3.1. Let \(\mathsf M = \mathcal{O}^n/\mathsf N\) have finite length. Then \(\mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf M) = \varpi^{\operatorname{len}\mathsf M}\mathcal{O}\) and \(\operatorname{len}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathsf M, \mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O}) = \operatorname{len}\mathsf M\). For the rows \((3, 0)\), \((1, 9)\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_3\), \(\mathsf M \cong \mathbb{Z}/27\).

In the model, the quotient \(\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{L}'\) of Problem 83.2.2 has \(\mathrm{Fitt}_0= 3\mathbb{Z}_3\). For the following problems let \(\mathsf R\) be a commutative \(\mathcal{O}\)-algebra. Let \(\Phi \colon \mathcal{O}^n \to \mathsf R^s\) be \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear, \(p \ne 0\) in \(\mathcal{O}\), and \(\mathsf N \subseteq \mathsf R^s\) an \(\mathsf R\)-submodule with \(\Phi(p\mathcal{O}^n) \subseteq \mathsf N\). The forms vanishing on \(\Phi^{-1}\mathsf N\) are the \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear \(\Psi \colon \mathcal{O}^n \to \mathcal{O}/p\) with \(\Psi(v) = 0\) whenever \(\Phi(v) \in \mathsf N\).

Problem 83.3.2. \(\Phi(u) \in \mathsf N\) if and only if every form vanishing on \(\Phi^{-1}\mathsf N\) kills \(u\).

Problem 83.3.3. Suppose \(\Phi(v) \notin \mathsf N\).

  1. Some \(\mathbf w = \Phi(\varpi^k v)\) has \(\mathbf w \notin \mathsf N\) and \(\varpi \mathbf w \in \mathsf N\).

  2. For such \(\mathbf w\), \(\varpi\,\mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf R^s/(\mathsf N + \mathsf R\mathbf w)) \subseteq \mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf R^s/\mathsf N)\), and the module of forms vanishing on \(\Phi^{-1}(\mathsf N + \mathsf R\mathbf w)\) is strictly smaller than that for \(\mathsf N\).

  3. If \(\mathsf R\) is noetherian, there is \(c \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\operatorname{ord}c\) at most the length of the module of forms vanishing on \(\Phi^{-1}\mathsf N\) and \(c\,\mathrm{Fitt}_0\bigl(\mathsf R^s/(\mathsf N + \mathsf R \cdot \Phi(\mathcal{O}^n))\bigr) \subseteq \mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf R^s/\mathsf N)\).

The proof uses a relative form of (c), for noetherian \(\mathsf R\). Let \(z \colon G \to \mathcal{O}^n\) be a family of vectors, \(V\) an \(\mathcal{O}\)-module of functions \(G \to \mathcal{O}/p\), and \(F\) the module of the functions \(\Psi \circ z\) for the forms \(\Psi\) vanishing on \(\Phi^{-1}\mathsf N\). Then there are \(c \in \mathcal{O}\) and an \(\mathsf R\)-submodule \(\mathsf K \supseteq \mathsf N\) with \(\operatorname{ord}c \le \operatorname{len}(F/(F \cap V))\), \(c\,\mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf R^s/\mathsf K) \subseteq \mathrm{Fitt}_0(\mathsf R^s/\mathsf N)\), and \(\Phi(v) \in \mathsf K\) for every \(v\) killed by every \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear \(\Psi \colon \mathcal{O}^n \to \mathcal{O}/p\) such that \(\Psi \circ z \in V\). Here is the block bound of the proof with no auxiliary indices. Choose \(g_1', \dots, g_m' \in G\) and coordinates \(z(g) \in \mathcal{O}^m\) with \(U(g) - 1 = \sum_i z(g)_i(U(g_i') - 1)\), and write \([g]\) for \(g\) in \(\mathcal{O}[G]\). Let \(\mathsf N_0 \subseteq \mathcal{O}[G]^m\) be a left submodule containing the rows \(z(gh) - z(g) - [g]z(h)\), \(z(g_i') - e_i\), and \(\sum_g \theta(g)z(g)\) for every finitely supported relation \(\sum_g\theta(g)(U(g) - 1) = 0\), and a row whose image under \([g] \mapsto \rho(g)\) agrees with that of \(z(g_*)\). If \(\rho(G)\) spans \(M_s(\mathcal{O})\) and the Ribet cocycles for \(\rho\) with values in \((\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O})^s\) normalized at \(g_*\) have length at most \(b_{\mathrm{main}} \in \mathbb{N}\), some \(m \times m\) matrix with rows in \(\mathsf N_0\) has, after applying \(\rho\) entrywise and flattening, determinant of order at most \(b_{\mathrm{main}}\). With auxiliary indices the proof takes tied rows over the polynomial group algebra, uses a scalar normalizer as in Problem 83.2.1, imposes vanishing also at the first element of each tie (the elements \(s_{jb}\) of the general lemma in Section 83.4), and sets all variables to zero before taking this determinant. An element of a Fitting ideal acts like a clearing scalar:

Problem 83.3.4. For a square matrix \(\mathsf B\) over a commutative ring, \(\det \mathsf B\) kills the cokernel of \(\mathsf B\).

Definition 83.3.1. Call a homomorphism \(U \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_q(\mathcal{O})\) a weak lift if \(\det(1 - U(g)) = 0\) for every \(g \in G\).

Used in: Chapter 84

For a system of forms the proof asks this of the characteristic polynomials of its normalized representation at every \(g \in G_E\). For an attached system satisfying the lift trace formula of Definition 80.1.8, the normalized representation is a weak lift, since its trace at every group element includes the trivial character \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^0\) among the stated summands.

Problem 83.3.5. If \(U = \rho \oplus 1\) in some basis, then \(U\) is a weak lift and \(\det \sum_g \omega(g)(U(g) - 1) = 0\) for every finitely supported \(\omega \colon G \to \mathcal{O}\). Conversely, if all these determinants vanish, \(U\) is a weak lift. In the model, \(U_2\) is not a weak lift.

Here is the statement of the proof that turns these lengths into a Hecke element, in its simplest case: no auxiliary places, no tame elements, one character. Let \(G\) be finite, \(U_1, \dots, U_n \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_q(\mathcal{O})\), and \(p \ne 0\) in \(\mathcal{O}\). Let \(\mathcal S \subseteq \mathcal{O}^n\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-subalgebra (componentwise operations) containing the families \((c_k(U_i(g)))_i\) of coefficients of characteristic polynomials, and \(\mathcal J \subseteq \mathcal{O}^n\) an \(\mathcal S\)-submodule containing the families \((p \operatorname{tr}U_i(g))_i\). Let \(\mathcal X \subseteq \{1, \dots, n\}\) contain \(1\), and suppose that, in one basis for all \(i \in \mathcal X\), \(U_i = \rho_i \oplus 1\), and some \(g_i\) has \(\rho_i(g_i) = y_i \cdot 1\) with \(y_i - 1 \in \mathcal{O}^\times\); suppose the values of \(\rho_1\) span \(M_{q-1}(\mathcal{O})\). Let \(b_{\mathrm{main}} \in \mathbb N\) bound the length of the Ribet cocycles for \(\rho_1\) with values in \((\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O})^{q-1}\) normalized at \(g_1\), and \(b_0\) the length of the Ribet cocycles for the trivial character with values in \(\mathcal{O}/p\). Then the families \(\delta_\omega := (\det \sum_g \omega(g)(U_i(g) - 1))_i\) lie in \(\mathcal S\) and vanish on \(\mathcal X\), and some \(\mathfrak f \in \mathcal S\) has \[\mathfrak f\,\delta_\omega \in \mathcal J \text{ for every } \omega, \qquad \operatorname{ord}\mathfrak f_1 \le b_{\mathrm{main}} + b_0 .\] Status: read from the proof [GL]; it is the specialization of its first-order lemma to a finite group, with no auxiliary places and no tame elements, one trivial character, the whole group as detecting set and separating scalar \(1\). We take it as given.

Problem 83.3.6. In the model take \(\mathcal S = \mathbb{T}\), \(p = 3^\nu\) with \(\nu \ge 1\), \(\mathcal J = 3^\nu\mathbb{T}\), \(\mathcal X = \{1\}\), \(\rho_1 = \mathrm{sgn}\), \(g_1 = \tau\). The hypotheses hold with \(b_{\mathrm{main}} = 1\), \(b_0 = 0\); every \(\delta_\omega\) is \((0, d)\) with \(d \in 3\mathbb{Z}_3\), and \(d = 3\) occurs. The element \(\mathfrak f = (3, 0)\) qualifies, and every \(\mathfrak f \in \mathbb{T}\) with \(\mathfrak f\delta_\omega \in \mathcal J\) for all \(\omega\) has \(\operatorname{ord}\mathfrak f_1 \ge 1\).

So the bound is attained by \(\mathfrak f = (3, 0)\), which vanishes at \(U_2\) and has value \(3\) at the lift; this value generates the congruence ideal \(3\mathbb{Z}_3\) of Problem 83.1.2.

§83.4. The costs and the bound for one operator

We now use the data of Chapter 36, the integer \(\mathcal N\) of Section 37.1, and the setting and definitions of Section 80.1: \(\ell\), \(\iota\), \(\operatorname{val}\), \(\operatorname{val}_+\), coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\), \(G_E\), \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\), and \(n_\ell = \operatorname{val}_\ell(\mathcal N)\) and \(\varepsilon_\ell\) of Definition 80.1.1; in this sheet \(o_\ell := \operatorname{val}_\ell(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\), so that the first term of \(\varepsilon_\ell\) is \(t\,o_\ell\). For an isotypic system containing a nonzero lift generator, the trace identity of Definition 80.1.8 gives the trace of the normalized representation as the trace of the main summand twisted by \(\hat\vartheta\), plus \(\sum_{0 \le e < t'}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\). Separating these \(t'\) characters costs a power of \(\ell\).

Definition 83.4.1. Put \(h_\ell := 0\) if \(\ell > (\Delta/2)^2\) and \(h_\ell := n_\ell\) otherwise, and \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}:= \ell^{(\Delta/2)h_\ell}\). (The proof of the generator lemma supplies \(h_\ell\) as a witness; with this witness the separation scalar is \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}\).)

Problem 83.4.3.

  1. In the general first-order lemma of the proof, \(\mathcal{O}\) is a discrete valuation ring, \(G\) is a group, \(p \in \mathcal{O}\) is nonzero, and \(U_i \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_q(\mathcal{O})\) is a finite family of representations. At every lift index \(i \in \mathcal X\) the matrices are, in common coordinates, \(\rho_i \oplus \bigoplus_{e < t'}\xi_e\), with \(1 \le t' \le q\) and characters \(\xi_e\), \(\xi_0 = 1\). At a fixed \(i_0 \in \mathcal X\) the values of \(\rho_{i_0}\) span \(M_{q-t'}(\mathcal{O})\); for each \(i \in \mathcal X\) some \(g_i\) has \(\rho_i(g_i) = y_i 1\), \(y_i - 1 \in \mathcal{O}^\times\), and \(\xi_e(g_i) = 1\) for all \(e\). An element \(g_{\mathrm{sep}} \in G\) and a scalar \(\mathsf C \in \mathcal{O}\) satisfy \(\prod_{k < t',\, k \ne e}(\xi_e(g_{\mathrm{sep}}) - \xi_k(g_{\mathrm{sep}})) \mid \mathsf C\) for every \(e\). Let \(J\) be a finite linearly ordered set of \(n_{\mathrm{aux}}\) indices with a nondecreasing assignment \(j \mapsto e_j \in \{0, \dots, t'-1\}\). For a commutative \(\mathcal{O}\)-algebra \(L\), let \(\mathcal S\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-subalgebra of \(\prod_i L[X_j : j \in J]\) containing the families of variables and of characteristic coefficients, and let \(\mathcal J\) be an \(\mathcal S\)-submodule of this product containing \((p\operatorname{tr}U_i(g))_i\) for every \(g\); coefficients from \(\mathcal{O}\) are read as constant polynomials via \(\mathcal{O}\to L\). For each \(j\) there are \(\epsilon_j \in \mathcal{O}[X]\) and two pairs \((s_{jb}, \mathrm{Fr}_{jb})\) in \(G\), \(b = 0, 1\), such that for every \(g \in G\) the family of traces of \(\bigl(U_i(s_{jb}) - 1 - X_j(U_i(\mathrm{Fr}_{jb}) - 1)\epsilon_j(U_i(\mathrm{Fr}_{jb}))\bigr)U_i(g)\) lies in \(\mathcal J\), and \(\epsilon_j(\xi_e(\mathrm{Fr}_{jb})) \equiv 1 \pmod p\) for all \(e\). A finite family \((\delta_w, o_w)\) in \(G \times \mathcal{O}[X]\) has \(\xi_e(\delta_w) = 1\) and \((X - 1)o_w(X)\) annihilating every \(U_i(\delta_w)\). Let \(b_{\mathrm{main}} \in \mathbb N\) and \(b_e \in \mathbb N \cup \{\infty\}\) for \(0 \le e < t'\). Suppose \(b_{\mathrm{main}}\) bounds the length of the Ribet cocycles for \(\rho_{i_0}\) with values in \((\mathrm{Frac}(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O})^{q-t'}\) that vanish at \(g_{i_0}\) and at all \(s_{jb}\). For each \(e\), every submodule \(\mathcal Z\) of Ribet cocycles for \(\xi_e\) with values in \(\mathcal{O}/p\) whose members vanish at all \(\delta_w\) and satisfy \(z(g_{i_0}) = 0 \Rightarrow (\xi_e(g) - 1)z(g_{\mathrm{sep}}) = 0\) for all \(g\), satisfies the triangle cost condition of the proof along the indices of type \(e\), with scalar \(\mathsf C\) and bound \(b_e\), and is detected by one fixed set of \(n_{\mathrm{det}}\) elements (a member vanishing there is zero). Then every \(\delta_\omega\), \(\omega\) finitely supported, lies in \(\mathcal S\) and vanishes on \(\mathcal X\); one \(\mathfrak f \in \mathcal S\) has \(\mathfrak f\delta_\omega \in \mathcal J\) for all \(\omega\); at every \(i \in \mathcal X\), \(\mathfrak f_i\) has no monomial properly dividing \(\prod_j X_j\); and the coefficient of \(\prod_j X_j\) in \(\mathfrak f_{i_0}\) is the image of some \(c \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\operatorname{ord}c\) at most \[b_{\mathrm{main}} + \sum_{0 \le e < t'} b_e + t'\Bigl(\sum_w \operatorname{ord}o_w(1) + 2n_{\mathrm{det}} \operatorname{ord}\mathsf C\Bigr) + (n_{\mathrm{aux}} + 1)\operatorname{ord}\mathsf C .\] For the exponent \(h\) and the element \(g_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) supplied by the generator lemma, the application takes \(\xi_e = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\), \(g_{\mathrm{sep}} = g_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-1}\) and \(\mathsf C = \ell^{(\Delta/2)h}\); at each auxiliary place the two ties pair its tame generator with its Frobenius element, and the inverses of their conjugates with one another. We write the estimates with the witness \(h = h_\ell\) of Definition 83.4.1. In this application \(p = \ell^\nu\). Over a sufficiently large coefficient ring choose the twisted lattice \(L_{\mathrm{tw}}\) at \(i_0\) with the prescribed Selmer bound, and write \(g_* = g_{i_0}\). The proof takes \(b_{\mathrm{main}}\) to be the sum of the local dual defects of the Tate duals of \(L_{\mathrm{tw}}\) above \(\ell\) and the length of its crystalline Selmer module with divisible coefficients normalized at \(g_*\), whose normalized length is at most \(\mathrm{sel}\). For \(0 \le e < t'\), \(b_e\) is the crystalline colength of the Ribet cocycles for \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) modulo \(\ell^\nu\), plus the length of the module of Definition 27.3.1 at \(e\) and \(\nu\) (at \(e = 0\), all everywhere unramified continuous homomorphisms to \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\)), whose normalized length is at most \(o_\ell\). The normalized sum of the main dual defects and of the character colengths is at most \(r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell\). Here \(n_{\mathrm{test}}\) is the number of test points plus one for the separating element, \(n_{\mathrm{det}} \le 2(n_{\mathrm{aux}} + n_{\mathrm{test}})\) and \((n_{\mathrm{aux}} + n_{\mathrm{test}})(\Delta/2)h_\ell \le (\Delta/2)^{22}h_\ell\). No length of a module of even classes enters.

Problem 83.4.4. Let \(t', h, n_{\mathrm{aux}}, n_{\mathrm{test}}, n_{\mathrm{det}}\) be natural numbers and \(\mathrm{tame}, \mathrm{ram} \in [0, \infty]\), with \(t' \le t\), \(h \le n_\ell\), \((n_{\mathrm{aux}} + n_{\mathrm{test}})(\Delta/2)h \le (\Delta/2)^{22}h\), \(n_{\mathrm{det}} \le 2(n_{\mathrm{aux}} + n_{\mathrm{test}})\) and \(\mathrm{tame} \le 2\,\mathrm{ram} + 8fn_\ell\). At auxiliary level the proof realizes the first-order element as a polynomial over the Hecke algebra. Let \(A\) be the indicator of the systems whose normalized characteristic coefficients are congruent modulo the maximal ideal to those of the distinguished lift at every \(g \in G_E\); multiplying the coefficient of \(\prod_j X_j\) by \(A^{n_{\mathrm{aux}} + 1}\) gives diagonal scalars. For a nonzero lift generator of type \(c\), the proof projects to type \(c\), passes to smooth forms, applies the diagonal operator and scales by \(\mathcal N^2\). One factor \(\mathcal N\) makes the type projection of an integral input integral, and the other comes from the norm operator at auxiliary level; their valuation is \(2n_\ell\). It does this for a basis of lift generators and takes a combination with coefficients integral at \(\iota\). The result is the theorem of this sheet.

Theorem 83.4.1. Let \(\vartheta\) be a twist that is auxiliary with ramification at most \(\mathrm{ram} \in [0, \infty]\), let \(\mathrm{sel} \in [0, \infty)\), let \(\mathfrak{O}\) be a descent pair of ranks \(r' < n'\) and \(t' = n' - r'\), \(e_1\) an element of the group algebra of \(\prod_w \mathrm{GL}_{r'}(k_w)\) over its chosen places, and \(\mathcal F \subseteq \mathcal A(\mathfrak{O})\) a subspace contained in the span of the lift generators for \((\vartheta, \mathfrak{O}, e_1, \mathcal F)\) with Selmer bound \(\mathrm{sel}\). Then some \(C_\ell \in \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal F\) has the following properties.

  1. For every split place \(w\) such that neither \(w\) nor \(\bar w\) is a place of \(\mathfrak{O}\), all \(T, a \in \mathbb{C}\) and \(y \in \mathcal F\): if the Hecke polynomial operator of \(\mathfrak{O}\) at \(w\) at the value \(T\) sends \(y\) to \(ay\), it sends \(C_\ell y\) to \(aC_\ell y\).

  2. \(\operatorname{val}_+(\iota\mu) \le \mathrm{sel} + 2t'\,\mathrm{ram} + r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell + \varepsilon_\ell\) for every eigenvalue \(\mu\) of \(C_\ell\).

  3. \(C_\ell(\mathrm{pr}(y))\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every form \(y\) integral at \(\iota\) and orthogonal to the false lifts for \((\mathfrak{O}, e_1, \mathcal F^\perp)\), where \(\mathrm{pr}\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal F\).

What remains is the first-order element itself. The construction ties the rows of the auxiliary places and uses an adjugate identity to turn the relations into a multiplier. Amitsur’s formula puts the coefficients of its scalar polynomial in \(\mathcal S\), which contains the characteristic coefficients and the auxiliary variables [Am] (see [Ch, formula (1.5)]). The passage from equal traces to block lattices uses the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem for trace-nondegenerate representations [CR]. These steps are taken from the proof here; the auxiliary places are those of Theorem 84.3.1.

What this sheet establishes.

On \(S_3\) at \(3\) the congruence exponent equals the length of the normalized Ribet cocycles, and the specialization of the first-order bound to this model is attained. In general the sheet proves the Fitting-ideal step in absolute form, the separation and tame costs, the arithmetic that turns the count into the bound of Theorem 83.4.1, and the deduction of Theorem 80.1.1. We take Theorem 83.4.1, the first-order lemma and its inputs \(b_{\mathrm{main}}\), \(b_e\) as given, with Chapter 82 for the local loss and Theorem 27.3.2 for the odd modules. The choice of the auxiliary places, and why no length of even classes enters the costs \(b_e\), are the subject of Chapter 84.
