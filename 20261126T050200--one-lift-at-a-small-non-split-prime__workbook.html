How divisible by \(p\) is a single lift of the chain, and where does it fall short of \(\operatorname{gain}(p)/m\)? Chapter 57 composes \(m\) lifts and needs, at each stage, a bound for the kernel of the lift at a prime \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) (its input (iii) in Section 57.3). The stage bound takes the predicted divisibility of the diagram after erasure, credits each erased box, and deducts a logarithmic counting term based on the original number of boxes. We compare it with \(\operatorname{gain}(p)/m\), allowing a uniform error and the collision sum. We work it out first on a model of the places, then for the data, and finally state how the bound reaches the kernel.

§59.1. The stage bound

Recall (Definitions 58.2.1 and 58.2.2) that a diagram \(\beta\) is read at heights below a fixed bound, the number of rows; \(\beta_1(\tau)\) is its number of columns of height one at \(\tau\), \(\beta_{>1}(\tau)\) the number of its other columns, \(\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)\) the number of boxes in these, and \(\lvert\beta\rvert\) the number of all its boxes; \(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi'\) deletes the columns of height one at the places of \(\Phi'\). With \(\tau_e := [-p^e]\tau\), \[\begin{align} \mathcal D(\beta) ={}& c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \Bigl[ \Bigl(\frac{\beta_1(\tau)}{p^e} - \beta_1(\tau_e) - \beta_{>1}(\tau_e)\Bigr)_+ + \Bigl(\frac{\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)}{p^e} - \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau_e)\Bigr)_+ \\ &\qquad - \frac{\beta_{>1}(\tau)}{p^e} \Bigr] - [p = d]\, \frac12 \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \operatorname{rc}(\tau) , \end{align}\] with (a notation of this chapter) \(\operatorname{rc}(\tau) := \min(\beta_1(\tau), \beta_1(\tau_0) + \beta_{>1}(\tau_0)) + \beta_{>1}(\tau) + \min(\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau), \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau_0))\) (Definition 58.2.3). The diagram of the data (Definition 37.2.1) has \[\beta(\tau, 1) = a\,\mathbf 1_{\Phi_a}(\tau), \qquad \beta(\tau, k) = \kappa_1(\tau) - \Bigl\lfloor \frac{k + t + 1}{2} \Bigr\rfloor,\] \(\beta(\tau, h) = \Delta - (t + 1)\) if \(h > k\) and \((t+1) \mid h - k\), and \(\beta(\tau, h) = 0\) otherwise. At an \(a\)-place the long row means the \(a\) boxes contributed by the height-one columns. For \(0 \le j < m\) we read the diagram at heights below \(n_{j+1}\) and keep the letter \(\beta\).

Definition 59.1.1. Let \(p\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), \(j \ge 0\), \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), and \(\beta\) the diagram of the data read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\). Define the stage bound by \[\mathcal D_j(\Phi') := \mathcal D(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi') + \gamma_p\bigl(\lvert\beta\rvert - \lvert\beta \smallsetminus \Phi'\rvert\bigr) - f n_{j+1}^3 \bigl(1 + \log_p \max(\lvert\beta\rvert, 1)\bigr) .\]

Used in: Chapter 57; Chapter 62

§59.2. A model on \(({\mathbb{Z}}/40)^\times\)

For \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) and \(e \in I_p\), the first positive part in \(\mathcal D(\beta)\) compares \(a/p^e\) with \(\beta_1(\tau_e) + \beta_{>1}(\tau_e)\); so does \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(a, \tau)\), with \(\kappa(\tau_e)\) in place of the columns. We follow the comparison on a model.

The model: \(\Phi = ({\mathbb{Z}}/40)^\times\) with \([\nu]\) the class of \(\nu\) for \((\nu, 40) = 1\), \(q_0 = 8\), \(\Phi_a = \{\tau \equiv 1 \bmod 8\}\), \(\Phi_b = \{7\}\), \(x = 6\), \(b = 343\), \(a = xb = 2058\), \(x_1 = 49\), \(k = 2\), \(\kappa = b\) on \(\Phi_b\) and \(\kappa = a/x_1 = 42\) elsewhere. The primes \(3\), \(7\) are treated as inert: \(c_p = 1\), \(I_p\) is the set of odd \(e \ge 1\), and \(\gamma_p := c_p\sum_{e \in I_p} p^{-e} = p/(p^2-1)\). The model diagram has \(a\) columns of height one at each \(a\)-place, \(\kappa(\tau)\) columns of height \(k\) at every \(\tau\), and no others; it is read at heights below \(3\). These numbers are not data (\(18480 \nmid 8\)); the model keeps only the bookkeeping.

Problem 59.2.1. In the model, \(-3\) has order \(2\) modulo \(8\), and at \(p = 3\) no pair is a collision. At \(p = 7\), \(-7 \equiv 33 \pmod{40}\), the powers of \(33\) are \(\{1, 33, 9, 17\} = \Phi_a\), and every \((\tau, e)\) with \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) and \(e\) odd is a collision outside \(\varnothing\).

Problem 59.2.2. In the model, \(\operatorname{gain}(3)/m = 25718/9\) and \(\operatorname{gain}(7)/m = 1022\); moreover \(C_7(\varnothing) = 2401/2\) and \(C_7(\{1, 9\}) = 0\).

Problem 59.2.3. \((\lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau)/p^e - \lVert \beta \rVert_{>1}(\tau_e))_+ \ge k(\kappa(\tau)/p^e - \kappa(\tau_e))_+ - \varepsilon\), with equality and \(\varepsilon = 0\) for the model diagram.

Problem 59.2.4. Let \(\tau \in \Phi_a \smallsetminus \Phi'\) and \(e \in I_p\). If \(\tau_e \notin \Phi_a \smallsetminus \Phi'\), the first positive part inside the brackets defining \(\mathcal D(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi')\) at \((\tau, e)\) is at least \((a/p^e - \kappa(\tau_e))_+ - \varepsilon\). If \((\tau, e)\) is a collision outside \(\Phi'\), it is \(0\), which is at least \((a/p^e - \kappa(\tau_e))_+ - a/p^e\).

Problem 59.2.5. Let \(1 \in I_p\), \(p < x_1\), and \(\kappa \ge a/x_1\) on \(\Phi\). For every \(\tau\), \(\gamma_p a \ge c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} (a/p^e - \kappa(\tau_e))_+ + c_p a/x_1\).

Problem 59.2.6. In the model, let \(p \in \{3, 7\}\), \(\beta\) the model diagram and \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\). §59.3. The data

Now let \(\beta\) be the diagram of the data read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\), \(0 \le j < m\), and let \(p < x_1\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\). In this section take \(\varepsilon = x^{60}\).

Problem 59.3.1. (i) If \(p^e \ge x_1\), then \((a/p^e - \kappa(\tau_e))_+ = 0\) and \((\kappa(\tau)/p^e - \kappa(\tau_e))_+ = 0\); and \(2c_p \lceil \log_p x_1 \rceil x^{60} \le a/(pm)\). (ii) \(\gamma_p \beta_{>1}(\tau) \le a/(pm)\) for every \(\tau\). (iii) \(n_{j+1}^3 (1 + \log_p \max(\lvert\beta\rvert, 1)) \le a/(pm)\).

Problem 59.3.2. Let \(p = d\). For every \(\tau\), the places \(\tau\) and \(\tau_0 = [-1]\tau\) are neither both in \(\Phi_a\) nor both in \(\Phi_b\), and \(\operatorname{rc}(\tau) \le 3a/(dm)\), also for \(\beta \smallsetminus \Phi'\) in place of \(\beta\). Hence the correction \(\frac12 \sum_\tau \operatorname{rc}(\tau)\) is at most \(2fa/(dm)\).

Problem 59.3.3. For every \(\Phi' \subseteq \Phi\), \(\mathcal D_j(\Phi') \ge \operatorname{gain}(p)/m - C_p(\Phi') + c_p\, \#(\Phi_a \cap \Phi')\, a/x_1 - 5fa/(pm)\). Deduce part (b) of Theorem 59.4.4.

§59.4. One kernel

Fix a twist \(\vartheta\) and \(j \ge 0\). A datum of the lift is an element of the finite part of the Schwartz space of \(\mathrm{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\), the complex span of the finite Heisenberg translates of the vacuum. Take the theta kernel from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\) with source level \(K^X_j\), the rank twists of \(\mu_{j+1}^0\vartheta^{j+1}\) on the two spaces, and scale \(\pi_Mc_{\mathrm{jet}}\) (defined below). This is the kernel of Definition 52.4.2 for \(j < m\), and we use the same prescription for \(j \ge m\). For a datum \(\varphi\), let \(\mathcal J_\varphi\) denote this kernel evaluated at \(\varphi\), a linear map from the Weyl vectors of the weight of \(X_j\) to those of \(X_{j+1}\). Its diagram is \(\beta\) read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\). Fix a prime \(p\) and a ring map \(\eta\colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_p\).

Definition 59.4.1. Let \(\delta \in \mathcal O_K\). A standard datum of denominator \(\delta\) on \(\mathrm{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\) is an element of the \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-span of the translates by \(\mathrm{Heis}(y)\) of the integral vacuum, over the finite adelic \(y\) such that \(\delta y\) is integral at every finite place of \(E\) above \(p\).

Used in: Chapter 57; Chapter 60; Chapter 61; Chapter 62; Chapter 65; Chapter Overview

Definition 59.4.2. A datum \(\varphi\) multiplies gauges by at most \(c \in \mathbb R\) if \(\lVert \mathcal J_{(g,g')\varphi}\, \omega \rVert_{g'} \le \max(c, 0)\, \lVert \omega \rVert_g\) for all finite adelic points \(g\) of the unitary group of \(X_j\), \(g'\) of that of \(X_{j+1}\), and all Weyl vectors \(\omega\); here \((g, g')\varphi\) is the translate of \(\varphi\) under the Weil representation.

Used in: Chapter 57; Chapter 62; Chapter 65

Definition 59.4.3. The jet series of a datum \(\varphi\) at scale \(c_0 \in {\mathbb{C}}\) is the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(z \mapsto \mathrm{th}_\varphi(c_0 z)\), where \(\mathrm{th}_\varphi\) is the theta function of \(\varphi\), an entire function of a family \(z = (z_\tau)_{\tau \in \Phi}\) of \(n_{j+1}\times n_j\) matrices in the standard bases of \(X_{j+1}\) and \(X_j\). Define the jet scale by \(c_{\mathrm{jet}}:= c_{\mathrm{per}}\sqrt{2\pi/\sqrt d}/\Omega_0\), where \(c_{\mathrm{per}}\) is the period factor fixed in the proof (the quotient by the Chowla–Selberg unit of the principal \(f\)-th root, \(f\) the number of infinite places of \(E\), of the inverse of the value at a finite idele fixed in the proof of the character \(x \mapsto \upsilon(x)\lvert x\rvert^{-1/2}\), \(\upsilon\) the splitting character of the Weil data of \(E\); only statements (I) and (IV) below are used of it) and \(\Omega_0\) (a letter of this chapter) is the complex number fixed in the proof such that \(\Omega_0\mathcal O_K\) is the period lattice of the CM curve of \(K\). A jet of a series \(F\) is \(Q(\partial)F(0)\) for a polynomial \(Q\).

The kernel of the lift is the theta kernel at scale \(\pi_M c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), and \(\lvert \eta \pi_M \rvert \le 1\) [GL]. The next four statements relate the jet series to the gauge bound.

(I) Integrality. If \(\delta \ne 0\) and \(\varphi\) is standard of denominator \(\delta\), then every jet of its jet series at scale \(\bar\delta d c_{\mathrm{jet}}\), along a polynomial with coefficients in \(\mathcal O_\eta\), is integral at \(\eta\).

Problem 59.4.1. Let \(F\) be a power series over \({\mathbb{C}}_p\), \(c' \in {\mathbb{C}}_p\), and \(Q\) homogeneous of degree \(l\) over \({\mathbb{C}}_p\). Then \(Q(\partial)[F(c'z)](0) = c'^l Q(\partial)F(0)\). Hence, under (I), for \(c \in {\mathbb{C}}\) and homogeneous \(Q\) of degree \(l\) with coefficients in \(\mathcal O_\eta\), the jet along \(Q\) of the jet series of \(\varphi\) at scale \(cc_{\mathrm{jet}}\) has absolute value at most \(\lvert \eta(c/(\bar\delta d)) \rvert^l\) after \(\eta\).

Used in: Chapter 60; Chapter 65

(II) Kernel and jets. Let \(c_0 \in {\mathbb{C}}\) and \(\mathfrak b \in \mathbb R\). If every entry of \(\mathrm{schur}_\beta(\partial)F(0)\) has absolute value at most \(\mathfrak b\) after \(\eta\), for the jet series \(F\) at scale \(c_0\) of every standard datum of denominator \(\delta\), then every standard datum of denominator \(\delta\) multiplies the gauges of the theta kernel at scale \(c_0\) by at most \(\mathfrak b\). Here \(\mathrm{schur}_\beta(z)\) is the matrix, in the bases of standard fillings, of the map of Weyl modules of shape \(\beta\) induced by \(z\); its entries are polynomials with integral coefficients.

(III) Schur entries. If no column of \(\beta\) has height strictly between \(n_j\) and \(n_{j+1}\) (note \(n_j < n_{j+1}\)), then for every series \(F\) over \({\mathbb{C}}_p\) and every entry of \(\mathrm{schur}_\beta(\partial)F(0)\) there is an integral family \(\mu\) such that the absolute value of the entry is at most \(\lvert P_\mu(\partial)F(0)\rvert\), for the product of minors \(P_\mu\) of the output filling indexing the row of that entry.

Problem 59.4.2. A polynomial over \(\mathcal O_{{\mathbb{C}}_p}\) in finitely many variables with a unit coefficient takes a unit value at some point with integral coordinates.

Problem 59.4.3. For every \(j \ge 0\), the diagram of the data has no column of height \(h\) with \(n_j < h < n_{j+1}\).

Problem 59.4.5. Let \(\mathfrak b \ge 0\). Over \({\mathbb{C}}_p\), a matrix with entries of norm at most \(\mathfrak b\) multiplies sup norms by at most \(\mathfrak b\).   1. §59.5. The size of a collision sum

How much can collisions cost? Problem 57.4.2 of Chapter 57 uses the crude bound \(m C_p(\varnothing) \le j_p := \lceil 2mfa/p \rceil\).

Problem 59.5.1.
