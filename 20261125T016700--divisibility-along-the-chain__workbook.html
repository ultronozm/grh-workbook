Prerequisites.

Formal power series over a commutative ring. Finite extensions of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), their valuations, and lattices over their rings of integers; the length of a module of finite length. Gaussian binomial coefficients \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q}\) [Mac, Ch. I, §2, Example 3]. From Chapter 5, Size against divisibility: that a sublattice of small colength has small index, so that a gain on it is charged its colength. From Chapter 8, The chain and the square root: Section 8.4. From Chapter 18, Hecke nondegeneracy on the tree: the loss, defined in Section 18.1. From Chapter 24, Hecke operators and theta lifts: Section 24.5, in particular Problems 24.5.3 and 24.5.4. Section 25.6 says where the proof uses this transfer; the step in the generality of the proof is Theorem 62.4.1 of Chapter 62, and the rest of the chapter does not use it.

Problem 8.4.3 adds the divisibility exponents that hold on the whole lattice at each step of the chain. In the proof, Theorem 62.4.1 bounds the gauge of the restricted chain on the image of the integral source forms under a power of a product of Hecke operators. The local model in Chapter 18 measures the colength of the values of a Hecke operator at a single lattice. How does a gain on a sublattice at the start of a chain become a gain of the composite? §25.1. Elementary and complete symmetric functions Tag 22PF

Let \(x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)\) be a finite family in a commutative ring. The elementary and complete symmetric functions \(e_l(x)\) and \(h_l(x)\) are the sums of the products \(x_{i_1} \cdots x_{i_l}\) over \(i_1 < \dots < i_l\) and over \(i_1 \le \dots \le i_l\) respectively, with \(e_0 = h_0 = 1\). Their generating series are \[E_x(X) := \prod_{i} (1 + x_i X) = \sum_{l \ge 0} e_l(x) X^l , \qquad H_x(X) := \prod_{i} (1 - x_i X)^{-1} = \sum_{l \ge 0} h_l(x) X^l .\] All degrees \(l\) are nonnegative integers, and \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q} = 0\) for \(l > n\).

Tag 22PG

Problem 25.1.1. \(E_x(X) H_x(-X) = 1\), that is, \(\sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l e_{r-l}(x)\, h_l(x) = 0\) for \(r \ge 1\). For \(x = (a, b)\), \(h_2(x) = a^2 + ab + b^2\).

This is [Mac, Ch. I, (2.6)]. For families \(x\) and \(y\) write \(x \sqcup y\) for the concatenated family.

Tag 22PH

Problem 25.1.2. \[e_r(x \sqcup y) = \sum_{l=0}^r e_l(x)\, e_{r-l}(y) , \qquad h_r(x \sqcup y) = \sum_{l=0}^r h_l(x)\, h_{r-l}(y) .\]

The families that occur are geometric progressions.

Tag 22PJ

Problem 25.1.3. For \(t \ge 0\), \[e_l(1, Q, \dots, Q^t) = Q^{l(l-1)/2} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{t+1}{l}_{Q} , \qquad h_l(1, Q, \dots, Q^t) = \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{t+l}{l}_{Q} .\] For instance \(h_2(1, Q) = 1 + Q + Q^2\).

These are [Mac, Ch. I, §2, Example 3]. Let \(q\) be invertible, \(Q := q^2\), \(g \ge 1\) and \(t := g - 1\). A block is a family \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:block}\tag{1} x = \beta\,(q^t, q^{t-2}, \dots, q^{-t}) \end{equation}\] of \(g\) terms with ratio \(Q^{-1}\), where \(\beta\) is invertible. For \(\beta = 1\) this is the family in Problem 24.5.3, where \(q^t\) is written \(Q^{t/2}\).

Tag 22PK

Problem 25.1.4. For the block (1), \(e_l(x) = \beta^l q^{-l(g-l)} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q}\) and \(h_l(x) = \beta^l q^{-lt} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{t+l}{l}_{Q}\).

For \(\beta = 1\) the first formula, in degrees \(0 \le l \le g\), is Problem 24.5.3.

§25.2. Valuations Tag 22PL

Let \(k\) be a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), with ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}\) and valuation \(v\) normalized by \(v(p) = 1\).

Tag 22PM

Problem 25.2.1. \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q}\) is a polynomial in \(Q\) with integer coefficients and constant term \(1\), for \(0 \le l \le n\). Hence \(v\bigl(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q}\bigr) = 0\) if \(Q \in k\) and \(v(Q) > 0\).

Tag 22PN

Problem 25.2.2. Let \(a \in k^\times\) and \(Q \in k^\times\) with \(v(Q) = v_Q > 0\). Then \[\begin{align*} v\bigl(e_l(a, aQ, \dots, aQ^t)\bigr) &= l\,v(a) + v_Q\,\frac{l(l-1)}{2} && (0 \le l \le t+1) , \\ v\bigl(h_l(a, aQ, \dots, aQ^t)\bigr) &= l\,v(a) && (l \ge 0) . \end{align*}\]

For \(0\le l\le t+1\), the difference \(v(e_l)-v(h_l)\) for this family is \(v_Q\,l(l-1)/2\).

Tag 22PP

Problem 25.2.3. Let \(x\) be the block (1) with \(\beta, q \in k^\times\) and \(v(q) = \nu > 0\). Then \(v(h_l(x)) = l\,(v(\beta) - t\nu)\) for \(l \ge 0\), and \(v(e_l(x)) = l\,(v(\beta) - t\nu) + \nu\, l(l-1)\) for \(0 \le l \le g\). In particular every \(h_l(x)\) lies in \(\mathcal{O}\) if and only if \(v(\beta) \ge t\nu\).

For a union of blocks only a bound survives.

Tag 22PQ

Problem 25.2.4. Let \(Z\) be a finite family in \(k\) whose least valuation is a real number \(\mu\). Then \(v(h_l(Z)) \ge l\mu\), with equality if exactly one member of \(Z\) has valuation \(\mu\). For \(Z = (1, 1)\) and \(Z = (1, -1)\) one has \(h_l(Z) = l + 1\) and \(h_l(Z) = (1 + (-1)^l)/2\).

Tag 22PR

Problem 25.2.5. Let \(Z = x_1 \sqcup x_2\), where \(x_i = \beta_i\,(q, q^{-1})\) are blocks with \(g = 2\), and let \(v(q) = \nu > 0\) and \(v(\beta_1) = v(\beta_2) = 0\). Then \(h_1(Z) = (\beta_1 + \beta_2)(q + q^{-1})\), so \(v(h_1(Z)) = v(\beta_1 + \beta_2) - \nu\), which exceeds \(-\nu\) exactly when \(\beta_1 \equiv -\beta_2\) modulo the maximal ideal of \(\mathcal{O}\).

§25.3. Moving a Hecke operator along a chain Tag 22PS

We use the setting of Problem 24.5.4. Let \(F_0, \dots, F_m\) be finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces, let \(K_j \colon F_j \to F_{j+1}\) be linear maps, and let \(\mathcal{K}_j := K_{j-1} \cdots K_0\), with \(\mathcal{K}_0 := 1\). Fix \(r \ge 1\). For each \(j\) let \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_0 = 1, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_1, \dots, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_r\) be operators on \(F_j\), and let \(x_1, \dots, x_m\) be finite families in \(k\) with \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:R}\tag{2} \mathcal{E}^{(j+1)}_i\, K_j = K_j \sum_{l=0}^i e_l(x_{j+1})\, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_{i-l} \qquad (0 \le i \le r,\ 0 \le j < m) . \end{equation}\] This is the case with source argument \(X\) of the split relation in Section 24.5. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 62.4.1, statement (B) of Chapter 62 gives this relation for every translation in the support of the datum, at every prime of a mark, on forms spherical there, for \(r \le N_0\). The family at each step is a block (1). Let \(Z_j := x_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup x_j\). In (2) the operators at the target are expressed through those at the source. We need the converse.

Tag 22PT

Problem 25.3.1. Let \(r = 1\) and \(m \ge 2\). Then \(K_0 \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_1 = \bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_1 - e_1(x_1)\bigr) K_0\), and \(\mathcal{K}_2 \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_1 = \bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(2)}_1 - h_1(Z_2)\bigr) \mathcal{K}_2\).

Tag 22PU

Problem 25.3.2. Let \(r = 2\) and \(m \ge 1\). Then \(K_0 \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_2 = \bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_2 - h_1(x_1)\, \mathcal{E}^{(1)}_1 + h_2(x_1)\bigr) K_0\).

Tag 22PV

Problem 25.3.3. \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i\, \mathcal{K}_j = \mathcal{K}_j \sum_{l=0}^i e_l(Z_j)\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_{i-l}\) for \(0 \le i \le r\).

Problem 24.5.4, applied to the first \(j\) maps, gives the following identity.

Tag 22PW

Theorem 25.3.1. For \(1 \le j \le m\), \(\displaystyle \mathcal{K}_j\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r = \sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l h_l(Z_j)\, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_{r-l}\, \mathcal{K}_j\).

Used in: Problem 25.3.4; Chapter 62

The relation for one lift has the elementary functions of a block. For a block \(x\) with \(v(q)=\nu>0\) and \(0\le l\le g\), one has \(v(e_l(x))-v(h_l(x))=\nu\,l(l-1)\) (Problem 25.2.3). The inverse relation has the complete functions, which may have denominators. For the lattice normalization in the next two problems assume \(1 \le r \le N_0\), where \(N_0\) is the dimension of the space underlying \(F_0\). The operators \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i\) are normalized forms of the sums \(T^{(j)}_i\) over edges of type \(i\) of Section 24.5: when \(F_j\) consists of functions of lattices in a space of dimension \(N_j\), with \(N_0 < N_1 < \dots < N_m\), one takes \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i = q^{-i(N_j - i)}\, T^{(j)}_i\) for \(0 \le i \le N_j\).

Tag 22PX

Problem 25.3.4. With this normalization, Theorem 25.3.1 reads \[\sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l h_l(Z_j)\, q^{l(N_j - 2r + l)}\, T^{(j)}_{r-l}\, \mathcal{K}_j = q^{r(N_j - N_0)}\, \mathcal{K}_j\, T^{(0)}_r .\]

Used in: Problem 25.3.5
Tag 22PY

Problem 25.3.5. Suppose that \(Z_j\) is a union of blocks (1) with the same \(t\), that \(v(q) > 0\), that \(v(\beta) \ge 0\) for each block, and that \(N_j \ge 2r + t - 1\). Then every coefficient \(h_l(Z_j)\, q^{l(N_j - 2r + l)}\) in Problem 25.3.4 lies in \(\mathcal{O}\).

§25.4. A gain on a sublattice Tag 22PZ

For each \(j\) let \(\mathrm{Int}_j \subset F_j\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-lattice of full rank. For \(w \in F_j\) let \(\operatorname{ord}(w)\) be the least valuation of a coordinate of \(w\) in a basis of \(\mathrm{Int}_j\), with \(\operatorname{ord}(0) = \infty\); we use \(v(0) = +\infty\) and \(\min\varnothing = +\infty\). For a linear map \(\phi \colon F_i \to F_j\) let \(\operatorname{gain}(\phi)\) be the least value of \(\operatorname{ord}(\phi y)\) over the members \(y\) of a basis of \(\mathrm{Int}_i\). For \(k = \mathbb{Q}_p\) and \(\phi \ne 0\), \(\operatorname{gain}(\phi)\) is the largest integer \(n\) with \(\phi(\mathrm{Int}_i) \subseteq p^n \mathrm{Int}_j\); so \(\operatorname{gain}(\phi) \log p\) plays at \(p\) the role of the divisibility \(-h_{\mathrm{fin}}\) of Section 8.4.

Tag 22Q2

Problem 25.4.1. \(\operatorname{ord}\) and \(\operatorname{gain}\) do not depend on the bases. \(\operatorname{ord}(\phi w) \ge \operatorname{ord}(w) + \operatorname{gain}(\phi)\), \(\operatorname{gain}(\phi\psi) \ge \operatorname{gain}(\phi) + \operatorname{gain}(\psi)\), \(\operatorname{gain}(\phi + \psi) \ge \min(\operatorname{gain}(\phi), \operatorname{gain}(\psi))\), and \(\operatorname{gain}(c\phi) = v(c) + \operatorname{gain}(\phi)\) for \(c \in k^\times\).

Assume (2). For \(1 \le j \le m\) and \(0 \le i \le r\) let \[\gamma_j(i) := \operatorname{gain}\bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i K_{j-1}\bigr) ,\] so that \(\gamma_j(0) = \operatorname{gain}(K_{j-1})\) is the gain of the \(j\)th step on the whole lattice, and \(\gamma_j(r)\) its gain after the Hecke operator \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_r\) at the target. Let \(c \in k^\times\) be such that \(Y := c\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r\) maps \(\mathrm{Int}_0\) into itself, and let \(A := v(c)\). To bound \(\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal{K}_j\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r w)\) in terms of \(\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal{K}_{j-1} w)\), define \[\delta_j := \min_{0 \le l \le r} \bigl( v(h_l(Z_j)) + \gamma_j(r - l) \bigr) .\] When \(\gamma_j(r) < +\infty\), define the price of the \(j\)th step by \(\mathrm{Pr}_j := \gamma_j(r) - \delta_j\). It is nonnegative.

Tag 22Q3

Problem 25.4.2. For \(w \in F_0\) and \(1 \le j \le m\), \(\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_j\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r w\bigr) \ge \operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_{j-1} w\bigr) + \delta_j\).

Tag 22Q4

Problem 25.4.3. Let \(S \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}\), and let \(n_j\) be the number of elements of \(S\) that are at most \(j\). For \(G \in \mathrm{Int}_0\) and \(0 \le j \le m\), \[\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_j\, Y^{n_j} G\bigr) \ge \sum_{1 \le i \le j,\ i \in S} \bigl(A + \delta_i\bigr) + \sum_{1 \le i \le j,\ i \notin S} \gamma_i(0) .\]

Tag 22Q5

Theorem 25.4.1. For every \(F \in \mathrm{Int}_0\), \[\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_m\, Y^m F\bigr) \ge \sum_{j=1}^m \max\bigl(\gamma_j(0),\ A + \delta_j\bigr) .\]

The gain holds on the image \(Y^m \mathrm{Int}_0\). When \(Y\) is injective this is a sublattice of full rank, and in Chapter 5 the gain on such a sublattice is charged its colength. In the proof one copy of the product \(Y_{\mathfrak{M}}\) of Hecke operators of a full mark (Section 25.6) goes into the datum of every lift, and the transfer is cleared by a power of \(p\) fixed in advance (Problem 62.4.2).

Tag 22Q6

Problem 25.4.4. If \(Y\) is injective, then the length of \(\mathrm{Int}_0/Y^m\mathrm{Int}_0\) is \(m\) times the length of \(\mathrm{Int}_0/Y\,\mathrm{Int}_0\).

In the tree model of Chapter 18, as \(F\) ranges over all integral forms the values \((YF)(L)\) fill \(S(L)\), and the loss at \(L\) is the length of \(H(L)/S(L)\). The proof does not assume that \(Y\) is injective; the colength bound it uses is in Chapter 57, Section 57.4.

§25.5. Stepwise maxima overcount Tag 22Q7

Theorem 25.4.1 uses \(\delta_j\) at step \(j\). Can one replace its lower bound for \(\operatorname{gain}(\mathcal{K}_m Y^m)\) by \(\sum_{j=1}^m \max(\gamma_j(0), A + \gamma_j(r))\)? We compare two chains with the same numbers \(\gamma_j(i)\). Let \(p\) be any prime, \(k = \mathbb{Q}_p\), \(m = 2\), \(r = 1\), \(F_j = \mathbb{Q}_p^2\) and \(\mathrm{Int}_j = \mathbb{Z}_p^2\). Let \(K_0 = K_1 = K\) be a diagonal matrix, let \(x_1 = (\alpha_1)\) and \(x_2 = (\alpha_2)\) be families of one element, and let \[Y := \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_1 := \operatorname{diag}(p, 1) , \qquad \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_1 := Y + h_1(Z_j) \quad (j = 1, 2) ,\] so that \(A = 0\).

Tag 22Q8

Problem 25.5.1. These data satisfy (2), and \(\operatorname{length}\mathbb{Z}_p^2/Y\mathbb{Z}_p^2 = 1\).

Tag 22Q9

Problem 25.5.2. Let \(K = \operatorname{diag}(1, p)\) and \(\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = p\). Then \(\gamma_j(0) = 0\), \(\gamma_j(1) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{Pr}_j = 0\) for \(j = 1, 2\), and \(\operatorname{gain}(\mathcal{K}_2\, Y^2) = 2\).

Tag 22QA

Problem 25.5.3. Let \(K = \operatorname{diag}(p, 1)\), \(\alpha_1 = p - 1\) and \(\alpha_2 = -2p\). Then \(\gamma_j(0) = 0\), \(\gamma_j(1) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{Pr}_j = 1\) for \(j = 1, 2\), and \(\operatorname{gain}(\mathcal{K}_2\, Y^2) = 0\).

The sum of the stepwise maxima is \(2\) in both chains. Theorem 25.4.1 gives the exact gain in each case.

Theorem 25.4.1 uses the gains \(\gamma_j(i)\), the scaling valuation \(A\), and the coefficients \(h_l(Z_j)\). Chapters 15 and 16 model divisibility for a single theta kernel. If \(Z_j\) has finite least valuation \(\mu_j\), then \(v(h_l(Z_j)) \ge l\mu_j\) (Problem 25.2.4). A separate colength estimate enters when the gain on the image is used; Chapter 18 computes a local colength at one lattice.

Exercises

  1. The Newton polygon of \(\prod_{i=0}^t (1 + aQ^iX)\) over \(k\), with \(a \in k^\times\), \(Q \in k^\times\) and \(v(Q) > 0\), has the vertices \(\bigl(l, l\,v(a) + v(Q)\,l(l-1)/2\bigr)\) for \(0 \le l \le t+1\).

  2. Suppose that (2) holds with \(u_{j+1}^i K_j\) in place of \(K_j\) on the right, for units \(u_j \in \mathcal{O}^\times\). Write \(E_j(X):=\sum_{i=0}^r \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i X^i\). Show first that the relation is equivalent modulo \(X^{r+1}\) to \(E_{j+1}(X)K_j=K_j E_{x_{j+1}}(u_{j+1}X) E_j(u_{j+1}X)\), the relation of Section 24.5 with \(P(X)=E_{u_{j+1}x_{j+1}}(X)\). Then Theorem 25.3.1 holds with \(\mathcal{K}_j\mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r\) multiplied by \((u_1 \cdots u_j)^r\), and with \(Z_j\) replaced by the family \(Z'_j\) of the \(u_l u_{l+1} \cdots u_j\, a\) for \(l \le j\) and \(a\) in \(x_l\). Theorem 25.4.1 holds with \(\delta_j\) computed from \(Z'_j\). For the same gains \(\gamma_j(i)\), the two values of \(\delta_j\) agree when \(Z_j\) has exactly one member of least valuation. For \(r = 1\), \(p\) odd, \(x_1 = (1)\), \(x_2 = (-1)\), \(u_1 = -1\), \(u_2 = 1\) and \(\gamma_2(1) > \gamma_2(0)\), the two values of \(\delta_2\) differ.

§25.6. The step in the proof Tag 236A

At an exceptional prime \(p < x_1\) the proof [GL] applies this transfer to the Hecke operators of a full mark and to the chain of \(m\) lifts, and obtains Theorem 62.4.1; Chapter 62 states and develops it, and Chapter 57, Section 57.4, uses it. There the relation (2) appears for the lift \(X_j \to X_{j+1}\) with one polynomial for all translations, in degrees \(i \le r_p\), with a block \(\chi(q^t, q^{t-2}, \dots, q^{-t})\) in place of \(x_{j+1}\), \(\chi\) a \(p\)-adic unit (this comparison is ours); the proof inverts that polynomial over the ring \(\mathcal{O}_\eta\) of Chapter 37 and puts one copy of the product of Hecke operators into the datum of every lift, as in the choice \(S = \{1, \dots, m\}\) of Problem 25.4.3.
