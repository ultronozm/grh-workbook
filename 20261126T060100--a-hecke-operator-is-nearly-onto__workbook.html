Prerequisites.

Elementary divisors over a discrete valuation ring [La, Ch. III, §7]. Chapter 18: Theorem 18.5.1, the colength and the phrase “\(U\) is onto \(\mathcal V_1\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \(c\)” (Section 18.6, Problems 18.6.1 and 18.6.2). Chapter 57: \(o_p\), \(r_p\), exceptional primes (Section 57.2), \(S_\eta\) (Section 57.3), \(v_\sigma\), marks, \(a_v\), \(Y_v = p^{a_v}T_v\) (Section 57.4). \(D_X = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} S\), \(\mathcal O_\eta\) (Chapter 37); \(f_p\) (Definition 62.1.1); \(f\) (Section 36.2), \(m, x, k, a\) (Definition 36.3.1 and the sentence after it).

At an exceptional prime \(p < x_1\), Chapter 57 needs the colength of \(Y_v S_\eta + p^u S_\eta\) in \(S_\eta\) for the scaled Hecke operators \(Y_v\) of a mark. The operator \(Y_v\) sums, with changes of basis and a scaling, the values of a form over the neighbours of index \(r_p\) at \(v\). Chapter 18 saw at one vertex that such a sum loses little when the residue field is larger than the degree (Theorem 18.5.1). Here the degree is \(\mu\) in each of \(N_0\) Frobenius-twisted letters, and the total colength is at most \(3c_{\mathrm{div}}+ \theta u\) times the number of source coordinates (Theorem 63.4.1). In Sections 63.1–63.4 all letters other than \(p\) are local to this chapter: \(\beta\) is a family of exponents, not the diagram of the data; \(X\), \(Z\), \(I\) are finite sets, \(U\) is an endomorphism, \(\theta\) a real number, \(P\) a prime power, \(q\) a residue field size, \(d\) a depth, and \(D\), \(R\), \(R'\), \(Q\), \(Q'\), \(C\), \(B\), \(M\) are matrices. Section 63.5 gives their values for the data.

§63.1. The edge Hecke operator

Let \(N_0 \ge 1\), let \(W'\) be a commutative local ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\), and let \(O\) be a valuation domain of characteristic \(0\).

Definition 63.1.1. A Frobenius cycle of length \(N_0\) from \(W'\) to \(O\) consists of a prime \(p\), a power \(P \ge 5\) of \(p\), ring homomorphisms \(\iota_s \colon W' \to O\) for \(s \in \mathbb{Z}/N_0\), a parity, even or odd, for each \(s\), and an element \(\varpi \in O\), such that \(\varpi^2 = p\); \(p\) divides \(\iota_{s+1}(w) - \iota_s(w)^P\) in \(O\) for all \(s\) and all \(w \in W'\); the parities of \(s\) and \(s+1\) differ for every \(s\) (so \(N_0\) is even); and \(W'/\mathfrak m\) has \(q := P^{N_0}\) elements. Fix integers \(n \ge 1\) (the rank), \(\mu \ge 0\) (the degree in each letter \(s\)) and \(r_0 \ge 0\). Let \(\mathcal B\) be the set of families \(\beta = (\beta_s)_{s \in \mathbb{Z}/N_0}\) of maps \(\beta_s \colon \{0, \dots, n-1\} \to \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\) with \(\sum_i \beta_s(i) = \mu\) for each \(s\). For \(\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_n(W')\) put \(\gamma_s = \iota_s(\gamma)\) if \(s\) is even and \(\gamma_s = \iota_s((\gamma^{-1})^{\mathsf T})\) if \(s\) is odd, and let \(\rho(\gamma)\) be the \(\mathcal B\times \mathcal B\) matrix whose \((\beta, \beta')\) entry is the product over \(s\) of the coefficients of \(z^{\beta_s}\) in \(\prod_i \bigl(\sum_j (\gamma_s)_{ji} z_j\bigr)^{\beta'_s(i)}\). The depth \(\mathrm{dp}(\beta)\) is the sum of \(\beta_s(i)\) over the pairs \((i, s)\) with \(s\) even and \(i \ge r_0\), or \(s\) odd and \(i < r_0\) (the deep pairs); and \(D\) is the diagonal matrix with entries \(p^{\mathrm{dp}(\beta)}\). Let \(\mathcal K_{r_0} \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_n(W')\) consist of the matrices whose \((i, j)\) entry lies in \(\mathfrak m\) whenever \(i < r_0 \le j\), and let \(\mathcal C= \mathrm{GL}_n(W')/\mathcal K_{r_0}\), the set of left cosets.

Used in: Chapter 64: Theorem 64.4.1

Definition 63.1.2. Let \(X\) and \(Z\) be finite sets. Edge coordinates on \((X, Z)\) are maps \(\pi \colon X \times \mathcal C\to X\), \(\gamma \colon X \times \mathcal C\to \mathrm{GL}_n(W')\), \(\gamma' \colon \mathcal C\to \mathrm{GL}_n(W')\) and \(\upsilon \colon X \times \mathcal C\to \mathrm{GL}_Z(O)\), such that \(\gamma'(\omega)^{-1} \mathcal K_{r_0} = \omega\) for every \(\omega \in \mathcal C\), and \(\varepsilon = \varepsilon'\) whenever \(\varepsilon, \varepsilon' \in X \times \mathcal C\) satisfy \(\pi(\varepsilon) = \pi(\varepsilon')\) and \(\gamma(\varepsilon)^{\mathsf T} (\gamma(\varepsilon')^{\mathsf T})^{-1} \in \mathcal K_{r_0}\). For a finite set \(I\) and a bijection \(J = (J_1, J_2) \colon I \to \mathcal B\times Z\), the edge Hecke operator \(\mathcal A\) is the \(O\)-linear endomorphism of \((O^I)^X\) given by \[(\mathcal AF)(\lambda)_i = \sum_{\omega \in \mathcal C} \sum_{i' \in I} \upsilon(\lambda, \omega)_{J_2 i,\, J_2 i'}\, \bigl(\rho(\gamma(\lambda, \omega))\, D\, \rho(\gamma'(\omega))\bigr)_{J_1 i',\, J_1 i}\; F(\pi(\lambda, \omega))_{i'} .\]

The model: \(N_0 = 2\), \(n = 2\), \(r_0 = 1\), \(W' = \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt2]_{(5)}\), \(O\) the valuation ring of a prime of \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt2, \sqrt5)\) above \(5\), \(\iota_0\) the inclusion (even), \(\iota_1\) the conjugation \(\sqrt2 \mapsto -\sqrt2\) (odd), \(\varpi = \sqrt5\); \(X\) and \(Z\) are points, \(\upsilon = 1\), and for \(h_c = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & i + j\sqrt2\\ 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) (\(i, j \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(\lvert i\rvert, \lvert j\rvert \le 2\)) and for the transposition \(h_c = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) put \(\gamma'(\omega_c) = h_c\), \(\gamma(\cdot, \omega_c) = h_c^{\mathsf T}\), where \(\omega_c = h_c^{-1}\mathcal K_1\). For \(\mu = 1\), \(ab\) is the index \(\beta\) using row \(a\) in letter \(0\) and row \(b\) in letter \(1\).

Problem 63.1.1. \((\sqrt2)^5 \equiv -\sqrt2 \pmod 5\), and these data are a Frobenius cycle with \(P = 5\), \(q = 25\). The \(26\) matrices \(h_c\) give \(26\) distinct cosets \(\omega_c\), which exhaust \(\mathcal C\), and the maps above are edge coordinates.

The columns of \(\mathcal E\) span a direct summand of \(O^{104}\): for \(\mu = 1\) the loss comes from the pairing and the sum. In general, with \(D_\varpi = \operatorname{diag}(\varpi^{\mathrm{dp}(\beta)})\), let \(R\) send \(F\) to \(D_\varpi\rho(\gamma(\varepsilon)^{\mathsf T})F(\pi(\varepsilon))\) at each edge \(\varepsilon = (\lambda, \omega)\) (on the \(\mathcal B\) factor), and \(R'\) send it to \(D_\varpi\rho(\gamma'(\omega))F(\lambda)\). Problem 63.1.6. Let \(O\) be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(p\), \(A\) a square matrix of size \(N'\) over \(O\), \(c_1, u \ge 0\) integers and \(s_0 \subset O^{N'}\) a finite set with \(p^{c_1}O^{N'} \subseteq \operatorname{im}A + \operatorname{span}(s_0)\). Then \(\operatorname{im}A + p^uO^{N'}\) has colength at most \(c_1N' + u\,\#s_0\) in \(O^{N'}\).

§63.2. Deep forms: the two ends

Let \(\Bbbk\) be a field of characteristic \(p\) with ring maps \(\iota_s \colon W' \to \Bbbk\), \(\iota_{s+1}(w) = \iota_s(w)^P\), and parities as in Definition 63.1.1; \(\rho(\gamma)\) acts on \(\Bbbk^{\mathcal B}\) as before. If \(p\) is not a unit in \(O\), reducing a Frobenius cycle to the residue field of \(O\) gives such maps.

Definition 63.2.1. For an integer \(d \ge 1\), \(\mathrm{Deep}_d \subseteq \Bbbk^{\mathcal B}\) is the space of \(F\) such that \((\rho(\gamma)F)_\beta = 0\) for every \(\gamma \in \mathrm{GL}_n(W')\) and every \(\beta\) with \(\mathrm{dp}(\beta) < d\): all translates of \(F\) have depth at least \(d\).

Used in: Chapter 64

At the ends take \(p\) a nonunit in \(O\) and \(\Bbbk\) its residue field with the reduced maps. The proof writes \(RC = Q\operatorname{diag}(t_j)\), with \(C\) invertible and \(Q\) having a left inverse; for each \(\delta \ge 0\) the columns \(C_j\) with \(t_j \nmid \varpi^\delta\) reduce to independent vectors of \((\mathrm{Deep}_{\delta+1})^{X \times Z}\). So \(R = Qd_{\mathrm e}\), \(d_{\mathrm e} = \operatorname{diag}(t_j)C^{-1}\), with \(\det d_{\mathrm e} \mid \varpi^{\#X\#Z\sum_{j \ge 1}\dim\mathrm{Deep}_j}\); likewise \(R' = Q'd'_{\mathrm e}\) (read from the proof). How large can deep forms be? Give the deep pair \((i, s)\) the cost \(P^{-2r_0}\) if \(i \ge 2r_0\) and \(P^{-1}\) if \(i < 2r_0\), the other pairs cost \(0\), and let \(c(\beta) = \sum_{(i,s)} \beta_s(i)\,\mathrm{cost}(i, s)\).

Problem 63.2.1. (i) A nonzero \(f \in \mathbb{F}_q[\mathsf T]\) vanishing to order at least \(d\) at every point of \(\mathbb{F}_q\) has degree at least \(qd\). [\((\mathsf T^3 - \mathsf T)^2 = \mathsf T^6 + \mathsf T^4 + \mathsf T^2\) over \(\mathbb{F}_3\).] (ii) Over a local ring \(O_1\), if \(f \in O_1[\mathsf T]\) has unit leading coefficient and is divisible by \((\mathsf T - \mathsf a)^{\nu_0}\) for \(q\) elements \(\mathsf a\) pairwise distinct modulo the maximal ideal, then \(\deg f \ge q\nu_0\).

§63.3. Defects: the middle

The proof chooses weights \(\mathrm{pw}_\beta, \mathrm{pw}'_\beta \in O\), \(\beta \in \mathcal B\), whose properties are stated with the average below. Let \(B\) act at each edge by \(\upsilon\) on \(Z\) and by \(\operatorname{diag}(\mathrm{pw}_\beta)\) on \(\mathcal B\); the middle matrix is \(M = Q'^{\mathsf T}BQ\). The proof builds a near right inverse of \(Q'^{\mathsf T}B\) whose edge values satisfy congruences, measured by an exponent defect (the signed weighted sum in Definition 63.3.1), which bound the generators needed to supplement \(\operatorname{im}M\). For \(i \in \mathbb{Z}/N_0\) write \([i]\) for its least residue in \(\{0, \dots, N_0 - 1\}\).

Definition 63.3.1. Let \(0 < r_0 < n\). For \(s \in \mathbb{Z}/N_0\) let \(u_s = r_0\) and \(\bar u_s = r_0 - 1\) if \(s\) is even, \(u_s = r_0 - 1\) and \(\bar u_s = r_0\) if \(s\) is odd. Define the exponent defect at the letter \(i\) of a real family \(\beta = (\beta_s)\) by \[d_i(\beta) = \frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{s \in \mathbb{Z}/N_0}\Bigl(P^{1 + [s - 1 - i]}\beta_s(u_s) - P^{[s - i]}\beta_s(\bar u_s)\Bigr) .\]

Used in: Chapter 64: Definition 64.2.1

The proof shows (read from the proof): with \(\Bbbk\) as in Section 63.2, \(0 < r_0 < n\), an integer \(t_0 \ge 2(n+1)\), \(0 < \theta \le 1\), \(0 < \mu\) and \(8N_0nt_0P^{2r_0} \le \theta\mu\); then \(\#\mathcal C\) times the dimension of the space of \(F \in \Bbbk^{\mathcal B}\) such that \((\rho(g)F)_\beta = 0\) for every block diagonal \(g\) (blocks of sizes \(r_0, n - r_0\)) and every \(\beta\) with \(d_i(\beta) > 3t_0\) at some letter \(i\), is at most \((1 + \theta)\#\mathcal B\).

For the defect count, give the variable in row \(j\) weight \(j\) in an even letter and \(-j\) in an odd letter. The root lemma (Theorem 64.4.1) gives nonpositive defect at every letter along each adjacent pair within either block for a term of least weight among all terms in all block diagonal translates of a nonzero polynomial over \(\Bbbk\). For rows \(j,j+1\), compute the defect by using \(j+1\) in place of \(r_0\) in Definition 63.3.1.

§63.4. The theorem

Problem 63.4.1. (i) If \(u \ge 0\), \(c_1 \le 2c_{\mathrm{div}}\), \(\#s_0 \le \theta\#X\#I\) and \(\#I = \#\mathcal B\,\#Z\), then \(\#X\#Z\lfloor c_{\mathrm{div}}\#\mathcal B\rfloor + c_1\#X\#I + u\,\#s_0 \le (3c_{\mathrm{div}}+ \theta u)\#X\#I\). (ii) For a Frobenius cycle, if \((N_0n\log q/\theta)^5P^{3r_0} \le \mu\) with \(0 < \theta \le 1\), then \(N_0c_0 = N_0n^2\log_p q \le c_{\mathrm{div}}\).

The following theorem is read from the proof, which bounds the colength for \(d'^{\mathsf T}_{\mathrm e}Md_{\mathrm e}\) by determinant divisibility over \(O\) (Problem 63.1.6 is a model over a discrete valuation ring) with the bound of Problem 63.4.1, \(c_1 = 2N_0c_0\), and passes to \(\mathcal L\) by \(\psi\).

Theorem 63.4.1. Let a Frobenius cycle of length \(N_0 \ge 1\) from a commutative local ring \(W'\) to a valuation domain \(O\) of characteristic \(0\) be given, with prime \(p\), power \(P\) and \(q = P^{N_0}\) as above, and suppose that \(p\) is not a unit in \(O\). Let \(n, \mu, r_0\) be integers and \(\theta\) a real number with, for the natural logarithm, \[0 < r_0, \quad 2r_0 \le n, \quad 0 < \theta \le 1, \quad \mu^4 \le q, \quad \Bigl(\frac{N_0 n \log q}{\theta}\Bigr)^5 P^{3r_0} \le \mu .\] Let \(X\), \(Z\) and \(I\) be finite sets (\(\mathcal C\) is finite), \(J \colon I \to \mathcal B\times Z\) a bijection, edge coordinates on \((X, Z)\) with edge Hecke operator \(\mathcal A\), \(\mathcal M\) an \(O\)-module, \(\psi \colon (O^I)^X \to \mathcal M\) an \(O\)-linear map and \(U\) an endomorphism of \(\mathcal M\) with \(U \psi = \psi \mathcal A\). Then for every integer \(u \ge 0\), \(U\) is onto \(\mathcal L = \psi\bigl((O^I)^X\bigr)\) modulo \(p^u\) up to colength \[\bigl(3\,c_{\mathrm{div}}+ \theta u\bigr)\, \#X\, \#I, \qquad c_{\mathrm{div}}= N_0 \mu \Bigl(\frac{1}{P^{2r_0}} + \frac{r_0^2}{n P}\Bigr) .\]

§63.5. At a marked prime

Let \(p < x_1\) be exceptional, \(\eta \colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\) a ring map, \(\mathfrak M\) a mark at \((p, \eta)\), \(v \in \mathfrak M\), and \(\tau_0 \in \Phi_a\) with \(v_{\tau_0} = v\). The proof realizes \(Y_v\) as an edge Hecke operator (read from the proof): put \(N_0 := f_p/o_p\) and \(\tau_i := [-p^{o_p}]^{[i]}\tau_0\) for \(i \in \mathbb{Z}/N_0\); these are distinct and are exactly the \(a\)-places \(\sigma\) with \(v_\sigma \in \{v, \bar v\}\). With \(W'\) the valuation ring of \(E\) at \(v\), \(O = \mathcal O_\eta\), \(P = p^{o_p}\), \(\iota_i = \Phi_{\tau_i}\) (even) if \(v_{\tau_i} = v\) and \(\iota_i = \Phi_{\tau_i} \circ (\text{complex conjugation})\) (odd) otherwise, and \(\varpi\) a square root of \(p\), they form a Frobenius cycle of length \(N_0\); and there are finite sets \(X, Z, I\) with \(\#X\#I = D_X\), a bijection \(J\), edge coordinates with \((n, \mu, r_0) = (k, a, r_p)\), and an \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-linear \(\psi \colon (\mathcal O_\eta^I)^X \to S\) with image \(S_\eta\) and \(Y_v\psi = \psi\mathcal A\). The \(\tau_i\) induce \(v\) and \(\bar v\) alternately (Problems 62.1.1 and 62.1.4; \(f_p\) is the order of \([-p]\), which is even), so exactly \(N_0/2\) of them induce \(v\), and \(a_v = aN_0/2\). The proof checks the hypotheses of Theorem 63.4.1 for \(\theta = 1/(m^2f)\).

Problem 63.5.1. With \((n, \mu, r_0, \theta, P, N_0) = (k, a, r_p, 1/(m^2f), p^{o_p}, f_p/o_p)\) and \(a_v = aN_0/2\): \(mp \le P^{2r_p}\), \(mr_p^2 \le k\), and \(3c_{\mathrm{div}}\le 12a_v/(mp)\).

So Theorem 63.4.1 gives the colength used in Chapter 57: for a mark \(\mathfrak M\), \(v \in \mathfrak M\) and every integer \(u \ge 0\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:mark-colength}\tag{63.5.1} Y_v \text{ is onto } S_\eta \text{ modulo } p^u \text{ up to colength } \Bigl(\frac{12a_v}{mp} + \frac{u}{m^2f}\Bigr) D_X . \end{equation}\] Problems 57.4.4 and 57.4.5 add the colength bounds over \(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\).
