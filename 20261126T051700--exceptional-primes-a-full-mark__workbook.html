At an exceptional prime \(p < x_1\) the total collision cost \(m C_p(\varnothing)\) can exceed the local loss allowance \(L_p\) (Problem 57.2.5). Chapter 57 therefore applies \(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\) for a full mark \(\mathfrak M\) before the chain and uses Theorem 62.4.1 below. How does a Hecke operator at primes above \(p\) remove the collisions, and what does it cost?

§62.1. The primes above an exceptional prime

Let \(p\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) = -1\) and \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\) a ring map. Then \(p \ne d\), and \(p \nmid q_1\) by Problem 57.1.1; so \(p\) is unramified in \(E\). For \(\sigma \in \Phi\), \(v_\sigma\) is the prime of \(E\) induced by \(\eta \circ \Phi_\sigma\), and \(\bar v\) is the complex conjugate of \(v\).

Definition 62.1.1. The residue degree above \(p\) is \(f_p := \operatorname{lcm}(2, o)\), where \(o\) is the order of \([-p]\) in \(\Phi \cong (\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times\).

Used in: Chapter 63

Problem 62.1.1. For \(\sigma, \tau \in \Phi\): \(v_\sigma = v_\tau\) if and only if \(\sigma = [-p]^i\tau\) with \(i\) even, and \(v_\sigma = \bar v_\tau\) if and only if \(\sigma = [-p]^i\tau\) with \(i\) odd. The residue field of every \(v \mid p\) has \(p^{f_p}\) elements.

Used in: Chapter 57; Chapter 63

Problem 62.1.2. For a numerical test of the exceptional-prime predicate alone, take \(q_0=18480\), \(d=138599\) and \(m=16384\). The prime \(479\) is inert in \(K\), with \(o_{479} = 15\) and \(r_{479} = 2\); it is not exceptional. The prime \(55439 \equiv 6287 \pmod m\) is inert, with \(o_{55439} = r_{55439} = 1\); it is exceptional.

Used in: Problem 62.1.3

Problem 62.1.3. If \(p \not\equiv \pm 1 \pmod m\), then \(m\) divides the order of \([-p]\); so \(f_p\) is this order, it is even, and \(f_p \ge m\). [For the numerical parameters of Problem 62.1.2, with \(s=308\), check that the order of \(-55439\) modulo \(q_1\) has \(2\)-adic valuation \(308\).]

Problem 62.1.4. Let \(p\) be exceptional. For \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), the partner \([-p^{o_p}]\tau\) (Chapter 36) lies in \(\Phi_a\) and \(v_{[-p^{o_p}]\tau} = \bar v_\tau\). No prime \(v \mid p\) equals \(\bar v\).

Used in: Chapter 63

Problem 62.1.5. If \(p < x_1\) is exceptional, a full mark at \((p, \eta)\) exists.

Used in: Chapter 57

For a full mark, \(C_p(\Phi_{\mathfrak M}) = 0\) (Problem 57.4.1). Let \(\bar{\mathfrak M}:= \{\bar v : v \in \mathfrak M\}\) and \(\Phi_{\bar{\mathfrak M}} := \{\sigma : v_\sigma \in \bar{\mathfrak M}\}\).

Problem 62.1.6. Let \(\mathfrak M\) be a mark. No \(\sigma \in \Phi_{\mathfrak M} \cup \Phi_{\bar{\mathfrak M}}\) is a \(b\)-place, so \(\kappa(\sigma) = a/x_1\) there. For \(v \in \mathfrak M\), \(\sigma \mapsto [-p]\sigma\) maps \(\{\sigma : v_\sigma = v\}\) bijectively onto \(\{\sigma : v_\sigma = \bar v\}\) and preserves \(\kappa\).

The model is that of Problem 59.5.1: \(\Phi = (\mathbb{Z}/4)^\times = \{1, 3\}\), both places are \(a\)-places, \(a = 24\), and \(p = 5\) is treated as inert, so that \([-5^e]\) exchanges \(1\) and \(3\) for every \(e \in I_5\), the odd \(e \ge 1\). Think of \(1\) as inducing \(v\) and \(3\) as inducing \(\bar v\), and put \(\Phi_{\mathfrak M} := \{1\}\). There are two rows, \(0\) and \(1\), and \(\beta\) has \(24\) columns of height one at each place and no others. The model is bookkeeping, not data.

Problem 62.1.7. In the model, \(C_5(\varnothing) = 10\) and \(C_5(\{1\}) = 0\); \(\mathcal D(\beta) = 0\), while \(\mathcal D(\beta \smallsetminus \{1\}) + \gamma_5 \cdot 24 = 10 = 48\gamma_5\), with \(\gamma_5 = 5/24\).

Without the mark the collision takes the whole credit \(48\gamma_5\); with it, nothing is lost. The erasure is not free: at a neighbour the charges and the diagonal cost \(a_v\) (Section 62.2), and \(Y_v\) carries the factor \(p^{a_v}\) that pays for them.

§62.2. One lift at a neighbour

Let \(\mathfrak M\) be a mark and \(j \ge 0\). For \(v \in \mathfrak M\) the pair \(\{v, \bar v\}\) is split (Problem 62.1.4), and the unitary group of \(X_{j+1}\) at it is \(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{j+1}}(E_v)\): \(g\) at \(v\) and \((\bar g^{\,t})^{-1}\) at \(\bar v\). As \(p\) is unramified, it is a uniformizer of \(E_v\).

Definition 62.2.1. A neighbour of index at most \(r_p\) at \(\mathfrak M\) is an element of the finite adelic unitary group of \(X_{j+1}\) which is \(1\) away from \(\mathfrak M\cup \bar{\mathfrak M}\) and whose component \(g_v\) at each \(v \in \mathfrak M\) lies in the \(\mathrm{GL}_{n_{j+1}}(\mathcal O_v)\)-double coset of \(\operatorname{diag}(p 1_i, 1)\) for some \(i \le r_p\).

Problem 62.2.1. An element of the double coset of \(\operatorname{diag}(p1_i, 1)\) in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(E_v)\) is \(g_1 \operatorname{diag}(p 1_{\mathcal I}, 1)\, g_2\) with \(g_1, g_2 \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_v)\), for every set \(\mathcal I\) of \(i\) rows, \(\operatorname{diag}(p1_{\mathcal I}, 1)\) having \(p\) at the rows of \(\mathcal I\) and \(1\) elsewhere.

Fix sets \(\mathcal I_v\) of rows, \(\#\mathcal I_v \le r_p\), for \(v \in \mathfrak M\). For \(\tau\) with \(v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\) or \(\bar v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\) let \(\mathcal I(\tau) := \mathcal I_v\) for that \(v \in \mathfrak M\); otherwise \(\mathcal I(\tau) := \varnothing\). Define the neighbour diagonal \(g_{\mathcal I}\) to be the neighbour (Definition 62.2.1) with component \(\operatorname{diag}(p1_{\mathcal I_v}, 1)\) at each \(v \in \mathfrak M\).

Problem 62.2.2. (i) At \(\tau\), the extension to finite adeles of \(u \mapsto \eta(\Phi_\tau(\bar u))\) sends \(g_{\mathcal I}\) to the diagonal matrix with entries \(p^{-\epsilon_\tau(i)}\), where \(\epsilon_\tau(i) = 1\) if \(v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\) and \(i \in \mathcal I(\tau)\), \(\epsilon_\tau(i) = -1\) if \(\bar v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\) and \(i \in \mathcal I(\tau)\), and \(\epsilon_\tau(i) = 0\) otherwise; so a box in row \(i\) at \(\tau\) has its gauge multiplied by \(\lvert p^{-\epsilon_\tau(i)}\rvert = p^{\epsilon_\tau(i)}\). (ii) For every integer \(c\), \(\sum_{\tau :\, \kappa(\tau) = c}\, \sum_i \epsilon_\tau(i) = 0\).

The following extends statement (II) of Section 59.4 from \(1\) to a diagonal; it is read from the proof. (C) Let \(\phi\) be a standard datum of denominator \(\delta\) on \(\mathrm{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\), let \(g_1, g_2\) be elements of the finite adelic unitary group of \(X_{j+1}\) whose components at the places above \(p\) are integral with integral inverses, and let \(g_0\) be an element of the same group sent at each \(\tau\), as in Problem 62.2.2, to a diagonal matrix with entries \(y_\tau(i)\). For a filling \(\mathbf i\) put \(\mathrm{dw}(\mathbf i) := \prod_\tau \lvert\det y_\tau\rvert^{e_\tau} \prod_{\text{boxes}} \lvert y_\tau(\text{row})\rvert\), where \(e_\tau\), a function of \(\kappa(\tau)\) and \(j\), is the exponent of the determinant in the weight on \(X_{j+1}\) at \(\tau\). Suppose that, for every standard datum of denominator \(\delta\) and every pair consisting of an output filling \(\mathbf i'\) and an input filling \(\mathbf i\), the \((\mathbf i',\mathbf i)\) entry of the Schur jet matrix of its jet series at the scale of the kernel has \(p\)-adic absolute value after \(\eta\) which, multiplied by \(\mathrm{dw}(\mathbf i')\), is at most \(\mathfrak b\). Then \((1, g_1g_0g_2)\phi\), the translate under the Weil representation as in Definition 59.4.2, multiplies gauges by at most \(\mathfrak b\).

For \(\tau \in \Phi\), \(h \ge 1\) and rows \(i\) let \(\mathfrak c(\tau, h, i) := 1\) if either \(v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\), \(h = 1\) and \(i \notin \mathcal I(\tau)\), or \(\bar v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\) and \(i \in \mathcal I(\tau)\); and \(\mathfrak c(\tau, h, i) := 0\) otherwise.

Problem 62.2.3. The numbers \(\mathfrak c\) are charges for \((\beta, \Phi_{\mathfrak M})\), with \(\beta\) read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\). [With two rows and \(\mathcal I_v = \{0\}\): \(\mathfrak c(\tau, 1, \cdot) = (0, 1)\) if \(v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\), \(\mathfrak c(\tau, h, \cdot) = (1, 0)\) for every \(h\) if \(\bar v_\tau \in \mathfrak M\), and in each pairing exempt by Definition 58.3.1 the two charges add up to \(1\) in each row.]

Problem 62.2.4. Let \(\phi\) be a standard datum of denominator \(1\) on \(\mathrm{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\) and \(g\) a neighbour of index at most \(r_p\) at \(\mathfrak M\). Then \((1, g)\phi\) multiplies gauges by at most \[p^{\sum_{v \in \mathfrak M} a_v + r_p \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi_{\mathfrak M}} \beta_{>1}(\sigma) - \mathcal D_j(\Phi_{\mathfrak M})} ,\] with \(\beta\) read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\).

In the model, with \(\mathcal I= \{0\}\) and one input coordinate, let \(F := \exp\bigl(\sum_{i=0,1}(z_{1i}^5 z_{3i} + z_{3i}^5 z_{1i})/5\bigr)\), which has the normal form with \(W = 1\). A filling puts \(a_i\) boxes in row \(i\) at place \(1\) and \(a'_i\) at place \(3\) (in this sheet), \(a_0 + a_1 = a'_0 + a'_1 = 24\); its jet is \(\partial_{z_{10}}^{a_0}\partial_{z_{11}}^{a_1}\partial_{z_{30}}^{a'_0}\partial_{z_{31}}^{a'_1}F(0)\), its charges total \(a_1 + a'_0\), and the diagonal weights it by \(5^{a_0 - a'_0}\) in absolute value.

Problem 62.2.5. (m) By machine: of the \(625\) pairs \((a_0,a'_0)\) with \(0\le a_0,a'_0\le24\), \(25\) have a nonzero jet; every nonzero jet has valuation \(0\); and \(\operatorname{val}(\text{jet}) + a_1 + a'_0 \ge 8\), with equality only at \((a_0, a'_0) = (20, 4)\).

The weighted jets multiplied by \(5^{24}\) have minimum valuation \(8\). The plain jets have minimum valuation \(0\). The shortfall from the predicted credit \(10\) is the factorial digit loss \(2\). In this model the error expression of Theorem 58.3.2 evaluates to \(16(1+\log_5 48)\), about \(54\).

§62.3. Moving the Hecke operators into the data

For \(v \in \mathfrak M\), \(q_v := p^{f_p}\), and \(0 \le i \le n_j\), let \(T^{(j)}_{v,i}\) be the Hecke operator at \(v\) of the double coset of \(\operatorname{diag}(p1_i, 1)\) on forms on \(X_j\), and \(\mathbf T_j(X) := \sum_{i \le n_j} (-X)^i q_v^{\binom i2} T^{(j)}_{v,i}\) for an indeterminate \(X\); so \(T_v = T^{(0)}_{v,r_p}\).

(A) The split unramified theta correspondence. Let \(w\) be a split place of norm \(q\) and fix the Weil data. Let the two hermitian spaces have ranks \(n\) and \(n+k'\), with automorphic characters \(a_0,b_0\) of the norm-one ideles whose base changes are unramified at \(w\). Write \(\omega(g,h)\) for the Weil action induced by \(x\mapsto hxg^{-1}\) on the space of maps from the source to the target, multiplied by \(a_0(\det h)b_0(\det g)^{-1}\). Let the data space of the bilinear pairing \(\Theta\) be a complex subspace of the Schwartz space of these maps containing a vacuum \(\phi_0\) at \(w\) and all its finite adelic translates under this action. For every pair \((g,h)\) in the source and target finite adelic groups and every source vector \(u\), assume \(\rho(h)\Theta(\phi_0;u)=\Theta(\omega(g,h)\phi_0;\pi(g)u)\), where \(\pi,\rho\) are the source and target representations. For \(\varphi\) spherical at \(w\), \(\Theta(\phi_0; \varphi)\) is spherical at \(w\) and \(\mathbf T'(X)\Theta(\phi_0; \varphi) = \prod_{l<k'}(1 - q^l\mathrm{fr}\, X)\,\Theta\bigl(\phi_0; \mathbf T(q^{k'}\mathrm{fr}\,\mathrm{fr}'^{-1}X)\varphi\bigr)\), where \(\mathbf T\), \(\mathbf T'\) are the Hecke polynomials of the two spaces and \(\mathrm{fr}=F_w(a_0,n)\), \(\mathrm{fr}'=F_w(b_0,n+k')\). Here \(F_w(c,N)=q^{N/2}c(x_w/\bar x_w)\upsilon_E(x_w)^{-N}\), where \(x_w\) is the idele of the chosen uniformizer at \(w\), equal to \(1\) elsewhere, and \(\upsilon_E\) is the splitting character of the Weil data. Status: classical [Ra; Ku]; the proof proves it.

(B) For the chain, (A) takes the following form, read from the proof. Let \(\Psi\) be a weak chain datum for \(\vartheta\), \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), \(v \in \mathfrak M\) and \(j \ge 0\). Let \(\chi_{v,j}\) be the value at a uniformizer of \(E_v\) of the base change of the character of the lift \(X_j \to X_{j+1}\); then \(\lvert\eta\chi_{v,j}\rvert = 1\), and for every \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of \(\Psi\) the lift \(\Theta\) from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\) with datum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) maps forms spherical at \(v\) to forms spherical at \(v\), and on them \[\mathbf T_{j+1}(X)\,\Theta = \mathcal Q_j(X)\, \Theta\, \mathbf T_j\bigl(p^{(t+1)f_p/2}X\bigr), \qquad \mathcal Q_j(X) := \prod_{l=0}^{t}\bigl(1 - q_v^l\, p^{n_jf_p/2}\chi_{v,j} X\bigr) .\]

Problem 62.3.1. In a model of (B), take \(q_v=25\), \(p^{f_p/2}=5\), \(\chi_{v,0}=1\), and ranks \(1\) and \(2\) (\(t=0\)). Suppose \(T^{(0)}_{v,1}\) acts by \(2\) on a subspace of forms spherical at \(v\). Then on its image under \(\Theta\), \(T^{(1)}_{v,1}\) acts by \(15\) and \(T^{(1)}_{v,2}\) by \(2\), and \(5\,\Theta T^{(0)}_{v,1}=(T^{(1)}_{v,1}-5)\Theta\) on the given subspace.

Problem 62.3.2. Let \(0\le n\le n'\), \(\mathcal Q\in\mathcal O_\eta[X]\) with \(\mathcal Q(0)=1\), and \(s_0=p^{(t+1)f_p/2}\). Let \(\Theta\colon V\to V'\) be a linear map of complex vector spaces, with endomorphisms \(T_i\) of \(V\) for \(0\le i\le n\) and \(T'_i\) of \(V'\) for \(0\le i\le n'\). Suppose \(\sum_{i=0}^{n'}(-X)^i q_v^{\binom i2}T'_i\Theta=\mathcal Q(X)\,\Theta\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-s_0 X)^i q_v^{\binom i2}T_i\) as polynomials in \(X\). Then for every \(0\le i\le n\) there are \(c_{ii'}\in\mathcal O_\eta\), depending only on \(\mathcal Q,q_v,i,i'\), with \(s_0^i q_v^{\binom i2}\Theta T_i=\sum_{i'\le i}c_{ii'}T'_{i'}\Theta\).

Put \(e_{\mathrm{tr}}:= r_p f(n_m + r_p)\), the transfer exponent.

Problem 62.3.3. For \(i \le r_p\), \(p^{(t+1)if_p/2}q_v^{\binom i2}\) divides \(p^{fr_pt}\); and \((j+1) f r_p t \le e_{\mathrm{tr}}\) for \(j < m\).

Fix \(v\in\mathfrak M\) and \(j\ge0\), with \(\Psi,\vartheta,p\) as in (B). For each translation \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of \(\Psi\), the datum \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) is fixed by the target maximal compact group at \(v\). Write \(C_{j+1}^{\mathfrak t}\) for the first \(j+1\) lifts with these translated vacua. Iterating Problem 62.3.2, the proof gives coefficients \(c_i\in\mathbb{C}\), independent of \(\mathfrak t\) and of the input form, such that on forms on \(X_0\) spherical at \(v\) one has \(p^{e_{\mathrm{tr}}} C_{j+1}^{\mathfrak t}T^{(0)}_{v,r_p}=\sum_{i\le r_p}c_i T^{(j+1)}_{v,i}C_{j+1}^{\mathfrak t}\). Source forms of the prescribed level are spherical at \(v\). For \(j<m\) the \(c_i\) belong to \(\mathcal O_\eta\) (Problem 62.3.3). Theorem 25.3.1 computes such coefficients in a model. Write \(H^{(j+1)}_{v,i}\) for the Hecke operator of the same double coset on the data of lift \(j\), acting by the sum of the translates by \((1,u)\) over coset representatives \(u\). Lifting with \(H^{(j+1)}_{v,i}\) applied to the datum gives \(T^{(j+1)}_{v,i}\) applied to the lifted form. For each \(v\in\mathfrak M\) in turn, apply \(\sum_{i=0}^{r_p}c_i p^{a_v-e_{\mathrm{tr}}}H^{(j+1)}_{v,i}\) to the current datum of lift \(j\), using the coefficients for this \(v,j\). For a source form \(\varphi\) of the prescribed level, the chain of \(m\) lifts with these new data equals the original chain applied to \(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\varphi\). For \(j < m\), by Problem 62.2.4 and as \(\lvert\eta(c_ip^{a_v - e_{\mathrm{tr}}})\rvert \le p^{e_{\mathrm{tr}}- a_v}\), the new datum of lift \(j\) multiplies gauges by at most \(p\) to the power \(\#\mathfrak M\cdot e_{\mathrm{tr}}+ r_p\sum_{\Phi_{\mathfrak M}}\beta_{>1}- \mathcal D_j(\Phi_{\mathfrak M})\): the \(a_v\) cancel. At each translated vacuum in the support of \(\Psi\), the operator making the replacement commutes with the action of the source and target levels. The weighted sum of tensors of the new data is therefore jointly invariant. The exponents add as in Problem 57.3.1, and restriction to \(Y_m\) at \(p\ne N\) preserves the gauge bound. The proof obtains, for a mark \(\mathfrak M\), a weak chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and \(\varphi \in S_\eta\):

(\(\star\)) \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi}(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\varphi)\) has valuation at least \[-\sum_{j<m}\Bigl(\#\mathfrak M\cdot e_{\mathrm{tr}}+ r_p \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi_{\mathfrak M}} \beta_{>1}(\sigma) - \mathcal D_j(\Phi_{\mathfrak M})\Bigr) ,\] \(\beta\) read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\) in the \(j\)th term.

§62.4. Paying for the transfer

Let \(\mathfrak M\) be a mark and \(0 \le j < m\), with \(\beta\) read at heights below \(n_{j+1}\).

Problem 62.4.1. For \(\sigma \in \Phi_{\mathfrak M}\), \(r_p\beta_{>1}(\sigma) \le a/(pm) + \mathbf 1_{\Phi_a}(\sigma)\,a/x_1\).

Problem 62.4.2. \(\#\mathfrak M\cdot e_{\mathrm{tr}}+ r_p \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi_{\mathfrak M}} \beta_{>1}(\sigma) \le L_p/m + \#(\Phi_a \cap \Phi_{\mathfrak M})\, a/x_1\).

Used in: Chapter 25

Problem 62.4.3. If \(\mathfrak M\) is full, then \(\#\mathfrak M\cdot e_{\mathrm{tr}}+ r_p\sum_{\sigma \in \Phi_{\mathfrak M}}\beta_{>1}(\sigma) - \mathcal D_j(\Phi_{\mathfrak M}) \le (2L_p - \operatorname{gain}(p))/m\).

In the model of Problem 59.2.6 at \(p = 7\), the set \(\{1, 9\}\) plays the part of \(\Phi_{\mathfrak M}\): \(C_7(\{1, 9\}) = 0\), and the left side there is \(46865/48\), against \(\operatorname{gain}(7)/m = 1022\) (Problems 59.2.7 and 59.2.2). Summing Problem 62.4.3 over \(j < m\) in (\(\star\)) gives the following theorem; (\(\star\)) is read from the proof.

Theorem 62.4.1. Let \(p\) be a prime, \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\) a ring map, \(\vartheta\) a twist, \(\Psi\) a weak chain datum for \(\vartheta\) with \(p \nmid \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\), and \(\mathfrak M\) a full mark at \((p, \eta)\) (so \(p < x_1\) is exceptional). For every \(\varphi \in S_\eta\), \(R_{\vartheta,\Psi}(Y_{\mathfrak M}^m\varphi)\) has valuation at least \(\operatorname{gain}(p) - 2L_p\).

Used in: Chapter 25; Chapter 57
