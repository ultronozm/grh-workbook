Prerequisites.

Finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces; Hadamard’s inequality and Fischer’s inequality for positive semidefinite hermitian matrices [HJ]. From Chapter 5, Size against divisibility: Sections 5.3 and 5.4, whose notation we keep: isomorphisms \(\iota \mid \ell\), \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_\iota\), \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\), \(\bar\iota\), families and their heights, lattice data, index and covolume. Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first variable. For vectors \(w_1, \dots, w_n\) in an inner product space let \(\Gamma(w) := \det(\langle w_i, w_k \rangle)_{i,k}\), with \(\Gamma(\varnothing) = 1\).

Theorem 5.4.3 compares the size and the divisibility of one Gram determinant, both read through one map \(\Theta\). The inequality below reads the size through a fixed map \(\Theta_1\). At each isomorphism \(\iota \mid \ell\) it takes a twist \(\theta_\iota\) allowed at \(\ell\) and reads the determinant valuation through \(\Theta_{\theta_\iota}\). For injective maps the Gram determinant of a basis under \(\Theta_1\) is its Gram determinant under \(\Theta_{\theta_\iota}\) times a number \(Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) that does not depend on the basis. This chapter works this out on \(\mathbb{C}^2\) and then states the general inequality.

§6.1. One twist on \(\mathbb{C}^2\) Tag 233M

A lattice datum asks for \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) with \(\varphi\) integral at \(\bar\iota\) and \(\varphi'\) integral at \(\iota\). The two isomorphisms cannot be taken equal.

Tag 233N

Problem 6.1.1. Some \(\iota \mid 5\) has \(\lvert 2 + \mathrm{i}\rvert_\iota = 1\). For such \(\iota\), \(v := (2 + \mathrm{i})^{-1}\) lies in \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\), and \(\langle v, v \rangle = 1/5\) does not.

A twisted lattice on a complex vector space \(V\), with twists in a set \(\mathrm{Tw}\) that has a distinguished element \(1\), consists of the following data.

  1. For each \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\), a complex inner product space \(W_\vartheta\) and a linear map \(\Theta_\vartheta\colon V \to W_\vartheta\).

  2. A predicate on \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathrm{Tw}\). At a prime \(\ell\) the predicate specifies the twists allowed at \(\ell\). In the toys take it to be false at nonprimes.

  3. For each \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) and each \(\iota \mid \ell\), an \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\)-submodule \(\Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota} \subseteq W_\vartheta\), such that \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) for all \(\varphi \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \bar\iota}\) and \(\varphi' \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) whenever \(\vartheta\) is allowed at \(\ell\).

  4. Rational coordinates on \(W_1\) alone: a finite set \(I\), a linear map \(b\colon W_1 \to \mathbb{C}^I\) and a matrix \(G \in M_I(\mathbb{Q})\), positive definite as a complex matrix, with \(\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle = b(\varphi)^* G\, b(\varphi')\) for all \(\varphi, \varphi' \in W_1\).

A vector \(y \in V\) is integral at \(\iota \mid \ell\) if \(\Theta_\vartheta y \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) for every \(\vartheta\) allowed at \(\ell\); these vectors form an \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\)-submodule \(M_\iota \subseteq V\). Sizes will be read through \(\Theta_1\) alone.

Throughout, \(p\) and \(r\) are distinct primes. The toy is the following twisted lattice on \(V = \mathbb{C}^2\) with \(\mathrm{Tw} = \{1, \vartheta\}\): \(W_1 = W_\vartheta = \mathbb{C}^2\) with the standard inner product, \(\Theta_1 = \mathrm{id}\), \(\Theta_\vartheta = \operatorname{diag}(p^{-1}, 1)\); the twist \(1\) is allowed at every prime and \(\vartheta\) at every prime except \(r\); \(\Lambda_{1, \iota} = \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota} = \mathcal{O}_\iota^2\) for every \(\iota\); \(I = \{1, 2\}\), \(b = \mathrm{id}\), \(G = 1\). Let \(e_1, e_2\) be the standard basis.

§6.2. Index, covolume and the inequality Tag 233T

Fix a twisted lattice on \(V\) with twists \(\mathrm{Tw}\). Heights of families are those of Section 5.3. We use two facts from there: Problem 5.3.2, that the product of families of heights at most \(a\) and \(a'\) has height at most \(a+a'\), and the \(m\)-th power of a family of height at most \(a\) has height at most \(ma\) for every integer \(m\ge0\); and the product formula for families, Theorem 5.3.1: if \(z \in \mathbb{C}\), \(z \ne 0\), the constant family \(z\) has height at most \(c\), and \(\lvert \sigma z \rvert \le e^{c'}\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), then \[-c \le c' .\] Index and covolume are defined as in Section 5.4, with two changes: \(M_\iota\) is now the module of vectors integral at \(\iota\) for every allowed twist, and sizes are read through \(\Theta_1\), with the coordinates \(b\) and the matrix \(G\) of \(W_1\). Thus \(v_\sigma(y)\) is \(b(\Theta_1 y)\) with \(\sigma\) applied to each coordinate, \(N(y, \sigma) = v_\sigma(y)^* G\, v_\sigma(y)\), a real number \(\ge 0\) because \(G\) is positive definite as a complex matrix, so that the real part in Chapter 5 may be omitted, and \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) if \(\lvert \det(v_\sigma(y_i)^* G\, v_\sigma(y_k))_{i,k} \rvert \le e^c\) for every \(\sigma\). A family \(y\) has index at most \(\delta\) if there is a function \(e\colon \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}\), nonnegative everywhere, with \(\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell) \log \ell \le \delta\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) some \(u \in M_n(\mathcal{O}_\iota)\) carries \(y\) into \(M_\iota\) with loss \(e(\ell)\): \(\sum_k u_{ik} y_k \in M_\iota\) for every \(i\), and \(\lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \ge \ell^{-e(\ell)}\). The matrix may depend on \(\iota\) but not on the twist. For the untwisted coordinates, the conjugate bound of Problem 5.4.8 holds for every twisted lattice, with the same proof: the modules \(\Lambda_{\vartheta,\iota}\) play no role. If \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\), then \[\lvert \sigma(\Gamma_1(y)) \rvert \le e^c \quad \text{for every } \sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}).\]

Let \(y_1,\dots,y_n\) and \(z_1,\dots,z_{n'}\) be vectors in \(V\). In Problems 6.2.1–6.2.3, assume that \(V\) is a complex inner product space. Write \(y \sqcup z\) for the family \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'}\), and \(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2\) for the complex number \(\bar\gamma\gamma\).

Problem 6.2.3. Let \(y\) have index at most \(\delta\). For each \(\iota \mid \ell\) let \(\theta_\iota \in \mathrm{Tw}\) be allowed at \(\ell\) and \(\gamma_\iota \in \mathbb{C}\), with \(\gamma_\iota\, \Theta_{\theta_\iota} z_i \in \Lambda_{\theta_\iota, \iota} \cap \Lambda_{\theta_\iota, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\). Tag 233X

Problem 6.2.4. If \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\), and \(N(z_i, \sigma) \le e^{h}\) for every \(i\) and every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), then \(y \sqcup z\) has covolume at most \(c + n' h\).

Now let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space. For \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) let \(Z(\vartheta)\) be the square of the absolute value of the determinant of \(\Theta_\vartheta\), as a map \(V \to \Theta_\vartheta(V)\), in orthonormal bases, when \(\Theta_\vartheta\) is injective, and \(Z(\vartheta) := 0\) otherwise. If \(W_\vartheta\) is finite-dimensional, the Gram calculation of Problem 6.1.3 gives \(Z(\vartheta) = \det(\Theta_\vartheta^* \Theta_\vartheta)\), as in Section 6.1.

Tag 233Y

Problem 6.2.5. Let all \(\Theta_\vartheta\) be injective, let \(y \sqcup z\) be a basis of \(V\), and let \(\gamma \in \mathbb{C}\), \(\gamma \ne 0\). Then \(\Gamma_1(y \sqcup z) \ne 0\), and for every \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) \[\Gamma_1(y \sqcup z) = (\lvert\gamma\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_\vartheta(y \sqcup z) \cdot (\lvert\gamma\rvert^{-2})^{n'} \cdot \frac{Z(1)}{Z(\vartheta)} .\]

Problems 6.2.3, 6.2.4 and 6.2.5, together with the height bounds and the product formula of Chapter 5, give the following theorem.

Tag 233Z

Theorem 6.2.1. Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, \(\mathrm{Tw}\) a set with a distinguished element \(1\), and \(L\) a twisted lattice on \(V\) with twists \(\mathrm{Tw}\) whose maps \(\Theta_\vartheta\) are all injective. Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'} \in V\) be linearly independent, with \(\dim V = n + n'\). For each \(\iota \mid \ell\), for every prime \(\ell\), let \(\theta_\iota \in \mathrm{Tw}\) be allowed at \(\ell\). Let \(c, \delta, c_{\mathrm{vol}}, c_{\mathrm{short}}, c_{\mathrm{den}}\in \mathbb{R}\), and suppose that \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) and index at most \(\delta\). Let \(\gamma\colon \mathrm{Tw} \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(\gamma(\vartheta) \ne 0\) for every \(\vartheta\), and suppose that

  1. \(\gamma(\vartheta)\, \Theta_\vartheta z_i \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) for every \(i\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) for every prime \(\ell\), and every \(\vartheta\) allowed at \(\ell\);

  2. \(N(z_i, \sigma) \le e^{c_{\mathrm{short}}}\) for every \(i\) and every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\);

  3. the family \(\iota \mapsto (\lvert \gamma(\theta_\iota) \rvert^2)^{-1}\) has height at most \(c_{\mathrm{den}}\);

  4. the family \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) has height at most \(c_{\mathrm{vol}}\).

Then \[0 \le c + 2\delta + n'(c_{\mathrm{short}}+ c_{\mathrm{den}}) + c_{\mathrm{vol}}.\]

Used in: Problem 6.3.1, Problem 6.3.2; Chapter 8; Chapter 67; Chapter 75

Hypothesis (1) concerns every twist allowed at \(\ell\), including \(\theta_\iota\) when it differs from \(\theta_{\bar\iota}\). To recover Theorem 5.4.3, give \(V\) the inner product in which its given basis \(y\) is orthonormal, and take \(\mathrm{Tw}=\{1\}\), with \(1\) allowed at every prime, \(n'=0\), \(\gamma=1\) and \(c_{\mathrm{short}}=c_{\mathrm{den}}=c_{\mathrm{vol}}=0\).

§6.3. A twist for each prime Tag 2342

We first test Theorem 6.2.1 on the toy, then add a twist that is allowed at \(r\).

Tag 2343

Problem 6.3.1. In the toy, let \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(s \ge 1\) an integer, and take \(n = n' = 1\), \(y_1 = p^k e_1\), \(z_1 = s^{-1} e_2\), \(\gamma(1) = \gamma(\vartheta) = s\), \(\theta_\iota = \vartheta\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell \ne r\), and \(\theta_\iota = 1\) for \(\iota \mid r\). The hypotheses of Theorem 6.2.1 hold with \[c = 2k \log p, \quad \delta = \max(1 - k, 0) \log p, \quad c_{\mathrm{short}}= -2\log s, \quad c_{\mathrm{den}}= 2 \log s, \quad c_{\mathrm{vol}}= -2 \log p ,\] and then \(c + 2\delta + n'(c_{\mathrm{short}}+ c_{\mathrm{den}}) + c_{\mathrm{vol}}= 2\max(k - 1, 0) \log p\). For \(p = 3\), \(k = -1\), \(s = 2\): \(c = -2\log 3\) and \(\delta = 2 \log 3\). For the constant choice \(\theta_\iota = 1\), with the same \(c_{\mathrm{short}}\) and \(c_{\mathrm{den}}\), the theorem gives only \(c + 2\delta \ge 0\).

The second toy adds to the toy a twist \(\vartheta'\): \(\mathrm{Tw} = \{1, \vartheta, \vartheta'\}\), \(W_{\vartheta'} = \mathbb{C}^2\), \(\Theta_{\vartheta'} = \operatorname{diag}(1, r^{-1})\), \(\Lambda_{\vartheta', \iota} = \mathcal{O}_\iota^2\), and \(\vartheta'\) is allowed at every prime except \(p\).

Tag 2345

Problem 6.3.3. In the second toy, suppose that \(\theta_\iota \in \{1, \vartheta\}\) for every \(\iota\). If \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) has height at most \(c''\), then \(c'' \ge -2\log p\). The same holds with \(\vartheta'\) and \(r\) in place of \(\vartheta\) and \(p\).

So for \(y\) as in Problem 6.3.2, a choice of twists with values in \(\{1, \vartheta\}\) or in \(\{1, \vartheta'\}\) yields through Theorem 6.2.1 at most \(c \ge 2\log p\) or \(c \ge 2\log r\), while the choice that depends on the prime yields \(c \ge 2\log(pr)\), which is attained.

Under the other hypotheses of Theorem 6.2.1, a height bound of \(-A\) for \(\iota\mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) gives \(c+2\delta\ge A-n'(c_{\mathrm{short}}+c_{\mathrm{den}})\). The proof of Theorem C (Chapter 67) contains the sole application of Theorem 6.2.1. There \(V\) is the space \(T\) of target forms, and \(\mathrm{Tw}\) is the set of \(m\)-fold twists. A twist \(\vartheta\) with modulus \(Q_\vartheta\) is allowed at \(\ell\) when \(\ell\) is prime to \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\). Its volume \(Z(\vartheta)\) is the Gram determinant of the images of an orthonormal basis under the chain of lifts (Chapter 68). The height of \(\iota\mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) is bounded by combining the bounds for the single lifts of Chapter 74 with a height bound for the inverse scalar in the chain factorization of Chapter 68. The auxiliary forms of Section 68.5 span \(T\), so we choose \(z_i\) among them to complete \(y\) to a basis. By the auxiliary condition, each \(\gamma(\vartheta)\Theta_\vartheta z_i\) is integral at every \(\iota\) at which \(\vartheta\) is allowed.
