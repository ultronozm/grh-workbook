\medskip
Which spaces, forms and characters does the proof attach to the data?  This chapter fixes them, following \cite{GL}; the later chapters use this notation.  The Poincar\'e map is defined in Chapter~\ref{bp-chap}, the composite lift and the data of each lift of the chain in Chapter~\ref{ss-chap}, and gains and exceptional primes in Chapter~\ref{dl-chap}; the gauge of a form at a prime, which measures its integrality, is defined below (Definition~\ref{def:gauge}).  Critical pairs and the target lattice are defined at the end of this chapter.

For a prime \(p\) and a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\), \(\operatorname{val}\) is the valuation of \(\C_p\) normalized by \(\operatorname{val}(p) = 1\), with \(\operatorname{val}(0) = \infty\), read after \(\eta\); after an isomorphism \(\iota\colon \C \to \overline{\Q}_\ell\) it is read through \(\iota\) and the inclusion \(\overline{\Q}_\ell \subset \C_\ell\).  Put \(\operatorname{val}_+ := \max(\operatorname{val}, 0)\).  Let \(\Oc_\eta\) be the ring of \(z \in \C\) with \(\operatorname{val}(\eta z) \ge 0\).  For a positive integer \(q\), let \(\Oc^{[q]}\) be the ring of numbers \emph{integral away from \(q\)}: the \(z \in \C\) with \(\operatorname{val}(\eta z) \ge 0\) for every prime \(\ell \nmid q\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_\ell\).

\section{Chosen places, the group \(\bar G\) and the idempotent \(e_0\)}\label{sec:chosen}

A finite place \(w\) of \(E\) is \emph{chosen} if it divides \(\pi_M\); let \(\mathcal M\) be the set of chosen places and \(\mathcal M_i\) those above \(p_i\) (\(i = 0, 1\)).  For \(w \in \mathcal M\) let \(k_w\) be the residue field, and put \(\bar G := \prod_{w \in \mathcal M} \mathrm{GL}_r(k_w) = \bar G_0 \times \bar G_1\), with \(\bar G_i := \prod_{w \in \mathcal M_i} \mathrm{GL}_r(k_w)\).  A \emph{label} of degree \(r\) over \(k_w\) is a monic squarefree polynomial of degree \(r\) with nonzero constant term; after fixing an injective character \(\overline{k_w}^{\,\times}\to\C^\times\) for each residue field, Green's construction associates to the label an irreducible character \(\Xi_c\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_r(k_w)\) and the central idempotent \(e_c := (\Xi_c(1)/\#\mathrm{GL}_r(k_w)) \sum_g \overline{\Xi_c(g)}\, g\).  A family of labels \((c_w)_{w \in \mathcal M}\) is \emph{good} if, for \(i = 0, 1\), no \(c_w\) with \(w \in \mathcal M_i\) has a root \(z \in k_w\) with \(z^{q_2} = 1\), and at least one \(c_w\) with \(w \in \mathcal M_i\) is irreducible with a root in \(\overline{k_w}\) of order \((\#k_w)^r - 1\).  Let \(e_0 \in \C[\bar G]\) be the sum, over the good families, of the tensor products \(\bigotimes_w e_{c_w}\).  The summands are orthogonal central idempotents, so their sum is a central idempotent.

The larger finite group \(\prod_{w \in \mathcal M} \mathrm{GL}_{rt}(k_w)\), with matrices of size \(rt = 5r^2\), has order \(\mathcal N := \prod_{w \in \mathcal M} \#\mathrm{GL}_{rt}(k_w)\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:group-order}
For \(i = 0, 1\) there are exactly \(f\) chosen places above \(p_i\), and \(\#k_w = p_i\) for \(w \in \mathcal M_i\).  Moreover \(\log \mathcal N \le (rt)^2 f \log M \le x^{32}\).
\end{problem}

\section{The gap and the weights of the lifts}\label{sec:weights}

Define the \emph{gap} by \(\Delta := 2r^8\).  For \(j \ge 0\) and \(\tau \in \Phi\) the \emph{weight of lift \(j\)} is \(\kappa_j(\tau) := \kappa(\tau) + (j + \frac12)\Delta\).  The rank-\(r\) target uses the following initial segment \(\varsigma_\tau\) of the target parameter at \(\tau\):
\[
  \varsigma_\tau := \Bigl(\tfrac k2 - 1,\ \tfrac k2 - 2,\ \dots,\ 1 - \tfrac k2;\ \ -\kappa(\tau) - \Delta,\ -\kappa(\tau) - 2\Delta,\ \dots,\ -\kappa(\tau) - m\Delta\Bigr) ,
\]
with \(k - 1\) entries before the semicolon and \(m\) after it (\(k\) is even).  By the definition of \(\kappa_j\), the \(j\)-th entry after the semicolon is \(\Delta/2 - \kappa_j(\tau)\).  The full sequence continues with \(-\kappa(\tau)-(m+1)\Delta,-\kappa(\tau)-(m+2)\Delta,\dots\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:parameter}
The entries of \(\varsigma_\tau\) are strictly decreasing.
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.tex#pr:parameter

\begin{definition}\label{def:diagram}
The \emph{diagram} of the data assigns to \(\tau \in \Phi\) and an integer \(h \ge 1\) the number \(\beta(\tau, h) \ge 0\) of its columns of height \(h\) at \(\tau\):
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\beta(\tau, 1) := a\) if \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), and \(\beta(\tau, 1) := 0\) otherwise;
\item \(\beta(\tau, k) := \kappa_1(\tau) - \lfloor (k + t + 1)/2 \rfloor\);
\item \(\beta(\tau, h) := \Delta - (t + 1)\) if \(h > k\) and \(t + 1\) divides \(h - k\);
\item \(\beta(\tau, h) := 0\) for every other \(h\).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The first two cases are disjoint since \(k \ge 2\) (Problem~\ref{da-pr:m-big}).  Both differences are positive for the data of Chapter~\ref{da-chap}.  For the algebraic forms on \(X_j\) (Section~\ref{sec:spaces}), define entry \(p\) of the highest weight at \(\tau\), for \(0 \le p < n_j\), to be \(\sum_{p < h < n_j} \beta(\tau, h)\) plus the last entry, which is \(0\) for \(j = 0\) and \(\lfloor n_{j-1}/2 \rfloor - \kappa_j(\tau)\) for \(j \ge 1\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:diagram}
At \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) the columns of height below \(k\) are precisely the \(a\) columns of height one, and at \(\tau \notin \Phi_a\) there are no columns of height below \(k\).  The corresponding highest weights on \(X_0\) are \((a, 0, \dots, 0)\) and \(0\), respectively, and their Weyl modules are the modules \(A_\tau\) of Section~\ref{sec:source}.  Formally, for \(k = 4\), \(t = 5\), \(\Delta = 8\), \(a = 3\) and \(\kappa_j(\tau) = 12 + 8j\) at a place \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) (values that no data have), the weight on \(X_1\) at \(\tau\) is \((0, -3, -3, -3)\) followed by six entries \(-18\), and that on \(X_2\) is \((-3, -6, -6, -6)\) followed by six entries \(-21\) and six entries \(-23\).
\end{problem}

\section{The spaces}\label{sec:spaces}

Let \(V\) be a Hermitian space of rank \(n\) with Gram matrix \(J\) over \(E\).  An \emph{adelic frame} is a basis after extension of scalars to the adeles of \(E\) in which the Gram matrix is the prescribed diagonal matrix of signs at every infinite place and the identity at every finite place.  Its columns in a fixed \(E\)-basis form an invertible \(n\times n\) matrix over the adeles of \(E\).

A \emph{framed space} is a space with a chosen adelic frame.  The \emph{finite adelic frame} is its restriction to the finite adeles, and the \emph{standard lattice} consists of vectors with integral coordinates at every finite place in that basis.  The proof uses the chosen frame for the rational subgroup and for all lattice and reduction conditions.  The \emph{standard lattice stabilizer} \(K^\circ(V)\) consists of the finite adelic unitary matrices that are integral in the finite adelic frame at every finite place.

The \emph{standard positive definite space} of rank \(n\) is \(E^n\) with Gram matrix \(I_n\) and the coordinate adelic frame.  For \(j \ge 0\), \(X_j\) is the standard positive definite space of rank \(n_j\).  The spaces \(Y_j\), \(0 \le j \le m\), are those of Section~\ref{da-sec:data}; for \(j < m\) the identity block of \(Y_j\) is \(X_j\), and \(Y_m\) is identified, through a chosen bijection of bases, with the orthogonal complement in \(X_m\) of its last basis vector.  For \(1 \le j \le m\), give \(W_j = E^r\) the diagonal Gram matrix with sign \(+1\) at indices \(0 \le i < k+j-1\) and \(-1\) at the other indices.  Let \(V_j\) be the standard positive definite space of rank \(r + t\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:signs-W}
\(W_j\) has exactly \(m - j\) negative signs for \(1 \le j \le m\).  Formally, for \(r = 9\), \(m = 4\), \(j = 1\), the negative basis vectors are the last three.
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.tex#pr:signs-W

\section{Principal forms, good forms and the target}\label{sec:forms}

Let \(V\) be such a framed space of rank \(r\), with numbered signs, such as \(Y_0\) or \(W_j\); for \(Y_0\) the sign paired with entry \(i\), numbered from \(0\), is \((-1)^{\max(i+1-k,0)}\).  A \emph{principal form} on \(V\) is a bounded continuous complex function on its adelic unitary group, invariant on the left under the rational points and on the right under the \emph{principal level} of integral finite adelic unitary matrices that reduce to the identity at every \(w\in\mathcal M\), such that, if the product \(e\) over \(\tau\) of the discrete-series kernels with doubled parameter \(2\varsigma_\tau\) and the numbered signs of \(V\) is integrable, right convolution by \(e\) fixes the form.  They form a space \(H(V)\), on which \(\bar G\) acts by right translation through integral lifts.  The \emph{good forms} are \(H_g(V) := e_0 H(V)\).  The \emph{target forms} and their dimension are \(T := H_g(Y_0)\) and \(d_T := \dim_\C T\).

\begin{definition}\label{def:petersson}
Give \(H(V)\) the \emph{Petersson inner product} \(\langle y', y\rangle := \int \overline{y'(g)}\,y(g)\,dg\), the integral over the quotient of the adelic unitary group of \(V\) by its rational points, for the quotient measure fixed in the proof; it is conjugate-linear in the first argument.
\end{definition}

Under the gap hypothesis of Chapter~\ref{c2-chap}, verified in Problem~\ref{c2-pr:gap-Y-W}, the proof shows that \(H(Y_0)\) and \(H(W_j)\) for \(1 \le j \le m\) are finite-dimensional.

\section{Source forms}\label{sec:source}

Algebraic forms take values in Weyl modules; their integrality at a prime is measured as follows.

\begin{definition}\label{def:gauge}
Fix a prime \(p\) and a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\).  At a point \(g\) of the finite adelic group, the \emph{gauge} \(\lVert\omega\rVert_g\) of a Weyl vector \(\omega\) is the largest absolute value of a coordinate of \(g^{-1}\cdot\eta(\omega)\), where at \(\tau\) the adelic matrix acts in the Weyl representation through the extension to finite adeles of \(u \mapsto \eta(\Phi_\tau(\bar u))\), and \(\Phi_\tau\) is the embedding of \(E\) selected by the CM type (the fixed embedding composed with the element of \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\) that corresponds to \(\tau\), Section~\ref{da-sec:field}).  A form \(\varphi\) \emph{has valuation at least \(c\)} at \(\eta\), for a real number \(c\), if \(\lVert\varphi(g)\rVert_g \le p^{-c}\) for every \(g\); it is \emph{integral at \(\eta\)} if it has valuation at least \(0\).
\end{definition}

The \emph{source forms} \(S\) are the algebraic modular forms on the unitary group of \(X_0\) with values in \(\bigotimes_{\tau\in\Phi} A_\tau\), where \(A_\tau\) is the Weyl module of highest weight \((a,0,\dots,0)\) for \(\tau\in\Phi_a\) and \(A_\tau=\C\) otherwise.  A form is evaluated on the finite adelic unitary group, and a rational element acts on its values through the entrywise conjugates of its archimedean components.  The level consists of the integral finite adelic unitary matrices reducing to the identity at every \(w\in\mathcal M\) and having last row congruent to \((0,\dots,0,1)\) modulo \(\pi_N\).  Put \(D_X := \dim_\C S\).  For each \(w \mid \pi_N\), lift a generator of the residue-field units to a local unit and form the finite idele equal to that unit at \(w\) and \(1\) elsewhere.  Divide it by its conjugate to obtain an integral norm-one idele \(z_w\) whose reduction at \(w\) has order \(N - 1\).  Right translation by the scalar \(z_w\) is an operator \(T_w\) on \(S\).  A source form \(\varphi\) is \emph{ramified} if \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-2} T_w^i\varphi = 0\) for every \(w \mid \pi_N\); the ramified forms are a subspace \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\).  Write \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}}\) for the ramified forms whose gauge (Definition~\ref{def:gauge}) is at most \(1\) at every finite adelic argument, for every prime \(\ell\) and every field isomorphism \(\iota\colon\C\to\overline{\Q}_\ell\), composed with the inclusion into \(\C_\ell\).

\section{Tame characters and twists}\label{sec:twists}

A \emph{tame character} \(\xi\) consists of a continuous unitary character of the norm-one ideles of \(E\), trivial on the norm-one elements of \(E^\times\), a finite set \(Q_\xi\) of finite places of \(E\), and characters \(\psi_w\) of \(k_w^\times\) for all finite places \(w\), such that: \(\xi\) has weight \(0\) and tame part \((\psi_w)\), that is, \(\xi(u) = \prod_w \psi_w(u_w \bmod w)\) for every norm-one idele \(u\) with integral finite part; \(\psi_w = 1\) for \(w \notin Q_\xi\); \(\bar w \notin Q_\xi\) for \(w \in Q_\xi\); and \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi := \prod_{w \in Q_\xi} \#k_w\) is prime to \(MN\).  A \emph{twist} \(\vartheta\) is a tame character with \(\psi_w \ne 1\) for every \(w \in Q_\vartheta\); it is \emph{\(m\)-fold} if \(\psi_w^n \ne 1\) for every \(w \in Q_\vartheta\) and \(1 \le n \le m\).  The trivial twist \(1\) has \(Q = \varnothing\).  For a nonnegative integer \(n\), the power \(\xi^n\) as a tame character has the same set \(Q_\xi\) and tame part \((\psi_w^n)\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:mfold}
A character \(\psi\) of \(k_w^\times\) has \(\psi^n \ne 1\) for \(1 \le n \le m\) if and only if its order exceeds \(m\).  For \(k_w = \mathbb F_{13}\), exactly \(8\) of the \(12\) characters have order exceeding \(3\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.tex#pr:mfold

\section{The characters of the lifts}\label{sec:characters}

The proof fixes two characters of the norm-one ideles, trivial on the rational points: \(\mu^\flat\), of weight \(-\kappa_0\) with trivial tame part, and \(\chi_M\), of weight \(0\), whose tame part \((\omega_w)\) is trivial off \(\mathcal M\) and has order \(q_2\) at each \(w \in \mathcal M\).  With \(\upsilon\) the splitting character of the Weil representation restricted to the norm-one ideles (weight \(1\), trivial tame part), the \emph{character of lift \(j\)} is \(\mu_j^0 := \mu^\flat\,(\upsilon^{-\Delta}\chi_M)^j\), of weight \(-\kappa_j\) and tame part \((\omega_w^j)\).

\section{The integral vacuum}\label{sec:vacuum}

The \emph{vacuum} is the normalized vector in the Weil model associated with the standard lattice, with a Gaussian at infinity and the twisted lattice characteristic function at the finite places.  For each \(s\) the proof fixes \(c_{\mathrm{vac}} \in \C^\times\) such that \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}\) times the vacuum of the standard rank-one space over \(E_s\) has values integral under every ring map \(\C \to \C_p\), for every prime \(p\), at all finite arguments, and all conjugates over \(K\) of these values have absolute value at most \(\exp(v_0 q_1^2)\), with \(v_0\) as in Section~\ref{da-sec:data}.  The \emph{integral vacuum} of a space of rank \(n\) is \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\,n}\) times its vacuum.

\section{The chain and the single lifts}\label{sec:chain}

Give \(Y_m\) the same last weight entry at \(\tau\) as \(X_m\).  Put \(\beta^\circ(h) := \Delta/2 - \lfloor (t+1)/2 \rfloor\) if \(h \ge k\) and \(t + 1\) divides \(h - k\), and \(\beta^\circ(h) := 0\) otherwise; for \(1 \le h < n_m - 1\) the weight on \(Y_m\) has \(\beta_Y(\tau, h) := \beta^\circ(h) + \beta(\tau, h+1) - \beta^\circ(h+1)\) columns of height \(h\), that is, its entry \(p\), numbered from \(0\) for \(0 \le p < n_m - 1\), is \(\sum_{p < h < n_m - 1} \beta_Y(\tau, h)\) plus the last entry.  The proof shows that this weight and the weights on the \(X_j\) agree with those obtained from their Harish--Chandra parameters.

For a tame character \(\xi\), the \emph{lower chain} \(\Theta_\xi\) is a linear map from \(H(Y_0)\) to algebraic forms of this weight on \(Y_m\): the composite of \(m\) normalized theta lifts \(Y_{j-1} \to Y_j\), \(1 \le j \le m\), whose \(j\)-th step is built from the character \(\mu_j^0\xi^j\), followed by the passage to algebraic forms on \(Y_m\).  For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \(\Theta_\vartheta\) also denotes its restriction to \(T\), with target the subspace of this weight on which the final level acts by the character prescribed by \(\vartheta\) (Definition~\ref{def:final-level}), and \(Z(\vartheta) := \operatorname{normDet}(\Theta_\vartheta)^2\).  For \(1 \le j \le m\), the \emph{single lift on principal forms} \(\hat u_{\xi,j}\) is the theta lift from \(H(W_j)\) to the algebraic level forms on \(V_j\) of the weight, level and character of the descent pair for \((\xi, j)\), built from \(\mu_j^0\xi\), and the \emph{single lift} \(u_{\xi,j}\) is its restriction to \(H_g(W_j)\); for an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \(Z_j(\vartheta) := \operatorname{normDet}(u)^2\) with \(u := u_{\vartheta^j, j}\).  Chapter~\ref{ss-chap} describes the datum, characters and scalar in each step of the lower chain.

\begin{definition}\label{def:final-level}
For a tame character \(\vartheta\), the \emph{final level} \(K_{\mathrm{last}}(\vartheta)\) is the group of elements of \(K^\circ(Y_m)\) that reduce to \(1\) at each \(w \in \mathcal M\) and, at each \(w \in Q_\vartheta\), to a block upper triangular matrix with diagonal blocks of sizes \(r, t, \dots, t\).  The product defining its \emph{character} runs over \(\mathcal M \cup Q_\vartheta\); the reduction is the identity at every \(w \in \mathcal M\), so the character on the final level is \(k \mapsto \prod_{w \in Q_\vartheta}\prod_{l=1}^{m}\psi_{l,w}(\det k_w^{(l)})\), where \(k_w^{(l)}\) is the \(l\)-th diagonal block of size \(t\) of the reduction of \(k\) at \(w\) and \((\psi_{l,w})_w\) is the tame part of \(\mu_l^0\vartheta^l\), the character of lift \(l\).  We call it the \emph{character prescribed by \(\vartheta\)}.
\end{definition}

The proof shows that the lower chain \(\Theta_\vartheta\) takes values in the forms of this level and character.

Here \(\operatorname{normDet}(u)\) is the nonnegative square root of the Gram determinant of the images of an orthonormal basis of the source, read in \(\C\) when forming \(Z\) and \(Z_j\).  Both maps use the Petersson inner product (Definition~\ref{def:petersson}) on the source.  The proof gives finite-dimensional targets and computes their inner products by class sums.  Thus \(\operatorname{normDet}(u)^2 = \det(u^*u)\).

\section{Critical pairs}\label{sec:pairs}

For a continuous unitary character \(\mu\) of the norm-one ideles of \(E\), trivial on the norm-one elements of \(E^\times\), and a finite set \(S\) of finite places of \(E\), write \(\bar S\) for the set of conjugate places.

\begin{definition}\label{def:pair}
The \emph{partial Hecke value} \(L^S(z, \mu)\), for real \(z > 1\), is the Euler product over the finite places \(v \notin S \cup \bar S\) of \((1 - c_v (\#k_v)^{-z})^{-1}\), where \(c_v\) is the value of the Hecke character \(x \mapsto \mu(x/\bar x)\) of \(E\) at a chosen uniformizer at \(v\).  At \(z = 1\) it is the limit from the right, where this limit exists and is finite.  For \(z > 1\), if \(S \cup \bar S\) contains the places where this Hecke character ramifies, the product is its \(L\)-function without the Euler factors at \(S \cup \bar S\).

A \emph{critical pair} \(P = (\nu, z)\) consists of such a character \(\nu\), of weight \(-\mathrm{wt}_P\) with \(\mathrm{wt}_P \colon \Phi \to \Z_{\ge 0}\) (the \emph{negative weight} of \(P\)), residue characters \(\psi_w\) that give its tame part and are trivial off \(\mathcal M\), and an integer \(z\) with \(1 \le z \le t\) and \(z \le \mathrm{wt}_P(\sigma) \le 4b\) for all \(\sigma \in \Phi\).  It is \emph{ramified} if \(\psi_w \ne 1\) exactly for \(w \in \mathcal M\).  For a tame character \(\xi\) and \(\epsilon = \pm1\) its \emph{ratio} is
\[
  R_P(\xi, \epsilon) := \frac{\lvert L^{\mathcal M \cup Q_\xi}(z, (\nu\xi^{\epsilon})^{-1})\rvert}{\lvert L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu^{-1})\rvert}, \qquad R_P(\xi) := R_P(\xi, 1) .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:ramified-pair}
For \(1 \le j \le m\), a critical pair with tame part \((\omega_w^j)\), the \(j\)-th power of the tame part of \(\chi_M\), is ramified.  Formally, if a family of residue characters is trivial off the nonempty set \(\mathcal M\) and has order \(6\) at every place of \(\mathcal M\), then for exactly \(9\) of the integers \(1 \le j \le 10\) its \(j\)-th power is nontrivial at every place of \(\mathcal M\).
\end{problem}

\section{The target lattice}\label{sec:lattice}

Recall the isomorphisms \(\iota \mid \ell\) of Chapter~\ref{sd-chap}.  For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), the target of \(\Theta_\vartheta\) is the space of algebraic forms on \(Y_m\) of the weight specified in Section~\ref{sec:chain} on which the final level acts by the character prescribed by \(\vartheta\), with the class-sum inner product.  A form \(F\) in it is \emph{integral at \(\iota\)} if its values have gauge (Definition~\ref{def:gauge}) at most \(1\) at every finite adelic argument, for the composite of \(\iota\) with the inclusion \(\overline{\Q}_\ell \subset \C_\ell\).

\begin{definition}\label{def:target-lattice}
We define the \emph{target lattice} as the following twisted lattice on \(T\), in the sense of Chapter~\ref{at-chap}, Section~\ref{at-sec:toy}.  Its twists are the \(m\)-fold twists, with the trivial twist \(1\); its maps are the lower chains \(\Theta_\vartheta\) on \(T\); for every \(n \in \mathbb N\), the twist \(\vartheta\) is \emph{allowed} at \(n\) if \(\gcd(n, \mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta) = 1\); and \(\mathfrak L_{\vartheta,\iota}\) is the \(\Oc_\iota\)-module of forms in the target of \(\Theta_\vartheta\) that are integral at \(\iota\).  The coordinates of a form \(F\) in the target of \(\Theta_1\) are the coordinates, in the standard basis of the Weyl module, of its values \(F(g_c)\) at fixed representatives \(g_c\) of the classes at its level; their rational Gram matrix is block diagonal, with one copy of the Gram matrix of the Weyl module for each class.  For \(v \in T\) and \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), \(\lVert v \rVert_\sigma^2\) is the squared length, for this Gram matrix, of the coordinate vector of \(\Theta_1 v\) with \(\sigma\) applied to each coordinate.  A vector \(v \in T\) is \emph{integral at \(\iota \mid \ell\)} if \(\Theta_\vartheta v \in \mathfrak L_{\vartheta,\iota}\) for every \(\vartheta\) allowed at \(\ell\).  A family \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) in \(T\) has \emph{covolume} at most \(c\) if for every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\) the determinant of the products of the coordinate vectors of the \(\Theta_1 y_i\), with \(\sigma\) applied, taken by the same Gram matrix, has absolute value at most \(e^c\).  It has \emph{index} at most \(\delta\) if there is a function \(e \colon \mathbb N \to \R_{\ge 0}\) satisfying both conditions below.  For every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0} e(\ell) \log \ell \le \delta\).  For every prime \(\ell\) and every \(\iota \mid \ell\), there is a matrix \(u \in M_n(\Oc_\iota)\) with \(\lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \ge \ell^{-e(\ell)}\) such that \(\sum_k u_{ik} y_k\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(i\).
\end{definition}

These are the index and covolume of Section~\ref{at-sec:general} for this twisted lattice.  The proof shows that these data satisfy the pairing condition of a twisted lattice (Problem~\ref{a1-pr:pairing}) and that the maps \(\Theta_\vartheta\) are injective on \(T\) (Problem~\ref{c4-pr:theta-injective}); by definition its volume at \(\vartheta\) is \(Z(\vartheta)\).  The stabilizers of the classes at the levels of the targets are trivial (Problem~\ref{a1-pr:free}), so \(\lVert v \rVert_\sigma^2\) is also the weighted sum of Chapter~\ref{ta-chap}, Section~\ref{ta-sec:theorem-a}, for the form \(\Theta_1 v\).
