At the level prime the gain is \(0\), but the glued pairs of the composite lift carry poles at \(N\): their data translate the vacuum by \(-y/N\). How much \(N\)-adic valuation does the composite lift lose there, and on how large a module of forms? This is the case \(p = N\) of Theorem 57.6.1; Section 57.5 uses it in the form of Theorem 65.4.1 below. On ramified forms every glued pair becomes two direct lifts (Definition 65.2.2) after an operator \(\mathrm{Up}\), and \(\mathrm{Up}^{m/2}\) is integral, after a change of base point, on a submodule of small colength.

§65.1. Ramified forms

Recall from Section 37.5 that for each prime \(w \mid \pi_N\) of \(E\) right translation by the idele \(z_w\) is an operator \(T_w\) on \(S\) with \(T_w^{N-1} = 1\), and \(S_\mathrm{ram}= \bigcap_{w \mid \pi_N}\ker\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}T_w^i\).

Problem 65.1.1. Let \(F_1, \dots, F_l\) be commuting operators on a finite-dimensional complex space with \(F_i^n = 1\). Then \(\bigcap_i \ker\sum_{s<n}F_i^s = \operatorname{im}\prod_i(1 - F_i)\). Hence \(S_\mathrm{ram}= \operatorname{im}\prod_{w \mid \pi_N}(1 - T_w)\), as in Problem 52.5.3. For the cyclic shift \(F\) of \({\mathbb{C}}^4\), \(F e_s = e_{s+1}\), the vector \((1-F)e_0 = (1,-1,0,0)\) is killed by \(\sum_{s<4}F^s\).

Used in: Chapter 49: Problem 49.3.2

Problem 65.1.2. Let \(N = 3\) and let \(F\) act on \({\mathbb{C}}\) through the sign character of \({\mathbb{F}}_3^\times\) at its generator. Then \(1 + F = 0\), \(1 - F = 2\), so the line is ramified and \(\lvert 1 - F\rvert^2 = 4\).

§65.2. The places above \(N\) and the direct lifts

Fix a ring map \(\eta\colon {\mathbb{C}}\to {\mathbb{C}}_N\). For \(\tau \in \Phi\) let \(w_\tau\) be the place of \(E\) above \(N\) at which \(\eta \circ \Phi_\tau\) is continuous (the prime \(v_\tau\) of Section 57.4, for \(p = N\); we write \(w\) for places above \(N\), as in Section 37.5). Let \(\Sigma_N\) (a notation of this chapter) be the set of the conjugates \(\bar w_\tau\). As \(N \equiv 1 \pmod{q_2^2}\), \(N\) splits completely in \(E\); so \(\tau \mapsto \bar w_\tau\) is a bijection from \(\Phi\) onto \(\Sigma_N\), which holds exactly one place of each conjugate pair above \(N\); each residue field is \({\mathbb{F}}_N\). A residue vector on \(X_j\) is a family \(y = (y_w)_{w \in \Sigma_N}\) of vectors \(y_w \in {\mathbb{F}}_N^{n_j}\) in the basis of \(X_j\); it is primitive if every \(y_w \ne 0\); \(e_{\mathrm{last}}\) is the last basis vector at every place.

Definition 65.2.1. Let \(\mathrm{diag}_N\) be the matrix \(\mathrm{diag}(1, \dots, 1, N)\) at every place of \(\Sigma_N\). Define the base point \(\delta_j\) to be the finite adelic point of the unitary group of \(X_j\), supported above \(N\), whose component at \(w \in \Sigma_N\) is \(\mathrm{diag}(1, \dots, 1, N)\) if \(w \mid \pi_N\) and \(1\) otherwise. Put \(\delta_j^\vee := \delta_j\,\delta_{j,\Sigma}^{-1}\), where \(\delta_{j,\Sigma}\) is the point with component \(\mathrm{diag}_N\) at \(\Sigma_N\).

For a matrix \(g_1\) over \(\prod_{w \in \Sigma_N}E_w\) let \(\mathrm{Rt}_j(g_1)\) be right translation of forms on \(X_j\) by \(\delta_j g_1\,\delta_j^{-1}\): matrices at \(\Sigma_N\) are read through \(\delta_j\). For a function \(f\) of \(n_{j+1} \times n_j\) matrices at \(\Sigma_N\) and a finite datum \(v\) of \(\mathrm{Hom}(X_j, X_{j+1})\), let \(\mathrm{Sch}(f)v\) put \(f\) on \(v\) at \(\Sigma_N\) in the Schrödinger model, and let \(\Theta^X_j[f, v]\) (in this chapter) be the definite lift \(X_j \to X_{j+1}\) for the twist \(\vartheta\) (lift \(j+1\) of Definition 52.4.2; here, as in Definition 57.3.3, a lift carries the index of its source space) whose datum is \(\mathrm{Sch}(f)v\) translated by \((\delta_j, \delta_{j+1})\) under the Weil representation. A function of residue matrices is read on the integral matrices through reduction modulo \(N\) and extended by \(0\).

Definition 65.2.2. Define the direct datum \(\mathrm{dir}\) to be the function \(\mathrm{col}_{e_{\mathrm{last}}}\), where \(\mathrm{col}_y(T) = 1\) if \(T\) is integral and its last column reduces to \(y\), and \(\mathrm{col}_y(T) = 0\) otherwise. Call \(\Theta^X_j[\mathrm{dir}, v]\) the direct lift \(j\) with datum \(v\).

Definition 65.2.3. Let \(\psi_N(c) := \psi_0(-c/N)\) on \(\prod_{w \in \Sigma_N}{\mathbb{F}}_N\), with \(\psi_0\) the standard character of the finite adeles. For a residue vector \(y\) on \(X_0\) whose last entry is a unit at every place, let \(\mathfrak g_y\) be an integral invertible matrix whose last column and last row reduce to \(y\). On forms on \(X_0\), \[\mathrm{Up}:= \mathrm{Rt}_0(\mathrm{diag}_N^{-1}) \sum_{y} \psi_N(y_{\mathrm{last}})\,\mathrm{Rt}_0(\mathfrak g_y),\] the sum over these \(y\).

A form \(\varphi\) on \(X_j\) is ramified at \(\Sigma_N\) (in this chapter) if for each \(w \in \Sigma_N\) there are a form \(\psi\) and a unit scalar \(c\) at \(\Sigma_N\), integral, with \(c \equiv 1 \bmod N\) at the places other than \(w\), such that \(\varphi = \psi - \mathrm{Rt}_j(c)\psi\).

Problem 65.2.1. Every \(\varphi \in S_\mathrm{ram}\) is ramified at \(\Sigma_N\), with \(\psi \in S\).

Read from the proof: the following lift law for \(\Theta^X_j[\,\cdot\,, v]\), for every datum \(v\). (i) For integral invertible matrices \(g_1\) of size \(n_j\) and \(g_2\) of size \(n_{j+1}\) at \(\Sigma_N\), with reductions \(\bar g_1\), \(\bar g_2\), and every function \(f\) of residue matrices, \(\mathrm{Rt}_{j+1}(g_2)\circ\Theta^X_j[T \mapsto f(\bar g_2T\bar g_1), v]\circ\mathrm{Rt}_j(g_1) = \Theta^X_j[f, v]\). (ii) \(\mathrm{Rt}_{j+1}(\mathrm{diag}_N^{-1})\circ\Theta^X_j[\mathrm{row}, v] = \varepsilon'_j\,\Theta^X_j[\mathrm{dir}, v]\circ\mathrm{Rt}_j(\mathrm{diag}_N^{-1})\), where \(\mathrm{row}\) is the indicator of the integral matrices whose last row reduces to \(e_{\mathrm{last}}\), and \(\varepsilon'_j\) (in this chapter) is the value at \(\mathrm{diag}_N\) on \(X_j\) of the character \(g \mapsto \chi(\det g)\lvert\det g\rvert^{-n_{j+1}/2}\), divided by the value at \(\mathrm{diag}_N\) on \(X_{j+1}\) of \(g \mapsto \chi(\det g)\lvert\det g\rvert^{-n_j/2}\), with \(\chi\) the base change to \(E\) of the character of the lift \(X_j \to X_{j+1}\). Here and below \(\lvert\det g\rvert := \prod_{w \in \Sigma_N}\lvert\det g_w\rvert_w\), with \(\lvert N\rvert_w = N^{-1}\), and \(\chi(\det g)\) reads \(\det g\) as an idele.

Problem 65.2.2. Let \(w \in \Sigma_N\) and let \(f\) be a function of residue matrices with \(f(cT) = f(T)\) for every unit \(c \in \prod_{\Sigma_N}{\mathbb{F}}_N^\times\) equal to \(1\) off \(w\). Then \(\Theta^X_j[f, v]\varphi = 0\) for every \(\varphi\) ramified at \(\Sigma_N\). In particular \(\Theta^X_j[\mathrm{col}_y, v]\varphi = 0\) if \(y\) vanishes at some place.

Let \(\mathrm{ch}_y(T) := \psi_N\bigl((\text{last row of } T)\cdot y\bigr)\) on integral matrices, \(0\) elsewhere.

Problem 65.2.3. \(\mathrm{GL}_n({\mathbb{F}}_N)\) is transitive on \({\mathbb{F}}_N^n \smallsetminus 0\), a set of \(N^n - 1\) elements; so there are \(\#\mathrm{Prim}_n = (N^n-1)^f\) primitive residue vectors in rank \(n\), a number prime to \(N\). For \(\varphi\) ramified at \(\Sigma_N\) and data \(v, v'\), \[\sum_y \Theta^X_{j+1}[\mathrm{ch}_y, v']\,\Theta^X_j[\mathrm{col}_y, v]\,\varphi = \#\mathrm{Prim}_{n_{j+1}}\cdot\Theta^X_{j+1}[\mathrm{ch}_{e_{\mathrm{last}}}, v']\,\Theta^X_j[\mathrm{dir}, v]\,\varphi ,\] the sum over all residue vectors on \(X_{j+1}\). For \(N = 3\), \(n = 2\), \(f = 1\): eight vectors, one orbit.

All lifts become direct. Two further statements are read from the proof. First, on data fixed by the projection on the vacuum above \(N\), the double lift \(X_{2i} \to X_{2i+2}\) with the glued datum \(\mathrm{Gl}_i(v \otimes v')\) is \(\varepsilon_i\,\mathrm{Rt}_{2i+2}(\mathrm{diag}_N^{-1})\sum_y\Theta^X_{2i+1}[\mathrm{ch}_y, v']\,\Theta^X_{2i}[\mathrm{col}_y, v]\), where \(\varepsilon_i\) (in this chapter) is the value of the character of the lift \(X_{2i+1} \to X_{2i+2}\), formed with \(\lvert\det\rvert^{-n_{2i+1}/2}\), at \(\mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,N)\) on \(X_{2i+2}\) at the places of \(\Sigma_N\) prime to \(\pi_N\), times the value of the character of \(X_{2i} \to X_{2i+1}\), also formed with \(\lvert\det\rvert^{-n_{2i+1}/2}\), at the same matrix on \(X_{2i}\) at the places dividing \(\pi_N\). Second, the following, whose proof sorts the terms of \(\Theta^X_{2i+1}[\mathrm{ch}_{e_{\mathrm{last}}}, v']\) by the last row and uses the lift law: there is \(c_{\mathrm{dir}}\in {\mathbb{C}}\) (in this chapter) such that for all data \(v_j\) and all \(\varphi \in S_\mathrm{ram}\), the chain of the \(m/2\) double lifts with the glued data of the projections of the \(v_j\) on the vacuum above \(N\) sends \(\varphi\) to \(c_{\mathrm{dir}}\) times the chain of the \(m\) direct lifts with data \(v_j\) applied to \(\mathrm{Up}^{m/2}\varphi\). Here \[c_{\mathrm{dir}}= \prod_{i < m/2}\Bigl(\varepsilon_i\,\#\mathrm{Prim}_{n_{2i+1}}\prod_{l < 2i+2}\varepsilon'_l\Bigr) .\] Status: read from the proof. Problems 65.2.2 and 65.2.3 are its first steps. We also read: for \(g\) equal to \(\mathrm{diag}(1, \dots, 1, N)\) at a set \(\Sigma'\subseteq\Sigma_N\) and \(1\) at the other places of \(\Sigma_N\), the value at \(g\) of the character of any of the lifts, formed with \(\lvert\det\rvert^{-r/2}\) for any \(r \ge 0\), has \(N\)-adic absolute value at most \(N^{2b\,\#\Sigma'}\) after \(\eta\).

Problem 65.2.4. \(\lvert\eta(\varepsilon_i)\rvert \le N^{2fb}\), \(\lvert\eta(\varepsilon'_l)\rvert \le 1\) and \(\lvert\eta(\#\mathrm{Prim}_n)\rvert \le 1\); hence \(\lvert\eta(c_{\mathrm{dir}})\rvert \le N^{mfb}\).

§65.3. The colength of the forms integral after \(\mathrm{Up}^{m/2}\)

A form \(\varphi\) is integral at a point \(h\) if \(\lVert \varphi(g) \rVert_{gh} \le 1\) for every \(g\); so \(S_\eta\) is the module of source forms integral at \(1\). Write \(S_\eta(h)\) (in this chapter) for the \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-module of source forms integral at \(h\); so \(S_\eta = S_\eta(1)\). By Section 37.5 the values of source forms lie in \(\bigotimes_{\tau \in \Phi_a}\mathrm{Sym}^a{\mathbb{C}}^k\), whose basis is the families \(I = (z^{\alpha_\tau})_{\tau \in \Phi_a}\) of monomials of degree \(a\) in \(k\) variables; let \(e_I := \sum_\tau \alpha_{\tau,k}\) be the degree of \(I\) in the last variable.

Problem 65.3.1. Let \(k = a = 2\), one place, \(N = 3\). The monomials \(z_1^2, z_1z_2, z_2^2\) have degrees \(0, 1, 2\) in \(z_2\); the change of lattice multiplies them by \(\mathrm{diag}(1, 3, 9)\), of determinant \(3^3\), and the colength of the span of \(3^{\alpha_2}z^\alpha\) in the span of the \(z^\alpha\) over \({\mathbb{Z}}_3\) divides \(3^3\). The mean degree in \(z_2\) is \(1 = a/k\).

A source form is determined by its values at representatives of the double cosets of its level, and these values are arbitrary, since (read from the proof) the stabilizers of the double cosets are trivial. So the pairs \((c, I)\) of a double coset and a basis vector are \(D_X\) in number. Let \(D_{\mathrm{last}}:= \sum_{(c, I)} e_I\).

Problem 65.3.2. The degree in the \(u\)-th variable, summed over the monomials of degree \(a\) in \(k\) variables, does not depend on \(u\), and \(k\) times it is \(a\) times the number of these monomials.

Problem 65.3.3. \(k\,D_{\mathrm{last}}= \#\Phi_a\,a\,D_X \le fa\,D_X\).

Definition 65.3.1. \(S_N\) is the \(\mathcal O_\eta\)-module of the forms \(\varphi\) on \(X_0\) such that \(\mathrm{Up}^{m/2}\varphi\) is a source form integral at \(\delta_0\).

Used in: Chapter 57

Read from the proof: every double coset has a representative \(r_c\) such that \(r_c\delta_0^\vee\) and \(\delta_{0,\Sigma}^{-1}r_c\delta_0\) are integral above \(N\), that is, lie in \(\mathrm{GL}_{k}(\mathcal O_{E_w})\) at every place \(w\) above \(N\).

Problem 65.3.4. \(S_\eta \subseteq S_\eta(\delta_0^\vee)\). The coordinates of \(\varphi(r_c)\) are integral for \(\varphi \in S_\eta(\delta_0^\vee)\), and a source form whose coordinate \((c, I)\) at \(r_c\) is divisible by \(N^{e_I}\) for all \((c, I)\) lies in \(S_\eta(\delta_0)\). Hence the colength of \(S_\eta(\delta_0)\) in \(S_\eta(\delta_0^\vee)\) divides \(N^{D_{\mathrm{last}}}\).

Problem 65.3.5. \((\mathrm{Up}\varphi)(g) = \sum_y\psi_N(y_{\mathrm{last}})\,\varphi(g\,\delta_0^\vee\mathfrak g_y\delta_0^{-1})\); hence \(\mathrm{Up}\) maps \(S_\eta(\delta_0)\) into forms integral at \(\delta_0^\vee\). As \(\mathrm{Up}\) preserves \(S\) (read from the proof), the colength in \(S_\eta\) of the forms \(\varphi\) with \(\mathrm{Up}^i\varphi \in S_\eta(\delta_0)\) divides \(N^{(i+1)D_{\mathrm{last}}}\), for every \(i \ge 0\).

Problem 65.3.6. \((m/2 + 1)\,a \le m b k\), and so \((m/2 + 1)D_{\mathrm{last}}\le mfb\,D_X\).

Problems 65.3.3, 65.3.5 with \(i = m/2\), and 65.3.6 prove part (a) of Theorem 65.4.1 below, with \(e = (m/2+1)D_{\mathrm{last}}\).

§65.4. Direct lifts lose little

The Fourier expansion of the direct datum has poles at \(N\), which an element of \(K\) must clear.

Problem 65.4.1. Let \(K = {\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-7})\), \(\theta_K = (1+\sqrt{-7})/2\), \(N = 11\). Then \(z^2 - z + 2\) has the roots \(5, 7\) modulo \(11\); with \(c_0 = 7\), \((1 - 2c_0)^2 \equiv -7\) and \((1 - 2c_0)\cdot 7 \equiv 8 \pmod{11}\). So \(\delta_K := \theta_K - c_0\) lies in the prime \(\mathfrak p = (11, \delta_K)\), and \(\bar{\delta}_K\cdot 7\) is a unit at \(\mathfrak p\).

Problem 65.4.2. Let \(\theta_K = (1 + \sqrt{-d})/2\), let \(c_0 \in {\mathbb{Z}}\) represent \(\theta_K\) modulo the prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\) below \(w_\tau\) (the same for all \(\tau\)), and \(\delta_K := \theta_K - c_0\). Then \(\delta_K \in w_\tau\) for every \(\tau\), and \(\lvert\eta(\bar{\delta}_K d)\rvert = 1\).

Read from the proof: for \(j < m\), every translation \(\mathfrak t\) and every \(\delta_K \in \mathcal O_K\) lying in all the \(w_\tau\), the datum \(N^{2fn_jn_{j+1}}\,\mathrm{Sch}(\mathrm{dir})v_j(\mathfrak t)\) is standard of denominator \(\delta_K\); the translates of the vacuum in the expansion of \(\mathrm{dir}\) have simple poles at the \(w_\tau\). Recall from Section 59.4 that the kernel of the lift \(X_j \to X_{j+1}\) is the theta kernel at scale \(\pi_Mc_{\mathrm{jet}}\) and \(\lvert\eta\pi_M\rvert \le 1\). A datum multiplies gauges from \(\delta_j\) to \(\delta_{j+1}\) by at most \(c\) if the inequality of Definition 59.4.2 holds with \(\lVert \cdot \rVert_{g'\delta_{j+1}}\) and \(\lVert \cdot \rVert_{g\delta_j}\) in place of \(\lVert \cdot \rVert_{g'}\) and \(\lVert \cdot \rVert_g\).

Problem 65.4.3. Let \(0 \ne \delta_K \in \mathcal O_K\) with \(\lvert\eta\pi_M\rvert \le \lvert\eta(\bar{\delta}_K d)\rvert\). Every standard datum of denominator \(\delta_K\) multiplies the gauges of the kernel of the lift \(X_j \to X_{j+1}\) by at most \(1\). Hence, for \(j < m\), the datum of the direct lift \(j\) with \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) multiplies gauges from \(\delta_j\) to \(\delta_{j+1}\) by at most \(N^{2fn_jn_{j+1}} \le N^{fb}\).

Three more statements are read from the proof. For \(\mathfrak t\) in the support of a chain datum, \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) is fixed by the projection on the vacuum above \(N\). The direct datum commutes with the action of the levels of \(X_j\) and \(X_{j+1}\); so for a chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\), if every \(\mathrm{Sch}(\mathrm{dir})v_j(\mathfrak t)\), translated by \((\delta_j, \delta_{j+1})\), multiplies gauges from \(\delta_j\) to \(\delta_{j+1}\) by at most \(N^{r_j}\), then \(\sum_{\mathfrak t}\Psi(\mathfrak t)\) times the chain of the \(m\) direct lifts with data \(v_j(\mathfrak t)\) maps source forms integral at \(\delta_0\) to forms with \(\lVert \cdot(g) \rVert_{g\delta_m} \le N^{r_0 + \dots + r_{m-1}}\) for every \(g\), as in Problem 57.3.1. And if a form on \(X_m\) satisfies \(\lVert \varphi(g) \rVert_{g\delta_m} \le C\) for every \(g\), then its restriction \(\mathrm{res}\,\varphi\) to \(Y_m\) has valuation at least \(-\log_N C\): the branching map is integral, and its scalar has the absolute value of the determinant factor of \(\delta_m\). The branching rule is classical [GW, Thm. 8.1.1]; the integrality of the branching map and the rest are read from the proof.

Problem 65.4.4. Let \(\Psi\) be a chain datum for a twist \(\vartheta\) and \(\varphi \in S_\mathrm{ram}\cap S_N\). Then \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi = c_{\mathrm{dir}}\,\mathrm{res}\bigl(\sum_{\mathfrak t}\Psi(\mathfrak t)\,(\text{chain of direct lifts with } v_j(\mathfrak t))(\mathrm{Up}^{m/2}\varphi)\bigr)\), and it has valuation at least \(-2mfb\). Moreover \(2mfb\log N \le 4fa(\log b)^2\log x_1\).

Problems 65.2.4, 65.4.3 and 65.4.4 prove part (b).

Theorem 65.4.1. Let \(\eta\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}_N\) be a ring map.

  1. There is an integer \(0 \le e \le mfb\,D_X\) such that the colength of \(S_N\) in \(S_\eta\) divides \(N^e\).

  2. For every twist \(\vartheta\), every chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) and every \(\varphi \in S_\mathrm{ram}\cap S_N\), the form \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) has valuation at least \(-2mfb\).
