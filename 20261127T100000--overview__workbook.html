§1. The proof in one paragraph Tag 23E6

An upper bound for one sum over primes excludes the zeros of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions in the critical strip off the line \(\Re s = 1/2\). The bound comes from comparing the size of a determinant with its divisibility, as in Chebyshev’s estimates for primes. The determinant is a Gram determinant of automorphic forms after a chain of \(m\) theta lifts, where \(m \approx \sqrt{x}\) and \(x\) is the cutoff in the prime sum. Its entries involve derivatives of theta functions with complex multiplication by \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\). These derivatives are highly divisible at the primes that do not split in \(K\), and this is how primes enter. The remainder of the prime sum occurs once for each lift, \(m\) times in all, and the proof bounds the total of the errors for the whole chain. Dividing by \(m\) gives the exponent \(-1/2\).

§2. From zeros to a sum over primes Tag 23E7

Let \(q_0\) be a multiple of \(18480\), let \(d\) be a prime with \(d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \pmod{q_0}\), and let \(\chi_K\) be the quadratic character of \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\). It is completely multiplicative, and at a prime \(p\) its value is \(1\), \(-1\) or \(0\) according as \(p\) splits, is inert, or ramifies in \(K\). For \(y \ge 1\) let \[R_{q_0}(y) := \sum_{\substack{n \le y \\ n \equiv 1 \, (q_0)}} \Lambda(n) (1 - \chi_K(n)) \Bigl( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{y} \Bigr) - \frac{\log y}{\varphi(q_0)} - c ,\] where \(\Lambda\) is the von Mangoldt function, \(\varphi\) is Euler’s totient, and \(c = c_{d,q_0}\) is the constant of Chapter 1. By Problem 1.4.7, \(R_{q_0}(y) \to 0\) as \(y \to \infty\). The target is the one-sided bound \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:target}\tag{1} R_{q_0}(y) \le y^{-1/2 + \varepsilon} \quad \text{for all large } y, \end{equation}\] for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) and every such \(d\).

Why does an upper bound suffice? The Mellin transform of \(R_{q_0}\) can have poles at the zeros of the \(L(s, \psi)\), with \(\psi\) modulo \(q_0\), and at the zeros of their twists \(L(s, \psi\chi_K)\), whose multiplicities enter with opposite signs. Character orthogonality gives weights \(\overline{\psi(r_0)}\) in a reduced class \(r_0\), so in class \(1\) all weights are \(1\) and the multiplicities within each family add. Given an untwisted zero \(\rho\) with \(1/2 < \Re \rho < 1\), the theorem of Chapter 2 (Inert primes and the choice of \(d\)) supplies a prime \(d\) in the prescribed class for which every twist is zero-free in a region containing \(\rho\) and the interval \([\Re \rho, 1]\). At \(\rho\) this leaves a nonzero residue from the untwisted zeros. If (1) holds, the function \(y^{-1/2+\varepsilon} - R_{q_0}(y)\) is eventually nonnegative. For \(\varepsilon\) small enough, its transform still has the pole at \(\rho\). Landau’s theorem forces a real singularity at or to the right of \(\Re \rho\). The only possible real poles there lie in \([\Re \rho,1)\), where the absence of twisted zeros gives negative residues, contrary to eventual nonnegativity.

For a character of modulus \(q\) one takes \(q_0 = 18480q\). See Chapter 1 (From zeros to a one-sided bound). The extra Euler factors of an induced character do not vanish in the open strip. The functional equation for primitive characters sends a zero \(s\) there to a zero \(1-s\) for the conjugate character, so nonvanishing to the right of the critical line forces the zeros in the strip onto the line (Problem 38.3.8).

The weight \(1 - \chi_K(n)\) vanishes at the powers of the primes that split in \(K\). For the other primes, the sum of the weights of the powers \(p^h\), each divided by \(p^h\) as in \(R_{q_0}\), is as follows.

§3. Size against divisibility Tag 23E9

A nonzero integer divisible by a positive integer \(D\) has absolute value at least \(D\). Chebyshev applied this to \(\binom{2n}{n}\), which is at most \(4^n\) and divisible by every prime in \((n, 2n]\).

Tag 23EA

Problem 3.1. For every integer \(n \ge 1\), \(\sum_{n < p \le 2n} \log p \le n \log 4\).

The proof applies the same principle to derivatives of theta functions. The model is \(u!\). Its logarithm is \(u \log u - u + O(\log u)\), and Legendre’s formula \(\log u! = \sum_{n \le u} \Lambda(n) \lfloor u/n \rfloor\) gives its divisibility: the prime power \(n\) contributes about \(u/n\).

The theta functions of the proof have complex multiplication by \(K\) and variables indexed by the infinite places \(\tau\) of a CM field \(E \supseteq K\). Each place has a weight \(\kappa(\tau)\), a nonnegative integer that sets the degree of differentiation in the variables at \(\tau\), and at some places there are extra derivatives of a large order \(u\). The sizes of such derivatives are governed by factorials. Their divisibility comes from Frobenius at the primes that do not split in \(K\). A power \(n\) of such a prime relates a place \(\tau\) to a second place \([-n]\tau\), its partner: for \(n = p^e\) with \(p\) inert and \(e\) odd, the partner is the image of \(\tau\) under \(\mathrm{Frob}_p^e\). The minus sign appears because \(\mathrm{Frob}_p\) is complex conjugation times the element \([-p]\) of \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\), and complex conjugation fixes every place (Chapter 3). The bound for the divisibility of the squared lengths of the derivatives of order \(u\) at \(\tau\) is, on a logarithmic scale and up to smaller losses, \[\sum_{n} \Lambda(n) (1 - \chi_K(n)) \Bigl( \frac{u}{n} - \kappa([-n]\tau) \Bigr)_+ ,\] where \(t_+ := \max(t, 0)\). The term \(u/n\) is the gain at \(n\), as for \(u!\). Frobenius couples \(n\) degrees at \(\tau\) with one degree at the partner, which has \(\kappa([-n]\tau)\) degrees; the positive part is the excess left uncoupled. For orders near the weights, the sharpness result in Part IX gives test data on twelve copies and a lower bound for the \(p\)-adic absolute value of a selected normalized Taylor coefficient. See Chapter 13 (Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime), Chapter 15 (The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime) and Chapter 19 (The bound is attained).

The subtraction produces a cutoff. If the order is \(a := xb\) and the partner has weight \(b\), only \(n \le x\) contribute, each with \(a\) times the factor \(1/n - 1/x\) of the sum defining \(R_{q_0}(x)\):

Tag 23EB

Problem 3.2. Let \(x, b, n > 0\) and \(a = xb\). Then \((a/n - b)_+ = a (1/n - 1/x)_+\).

The weight \(1 - \chi_K(n)\) is \(2\) at the odd powers of an inert prime: this factor of two is the one that passes from lengths to squared lengths. Exponents of primes are integers, so the proof rounds down, once for each prime; the total cost is at most \(b = a/x\) in the logarithm of the scaling factor, hence \(2b\) for squared lengths.

If every partner had weight \(b\), the sum would be \(a (\log x + O(1))\), by Mertens’ estimate \(\sum_{n \le y} \Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_K(n))/n = \log y + O(1)\); more precisely, it would be an explicit main term plus \(a\) times a remainder of the kind bounded in (1). Comparing divisibility with size bounds the remainder from above. Chapter 7 (Stepanov without multiplicity: a determinant against Siegel zeros) gives models for this comparison with one determinant.

§4. The chain Tag 23EC

To make the remainder occur \(m\) times, the proof composes \(m\) lifts. A theta lift is a map between spaces of automorphic forms on two unitary groups whose kernel is a theta function. The lifts of the proof pass through unitary groups of increasing rank, the last of them definite, and the proof takes Gram determinants of the images of a family of forms. Each lift contributes the divisibility above. The outcome is \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:main}\tag{2} m \cdot \mathrm{rem}\le f a (\log b)^5 , \end{equation}\] with the following notation. The scales are \(x < x_1 < b\), and \(a = xb\). The field \(E\) is cyclotomic over \(K\), with \(f\) infinite places. The data specify a subset \(\Phi_b\) of the places of class \(-1\) modulo \(q_0\). The weight is \(b\) on \(\Phi_b\) and \(a/x_1\) on the other places. The places of class \(1\) modulo \(q_0\) form \(\Phi_a\), where the extra derivatives have order \(a\). For each \(n \equiv 1\) modulo \(q_0\), exactly \(\lvert \Phi_b \rvert\) of the places \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) have their partner in \(\Phi_b\), with cutoff \(x\); for all other pairs \((n, \tau)\) the cutoff is \(x_1\). The quantity \(\mathrm{rem}\) is the sum over \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) of the divisibility sums of order \(a\), less their main term, and the count gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:rem}\tag{3} \frac{\mathrm{rem}}{a} = \lvert \Phi_b \rvert \bigl( R_{q_0}(x) - R_{q_0}(x_1) \bigr) + \lvert \Phi_a \rvert R_1(x_1) , \end{equation}\] where \(R_1\) is the remainder with modulus \(1\). The second cutoff \(x_1\) lets the progression constant cancel in the difference (Problem 3.2.6). The CM period and Stirling’s formula give the constant of the unrestricted sum on the size side, and the level at the prime \(N\) pays for the remaining excess up to the allowed error (Chapter 66, Problems 66.1.3 and 66.1.4).

The prime number theorem in progressions bounds the remainders at \(x_1\). The existence theorem in Chapter 36 (The data) lets us take \(\log b\) at most a small power of \(x\), so (2) bounds \(R_{q_0}(x)\) by a small power of \(x\) divided by \(m\). See Chapter 3 (The places, the weights and the remainder) and Chapter 38 (The main inequality).

The costs of the chain limit \(m\): two of them, relative to what is available, are of order \(m^2/x\) up to powers of \(\log b\), and the proof takes \(\sqrt{x} \le m < 2\sqrt{x}\). See Chapter 8 (The chain and the square root), which starts from the following model.

§5. Three theorems Tag 23EE

The inequality (2) follows from three statements. They concern a space \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) of forms on a definite unitary group, the source of the translates (Section 37.5); a space \(T\) of dimension \(d_T\) of forms on the first group of the chain, the target; maps \(S_{\mathrm{ram}} \to T\), the translates of one map defined by Poincaré series; and the chain on \(T\). The forms have integral structures, and the chain can be twisted by a character; the trivial twist is the chain itself. These objects are defined in Chapter 37 (Spaces, forms and twists). The quadratic character \(\chi_K\) in the prime sum is fixed with \(d\) as \(x\) grows. Theorem C chooses a tame twist of the chain for each isomorphism \(\mathbb{C} \simeq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) at each prime \(\ell\), to control projection denominators and Hecke \(L\)-value ratios.

Theorem A: short forms. The integral forms in \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) whose images in \(T\) have short lifts span \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), and families of images of integral forms have bounded index: a change of columns of bounded determinant makes them integral. Let \(H\) denote the main amount \(h_{\mathrm{main}}\) of Chapter 36 and set \(F := f a (\log b)^4\). Here “short” means that for every translate and every conjugate the squared length of the lift is at most \(\exp(H - m \cdot \mathrm{rem}+ F)\). The divisibility of the theta derivatives has been divided out. The chain on \(T\) starts on an indefinite group. A seesaw identity expresses its composition with the maps \(S_{\mathrm{ram}} \to T\), up to a scalar, through a chain of lifts between definite unitary groups followed by a restriction. The kernels of the definite lifts are derivatives of CM theta functions, whose size and divisibility can be computed. See Chapter 49 (Theorem A: short forms).

Theorem B: enough forms. The images of \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) under a few translates span a subspace of \(T\) of codimension at most \(4 d_T/b^2\). The proof is a second moment estimate for the Poincaré series: on average the translates are nearly orthogonal, so, as long as much of \(T\) is missing, some translate reduces the missing dimension by a fixed proportion. The proof also shows that \(d_T > 0\) and bounds \(d_T\) from above; the bound uses the equal multiplicities of Part IX. See Chapter 9 (Few translates span) and Chapter 39 (Theorem B: enough forms).

Theorem C: the lower bound. For an independent family in \(T\) spanning a subspace of codimension at most \(4 d_T/b^2\), let \(c\) bound the logarithm of the Gram determinant of the lifts at every conjugate of the coordinates, and let \(\delta\) bound the logarithm of the index. Then \(c + 2\delta \ge d_T (H - 3F)\). This replaces “a nonzero integer has absolute value at least \(1\)”. Integrality is not preserved exactly along the chain: projections onto images of lifts have denominators, at primes where forms are congruent. The family is completed to a basis of \(T\) by auxiliary forms whose total cost is small because the codimension is small. The product formula is applied to the untwisted Gram determinant of this basis, and the twisted determinants bound its local terms. Sizes are read at the trivial twist. At each isomorphism above a prime \(\ell\), a target form is integral if its lift is integral at every twist allowed at \(\ell\). To estimate the determinant valuation there, C uses the twist indexed by that isomorphism in the family supplied by Chapter 85. The twist may vary because the untwisted and twisted Gram determinants of this basis differ by a scalar, the ratio of the determinants of the two chains. This ratio is the product of the ratios for the single lifts, with a correction from the conductor of the twist and Hecke \(L\)-values. The \(L\)-value product has one factor for each pair of lifts. The ratio for one lift is bounded using forms for which the divisibility is attained. These forms also account for \(H\). Besides the derivatives of large order, each lift has derivatives of the orders given by the weights, and their size exceeds their guaranteed divisibility. A pays this excess, \(H\) in all, in the length; the forms that attain the divisibility return it in the lower bound. Only the remainder from the derivatives of large order is left. See Chapter 6 (A twist for each prime), Chapter 67 (Theorem C), Chapter 68 (The determinant of a chain), Chapter 74 (The cost of one lift) and Chapter 85 (Twists with few congruences).

By B and A there are \(n \ge d_T/2\) independent vectors in \(T\), images of integral forms with short lifts, spanning a subspace of codimension at most \(4 d_T/b^2\). Let \(F' := f a (\log b)^3 \log(1 + 2b^2)\). By Hadamard’s inequality (Problem 5.1), A gives the bound \(c := n (H - m \cdot \mathrm{rem}+ F)\) for the logarithm of the Gram determinant of the lifts, at every conjugate. A bounds the index for each translate and B the number of translates; together they bound the logarithm of the index by \(\delta := 10 d_T F'\). The index enters twice, once for each side of the Gram pairing (Problem 5.2).

Since \(H \ge 0\) (Problem 36.4.6), \(F \ge 0\) and \(n \le d_T\), we may replace \(n(H+F)\) by \(d_T(H+F)\); in the other direction, C uses \(n \ge (1 - 4/b^2)d_T\), and \(n \ge d_T/2\) suffices for Problem 5.3. We obtain \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:compare}\tag{4} \begin{aligned} d_T (H - 3F) &\overset{\mathrm{C}}{\le} c + 2\delta \\ &\overset{\mathrm{A, B}}{\le} d_T (H + F) - n m \, \mathrm{rem}+ 20 \, d_T F' . \end{aligned} \end{equation}\] Subtracting \(d_T H\) leaves (2) (Problem 5.3), and (2) gives the target at integers (Problem 5.4). The passage from integers to real \(y\) is in Chapter 38 (The main inequality).

§7. Where the difficulty is Tag 23EL

The comparison and the passage from (2) to (1) are elementary. The difficulty is in the three theorems, in particular in the following.

  1. In C, the twist chosen for each isomorphism above a prime must control the projection denominators and the ratios of Hecke \(L\)-values for all \(m\) lifts.

  2. In B, a bound for the second moment of the right order of magnitude is not enough: it must nearly equal the bound that the dimension of \(T\) imposes.

  3. In A and C, the divisibility of the derivatives must be bounded in several variables at once, and there must be forms for which the bound is attained.

  4. In C, the identities between lifts that factor the determinant of the chain.

The identities between lifts that the proof uses are collected in the following table. “Hecke ratio” means a ratio of values of Hecke \(L\)-functions; the moduli of the scalars are determined up to positive constants and powers of the norm of the conductor of the twist.

§9. Notation Tag 23EN

Each sheet defines the notation it uses; the following conventions are common to all sheets.

\(q_0,d,K,\chi_K\)

\(q_0\) and \(d\) are the parameters and \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), with character \(\chi_K\).

\(E,f,\Phi_a,\Phi_b,\kappa(\tau)\)

\(E\) is the CM field of the data, with \(f\) infinite places, among them the \(a\)-places \(\Phi_a\) and the \(b\)-places \(\Phi_b\), and \(\kappa(\tau)\) is the weight of a place.

\(x,x_1,b,a\)

The scales are \(x < x_1 < b\), with \(a = xb\).

\(m,r,k,t\)

\(m\) is the number of lifts, \(r\) and \(k = r + 1 - m\) are the ranks of the target and of the source, and \(t = 5r\): the ranks of the spaces \(Y_j\) increase by \(t\), those of the \(X_j\) by \(t + 1\).

\(M,N\)

At the primes \(p_0,p_1\) of \(M=p_0p_1\), the principal level imposes identity reduction at the chosen places (Section 37.4). The source level also imposes a last-row condition above \(N\) (Section 37.5).

\(h_{\mathrm{main}},\mathrm{rem},U\)

The main amount is \(h_{\mathrm{main}} = mk\,h_{\mathrm{dir}}\), the remainder is \(\mathrm{rem}\), and \(U = fa\log^3 b\) is the unit of the errors (Section 36.4).

\(\vartheta,\theta\)

A twist (Section 37.6) is \(\vartheta\); a family of twists \(\theta = (\theta_\iota)\) has one member for each isomorphism \(\iota\colon \mathbb{C} \simeq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\).

\(S,S_{\mathrm{ram}},T,d_T\)

\(S \supseteq S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) are the source forms and the ramified ones, \(T\) the target forms, of dimension \(d_T\).

\(\beta,\operatorname{val}\)

The diagram \(\beta\) counts columns by height. The counts and the last entry specify the weight (Chapter 37). The symbol \(\operatorname{val}\) denotes the valuation with \(\operatorname{val}(p) = 1\) after a ring map to \(\mathbb{C}_p\).

\(R_{d,q}\)

The remainder \(R_{d,q}\) of the prime sum is as in Chapter 1 (From zeros to a one-sided bound).

The numbers are defined in Chapter 36 (The data), the spaces, forms and twists in Chapter 37 (Spaces, forms and twists); a chapter that uses one of these letters otherwise says so.
