Two steps of the proof use the same formula for a matrix coefficient at the real places. To form the full Bessel functions of Theorem 40.3.1, the proof shifts the coefficient, read in the sign frame (the infinite-place basis of Section 37.3, in which the Gram matrix is diagonal with the prescribed signs), by a chosen position. At each subgroup element it multiplies the shifted coefficient by the inverse subgroup action on a fixed unit vector, including the inverse Bessel character. It averages over the Bessel subgroup and scales the result. The proof of Theorem 70.3.3 uses conjugacy-class integrals of the corresponding kernels to compare Hecke traces for spaces with different real signatures. Here we state the inputs for both steps and work out the coefficient and its integrals for two-by-two matrices.

Prerequisites.

Schur orthogonality for compact groups and Weyl’s character formula for \(U(n)\). Problem 10.5.5 gives the formal degree on the upper half-plane with hyperbolic area. For the comparison we use the framed spaces, standard lattice stabilizers and principal forms from Sections 37.3 and 37.4. In this chapter \(K\) denotes a subgroup. We use inner products conjugate-linear in the first argument, as in the definition of the Petersson product in Chapter 37.

§41.1. One real group Tag 23CK

In this chapter let \(n\ge 1\) and let \(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_i)_{i=1}^n\) have \(p\) plus and \(q\) minus signs. Put \(J=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon)\), \(G=U(J)\) and \(K=G\cap U(n)\cong U(p)\times U(q)\). Let \(l\) have distinct integer entries congruent to \(n-1\) modulo \(2\), and put \(\lambda=l/2\). The positive roots are the coordinate differences \(e_i-e_j\) for \(l_i>l_j\), with \(e_i\) the \(i\)-th coordinate vector. Write \(\rho\) for their half sum and \(\rho_c\) for the half sum over \(\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon_j\). For a nonnegative integer \(h\), we say that \(l\) has gap at least \(h\) if \(|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\ge h\) for every opposite-sign pair.

Tag 23CL

Theorem 41.1 (Discrete series). There is an irreducible unitary representation \(\pi_l\) of \(G\) with square-integrable matrix coefficients and Harish-Chandra parameter \(\lambda\). In the restriction to \(K\), the irreducible constituent of highest weight \(\Lambda=\lambda+\rho-2\rho_c\) occurs once and is called the lowest \(K\)-type. The other constituents have highest weights obtained from \(\Lambda\) by adding positive roots. For a unit highest-weight vector \(u\) in that constituent, put \(\mathsf c_l(g)=\langle u,\pi_l(g)u\rangle\), so \(\mathsf c_l(1)=1\).

Used in: Chapter 42

Existence is classical [HC, Theorem 16]. For the \(K\)-types see [AS, Theorem (8.5)], [BV]. For an account of the parametrization see Knapp [Knapp]. The proof uses the integral construction in Theorem 41.3 for parameters of gap at least \(500n^3+10n^5\) in the trace comparison.

Write \(\mathrm{ch}_l\) for the character of the irreducible \(U(n)\)-representation of highest weight \(\lambda-\rho\), with the order on coordinates determined by \(l\). In the compact-dual Haar normalization, \(dg\) on \(G\) has the same density at the identity as probability Haar measure on \(U(n)\) when the tangent vectors are identified by \(X\mapsto JX\). Write \(dk\) for probability Haar measure on \(K\).

Tag 23CM

Theorem 41.2 (Schur orthogonality and formal degree). With \(d_l=(\int_G|\mathsf c_l(g)|^2\,dg)^{-1}\), for \(x,y\in G\) one has \[d_l\int_G \mathsf c_l(xgy)\overline{\mathsf c_l(g)}\,dg=\mathsf c_l(x)\mathsf c_l(y), \qquad d_l=\mathrm{ch}_l(1)= \frac{\prod_{l_i>l_j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j)}{\prod_{a=0}^{n-1}a!}.\]

Used in: Chapter 40; Chapter 42; Chapter 48

Both identities are classical. The degree uses the compact-dual Haar normalization [AS, Theorem (8.1)]. For the coefficient defined by the integral below, the proof establishes the Schur identity for gap at least \(500n^3\) and the degree formula for gap at least \(500n^3+10n^5\).

Tag 23CN

Theorem 41.3 (Flensted-Jensen’s formula). For an integer weight \(\mathbf w=(w_i)\) and an invertible matrix \(g\), let \(m_{\mathbf w}(g)\) be the product over integers \(s\) of the determinant of the submatrix on rows and columns \(\{i:w_i\ge s\}\), with each factor for \(s\le0\) divided by \(\det g\). The empty minor is \(1\) and only finitely many factors differ from \(1\). For \(g\in G\), keep the two same-sign diagonal blocks of \(g\) and set the other blocks to zero to obtain an invertible matrix \(D_g\). With \(z=D_g^{-1}g\), the integral formula is \[\mathsf c_l(g)= \frac{\displaystyle\int_K \frac{m_\Lambda(D_g k)\overline{m_\Lambda(k)}}{m_{-\lambda-\rho}(k^*zk)} \,dk} {\displaystyle\int_K |m_\Lambda(k)|^2\,dk},\] where \(k^*\) is the conjugate transpose.

Used in: Problem 41.1.3; Chapter 42

The proof defines the coefficient by the displayed integral. Its identification with the lowest-type coefficient is classical, by the construction of Flensted-Jensen and Sun [FJ; Sun].

Tag 23CP

Theorem 41.4 (Decay). If \(l\) has gap at least \(h\), then for each integer \(0\le c\le h\) and each \(g\in G\), \[|\mathsf c_l(g)|\le (2/\|g\|_{\mathrm{op}})^c ,\] with the Euclidean operator norm. In particular \(|\mathsf c_l(g)|\le 1\).

Used in: Chapter 42

The estimate with constant \(2\) is read from the proof. For asymptotic decay of discrete-series matrix coefficients see Miličić [Mi].

Tag 23CQ

Theorem 41.5 (Character on the diagonal torus). Let \(x=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) have distinct entries \(t_i\) of modulus one. In this chapter set \(\mathsf k_i=(l_i+n-1)/2\). Write \(A_K(\mathsf k)(x)\) for the alternating sum of the monomial \(\prod_i t_i^{\mathsf k_i}\) over permutations preserving the signs. The character \(\mathrm{Ch}_l\) of \(\pi_l\) on the regular diagonal torus satisfies \[\mathrm{ch}_l(1)\int_G\mathsf c_l(g^{-1}xg)\,dg = \mathrm{Ch}_l(x) = (-1)^{pq}\frac{A_K(\mathsf k)(x)}{\prod_{l_i>l_j}(t_i-t_j)} .\] Define the kernel \(\mathsf e_l=d_l\overline{\mathsf c_l}\). For gap at least \(500n^3+10n^5\) the proof gives \(d_l=\mathrm{ch}_l(1)\) for the integral coefficient. With \(x_\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(t_{\sigma(1)},\dots,t_{\sigma(n)})\) for each of the \(n!\) permutations \(\sigma\), the proof gives \[(-1)^{pq}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\int_G \mathsf e_l(g^{-1}x_\sigma g)\,dg =p!\,q!\,\overline{\mathrm{ch}_l(x)}.\] The classical single orbital integral converges absolutely. The proof gives absolute convergence for the integral coefficient at the gap of the sum.

Used in: Problem 41.1.8

The first identity uses the classical character and orbital-integral formulas, with the compact centralizer given mass one [AS, Theorem (8.1)], [Ar, formula (1)]. The sum over permutations is read from the proof.

The next problems check the degree in the compact case and then find the coefficient, measure and degree for \(U(1,1)\). The smallest weights give rank-one models of the large-gap identities.

Tag 23CR

Problem 41.1.1. For an integer \(j\ge 0\), the normalized coefficient of the vector \(e_1^{\otimes j}\) in \(\mathrm{Sym}^j\mathbb C^2\) is \(g_{11}^j\), with \(e_1=(1,0)\) and the inner product induced from tensor powers of \(\mathbb C^2\). For probability Haar measure on \(U(2)\), \(\int_{U(2)} |g_{11}|^{2j}\,dg=1/(j+1)\), equal to \(1/2\) at \(j=1\).

In the rank-one problems \(\mathsf a\ge1\) and \(\mathsf N\ge2\) are integers, and \(K\) is the diagonal torus of \(U(1,1)\). Use the same diagonal torus in \(U(2)\).

Tag 23CT

Problem 41.1.3. For the parameter in Problem 41.1.2, Theorem 41.3 gives \(\mathsf c_l(g)=\det(g)^{-\mathsf a}g_{22}^{-\mathsf N}\). The coefficient transforms on the left and the right under \(K\) by the character \((u_1,u_2)\mapsto u_1^{-\mathsf a}u_2^{-\mathsf a-\mathsf N}\).

Used in: Problem 41.1.5, Problem 41.1.7

In the chart of complex lines \([z:1]\), \(U(2)/K\) is the sphere and \(U(1,1)/K\) is the disk \(|z|<1\), by the action on the line \([0:1]\). The two invariant measures have densities proportional to \((1+|z|^2)^{-2}\) and \((1-|z|^2)^{-2}\), respectively, against Euclidean area. For the compact-dual normalization the two quotient densities agree at \(z=0\). Write \[a_t=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh t&\sinh t\\\sinh t&\cosh t\end{pmatrix}\qquad(t\ge0).\]

Problem 41.1.8. For signs \((+,-)\) and \(\mathsf k_1>\mathsf k_2\), write \(\mathrm{Ch}(t_1,t_2)=t_1^{\mathsf k_1}t_2^{\mathsf k_2}/(t_2-t_1)\) for the character in Theorem 41.5 at distinct unit entries. Then \[-\bigl(\mathrm{Ch}(t_1,t_2)+\mathrm{Ch}(t_2,t_1)\bigr) =\sum_{i=0}^{\mathsf k_1-\mathsf k_2-1} t_1^{\mathsf k_1-1-i}t_2^{\mathsf k_2+i}.\] The sum is the \(U(2)\)-character of highest weight \((\mathsf k_1-1,\mathsf k_2)\), and is \(t_1^2+t_1t_2+t_2^2\) at \((\mathsf k_1,\mathsf k_2)=(3,0)\). The local sign is \((-1)^{pq}=-1\). With one fixed signature at an even number \(f\) of infinite places, the product of the local signs is \(1\).

§41.2. The comparison Tag 23D3

Let \(E\) be a CM field with a fixed CM type and \(V\) a framed space of rank \(n\ge2\) as in Section 37.3. The frames identify the finite adelic unitary groups of all such spaces with the group \(G_f\) for the identity form. For each infinite place \(\tau\), choose a doubled parameter \(l_\tau\) for the signs of \(V\) of gap at least \(500n^3+10n^5\). Use the product of the corresponding kernels \(\mathsf e_{l_\tau}\) on the real factors.

Tag 23D4

Theorem 41.6 (Selberg principle). Let \(\phi\) be a locally constant compactly supported function on \(G_f\). For a rational unitary element \(\gamma\) of \(V\), integrate the product of the real kernels and \(\phi\), evaluated at the real and finite factors of \(g^{-1}\gamma g\), over the left quotient of the adelic group by the rational centralizer of \(\gamma\). Use the quotient of product Haar measure by counting measure on the rational centralizer. At infinity use the compact-dual measures, and on \(G_f\) give the standard lattice stabilizer mass one. The orbital integral is zero if the characteristic polynomial over \(E\) is irreducible and has a root off the unit circle under the embedding selected by the CM type at some infinite place.

The adelic statement is read from the proof. The local vanishing for discrete-series coefficients on regular nonelliptic classes is classical [Ar, formula (1)].

Tag 23D5

Theorem 41.7 (Comparison of traces). With \(E\), \(V\) and \(l_\tau\) as in the setup for this section, suppose that

  1. \(E\) contains the \(5040\)th roots of unity and has an even number of infinite places.

  2. \(V'\) is a framed space of the same rank over \(E\), and the parameters \(l_\tau\) meet the same gap condition for the signs of \(V'\).

  3. \(K_f\) is a subgroup of the integral elements of \(G_f\) containing a neighbourhood of the identity, and \(a\in\mathbb C[G_f]\) preserves \(K_f\)-fixed vectors in every representation of \(G_f\).

  4. The function \(\phi=a\star 1_{K_f}\) is cuspidal at a split place \(w\). Here \(\star 1_{K_f}\) sends each \(b\in G_f\) to \(1_{bK_f}\) and extends linearly. For cuspidality, let \(P\) be the coordinate projection onto any nonempty proper coordinate subset and let \(\bar b\in\mathrm{GL}_n(k_w)\), where \(k_w\) is the residue field. For each matrix \(A\in M_n(k_w)\) take any integral invertible lift \(u_A\) of \((1+PA(1-P))\bar b\), viewed in \(G_f\) with inverse conjugate transpose at the conjugate place and identity components elsewhere. For every choice of lifts and every \(g\in G_f\), the sum of \(\phi(gu_A)\) over \(A\) is zero.

Then the operators induced by \(a\) through right translation have equal traces on the spaces of forms for \(V\) and \(V'\). The spaces are defined as for principal forms in Section 37.4, using level \(K_f\) and the real kernels of the parameters \(l_\tau\) for the respective signs.

Used in: Chapter 70

The method is classical [AC; FK; Lab]; for the use of a cuspidal component see Harris–Labesse [HL, Proposition 1.10.5]. The statement with the discrete-series kernels and the split cuspidality condition above is read from the proof. Chapter 70 checks the comparison hypotheses for the good forms.

Tag 23D6

Theorem 41.8 (Landherr’s theorem). Hermitian spaces for a quadratic extension of number fields that are isometric at each completion of the base field are isometric over the extension.

The theorem is classical [Kn]. The proof establishes the case of framed spaces with the same numbered signs over a CM field containing the \(5040\)th roots of unity.

Theorem 41.2.9 (Bessel models). Use the Bessel subgroup and character of Section 39.1 at a real place. A Bessel functional is a continuous linear form on the completed tensor product of two smooth representations, equivariant for the generic Bessel character. The subgroup acts on the smaller representation through the quotient by its unipotent radical. For an irreducible pair of smooth admissible Fréchet representations of moderate growth with finite-length modules of \(K\)-finite vectors, the space of Bessel functionals has dimension at most one. For fixed tempered Langlands parameters for the two groups, exactly one pair across the relevant pure inner forms has a nonzero Bessel functional.

The statements are context from the literature [JSZ, Theorem A], [BP; Xue]. In codimension one for discrete series see also He [He]. In Chapter 42, Theorem 42.3.1 gives the lower bound for the Bessel period used in normalizing the real Bessel functions.
