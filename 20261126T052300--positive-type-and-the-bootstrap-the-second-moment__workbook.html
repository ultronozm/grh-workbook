§47.1. Positive type and singular minors

As in Chapter 39, a real function \(\Theta\) on a finite group \(G\) is of positive type if there are vectors \(u_a\), \(a\in G\), of a complex inner product space with \(\langle u_a,u_b\rangle=\Theta(a^{-1}b)\) for all \(a,b\). We say that a subset of \(G\) carries the sum of \(\Theta\) over that subset. By Problem 9.2.2, if \(\rho\) is a unitary representation of \(G\) and \(L\) a linear map into its space, then \(s\mapsto\lVert L^*\rho(s)L\rVert^2\) is of positive type, with \(u_a=\rho(a)LL^*\rho(a)^*\) in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.

Problem 47.1.1. Let \(\Theta\) be of positive type on \(G\), with vectors \(u_a\). (a) \(\Theta(s^{-1})=\Theta(s)\) and \(\lvert\Theta(s)\rvert\le\Theta(1)=\lVert u_a\rVert^2\) for all \(s,a\). (b) For a subgroup \(\Gamma'\subseteq G\), \(\sum_{s\in\Gamma'}\Theta(s)=\lVert\sum_{a\in\Gamma'}u_a\rVert^2/\#\Gamma'\ge0\), and the restriction of \(\Theta\) to \(\Gamma'\) is of positive type.

Let \(\psi\) be a nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb{F}_q\); vectors are columns, \(y^t\) is a transpose and \(g^{-t}\) the inverse transpose of \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\).

Problem 47.1.2. For a \(j\times j\) matrix \(Z\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\), \(\#\{y\in\mathbb{F}_q^j:y^tZ=0\}=q^{-j}\sum_{y,v\in\mathbb{F}_q^j}\psi(y^tZv)\), and this number is at least \(q\) if \(\det Z=0\).

The toy.

In this sheet let \(J\) be the permutation matrix exchanging \(e_1\) and \(e_3\) and fixing \(e_2\), the Gram matrix of the symmetric bilinear form \((x,x')\mapsto x^tJx'\) on \(\mathbb{F}_p^3\). Take \((e_1,e_2)\) as the Bessel tuple: \(e_1\) is isotropic and paired with \(e_3\). The only proper leading minor of the pairing matrix of \(s\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_p)\) is \(e_1^tJse_1=s_{31}\), so the open elements are those with \(s_{31}\ne0\). Here a span vector is a nonzero \(x\in\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2)\) with a choice of side. On one side the fixer consists of the \(s\) with \(sx=x\). On the other side \(s^{-t}(Jx)=Jx\), so \(s\) fixes the linear form \(x'\mapsto x^tJx'\). For \(x=e_1\) the latter condition is \(e_3^ts=e_3^t\).

Problem 47.1.3. By machine: in \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_p)\) the group, the set \(\{s_{31}=0\}\), the fixer of \(e_1\) and the fixer \(\{s:e_3^ts=e_3^t\}\) of the form \(e_3^t\) have \(168, 72, 24, 24\) elements for \(p=2\) and \(11232, 3456, 432, 432\) for \(p=3\). Both fixers lie in \(\{s_{31}=0\}\).

Used in: Problem 47.1.6

So a weight concentrated on a fixer lives entirely where the minor vanishes. The next two problems show that this is the only obstruction: a vanishing minor is detected by characters, and the character sums are bounded by the weight of stabilizers.

Problem 47.1.4. Let \(\pi\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\) be a homomorphism, \(\Theta\) of positive type on \(G\), \(y,v\in\mathbb{F}_q^n\) and \(Y\) real. If the \(s\) with \(\pi(s)v=v\), and the \(s\) with \(\pi(s)^{-t}y=y\), each carry at most \(Y\), then \[\Bigl\lvert\sum_{s\in G}\Theta(s)\,\psi\bigl(y^t\pi(s)v\bigr)\Bigr\rvert\le q^{n/2}Y .\]

Used in: Problem 47.1.5

Problem 47.1.5. Let \(\pi\) be as in Problem 47.1.4, \(\Theta\ge0\) of positive type, \(A_0,A_1\) two \(n\times j\) matrices over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) and \(Y\ge0\). Suppose that for every \(v\in\mathbb{F}_q^j\smallsetminus0\) the \(s\) with \(\pi(s)A_0v=A_0v\), and the \(s\) with \(\pi(s)^{-t}A_1v=A_1v\), each carry at most \(Y\). Then \[q\sum_{\det(A_1^t\pi(s)A_0)=0}\Theta(s)\le2\sum_{s\in G}\Theta(s)+q^{j}q^{n/2}\,Y .\]

Used in: Problem 47.1.6

Problem 47.1.6. In the toy take \(j=1\), \(A_0=e_1\), \(A_1=Je_1=e_3\), so that \(\det(A_1^tsA_0)=s_{31}\). (a) For \(\Theta\equiv1\) and \(Y\) the order of a fixer in Problem 47.1.3, Problem 47.1.5 reads \(144\le471.764\ldots\) for \(p=2\) and \(10368\le29198.213\ldots\) for \(p=3\). (b) For \(p=2\) the indicator \(\Theta\) of the fixer of \(e_1\) is of positive type; all of its mass \(24\) lies in \(\{s_{31}=0\}\), the least admissible \(Y\) is \(24\), and Problem 47.1.5 reads \(48\le183.764\ldots\).

For the uniform weight the elements with \(s_{31}=0\) are a proportion \(3/7\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)\); for the weight of (b) they are everything. The function \(s\mapsto\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\) is neither, and the proof never replaces its non-open part (where the minor condition in Definition 43.2.6 fails) by a proportion for the uniform measure. It chooses a subgroup on which stabilizers carry little, and then Problem 47.1.5 makes the singular part small. In the toy the lemma gains almost nothing: for \(p=2\) its right side \(471.7\ldots\) exceeds the trivial bound \(2\cdot168\), and for \(p=3\) it is \(29198.2\ldots\) against \(3\cdot11232=33696\). It gains when \(q^{j+n/2}\,Y\) is small against \(q\sum\Theta\); for the data this is Problem 47.2.1 with \(Y=b^{-6k}H\).

§47.2. The open part and the terminal fixer

We work as the proof does: with an admissible filling (Definition 39.4.3) over a CM field \(E\) with Weil data (Chapter 45); the data provide one, the group \(\bar G=\prod_{w\in\mathcal M}\mathrm{GL}_r(k_w)\), \(\mathcal M=\mathcal M_0\cup\mathcal M_1\) the chosen places above \(p_0\) and \(p_1\), a unitary representation \(\rho\) of \(\bar G\) on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, and a linear map \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) from the coordinate space with the block identity of Theorem 40.3.2. In this sheet \(\mathrm{en}(s):=\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\), formed with the full Bessel function. By Problem 40.4.5, \(\mathrm{en}\) is of positive type, with \(u_a=\rho(a){\mathcal P^\circ}{\mathcal P^\circ}{}^*\rho(a)^*\), and \(\mathrm{en}(s)\le\mathrm{en}(1)\). The proof also gives \(\mathrm{en}(1)=\lVert\mathcal K_1\rVert^2\le2D_X\) in this generality (see the remark), by Theorem 43.3.3 and Problems 40.4.3 and 40.4.4, whose hypotheses (\(\mathcal K_{st}=\mathcal K_s\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(t)\) for \(t\in\bar R_0\), and \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) a unitary representation of \(\bar R_0\)) it states for every admissible filling with the block identity. Of the conditions of admissibility we use: \(b\ge1000\); \(b^{10}\le p_0\le b^{11}\) and \(M=p_0p_1\le b^{23}\); \(1000(2\varrho+2)\le k\le f\) and \(\varrho+1\le b\); there are \(f\) chosen places above each \(p_i\), each with residue field of \(p_i\) elements, all distinct, and no conjugate of a chosen place is chosen; \(b^{11kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}\le D_{\mathrm{main}}\). The matrices \(s_w\) have size \(r=k+2\varrho+1\).

We recall Definitions 43.2.6, 43.2.7 and 43.2.5 at a chosen place \(w\) above \(p_0\). For a span vector \(x\), \(s\) fixes it on the side of \(w\) if \(s_wx=x\), and on the side of \(\bar w\) if \(s_w^{-t}\) fixes \(Jx\), where \(J\) is the Gram matrix of the lattice basis modulo \(w\). The fixer of span vectors given at a set \(\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal M_0\) of such places is the subgroup of the \(s\) fixing each. An element \(s\) is open if at some \(w\in\mathcal M_0\) the leading minors \(\langle s\rangle_1,\dots,\langle s\rangle_\varrho\) of its pairing matrix are nonzero. With \(A_0\) the matrix whose columns are the first \(j\) members \(v_1,\dots,v_j\) of the Bessel tuple and \(A_1=JA_0\), the minor \(\langle s\rangle_j\) at \(w\) is \(\det(A_1^ts_wA_0)\). An element meets at \(w\) (or at \(\bar w\)) if modulo that place the span of the Bessel tuple and that of its image share a nonzero vector, and \(\operatorname{mt}(s)\) is the product of \(\#k_w\) over the places \(w\) and \(\bar w\), \(w\in\mathcal M\), at which \(s\) meets; an element that fixes a span vector on a side meets at that place. An element of \(\bar R_0\) fixes \(v_1\) modulo every chosen place: \(\mathcal U\) and \(U(X_0)\) fix \(v_1\) (Section 39.1), and \(v_1\) is a vector of the lattice basis. For \(s\notin\bar R_0\) we have \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)=0\), and \(\mathrm{en}(s)\) is the second moment at \(s\) of the terms outside the Bessel subgroup.

Three statements are used, for sets \(\mathcal B\subseteq\bar G\). Theorem 43.3.1: if \(z_0\) is a positive integer with \(z_0\le\operatorname{mt}(s)\) for all \(s\in\mathcal B\), the link second moment of the terms with \(\operatorname{disc}\ne0\) over \(\mathcal B\) is at most \[(b^{2f}/z_0)^k\,D_{\mathrm{main}}\,\nu^2 .\] The truncation bound (Theorem 43.3.2) uses that link second moment, formed with Bessel functions truncated by length. If \(\mathcal B\) avoids the scalar cosets and that moment is at most \(C_0\nu^2\), \(C_0\ge0\), then the second moment over \(\mathcal B\) of the terms outside the Bessel subgroup, for the full Bessel function, has square root at most \[\sqrt{C_0}+b^{-15}\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{id}}}.\] Theorem 45.3.3: for a set \(\mathcal D\) of \(h\) chosen places above \(p_0\) with a span vector at each, the link second moment of the terms with \(\operatorname{disc}\ne0\) over the open elements of their fixer is at most \[(1+b^{-4})\,b^{-6kh}\,D_{\mathrm{main}}\,\nu^2 .\] In this sheet \(y_h:=b^{-6kh}D_{\mathrm{main}}\) for \(0\le h\le f\); since \(b^{11kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}\le D_{\mathrm{main}}\), we have \(b^{5kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}\le y_h\).

Problem 47.2.1. \(p_0^j\sqrt{p_0^{\,r}}\le b^{6k}\) for \(0\le j\le\varrho\).

Problem 47.2.2. Let \(\Gamma'\subseteq\bar G\) be a subgroup, \(H:=\sum_{s\in\Gamma'}\mathrm{en}(s)\) (in this sheet), and \(w\) a chosen place above \(p_0\). Suppose that for every span vector at \(w\), on either side, the elements of \(\Gamma'\) fixing it carry at most \(b^{-6k}H\). Then the elements of \(\Gamma'\) that are not open carry at most \(b^{-4}H\).

Problem 47.2.3. If \(s_wv_1\in k_wv_1\) at a chosen place \(w\) above \(p_0\), then \(\langle s\rangle_1=0\) at \(w\). Hence no \(s\in\bar G\) with \(s_w=\zeta_wt_w\) for every \(w\in\mathcal M\), for some \(t\in\bar R_0\) and some integral finite adele \(\zeta\) with residues \(\zeta_w\), is open; in particular the open elements avoid the scalar cosets and lie outside \(\bar R_0\).

Used in: Problem 47.2.5

Problem 47.2.4. Let \(\Gamma'\) be the fixer of span vectors at a set of \(h\) chosen places above \(p_0\). The open elements of \(\Gamma'\) carry at most \((1+4b^{-4})\,y_h\).

The terminal case fixes vectors at all \(f\) places above \(p_0\).

Problem 47.2.5. (a) \((b^{2f}/p_0^f)^k\,D_{\mathrm{main}}\le y_f/8\). (b) \(8M^fD_{\mathrm{id}}\le y_f\). (c) At most \(M^f\#\bar R_0\) elements of \(\bar G\) have the form \(s_w=\zeta_wt_w\) of Problem 47.2.3.

Problem 47.2.6. Let \(\Gamma'\) be the fixer of span vectors at all \(f\) chosen places above \(p_0\). Then \(\operatorname{mt}(s)\ge p_0^f\) for \(s\in\Gamma'\), and \(\Gamma'\) carries at most \(y_f\).

§47.3. The bootstrap

Problem 47.2.2 needs a subgroup on which stabilizers carry little, and fixing a vector costs a factor \(b^{-6k}\) in Problem 47.2.4. For a set \(\mathcal D\) of chosen places above \(p_0\) and span vectors at its places, let \(\Gamma_{\mathcal D}\) be their fixer (the vectors understood); \(\Gamma_\varnothing=\bar G\). The proof maximizes the objective \(b^{6k\#\mathcal D}\sum_{s\in\Gamma_{\mathcal D}}\mathrm{en}(s)\) over the finitely many choices, the empty one included.

Problem 47.3.1. In the toy with \(p=2\), \(\Theta\equiv1\), one place, and the factor \(b^{6k}\) replaced by \(c\): every span vector, on either side, has a fixer of \(24\) elements, and the objective at (no place, the place) is \((168,24c)\); it is \((168,96)\) for \(c=4\) and \((168,192)\) for \(c=8\). Either choice can be the maximum.

Problem 47.3.2. Let \(\mathcal D\) with its span vectors maximize the objective, \(h:=\#\mathcal D\) and \(H:=\sum_{s\in\Gamma_{\mathcal D}}\mathrm{en}(s)\). If \(h<f\) and \(w\notin\mathcal D\) is a chosen place above \(p_0\), then the elements of \(\Gamma_{\mathcal D}\) fixing a span vector at \(w\), on either side, carry at most \(b^{-6k}H\), and \[H\le(1+4b^{-4})\,y_h+b^{-4}H .\]

Used in: Problem 47.3.4

Problem 47.3.3. If \(H\le(1+4\delta)Y+\delta H\) with \(0\le\delta\le1/10\) and \(Y\ge0\), then \(H\le(1+9\delta)Y\). For \(\delta=1/16\): \((1+4\delta)/(1-\delta)=4/3\le25/16=1+9\delta\).

Problem 47.3.4. With \(\mathcal D\) as in Problem 47.3.2, \(H\le(1+9b^{-4})\,y_h\); hence \(\sum_{s\in\bar G}\mathrm{en}(s)\le(1+9b^{-4})\,D_{\mathrm{main}}\).

Problems 47.1.1, 47.1.2, 47.1.4, 47.1.5 and 47.2.1–47.3.4, with Theorems 43.3.1, 43.3.2 and 45.3.3 and the two properties of \(\mathrm{en}\) recalled in Section 47.2 (positive type, and \(\mathrm{en}(1)\le2D_X\)), prove the following.

Theorem 47.3.1 (the second moment). Let an admissible filling over a CM field with Weil data be given, \(\rho\) a unitary representation of \(\bar G\) on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, and \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) a linear map from the coordinate space satisfying the block identity of Theorem 40.3.2. Then \[\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\le(1+9b^{-4})\,D_{\mathrm{main}}.\] In particular this holds for the data, with \({\mathcal P^\circ}\) the scaled Poincaré map and \(\rho\) the representation of Chapter 39.

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 43; Chapter 45; Chapter 48

What has been proved is the second moment over the whole group, from the link bound and the truncation bound, which Chapter 43 reads from the proof, and the open-cell bound, whose steps Chapter 45 models with statements read from the proof. With the count of Theorem 48.3.1 it gives the hypothesis of Theorem 9.3.1 on the second moment (Problem 48.3.6); the other hypothesis, the near isometry of the projected map, is Theorem 48.2.2.
