We write \(\lvert y\rvert_\iota := \lvert\iota(y)\rvert_\ell\), \(\lvert\ell\rvert_\ell = 1/\ell\).

For a regular family \(\theta\) of \(m\)-fold twists (Definition 79.1.3) and \(1 \le j \le m\), Chapter 74 bounds the height of \(Z_j(1)/Z_j(\theta_\iota)\), and Problem 75.3.2 expresses this quotient through the compression determinants and the quotient \(\mathcal{H}_j(\vartheta)/\mathcal{H}_j(1)\) of products of Hecke values at \(\vartheta = \theta_\iota^j\). How can a ratio of \(L\)-values be bounded at every \(\iota \mid \ell\)? For a critical pair with \(z \ge 2\), the squared norm of a theta lift of a character from \(\mathrm U(1)\), divided by \((\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi)^{2z-1}\), is the partial Hecke value times a constant independent of the twist \(\xi\); an integer multiple of it is integral away from \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi\); and for a main pair the untwisted squared norm has bounded height. We begin with a lift between two lines over \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and with the local factors in rank four over \(\mathbb{F}_3\).

§77.1. A Hecke value as a squared norm

In this section only, \(c_a\), \(\vartheta_0\) and \(L(\bar\upsilon_n, s)\) have the meanings of Sections 23.2 and 23.3. By Problem 23.2.3 the lift \(\Theta_{(4)}\) between two lines is multiplication by \(c_4/4\), and Problem 23.3.4 gives \[\frac{c_4^2}{16} = 2100\,\vartheta_0^2\,(2\pi)^{-8}\,L(\bar\upsilon_8, 8) .\]

Problem 77.1.1. To the precision shown, \(c_4 \approx 0.14319133702\), \(\vartheta_0 \approx 1.18034059902\), \(L(\bar\upsilon_8, 8) \approx 1.06394325884\), and both sides of the display are \(\approx 0.00128148493742\).

This lift has rank one, which Theorem 77.4.1 excludes (it needs rank \(2z \ge 4\)); it shows only the shape of part (i), a squared norm equal to a fixed constant times a Hecke value. The size of the \(L\)-value itself is controlled by its Euler product, once the coefficients are unitary.

Problem 77.1.2. \(L(\bar\upsilon_8, 8) = \sum_{\mathfrak a} (\bar\alpha/\lvert\alpha\rvert)^8\, \operatorname{Nm}\mathfrak a^{-4}\), the sum over the nonzero ideals \(\mathfrak a = (\alpha)\) of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), with coefficients of absolute value \(1\). Hence \(\log L(\bar\upsilon_8, 8) \le \log\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})}(4)\); numerically \(0.0620 \le 0.0680\).

In the proof the character is lifted to a definite space of rank \(n\), with a finite datum on columns of length \(n\) at finitely many places. At each such place \(w\), with residue field \(\mathbb{F}_q\), three factors enter: the mean of the squared absolute value of the datum on \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\), the index of the target level in the integral group, and \((1 - q^{-n})^{-1}\), restored from the denominator Euler product in the vacuum sum. At a chosen place the datum is the indicator of a fixed basis vector, and the level fixes that vector. At a place of the modulus \(Q_\xi\) it is the residue character of one coordinate, extended by zero, whose squared absolute value is the indicator of the complement of the coordinate hyperplane; the level preserves the hyperplane.

Problem 77.1.3. For \(q = 3\), \(n = 4\): one fixed vector is a proportion \(1/81\) of \(\mathbb{F}_3^4\), \(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{F}_3)\) acts transitively on the \(80\) nonzero vectors, \((1 - 3^{-4})^{-1} = 81/80\), and the product is \(1\). In general \(q^{-n}(q^n - 1)(1 - q^{-n})^{-1} = 1\).

Problem 77.1.4. For \(q = 3\), \(n = 4\): the vectors with nonzero first coordinate are a proportion \(2/3\), \(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbb{F}_3)\) acts transitively on the \(40\) hyperplanes, and \(\tfrac23\cdot 40\cdot\tfrac{81}{80} = 27 = 3^3\). In general \(\frac{q-1}{q}(1 + q + \dots + q^{n-1})(1 - q^{-n})^{-1} = q^{n-1}\).

So the three factors give \(1\) at a chosen place and \(q^{n-1}\) at a place of the modulus: this is the power of \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi\) in Theorem 77.4.1. (The index of the source level of \(\mathrm U(1)\) supplies a separate factor \(q - 1\) at each chosen place.)

§77.2. The lift of a character

Critical pairs \(P = (\nu, z)\), with \(\nu\) of weight \(-\mathrm{wt}_P\), the partial Hecke values \(L^S(z, \mu)\) and the ratios \(R_P(\xi, \epsilon)\), with \(R_P(\xi) := R_P(\xi, 1)\), are those of Definition 37.10.1.

Definition 77.2.1. The row of a critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) is \(\lambda_P(\tau) := \mathrm{wt}_P(\tau) - z \ge 0\), for \(\tau \in \Phi\). In this chapter \(\lvert\lambda_P\rvert := \sum_{\tau\in\Phi}\lambda_P(\tau)\).

Used in: Chapter 78; Chapter 79

Definition 77.2.2. Let \(P = (\nu, z)\) be a critical pair with \(z \ge 2\) and \(\xi\) a tame character. Consider the theta lift of the constant function \(1\) on \(\mathrm U(1)\) to the standard definite space of rank \(2z\), for the character \(\nu\xi\), with the following datum: at infinity the highest vector of the weight with one row of length \(\lambda_P(\tau)\) at each \(\tau\); at the places of \(\mathcal{M}\) the indicator of one fixed basis vector, at the places of \(Q_\xi\) the residue characters of \(\xi\) at one fixed coordinate, extended by zero (as in Section 77.1); the vacuum elsewhere. Define the lift of the constant \(\phi_{P,\xi}\) as the theta lift of \(1\) with the specified datum times the index of the chosen level of \(\mathrm U(1)\) and times \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{2z}(\pi_Mc_{\mathrm{jet}})^{\lvert\lambda_P\rvert}\).

Used in: Chapter 79: Definition 79.1.1

The class sum of the theta kernel of this datum over \(\mathrm U(1)\) at the chosen level equals the theta lift of \(1\) times the source level index; multiplying this class sum by \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{2z}(\pi_Mc_{\mathrm{jet}})^{\lvert\lambda_P\rvert}\) gives \(\phi_{P,\xi}\). Its squared norm is computed by Theorem 69.2.1 for this sharp datum, which the proof checks (Status: the Rallis inner product formula [Ra; Li]). At infinity the Fock vector is paired with itself.

Problem 77.2.1. In the Fock space of Section 23.3, with variables \(u_{\tau,1},\dots,u_{\tau,2z}\) at \(\tau\), the vector \(\prod_\tau u_{\tau,1}^{\lambda_P(\tau)}\) has squared norm \(\prod_\tau \lambda_P(\tau)!\).

Fix a critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) with \(z \ge 2\). The proof combines the formula of Theorem 69.2.1, the vacuum sum (the partial Hecke value divided by the product of \((1-\varepsilon_w q_w^{-z})^{-1}\) over the finite places outside \(\mathcal{M}\cup Q_\xi\) and its conjugates, with opposite signs \(\varepsilon_w\) at distinct conjugate places and \(+1\) at a self-conjugate place), the local factors of Problems 77.1.3 and 77.1.4 at the places of \(\mathcal{M}\cup Q_\xi\), and Problem 77.2.1. It obtains a complex number \(C_P \ne 0\) (in this sheet; not the \(C_p\) of Chapter 57), independent of \(\xi\), with \[\lVert\phi_{P,\xi}\rVert^2 = C_P\, L^{\mathcal{M}\cup Q_\xi}\bigl(z, (\nu\xi)^{-1}\bigr)\,(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi)^{2z-1}\] for every tame character \(\xi\). Here \(C_P\) is the squared modulus of the scale times the volumes of the two adelic quotients, divided by the full signed Euler product at \(z\), times \(\prod_{w\in\mathcal{M}}(q_w - 1)\), the hook number \(\prod_\tau\lambda_P(\tau)!\) and the dimension of the weight. The lift is nonzero for every tame \(\xi\).

Problem 77.2.2. Let \(\chi\) be a unitary Hecke character of \(E\), \(S\) a finite set of finite places, and (in this problem) \(L^S(s,\chi)\) its \(L\)-function without the factors at \(S\). For real \(s \ge 2\), \(L^S(s,\chi)\) is nonzero and \(\lvert\log\lvert L^S(s,\chi)\rvert\rvert \le [E:\mathbb{Q}]\log\zeta(2) = 2f\log\zeta(2) < 4f\).

The proof uses the bound \(4f\).

§77.3. Integrality, and the direction of the estimate

Definition 77.3.1. For a critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) with \(z \ge 2\) let \(d_P := M^{4fz}(Md)^{\lvert\lambda_P\rvert}\) (in this sheet; not to be confused with the prime \(d\) or with \(d_T\)), and for a tame character \(\xi\) let the algebraic part be \[\mathrm{alg}_P(\xi) := \mathcal{N}\, d_P^2\, \frac{\lVert\phi_{P,\xi}\rVert^2}{(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi)^{2z-1}} .\]

Used in: Chapter 79

At primes \(\ell \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi\) the number \(d_P\) clears the denominators of the scaled theta kernel: \(M^{4fz}\) those of the finite datum, and \(Md\) per box of the row those of the derivatives. The proof shows that for every such \(\ell\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) the form \(d_P\phi_{P,\xi}\) is integral at \(\eta\) (Status: read from the proof; not proved here). The squared norm is a class sum, \(\lVert\phi\rVert^2 = \sum_c \lvert\Gamma_c\rvert^{-1}\langle\phi(g_c),\phi(g_c)\rangle\), over representatives \(g_c\) with finite stabilizers \(\Gamma_c\).

Problem 77.3.1. Let \(I\) and \(T\) be finite sets, \(G\) an \(I\times I\) hermitian matrix with entries in \(\mathbb{Z}\), and \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) a ring homomorphism. For \(c \in T\) let \(a_c \in \mathbb{C}^I\), and let \(\gamma_c\) be a positive integer dividing a fixed integer \(A \ge 1\). If \(\lvert\eta(a_{c,i})\rvert \le 1\) and \(\lvert\eta(\bar a_{c,i})\rvert \le 1\) for all \(c, i\), then \(\lvert\eta(A\sum_c \gamma_c^{-1}\, a_c^* G a_c)\rvert \le 1\).

Used in: Chapter 76

Problem 77.3.1 models two inputs read from the proof and not proved here. At any finite adelic point, the Gram pairing of two values of gauge at most one, the first at \(\eta\circ(\text{complex conjugation})\) and the second at \(\eta\), is integral at \(\eta\). On the standard definite space of rank at most \(2n_m\), for every level contained in the stabilizer of the standard lattice, every class stabilizer has order dividing \(\mathcal{N}\).

Problem 77.3.2. Let \(P = (\nu, z)\) be a critical pair with \(z \ge 2\) and \(\xi\) a tame character. Given the norm formula of Section 77.2, the integrality of \(d_P\phi_{P,\xi}\), the Gram-pairing integrality and the divisibility of the stabilizer orders: \(\lvert\eta(\mathrm{alg}_P(\xi))\rvert_\ell \le 1\) for every prime \(\ell \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi\) and every \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\); at \(\xi = 1\) this holds at every prime; and \(R_P(\xi) = \mathrm{alg}_P(\xi)/\mathrm{alg}_P(1) = \bigl(\lVert\phi_{P,\xi}\rVert^2/(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi)^{2z-1}\bigr)/\lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\).

Fix a critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) with \(z \ge 2\). For \(1 \le j \le m\) and a regular family \(\theta\) of \(m\)-fold twists (Definition 79.1.3), write \(\vartheta_\iota := \theta_\iota^j\). The family to be estimated has terms \(R_P(\vartheta_\iota)^{-1} = \mathrm{alg}_P(1)/\mathrm{alg}_P(\vartheta_\iota)\). The denominator is integral at \(\iota\), as \(\ell \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_{\vartheta_\iota}\); its inverse need not be small there.

Problem 77.3.3. For \(n \ge 1\), the number \(y_n := (2+\mathrm{i})^n\) is integral at every \(\iota\), and \(\lvert\sigma(y_n)\rvert = 5^{n/2}\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\); yet \(\lvert y_n^{-1}\rvert_\iota = 5^n\) for \(\iota \mid 5\) with \(\lvert 2+\mathrm{i}\rvert_\iota = 1/5\).

Thus an integral denominator can have a large inverse at the specified embedding, while Theorem 77.4.1(ii) gives only an upper bound for the absolute value of the denominator.

§77.4. The size of the untwisted norm

Definition 77.4.1. A critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) is main for \(j\) if \(z \ge 10\), \(m < z \le r\), \(1 \le j \le m\), the tame part of \(\nu\) is \(\omega^j\), and \(\kappa_j(\tau) + r - t \le \mathrm{wt}_P(\tau) \le \kappa_j(\tau)\) for every \(\tau\). It has empty row if \(z \ge 2\) and \(\mathrm{wt}_P(\tau) = z\) for every \(\tau\), or equivalently if \(z \ge 2\) and \(\lambda_P(\tau) = 0\) for every \(\tau\).

Used in: Chapter 74; Chapter 78; Chapter 79

Problem 77.4.1. \(s_\infty(y+1) \ge s_\infty(y)\) for every integer \(y \ge 0\).

Problem 77.4.2. If \(P\) is main for \(j\), then \(\kappa(\tau) \le \lambda_P(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau \in \Phi\).

Used in: Chapter 78; Chapter 79

The conjugates of the squared norm are read from the proof. For \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) fixing \(K\) and each integer \(z \ge 2\) there is \(c_{\sigma,z} \in \mathbb{R}\) such that every critical pair \(P = (\nu, z)\) satisfies \[\log\bigl\lvert\sigma\bigl(\lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\bigr)\bigr\rvert = c_{\sigma,z} + r_{\sigma,P} + \sum_{\tau\in\Phi} s_\infty(\lambda_P(\tau))\] with a real \(r_{\sigma,P} \ge -4f\), and \(r_{\sigma,P} \le 4f\) if \(P\) has empty row. Here \(r_{\sigma,P}\) is the logarithm of the dimension of the transported weight (which is \(1\) for an empty row) plus \(\log\lvert L\rvert\) for the inverse transported character at \(z\) away from \(\mathcal{M}\), bounded by the \(4f\) of Problem 77.2.2. The factors \(M^{\lvert\lambda_P\rvert}\), \(\Omega^{-\lvert\lambda_P\rvert}\) and \(\prod_\tau\lambda_P(\tau)!\) come from \(\lvert\pi_M\rvert^2 = M\), from conjugating the scaled jets, and from Problem 77.2.1. (Status: read from the proof, not proved here; the proof uses the conjugation of CM theta series and the Chowla–Selberg formula for the common area of the lattices \(\sigma(u)\Lambda^\sigma\), with \(\Lambda^\sigma\) the conjugate lattice and \(u\) a number of absolute value \(1\) at every finite place; classical background: [Sh, Thm. 5.4], Theorem 20.3.1.) The proof also uses that for \(2 \le z \le r\), with the same \(z\) as \(P\), the pair \(P_0 := (\upsilon^{-z}, z)\) is a critical pair with empty row and trivial tame part, and that \(\mathcal{N}d_{P_0}^2 \le e^{x^{33}}\).

Problem 77.4.3. For the data, \(x^{33} + 8f \le B\).

Problem 77.4.4. Let \(P = (\nu, z)\) be main for \(j\) and \(H := \mathrm{alg}_{P_0}(1)/\lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\). Then \(\log\lvert\sigma H\rvert \le B - \sum_\tau s_\infty(\kappa(\tau))\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) and \(\lvert H\lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\rvert_\iota \le 1\) for every \(\iota\). Hence the constant family \(\iota \mapsto \lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\) has height at most \(B - \sum_\tau s_\infty(\kappa(\tau))\).

Problem 77.3.2 proves (i) and (ii) of the following theorem, and Problem 77.4.4 proves (iii).

Theorem 77.4.1. Let \(P = (\nu, z)\) be a critical pair with \(z \ge 2\). (i) For every tame character \(\xi\), \(R_P(\xi) = \bigl(\lVert\phi_{P,\xi}\rVert^2/(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi)^{2z-1}\bigr)/\lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\). (ii) For every tame character \(\xi\), every prime \(\ell \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\), \(\lvert\eta(\mathrm{alg}_P(\xi))\rvert_\ell \le 1\). (iii) If \(P\) is main for \(j\), the constant family \(\iota \mapsto \lVert\phi_{P,1}\rVert^2\) has height at most \(B - \sum_{\tau\in\Phi} s_\infty(\kappa(\tau))\).

Used in: Chapter 79; Chapter 89

§77.5. The Hecke product as a product of ratios

The lengths \(z_i\), signs \(\epsilon_i\) and characters \(\nu_i\), \(i \in \{\varnothing, +, -\}\), and the Hecke value product \(\mathcal{H}_j(\vartheta)\) are those of Definition 75.3.1; each \((\nu_i, z_i)\) is a critical pair. At \(v = 1\) we use that \(L^S(1, \mu) \ne 0\) for an automorphic character \(\mu\) of \(\mathrm U(1)\) with nonzero weight and a tame part, for every finite set \(S\) of finite places of \(E\) (Status: classical [La, Ch. XV]).

Problem 77.5.1. Let \(1 \le j \le m\) and let \(\vartheta\) be a twist. (a) For \(1 \le v \le z_i\), \((\nu_i, v)\) is a critical pair, and \[\frac{\mathcal{H}_j(\vartheta)}{\mathcal{H}_j(1)} = (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{z_\varnothing^2 + z_+^2 + z_-^2} \Bigl(\prod_{i}\prod_{v=1}^{z_i} R_{(\nu_i, v)}(\vartheta, \epsilon_i)\Bigr)^2 .\] (b) All these pairs are ramified; the \(k/2\) pairs \((\nu_\varnothing, v)\) with \(7m/2 < v \le z_\varnothing\) are moreover main for \(j\), and the remaining indices number \(5m\). (c) Formally for \(m = 2\), \(k = 4\): \((z_\varnothing, z_+, z_-) = (9, 2, 1)\), the exponent is \(86\), and the index range \(7m/2 < v \le z_\varnothing\) has \(2\) positions, leaving \(10\) others.

Used in: Chapter 74; Chapter 79

At \(\vartheta_\iota = \theta_\iota^j\) for a regular family, the inverse of \(\mathcal{H}_j(\vartheta_\iota)/\mathcal{H}_j(1)\) has the factor \((\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\vartheta_\iota})^{-(z_\varnothing^2+z_+^2+z_-^2)}\), a unit at \(\iota \mid \ell\). For the \(5m\) remaining factors, regularity bounds the height of the inverse ratios by \(x_1\) (Definition 79.1.2). For the \(k/2\) main factors see Theorem 79.3.1.
