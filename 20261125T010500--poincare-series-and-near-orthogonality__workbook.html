Prerequisites.

Modular forms for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\): \(q\)-expansions, the cusp form \(\Delta\) and the dimension formula [SeA, Ch. VII, §3]. The residue theorem and the mean value property of holomorphic functions. The power series \(J_\nu(x) = \sum_{j \ge 0} (-1)^j (x/2)^{\nu + 2j}/(j!\,\Gamma(\nu + j + 1))\). Gershgorin’s theorem on the eigenvalues of a matrix. Chapter 9, Few translates span, for the closing paragraphs only.

Chapter 9 asked for a map into a space of automorphic forms that is almost an isometry. If the weight is large, Poincaré series of small index are almost orthogonal, and Petersson’s formula proves it with nothing more than a bound for a Bessel function. This chapter works it out on \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), where the vectors are holomorphic Poincaré series and their Petersson inner products recover Fourier coefficients up to an explicit factor.

Throughout, \(k \ge 4\) is even, \(\Gamma := \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), \(\Gamma_\infty := \{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1 & n\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\), \(j(\gamma,z) := cz + d\) for \(\gamma = \begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix}\), \(e(z) := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\), and \(d\nu := y^{-2}\,dx\,dy\) on the upper half-plane \(\mathbb{H}\).

§10.1. Poincaré series Tag 22AZ

Let \(S_k\) be the space of cusp forms of weight \(k\) for \(\Gamma\), with the Petersson inner product \(\langle f, g\rangle := \int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}} f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,y^k\,d\nu(z)\). For \(f \in S_k\) write \(f(z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_f(n)\,e(nz)\). For each positive integer \(m\) define the Poincaré series by \[P_m(z) := \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma} j(\gamma,z)^{-k}\, e(m\gamma z) .\] The series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets, and \(P_m \in S_k\) [Iw].

§10.2. Petersson’s formula Tag 22B5

What is the Fourier expansion of \(P_m\)? For integers \(m, n\) and \(c \ge 1\) let \[S(m,n;c) := \sum_{\substack{d \bmod c\\ (d,c) = 1}} e\Bigl(\frac{m\bar d + nd}{c}\Bigr), \qquad d\bar d \equiv 1 \pmod c,\] be the Kloosterman sum. It is real, and \(S(m,n;c) = S(n,m;c)\).

§10.3. Near orthogonality Tag 22BC

Normalize the Poincaré series by \[\widetilde{P}_m := \frac{(4\pi m)^{(k-1)/2}}{\Gamma(k-1)^{1/2}}\, P_m ,\] and for each positive integer \(M\) let \(G_M := \bigl(\langle \widetilde{P}_m, \widetilde{P}_n\rangle\bigr)_{1 \le m,n \le M}\) be the \(M \times M\) Gram matrix. We use the operator norm on \(\mathbb{C}^M\) with its Euclidean inner product.

Tag 22BD

Problem 10.3.1. \(\langle \widetilde{P}_m, \widetilde{P}_n\rangle = \delta(m,n) + T_k(m,n)\).

So \(G_M\) is the identity plus the matrix of Kloosterman terms, and the question is how small \(J_{k-1}\) is. We use Poisson’s integral \[J_\nu(x) = \frac{(x/2)^\nu}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\nu + \frac12)} \int_{-1}^1 (1 - t^2)^{\nu - 1/2} \cos(xt)\, dt \qquad (\nu > -\tfrac12,\ x > 0),\] see [DLMF].

Tag 22BE

Problem 10.3.2. For \(\nu > -\frac12\) and \(x > 0\), \(\lvert J_\nu(x)\rvert \le (x/2)^\nu/\Gamma(\nu + 1)\).

Tag 22BH

Theorem 10.3.1. If \(1 \le M \le (k-1)/(4\pi e)\), then \(\lVert G_M - 1\rVert \le k\,2^{-k}\). In particular \(P_1, \dots, P_M\) are linearly independent, and \(\dim S_k \ge \lfloor (k-1)/(4\pi e)\rfloor\).

Here \(e = 2.718\ldots\) is Euler’s number, and \(4\pi e = 34.15\ldots\). The threshold is not the true one. For \(0<x\le r\nu\) with \(0<r<1\) fixed, the function \(J_\nu(x)\) is exponentially small in \(\nu\), and Siegel’s inequality makes this precise: for \(\nu \ge 0\) and \(0 < x \le 1\), \[\lvert J_\nu(\nu x)\rvert \le \Bigl(\frac{x\, e^{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}}{1 + \sqrt{1 - x^2}}\Bigr)^{\nu}\] [DLMF].

Tag 22BJ

Problem 10.3.5. Let \(0 < \theta < 1/(4\pi)\). There are constants \(0 < \varrho < 1\) and \(C > 0\), depending only on \(\theta\), such that \(\lVert G_M - 1\rVert \le C k\varrho^k\) for every even \(k \ge 4\) and every integer \(M\) with \(1 \le M \le \theta(k-1)\).

So for large \(k\) the \(\widetilde{P}_m\) with \(m \le \theta(k-1)\), for a fixed \(\theta < 1/(4\pi)\), are almost orthonormal. For \(m\) near \((k-1)/(4\pi)\) the argument \(4\pi m\) of the term \(c = 1\) of \(T_k(m,m)\) reaches the transition region \(x \approx \nu\) of \(J_\nu\), where \(J_\nu(x)\) is no longer exponentially small but of size up to about \(\nu^{-1/3}\), and the diagonal entry is in general no longer exponentially close to \(1\). For \(k = 100\), where \((k-1)/(4\pi) = 7.87\ldots\) and \(\dim S_{100} = 8\), a computation of the Kloosterman terms gives the following. The eigenvalues of \(G_7\) lie in \([0.99999, 1.0094]\). Those of \(G_8\) lie in \([0.982, 1.808]\), so \(P_1,\dots,P_8\) form a basis of \(S_{100}\), and the Gram matrix of the normalized basis \(\widetilde{P}_1,\dots,\widetilde{P}_8\) has an eigenvalue near \(1.808\). \(G_9\) is singular, as it must be.

§10.4. From Gram blocks to the second moment Tag 23DX

To model the second-moment estimate used in Theorem B of Chapter 39, we unfold finite Gram blocks between a subspace and its translates and sum their squared norms.

Let \(G:=\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)\) be the group of invertible two by two matrices over the field of three elements, and let \(U:=\{u(t):t\in\mathbb{F}_3\}\), where \(u(t):=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\). Write \(I\) for the identity of \(G\), \(\zeta:=e(1/3)\), \(\psi(t):=\zeta^t\) (using any integer representative of \(t\)), and \(\chi(u(t)):=\psi(t)\). Thus \(\psi\) and \(\chi\) are nontrivial characters, homomorphisms to the complex numbers of absolute value one. Give \(V:=\mathbb{C}[G]\) the orthonormal basis \((\delta_x)_{x\in G}\) and let \(\rho(g)\delta_x:=\delta_{gx}\).

Tag 23DY

Problem 10.4.1. The operator \(P:=\frac13\sum_{u\in U}\overline{\chi(u)}\,\rho(u)\) is the orthogonal projector onto the vectors \(v\) with \(\rho(u)v=\chi(u)v\) for all \(u\in U\). The group \(G\) has order \(48\) and \(P\) has rank \(16\).

Let \(W:=PV\). Write \(B_g:=P\rho(g)P\) for the Gram block, viewed as an operator on \(V\). Its restriction to \(W\) gives the inner products between an orthonormal basis of \(W\) and its translate under \(\rho(g)\), and \(B_g\) vanishes on \(W^\perp\).

Tag 23DZ

Problem 10.4.2. For \(g,h\in G\), let \(c_g(h)\) be the coefficient of \(\delta_h\) in \(B_g\delta_I\). The coefficient of \(\delta_x\) in \(B_g\delta_y\) is \(c_g(xy^{-1})\), with \[c_g(h)=\frac19\sum_{\substack{u,v\in U\\ugv=h}} \overline{\chi(u)\chi(v)} .\] In particular \(c_I(I)=1/3\), and \(B_I\) is the identity on \(W\).

Tag 23E2

Problem 10.4.3. (i) The double coset \(UgU:=\{ugv:u,v\in U\}\) supports a nonzero coefficient of \(B_g\) exactly when \(\chi(u)=\chi(g^{-1}ug)\) for every \(u\in U\cap gUg^{-1}\). In that case \(\lvert c_g(h)\rvert=\#(U\cap gUg^{-1})/9\) for every \(h\in UgU\).

(ii) The double cosets have the following representatives, one for each choice of \(a,b\in\mathbb{F}_3^\times=\{1,-1\}\) in each matrix shape. \[\begin{array}{c|c|c} g & \# UgU & c_g(g) \\ \hline \begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&b\end{pmatrix} & 3 & 1/3\text{ if }a=b,\quad 0\text{ if }a\ne b\\[2pt] \begin{pmatrix}0&a\\b&0\end{pmatrix} & 9 & 1/9 \end{array}\] Six double cosets survive, including \(UIU=U\).

For operators on \(V\), use the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product \(\langle A,D\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}:=\operatorname{tr}(AD^*)\), where \(D^*\) is the adjoint and \(\operatorname{tr}\) is the trace. Its squared norm is the sum of the squared absolute values of the matrix entries. Let \(\Theta(g):=\lVert B_g\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\) and let \(H:=\{h\in G:h e_1=e_1\}\), the stabilizer of the column vector \(e_1=(1,0)^t\).

Tag 23E3

Problem 10.4.4. (i) The squared norm \(\Theta(g)\) equals \(\langle P,\rho(g)P\rho(g)^{-1}\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\), the matrix coefficient of the action by conjugation on operators. Its average over \(H\) is \[\left\lVert \frac1{\#H}\sum_{h\in H}\rho(h)P\rho(h)^{-1} \right\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = 8, \qquad \#H=6 .\]

(ii) The values of \(\Theta\) on \(G\) are \(16,0,16/3\) with multiplicities \(6,6,36\), respectively. The second moment \(\sum_{g\in G}\Theta(g)\) is \(288\), of which the identity double coset \(U\) contributes \(48\).

Tag 23E4

Problem 10.4.5. (i) The leading entry \(g_{11}\), viewed as a one by one matrix, has nonzero kernel exactly when \(\frac13\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_3}\psi(tg_{11})=1\); otherwise the sum divided by \(3\) is \(0\). The weighted sums \(\sum_{g\in G}\Theta(g)\psi(tg_{11})\) for \(t=0,1,-1\) are \(288,-48,-48\), respectively. Thus the matrices with singular leading entry carry \(64\) of the second moment and the others carry \(224\).

(ii) Write \(T\) for the total second moment, \(E\) for the sum over singular leading entries, and \(A:=T-E\) for the sum over nonsingular leading entries. A supplied bound \(E\le\delta T\), with \(0\le\delta<1\), gives \(A\le T\le A/(1-\delta)\). For example, \(\delta=1/4\) and \(A=224\) give \(T\le896/3\).

§10.5. The dimension as formal degree times covolume Tag 22BK

How large a proportion of \(S_k\) does Theorem 10.3.1 fill? Let \(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}\) carry the measure \(\nu\).

Tag 22BL

Problem 10.5.1. \(\nu(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}) = \pi/3\).

Tag 22BM

Problem 10.5.2. \(\bigl\lvert \dim S_k - \frac{k-1}{12}\bigr\rvert \le \frac{13}{12}\) for every even \(k \ge 4\).

Used in: Chapter 39

For fixed \(0<\theta<1/(4\pi)\), the first \(\lfloor\theta(k-1)\rfloor\) series in Problem 10.3.5 span a proportion of \(S_k\) tending to \(12\theta\) as \(k\) tends to infinity through even weights. These limits approach \(3/\pi=0.954\ldots\) as \(\theta\) increases to \(1/(4\pi)\). The full initial segment in Theorem 10.3.1 gives limiting proportion \(3/(\pi e)=0.351\ldots\). Where does \((k-1)/12\) come from? It is the product of \(\pi/3\) and a constant attached to the weight alone.

Let \(G:=\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R})\) act on \(\mathbb{H}\), and normalize Haar measure \(dg\) by \(\int_G F(g\mathrm{i})\,dg=\int_\mathbb{H}F\,d\nu\) for compactly supported continuous \(F\). With Haar probability on the stabilizer \(\mathrm{PSO}(2)\) in the quotient integral formula, \(\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash G)=\pi/3\). Let \(\mathcal{A}_k\) be the space of holomorphic functions \(f\) on \(\mathbb{H}\) with \(\lVert f\rVert^2 := \int_\mathbb{H}\lvert f\rvert^2 y^k\,d\nu < \infty\), on which \(G\) acts unitarily by \((\pi(g)f)(z) := f(g^{-1}z)\,j(g^{-1},z)^{-k}\). This is the holomorphic discrete series of weight \(k\). For \(w \in \mathbb{H}\) put \[h_w(z) := \Bigl(\frac{z - \bar w}{2\mathrm{i}}\Bigr)^{-k} .\] For a unit vector \(u\) of an irreducible unitary representation \(\pi\) with square-integrable matrix coefficients, the formal degree \(d_\pi\) is defined by \(\int_G \lvert\langle\pi(g)u, u\rangle\rvert^2\,dg = 1/d_\pi\). It does not depend on \(u\), by the Schur orthogonality relations for such representations [HC]. We compute it with \(u\) proportional to \(h_\mathrm{i}\).

Tag 22BN

Problem 10.5.3. For every \(w \in \mathbb{H}\), \(h_w \in \mathcal{A}_k\). For every \(f \in \mathcal{A}_k\), \(\langle f, h_\mathrm{i}\rangle = \frac{4\pi}{k-1}\, f(\mathrm{i})\).

Tag 22BP

Problem 10.5.4. \(\pi(g)h_w = \overline{j(g,w)}^{\,-k}\, h_{gw}\) for \(g \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})\) and \(w \in \mathbb{H}\).

Tag 22BQ

Problem 10.5.5. Let \(u := h_\mathrm{i}/\lVert h_\mathrm{i}\rVert\), and for \(g \in G\) write \(w := g\mathrm{i}\). Then \(\lvert\langle\pi(g)u, u\rangle\rvert^2 = \bigl(4\operatorname{Im} w/\lvert w + \mathrm{i}\rvert^2\bigr)^k\), and the formal degree of \(\pi\) is \((k-1)/(4\pi)\).

Used in: Chapter 41: Problem 41.1.6; Chapter 42; Chapter 45: Problem 45.3.7

With Problem 10.5.1 this gives \[d_\pi \cdot \operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash G) = \frac{k-1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac\pi3 = \frac{k-1}{12} ,\] which is \(\dim S_k\) up to the bounded error of Problem 10.5.2. This is not a coincidence. As in Problem 10.1.1, and using Problems 10.5.3 and 10.5.4, one checks that the reproducing kernel \(B(z,w) := \sum_j f_j(z)\overline{f_j(w)}\) of \(S_k\), for an orthonormal basis \((f_j)\), is \[B(z,w) = \frac{k-1}{4\pi} \sum_{\gamma \in \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})} j(\gamma,z)^{-k}\, h_w(\gamma z) .\] Since \(\dim S_k = \int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}} B(z,z)\,y^k\,d\nu(z)\), the term \(\gamma = 1\) contributes exactly the formal degree times the covolume, and by Problem 10.5.2 all other terms together contribute a bounded amount. For a compact unitary group with Haar probability, the formal degree of an irreducible representation is its dimension. In the definite example of Chapter 21, for rank \(n\ge1\) and weight \(\lambda\) with \(4\mid\lvert\lambda\rvert\), the central scalars contribute \(4M_n\dim V_\lambda\), where \(M_n\) is the mass (Problem 21.3.4).

Theorem 10.3.1 says that the map \(\mathbb{C}^M \to S_k\), \(e_m \mapsto \widetilde{P}_m\), is almost an isometry when \(M\) is at most a fixed proportion of \(\dim S_k\). Its image therefore occupies only part of \(S_k\). Problem 10.3.5 extends near orthonormality to a longer initial segment, and Problem 10.1.2 shows that the full family spans \(S_k\). In the setting of Chapter 9, a finite group acts on the ambient space. A near-isometry estimate for the projected map and a second-moment bound for the Gram operators of the unprojected map and its translates give a lower bound for the dimension spanned by a few projected translates in the target (Theorems 9.3.1 and 9.4.1).

The analogue in the proof.

This chapter gives a classical model for the estimates in Chapter 39. In the proof, the principal-level forms on the unitary group of a hermitian space \(Y_0\) over a CM field \(E\) play the role of \(S_k\). Bessel periods play the role of Fourier coefficient extraction and are represented by a Poincaré map. Its values on the source coordinate basis are Bessel–Poincaré series. The two estimates below concern the map followed by the projector onto the good forms and the dimension of those forms. The next paragraph gives the declarations for these spaces, the period map and its values on the basis, and the two estimates. For the definition of Bessel periods see [GGP].

In the Lean formalization.

Fix admissible data \(D\) (New.Data) of the Lean proof [GL]. Write \(E\) for Data.E, \(Y_0\) for D.spaceY 0, and \(r\) for the rank of \(Y_0\) over \(E\). Write \(S\) for the source forms Data.SourceForm, with coordinates Data.sourceFormCoords, and \(D_X\) for their complex dimension. The Poincaré map Data.poincare is Data.poincareAt at the chosen archimedean point Data.besselPoint and linear form Data.besselForm. By Data.inner_poincareAt it represents the Bessel period for each source form and each principal-level test form. By Data.poincare_eq_poincareMap_comp and Filling.Fits.coe_poincareMap, the inverse coordinate images of the standard basis vectors map to the corresponding Bessel–Poincaré series.

Let \(P\) be Data.scaledPoincare, the Poincaré map in these Euclidean coordinates multiplied by the positive factor Data.poincareScale. Its codomain is the space Data.principalForms on \(Y_0\), of the specified discrete-series type at infinity and fixed by the kernel of reduction at the chosen finite places inside the standard finite level. Let \(e_0\) denote the action of the group-algebra idempotent D.e\(_0\) through Data.principalRep on this space. It projects onto the space \(T\) of good forms (Data.targetForms), of complex dimension \(d_T\) (Data.targetDim). Let \(b\) be the weight D.b at the infinite places D.bPlaces. For every complex subspace \(U\) of the source coordinate space, Data.isNearIsometry_e\(_0\)_comp_scaledPoincare gives \[\lVert e_0 P\,\mathrm{pr}_U\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 \ge \dim U - 5b^{-4}D_X ,\] where \(\mathrm{pr}_U\) is the orthogonal projection and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\) is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

By Data.targetDim_le_card_mul_besselDegree_mul_mass, \[d_T \le \#\bar G \cdot \deg \cdot \mathrm{mass}(E,r) .\] Here \(\bar G\) is Data.chosenGL, the product of \(\mathrm{GL}_r\) over the residue fields at the chosen places above the two level primes. The constant \(\deg\) is D.filling.besselDegree, the product of the degrees of the local Bessel systems over all infinite places of \(E\). The factor \(\mathrm{mass}(E,r)\) is New.mass, evaluated at \(E\) and \(r\), the mass of the genus of the standard hermitian lattice \(\mathcal O_E^r\). Both inequalities are in grh/src/New/Spanning/SecondMoment/SpanningTheorem.lean.

The standard fundamental domain \(\lvert x\rvert \le \frac12\), \(\lvert z\rvert \ge 1\); integrate in \(y\) first.

10.5.2

Write \(k = 12j + r\) with \(0 \le r < 12\) and use the dimension formula.

10.5.3

Put \(\zeta := (z - \mathrm{i})/(z + \mathrm{i})\) and \(F(\zeta) := f(z)\bigl((z+\mathrm{i})/2\mathrm{i}\bigr)^k\). Express \(\lvert f\rvert^2 y^k\,d\nu\) in terms of \(F\) and \(\zeta\), and use the mean value property on the circles \(\lvert\zeta\rvert = r\). For \(h_w \in \mathcal{A}_k\), use \(4\operatorname{Im}z\operatorname{Im}w/\lvert z - \bar w\rvert^2 = 1 - \lvert\zeta_w\rvert^2\) with \(\zeta_w := (z - w)/(z - \bar w)\).
