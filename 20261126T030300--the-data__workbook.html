Prerequisites.

Cyclotomic fields: the degree of \({\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_n)\), the splitting of primes in it, and the conductor–discriminant formula [Wa, Ch. 2–3]. The discriminant in a tower [Ne, Ch. III, §2]; finiteness of the class group [Ne, Ch. I, §6]. Quadratic reciprocity [IR, Ch. 5]. Chebyshev’s function satisfies \(\psi(u) \le 2u\) for \(u \ge 1\) [RS, Thm. 12]. From the earlier chapters: the character \(\chi_K\) and the weight \(1 - \chi_K\) (Chapter 2), the infinite places of the CM field of Chapter 3 and the prime sums over them, sparse sets of places (Chapter 4), and the chain of \(m \approx \sqrt x\) steps (Chapter 8).

Every chapter of Parts VI–IX works with one collection of objects: two integers \(q_0\) and \(d\), a CM field \(E\) obtained by adjoining a root of unity to \({\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})\), three scales \(x < x_1 < b\), the number \(m\) of lifts, the ranks \(r\) and \(k\) and the step size \(t\), a set of places of \(E\) with weights, and three large primes. This chapter lists them, checks that their sizes fit together, defines the direction amount, the main amount and the total remainder used in the later bounds, and states that such data exist for every large integer \(x\). The spaces, forms and twists that the later chapters attach to the data are fixed in Chapter 37, Spaces, forms and twists. We write \(\log\) for the natural logarithm.

§36.1. The modulus and the prime \(d\)

How must \(d\) be chosen so that every prime dividing \(q_0\) splits in \(K = {\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d})\)?

Definition 36.1.1. Parameters are natural numbers \(q_0\) and \(d\) with \(18480 \mid q_0\), \(d\) prime, \(q_0 \mid 2(d+1)\) and \(q_0 \nmid d + 1\).

Used in: Chapter 3; Chapter 60

Let \(K := {\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-d}) \subset {\mathbb{C}}\), and let \(\chi_K(n) := \bigl(\frac nd\bigr)\) be the Legendre symbol.

Problem 36.1.1. \(18480 = 2^4 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 \cdot 11\). Parameters satisfy \(q_0 \ne 0\), \(d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \pmod{q_0}\) and \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\). For \(q_0 = 18480\) the least admissible \(d\) is \(9239\), and \(\varphi(18480) = 3840\).

Used in: Chapter 38; Chapter 82

Problem 36.1.2. Let \(c, s\) be nonnegative integers. (i) Every prime \(p \mid 3^c 2^s q_0\) divides \((d+1)/4\), and \(\chi_K(p) = 1\). (ii) A prime \(p\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) satisfies \(p \ge 13\).

Used in: Chapter 61; Chapter 82

Since \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(\chi_K\) is the character of \(K\) (Problem 2.1.1), so the primes of Problem 36.1.2(i) split in \(K\). For \(d = 9239\) the least prime \(p\) with \(\chi_K(p) = -1\) is \(19\). The bound \(p \ge 13\) for non-split primes follows from (i) since \(2, 3, 5, 7, 11\) divide \(q_0\), and is used in the proof at marked primes.

§36.2. The field and its places

For a nonnegative integer \(s\) let \(\lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor\) be the floor of the logarithm of \(s\) to base \(3\), that is, the largest integer \(e\) with \(3^e \le s\) (zero for \(s = 0\)), and \[l_0(s) := 3^{\lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor + 4}, \qquad T(s) := 3^{\lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor + 5}\, 2^s, \qquad q_1 := q_0 T(s), \qquad q_2 := (q_0 d)^{s+6}.\] Let \(E = E_s := K(\zeta_{q_1})\), with \(\zeta_{q_1}\) a primitive \(q_1\)-th root of unity, let \(\Phi\) be the set of infinite places of \(E\), \(f := \#\Phi\), and let \(\mathrm{rd}_E := \lvert \operatorname{disc} E \rvert^{1/[E:{\mathbb{Q}}]}\) be the root discriminant. Fix an embedding \(E \to {\mathbb{C}}\) over \(K\). By Problem 3.1.2 of Chapter 3, composition with this embedding gives a bijection from \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\) to \(\Phi\). Give \(\Phi\) the transported group law. By Problem 3.1.1, the action on \(\zeta_{q_1}\) identifies \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\) with \(({\mathbb{Z}}/q_1)^\times\). For \(\nu \in {\mathbb{Z}}\) let \([\nu] \in \Phi\) be the place of \(\nu \bmod q_1\) if \(\gcd(\nu, q_1) = 1\), and the identity otherwise. For \(n \ge 1\) and \(\tau \in \Phi\), the partner of \(\tau\) for \(n\) is \([-n]\tau\), as in Chapter 3; for \(n\) prime to \(q_1\) its label is \(-n\) times that of \(\tau\). The class of \(\tau \in \Phi\) modulo \(n \mid q_1\) is its image in \(({\mathbb{Z}}/n)^\times\). Let \(\Phi_a\) be the set of places of class \(1\) modulo \(q_0\).

Problem 36.2.1. \(T(s) = 3 l_0(s) 2^s\), and \(27 s < l_0(s)\); if \(s \ge 1\), then \(l_0(s) \le 81 s\).

Used in: Chapter 82

Problem 36.2.2. \(\varphi(q_1) = T(s)\varphi(q_0)\), and \([E : K] = \varphi(q_1)\). Hence \(f = T(s)\varphi(q_0)\), and exactly \(T(s)\) places have class \(1\) modulo \(q_0\), and exactly \(T(s)\) have class \(-1\).

Used in: Chapter 37; Chapter 38; Chapter 51; Chapter 74; Chapter 79

Problem 36.2.3. \(E \subset {\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{q_1 d})\), \(q_1 d \mid q_2\), and \(1 \le \mathrm{rd}_E \le q_1 d\). A prime \(p \equiv 1 \pmod{q_1 d}\) splits completely in \(E\).

Used in: Chapter 37; Chapter 66

Problem 36.2.4. For \(s \ge 1\), \(\mathrm{rd}_E \ge \mathrm{rd}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{2^s})} = 2^{s-1}\).

§36.3. Scales, ranks and signs

Let \(\gamma\) be Euler’s constant, \(\mathrm{CS}(d) := \gamma - L'(1, \chi_K)/L(1, \chi_K)\), and \[C := v_0 + (2d + 6) + q_0 + \lvert \mathrm{CS}(d) \rvert + \sum_{w} \bigl\lvert \log \lvert \Phi_{q_0}'(w) \rvert \bigr\rvert ,\] the sum over the primitive \(q_0\)-th roots of unity \(w \in {\mathbb{C}}\), with \(\Phi_{q_0}\) the cyclotomic polynomial, and \(v_0 \ge 0\) the integer, depending only on \(q_0\) and \(d\), that the proof fixes for the integral vacuum (Chapter 37, Section 37.8). For a finite set \(\mathcal F \subset \Phi\) and a nonnegative integer \(\ell_0\), call \(\mathcal F\) sparse up to \(\ell_0\) if for every prime \(\ell \le \ell_0\) and all integers \(0 < i < j < l_0(s)\) there are no \(\sigma, \tau, \rho \in \mathcal F\), distinct or not, with \(\tau/\sigma \equiv \pm \ell^i\) and \(\rho/\sigma \equiv \pm \ell^j\) (the two signs independent) modulo the largest divisor of \(q_1\) prime to \(\ell\). For \(\kappa \colon \Phi \to {\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge 0}\), integers \(m \ge 0\) and \(M \ge 0\), and real \(L\), let \[L_-(\kappa, m, M, L) := \frac mf \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \log \kappa(\tau) + (m - 1) \log \frac{M \mathrm{rd}_E}{2\pi} - \frac{15}{2} L ,\] with \(\log 0 := 0\). It depends on \(s\) through \(\Phi\), \(f\) and \(\mathrm{rd}_E\).

Definition 36.3.1. An admissible datum for \((q_0, d)\) consists of the following data.

  1. Integers \(x, x_1, b \ge 1\) with \(10^{12} C \le \log\log b\), \((\log b)^{100} \le x\), \(x^{10^6} \le x_1\), \(x_1^{10^6} \le b\), and \((\log x_1)^6 \le \log b\).

  2. Integers \(m, r, s \ge 0\) with \(\sqrt x \le m \le 2\sqrt x\), \(x^5 \le r \le 3x^5\), \(m = 2^{e}\) and \(s = 22e\) for some integer \(e \ge 1\), \(r = 3^{e'}\) for some integer \(e' \ge 1\), \(s \le 32 \log x\), and \(q_1 x_1 \mid b\), where \(q_1 = q_0 T(s)\). The field is \(E = E_s\).

  3. A set \(\Phi_b \subset \Phi\) of places of class \(-1\) modulo \(q_0\), with \(T(s) \le \#\Phi_b \log b\), sparse up to \((r + 1 - m) \cdot 5r\).

  4. The weight \(\kappa \colon \Phi \to {\mathbb{Z}}_{\ge 0}\): \(\kappa(\tau) = b\) for \(\tau \in \Phi_b\), and \(\kappa(\tau) = \lfloor xb/x_1 \rfloor\) otherwise.

  5. Distinct primes \(p_0, p_1 \equiv 1 \pmod{q_2^2}\) in \([b^{10}, b^{11}]\), and \(\alpha \in \mathcal O_K\) with \(\alpha\bar\alpha = p_0 p_1\); let \(\pi_M \in \mathcal O_E\) be the image of \(\bar\alpha\) under the inclusion \(\mathcal O_K \subseteq \mathcal O_E\).

  6. A prime \(N \equiv 1 \pmod{q_2^2}\) with \(L_- \le \log N \le L_- + \sqrt{\log b}\), where \(L_- := L_-(\kappa, m, p_0p_1, \log b)\), and \(\pi_N \in \mathcal O_E\) with \(\pi_N \bar\pi_N = N\).

The hermitian spaces \(Y_0,\dots,Y_m\) over \(E\) are defined as follows. For \(0 \le j < m\), \(Y_j\) is the space of column vectors of length \(n_j + m - 1 - j\) over \(E\) with the hermitian form whose Gram matrix in the standard basis is the orthogonal sum of the identity matrix of size \(n_j\) and the matrix \(\Sigma_j\) of size \(m - 1 - j\) with rows and columns indexed by \(0, \dots, m - 2 - j\), whose \((u, v)\) entry is \(0\) unless \(u + v = m - 2 - j\), is \(1\) if \(u + v = m - 2 - j\) and \(u \ne v\), and is \(-N\) if \(u = v = (m - 2 - j)/2\). The space \(Y_m\) is the standard positive definite space of rank \(n_m - 1\). These Gram matrices are part of the definition. For \(j < m\) the proof chooses an adelic frame with prescribed signs at infinity and an orthonormal basis of a prescribed unimodular lattice at the finite places (Section 37.3). For \(Y_m\) it uses the coordinate frame.

For \(0 \le j \le m\), \(Y_j\) has rank \(r + jt\) and exactly \(\lfloor (m - j)/2 \rfloor\) negative eigenvalues at each infinite place. \(Y_0\) is the orthogonal sum of the identity of size \(k\), \(m/2 - 1\) hyperbolic planes and \(\langle -N \rangle\). Formally, for matrices defined in the same way with \(m = 4\) (no data have \(m = 4\)), the numbers of negative eigenvalues of \(Y_0, \dots, Y_4\) are \(2, 1, 1, 0, 0\).

§36.4. The weights and the two amounts

Let \(\Lambda_K(\nu) := \Lambda(\nu)(1 - \chi_K(\nu))\). For real \(y\), \(\sigma \in \Phi\) and integers \(j \ge 0\) let \[s_{\mathrm{fin}}(y, \sigma) := \sum_{\nu = 1}^{a} \Lambda_K(\nu) \Bigl(\frac y\nu - \kappa([-\nu]\sigma)\Bigr)_+ , \qquad s_\infty(j) := \log j! + j \log \frac M\Omega ,\] where \(\Omega := e^{\mathrm{CS}(d)}\, 2\pi/\mathrm{rd}_E\) and \([-\nu]\sigma\) is the product in the group \(\Phi\). We call \(s_\infty(j)\) the size of a derivative of order \(j\), and use \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\sigma),\sigma)\) to measure its guaranteed divisibility when \(j=\kappa(\sigma)\). We define the direction amount and the main amount by \[h_{\mathrm{dir}}:= \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi} \bigl( s_\infty(\kappa(\sigma)) - s_{\mathrm{fin}}(\kappa(\sigma), \sigma) \bigr), \qquad h_{\mathrm{main}}:= m k\, h_{\mathrm{dir}}.\] We define the unit of error by \(U := f a \log^3 b\).

Definition 36.4.1. The denominator budget is \(B := U/(mk) = fa\log^3 b/(mk)\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 74; Chapter 77; Chapter 79; Chapter 80; Chapter 82

Evaluating the logarithmic derivatives at \(1\) gives \(c_1 = -1 - \mathrm{CS}(d)\) for the constant recalled in Chapter 3. Define the main prime sum and the total remainder by \[s_{\mathrm{main}}:= a \Bigl( \log a - \frac1f \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \log \kappa(\tau) + c_1 \Bigr), \qquad \mathrm{rem}:= \sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} \bigl( s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) - s_{\mathrm{main}}\bigr) .\] Chapter 3 expresses \(\mathrm{rem}\) through remainders of prime sums in progressions.

§36.6. Existence

How should the scales be chosen, as functions of \(x\), so that Definition 36.3.1(1)–(2) hold?

Problem 36.6.1. Let \(x > 1\), let \(m\) be the least power of \(2\) with \(m \ge \sqrt x\), and \(r\) the least power of \(3\) with \(r \ge x^5\). Then \(2 \le m < 2\sqrt x\) and \(3 \le r < 3x^5\). If \(c \ge 0\) and \(b\) is the least multiple of an integer \(n \ge 1\) with \(b > c\), then \(b \le c + n\).

Problem 36.6.2. Fix parameters and \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/100\), and for each \(x\) let \(x_1 = \lfloor \exp(x^{\varepsilon/6}) \rfloor\) and let \(b\) be an integer with \(x^\varepsilon \le \log b \le x^\varepsilon + 1\). Then the five inequalities of Definition 36.3.1(1) hold for all large \(x\). If instead \(x_1 = \lfloor \exp(x^{\varepsilon'}) \rfloor\) with \(\varepsilon' > \varepsilon/6\), then \((\log x_1)^6 > \log b\) for all large \(x\). If instead \(\varepsilon > 1/100\) and \(x^\varepsilon \le \log b \le x^\varepsilon + 1\), then \((\log b)^{100} > x\) for all large \(x\).

For fixed parameters and \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/100\), the construction takes \(m\) and \(r\) as in Problem 36.6.1, sets \(s = 22e\) for \(m = 2^e\) and \(x_1 = \lfloor \exp(x^{\varepsilon/6}) \rfloor\), and takes \(b\) to be the least multiple of \(q_1 x_1\) exceeding \(\exp(x^\varepsilon)\). For large \(x\), \(q_1 x_1 \le \exp(x^\varepsilon)\), so Problem 36.6.1 gives \(x^\varepsilon \le \log b \le x^\varepsilon + 1\).

The other choices need more. The proof uses the following theorem on windows for \(\log p\). Fix integers \(n_0 \ge 1\) and \(A \ge 0\) with \(4 \mid n_0\). For every sufficiently large real \(w_0\), every integer \(v > 1\) with \(n_0^v \le w_0^A\), and every real \(u_0 \in [w_0, w_0^A]\), there is a prime \(p \equiv 1 \pmod{n_0^v}\) with \(u_0 \le \log p \le u_0 + w_0\). The window theorem is a consequence of the Siegel–Walfisz theorem [MV], which the proof replaces by a direct contour argument. For \(Q \ge 1\) let \(\vartheta_1(z; Q)\) be the sum of \(\log p\) over the primes \(p \le z\) with \(p \equiv 1 \pmod Q\). By the Siegel–Walfisz theorem there is an absolute \(c > 0\) such that for every \(A\), \(\vartheta_1(z; Q) = z/\varphi(Q) + O_A(z e^{-c\sqrt{\log z}})\) uniformly for \(Q \le (\log z)^A\).

Problem 36.6.3. With \(z = e^{u_0}\) and \(Q = n_0^v\) as in the window theorem, \(\vartheta_1(2z; Q) - \vartheta_1(z; Q) > 0\) for all large \(w_0\). Hence there is a prime \(p \equiv 1 \pmod Q\) with \(u_0 < \log p \le u_0 + \log 2\), which proves the window theorem.

Taking \(n_0 = q_0 d\) and \(v = 2(s + 6)\) gives the modulus \(q_2^2\) for \(p_0, p_1, N\). The proof takes windows of length \(x^{\varepsilon/6}\) in \(\log p\) and combines them with the norm constructions of Problems 36.5.1 and 36.5.2. It obtains the sparse set \(\Phi_b\) from Behrend’s construction (Chapter 4).

Problem 36.6.4. For all sufficiently large integers \(x\), the choices in the construction satisfy \(\log b \le 2x^\varepsilon\), \(m \ge \sqrt x\) and \(\log x_1 \ge x^{\varepsilon/12}\).

Used in: Chapter 38
