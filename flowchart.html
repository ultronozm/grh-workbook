How the chapters fit together

Overview · Index

Each box is a chapter and links to it. The shaded section on top is the proof: a column for each of its Parts, with the chapter stating the Part's results at the head and the sheets that prove them hanging below it, each under the chapter it serves. All of it feeds The main inequality at the top. Below, set apart, Parts I–V treat each ingredient in its simplest setting, a column per Part read from the bottom up: a chapter sits above the chapters it builds on. Hover over a chapter to pick out its arrows. The side series, a model in the function-field setting that the proof does not use, is drawn under the shortest column with dashed boxes.

Legend. Every arrow comes from the Prerequisites paragraph of the chapter it points to.

  • Red arrow: into the main inequality, from the other chapters of Part VI (Data, spaces and the main inequality) and from the chapters stating Theorems A, B and C.
  • Amber line: within a Part of the proof, a sheet hangs under the chapter that names it (a sheet that no chapter of its Part names hangs under the chapter before it). Other arrows between chapters of the proof are left out; each sheet's Prerequisites lists them.
  • Light dashed arrow: a chapter of the proof builds on a chapter of Parts I–V, usually its simplest setting. Arrows from one chapter into one column of the proof are drawn as one branching line.
  • Dark arrow: within Parts I–V, the later chapter builds on the earlier one.
  • An arrow is left out when a path of other arrows already leads from the one chapter to the other.

Sample seminar plans

These are suggestions drafted by AI agents and have not been tested in a seminar. The sessions are 90 minutes. Both plans cover the ingredients of the proof in their simplest settings (Parts I-V) and end with the statements of Theorems A, B and C and the deduction of the main inequality; neither covers the proofs of Theorems A, B and C (Parts VII-IX). Most sessions contain far more problems than can be presented in 90 minutes, so a presenter would choose a few and leave the rest as reading.

One semester, 13 sessions (main line)

  1. Chapter 1, From zeros to a one-sided bound
  2. Chapter 2, Inert primes and the choice of \(d\), with Chapter 3, The places, the weights and the remainder
  3. Chapter 5, Size against divisibility, with Chapter 7, Stepanov without multiplicity as its first application
  4. Chapter 6, A twist for each prime
  5. Chapter 8, The chain and the square root
  6. Chapter 9, Few translates span, with the main theorem of Chapter 10, Poincaré series and near orthogonality stated
  7. Chapter 13, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime, with a recap of Chapter 11, Complex tori, theta functions and periods
  8. Chapter 15, The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime
  9. Chapter 16, The isotropic plane, with the main theorem of Chapter 17, Pairings on a formal group of height two stated
  10. Chapter 20, The Kronecker limit formula and Chowla–Selberg and Chapter 23, Definite theta lifts and their norms
  11. Chapter 29, Congruence modules, with Chapter 30, Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes
  12. Chapter 36, The data and Chapter 37, Spaces, forms and twists, with the statements of Theorems A and B (Chapters 39 and 49)
  13. Chapter 38, The main inequality, with the statement of Chapter 67, Theorem C

Optional on a first pass: Chapters 4, 12, 14, 18, 19, 21, 22, 24–28, 31 and 32; the chapters of Parts VII–IX that prove Theorems B, A and C, Chapters 39, 49, 52, 57, 68, 74, 80, 85 and 86, are reading for the presenters or a second semester.
