Prerequisites.

Valuations on \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) [Ne, Ch. II]; roots of unity of prime power order [Wa, Ch. 1–2]; inertia groups and tame inertia at a finite place. Chapter 87, Lengths as values of polynomials: Definitions 87.2.1, 87.3.1, 87.3.2, 87.3.3 and 87.5.1, the evaluation of characters on \(\mathrm{Cl}_Q\) at the beginning of Section 87.5, and Theorem 87.6.1. Chapter 80, Denominators of projections to lifts: Section 80.1 with its definitions (coefficient rings and normalized lengths, auxiliary twists, main-summand-like representations, Selmer bounds, the numbers \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\) and \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell\)) and Theorem 80.1.1. The data of Chapter 36; twists and critical pairs (Chapter 37, Section 37.6 and Definition 37.10.1); heights of families (Section 5.3) and the twist at each prime of Chapter 6; regular families (Definitions 79.1.2 and 79.1.3). For the deduction of the twisting theorem, use Theorem 89.3.1 and the Selmer data of Chapter 90. As background: Stickelberger elements (Chapter 26) and Selmer classes (Chapter 30).

Chapter 80 bounds the denominator of a projection to lifts through the Selmer groups of twisted Galois representations, and the cost of comparing a lift at two twists involves ratios of Hecke \(L\)-values (Chapter 74). For each prime \(\ell\) and each \(\iota \mid \ell\), can one twist bound the Selmer lengths for a prescribed finite family satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem 85.1.2 and the valuations of the ratios for every ramified critical pair and both signs? The proof reduces both to values of polynomials at points \((\zeta_w-1)_w\), with \(\zeta_w\) roots of unity of \(\ell\)-power order. The lemma that chooses the roots of unity is proved in Chapter 86; Section 85.1 recalls the polynomial input (Theorem 87.6.1), deduces the existence of small twists (Theorem 85.1.1) and states the twisting theorem deduced in Chapter 90; Section 85.2 gives the regular family to which Chapter 67 applies the one-lift bound of Chapter 74.

Throughout, \(\ell\) is a prime and \(\operatorname{val}\) is the valuation of \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) with \(\operatorname{val}(\ell) = 1\) and \(\operatorname{val}(0) = \infty\), as in Chapter 86; \(\operatorname{val}_+ := \max(\operatorname{val}, 0)\), and \(\operatorname{val}_\ell\) is the \(\ell\)-adic valuation of integers. Coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\) and normalized lengths are those of Section 80.1.

§85.1. The twisting theorem Tag 23AQ

Fix a CM field \(E\subseteq\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\), with \(G_E := \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\) and \(g^c := cgc\) for the complex conjugation \(c\) of Section 80.1, a finite set \(\Sigma_0\) of finite places of \(E\) containing those above \(\ell\), and an element \(g_0\) of inertia at a place of \(\Sigma_0\) prime to \(\ell\). Fix finitely many Selmer data over one coefficient ring \(\mathcal{O}\) and finitely many Stickelberger systems for \((\Sigma_0, g_0)\) (Definitions 87.3.1 and 87.5.1). Chapter 30 gives examples of Selmer classes arising from congruences, and Chapter 26 treats the classical cyclotomic Stickelberger element.

From Chapter 87 we use: the rank \(\operatorname{rk}T_{\mathrm{Sel}}\), the set \(\Sigma(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\), the local relations, the terms \(\mathrm{glob}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\), \(\mathrm{plc}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\), \(\mathrm{inr}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\) and the number \(\mathrm{bd}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\) of a Selmer datum (Definition 87.3.1), with \(e_{\mathcal{O}} := \mathrm{ord}_{\mathcal{O}}(\ell)\); the bounds \(\mathrm{len}\,\mathrm{Sel}_Q(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}\chi) \le d\) of Definition 87.3.2; sets \(Q\) admissible for \(\Sigma_0\) and characters for \(Q\) (Definition 87.2.1), that is, locally constant characters of \(G_E\) coming by \(y \mapsto y/\bar y\) from a finite idele class character of tame level \(Q\) trivial above \(\ell\); the elements \(\mathrm{St}_Q \in \bar{\mathbb{Z}}_\ell[\mathrm{Cl}_Q]\) and the number \(\mathrm{bd}(\mathrm{St})\) of a Stickelberger system (Definition 87.5.1), with the value \(\chi(\mathrm{St}_Q)\) of a character \(\chi\) for \(Q\) (Section 87.5); and torsion levels (Definition 87.3.3).

The insertion identity of Definition 87.5.1 has a classical model over \(\mathbb{Q}\). For \(u \ge 2\) put \(\theta^{\mathbb{Q}}_u := \sum_{1 \le a < u,\ (a,u)=1}\{a/u\}\,\sigma_a^{-1}\), so that \(\theta^{\mathbb{Q}}_p\) is the element \(\theta\) of Chapter 26, and evaluate a character \(\chi\) of \((\mathbb{Z}/u)^\times\) on it by \(\chi(\sigma_a) := \chi(a)\); for a prime \(v \nmid u\) we also write \(\chi\) for its pullback to \((\mathbb{Z}/uv)^\times\).

Tag 23AR

Problem 85.1.1. For a nontrivial character \(\chi\) of \((\mathbb{Z}/u)^\times\) and a prime \(v \nmid u\), \(\chi(\theta^{\mathbb{Q}}_{uv}) = (1 - \chi(v)^{-1})\,\chi(\theta^{\mathbb{Q}}_u)\): an insertion identity with \(\alpha\) trivial, one sign and \(L = [0]\). For \(u = 5\), \(\chi(2) = \mathrm i\) and \(v = 3\): \(\chi(\theta^{\mathbb{Q}}_5) = (-3 + \mathrm i)/5\) and \(\chi(\theta^{\mathbb{Q}}_{15}) = (-2 + 4\mathrm i)/5\).

The polynomial input is Theorem 87.6.1; it is read from the proof and developed in Chapter 87. It holds for any finite \(\Sigma_0\) with \(g_0\) inertial at a place of \(\Sigma_0\) prime to \(\ell\); we use it with \(\Sigma_0\) containing the places above \(\ell\). Fix a torsion level \(c_{\mathrm{tor}}\) for the data. The theorem gives an integer \(s_0 \ge 1\) and, for every rational \(\rho > 0\) with \(s_0\rho < 1\) and every sufficiently large \(h_1\), a set \(Q\) with homomorphisms \(\lg_w\colon G_E \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^{h_1}\), \(w \in Q\), and for each datum and each system a polynomial \(F\) and a point, with the properties (1)–(3) stated there. For \(\ell^{h_1}\)-th roots of unity \(\zeta_w\), the product \(\chi_\zeta := \prod_{w \in Q} \zeta_w^{\lg_w}\) is a character for \(Q\) in the sense of Definition 87.2.1. Each \(\lg_w\) is a character for \(\{w\}\) with the value \(1\) at an element of an inertia group at \(w\); thus it is onto on inertia at \(w\), anticyclotomic (meaning \(\lg_w(g^c) = -\lg_w(g)\)), unramified outside \(w\) and \(\bar w\), and trivial on the decomposition groups above \(\ell\).

Two local conditions of the same dimension can give Selmer groups of different lengths.

Tag 23AS

Problem 85.1.2. A continuous cocycle of \(G_{\mathbb{Q}_{11}}\) with values in \(\mathbb F_5\) and trivial action is a homomorphism; it is trivial on wild inertia and is determined by its values \((a, b)\) at a Frobenius lift and at a generator of tame inertia, and all \(25\) pairs occur. The unramified classes form the line \(b = 0\), and the line \(b = 2a\) meets it only in \(0\).

If restriction maps a space of global classes onto the line \(b = 0\), the classes satisfying \(b = 2a\) form the kernel of restriction, of codimension one in that space. Problem 88.2.4 gives conditions under which some choice of slope at a new tied prime decreases the sum of the tied Selmer lengths.

Problem 85.1.3. For \(\chi_\zeta = \prod_{w} \zeta_w^{\lg_w}\) and \(n \ge 1\), \(\chi_\zeta^n = \prod_{w} (\zeta_w^n)^{\lg_w}\), and \(\chi_\zeta^{\ell^{h_1}} = 1\). For \(w \in Q\) some \(g\) in an inertia group at \(w\) has \(\chi_\zeta(g) = \zeta_w\).

Tag 23AU

Problem 85.1.4. Let \(0 < \rho < 1\) and \(m \ge 1\), and let the \(\zeta_w\), \(w \in Q\), be \(\ell^{h_1}\)-th roots of unity with \(\operatorname{val}(\zeta_w - 1) \le \rho/m\). Then \(\operatorname{val}\bigl(\chi_\zeta^n(s_{w'}) - 1\bigr) \le \rho\) for every \(1 \le n \le m\), every \(w' \in Q \cup \bar Q\) and every generator \(s_{w'}\) of tame inertia at a place of \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) above \(w'\). Hence \(\sum_{w' \in Q \cup \bar Q}\operatorname{val}\bigl(\chi_\zeta^n(s_{w'}) - 1\bigr) \le 2\,\#Q\,\rho\).

Problem 85.1.5. Let \(0 < \varepsilon < 1\) and \(m \ge 1\). There are an admissible set \(Q\) and homomorphisms \(\lg_w\) as in Theorem 87.6.1, and a character \(\chi_\zeta = \prod_{w} \zeta_w^{\lg_w}\) such that, for all \(1 \le n \le m\), \(\mathrm{len}\,\mathrm{Sel}_Q(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}\chi_\zeta^n) \le \mathrm{bd}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}) + \varepsilon\) for every datum, \(\operatorname{val}(\chi_\zeta^n(\mathrm{St}_Q)) \le \mathrm{bd}(\mathrm{St}) + \varepsilon\) for every system, \(\chi_\zeta^n\) is ramified at every place of \(Q\), and \(\sum_{w' \in Q \cup \bar Q}\operatorname{val}\bigl(\chi_\zeta^n(s_{w'}) - 1\bigr) \le \varepsilon\) for any generators \(s_{w'}\) of tame inertia.

Tag 23AW

Theorem 85.1.1 (small twists). Let \(\Sigma_0\), \(g_0\), finitely many Selmer data over one coefficient ring \(\mathcal{O}\), finitely many Stickelberger systems and a torsion level \(c_{\mathrm{tor}}\) be as above, and let \(m \ge 1\) be an integer and \(\varepsilon > 0\) real. There are a finite set \(Q\) of places, admissible for \(\Sigma_0\), and a character \(\chi\colon G_E \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^\times\) such that for every \(1 \le n \le m\):

  1. \(\chi^n\) is a character for \(Q\) (Definition 87.2.1), of \(\ell\)-power order, and ramified at every place of \(Q\);

  2. \(\mathrm{len}\,\mathrm{Sel}_Q(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}\chi^n) \le \mathrm{bd}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}) + \varepsilon\) for every datum, and \(\operatorname{val}(\chi^n(\mathrm{St}_Q)) \le \mathrm{bd}(\mathrm{St}) + \varepsilon\) for every system;

  3. \(\sum_{w' \in Q \cup \bar Q}\operatorname{val}\bigl(\chi^n(s_{w'}) - 1\bigr) \le \varepsilon\) for any generators \(s_{w'}\) of tame inertia at any places of \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) above the \(w'\).

Used in: Chapter 87; Chapter 90: Problem 90.4.2

Problems 85.1.3–85.1.5 give Theorem 85.1.1 for \(0 < \varepsilon < 1\). The proof uses the same reduction to the polynomial input.

For the data of Chapter 36, let \(\Sigma_0\) be the set of places dividing \(\ell MN\), and let \(g_0\) be the tame generator at the fixed place above a chosen \(w_0 \nmid \ell\). The proof applies Theorem 85.1.1, with \(\varepsilon = 2^{-\ell}\), to Selmer data on lattices in the given representations and to one Stickelberger system for each ramified critical pair and sign. We use the notation of Chapters 36 and 37 (\(f\), \(r\), \(t\), \(m\), \(b\), \(p_0\), \(p_1\), \(M = p_0p_1\), \(N\), the chosen places \(\mathcal M\), the integer \(\mathcal N\), \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\) with their sets \(Q_\vartheta\)), the critical pairs \(P = (\nu, z)\), their ratios \(R_P(\vartheta, \epsilon)\), \(\epsilon = \pm1\), and ramified pairs of Definition 37.10.1, and the number of Definition 80.1.1, \[\mathrm{tw}_\ell := (4f + 3)\, r\, \operatorname{val}_\ell(\mathcal N) + 2^{-\ell}.\] For a twist \(\vartheta\) and \(\iota\colon \mathbb{C}\simeq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), \(\hat\vartheta\) is the inverse avatar of Definition 80.1.5.

Theorem 85.1.2 (twisting theorem). Let \(\iota\colon \mathbb{C}\simeq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), and let \(R_1, \dots, R_n\) (\(n \ge 0\); not to be confused with the ratios \(R_P\) of clause 3) be \(\ell\)-adic representations of \(G_E\) such that each \(R_i\) is main-summand-like for \((t'_i, \mathrm{HT}_i)\), for some integer \(t'_i \ge 0\) and some family \(\mathrm{HT}_i\) (Definition 80.1.7). There is an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) such that for every \(1 \le j \le m\):

  1. the twist \(\vartheta^j\) is auxiliary with ramification at most \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\) (Definition 80.1.5);

  2. for each \(i\), the twist \(\widehat{\vartheta^j}R_i\) has Selmer bound \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell\) (Definition 80.1.9);

  3. for each ramified critical pair \(P\) and each sign \(\epsilon\), the ratio \(R_P(\vartheta^j, \epsilon)\) of Definition 37.10.1 has \(\operatorname{val}_+\bigl(\iota(R_P(\vartheta^j, \epsilon))\bigr) \le \mathrm{tw}_\ell\).

§85.2. Regular families Tag 23B3

As in Chapter 6, the twist may depend on the prime: it is chosen for every \(\iota \mid \ell\), and families \(y = (y_\iota)\) are measured by their heights (Section 5.3). Regular families and small ratios are those of Definitions 79.1.3 and 79.1.2; the denominators are measured against the budget \(B = U/(mk)\), \(U = fa\log^3 b\).

Tag 23B4

Problem 85.2.1. Let \(e_\ell \ge 0\) be reals with \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0} e_\ell\log \ell \le c\) for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes. If \(\lVert \iota(y_\iota)\rVert \le \ell^{e_\ell}\) for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(\iota \mid \ell\), then \(y\) has height at most \(c\).

Problem 85.2.5. Let \(C_1 \ge 1\) be an integer, \(y \in \mathbb{C}\) and \(c\) real. If \(\lVert\iota(C_1y)\rVert \le e^{c}\), then \(\lVert\iota(y)\rVert \le e^{c + \operatorname{val}_\ell(C_1)\log\ell}\).

For the denominator clause of regularity, the proof uses the ramification and Selmer bounds of Theorem 85.1.2. It takes one main summand for each lift index and each eigensystem occurring on the good forms of \(W_m\), and one rank-one main summand for the untwisted lift from \(U(1)\) for each main pair and its lift index. At \(\vartheta = \theta_\iota^j\) it applies Theorem 80.1.1 to the image of the good-form lift from \(W_j\) and to the line of the twisted lift of the constant function for each main pair for \(j\). It uses that the \(U(1)\) stabilizers at the chosen level are trivial. The logarithmic allowance for the pairing of the projections of two test forms onto that line is \((\mathrm{cl}_\ell + \operatorname{val}_\ell(C_1))\log\ell\); the class number enters as in Problem 85.2.5. This allowance also suffices for clearing the good-form projection. The preceding finite-prime bound puts the sum of these allowances within \(B\).
