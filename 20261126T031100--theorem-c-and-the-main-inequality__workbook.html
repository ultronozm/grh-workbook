Prerequisites.

Hadamard’s inequality for positive definite Hermitian matrices [HJ]. Dirichlet \(L\)-functions: \(L(1, \chi) \ne 0\) for \(\chi\) nonprincipal, induced and primitive characters, and the functional equation [Dav, Ch. 4, Ch. 5, Ch. 8, Ch. 9]. From Chapter 1 the remainder \(R_{d,q}\), written \(R_q\) here for fixed \(d\), the one-sided criterion and the prime number theorem in progressions; from Chapter 3 the identity for the total remainder; from Chapter 36 the data and Problem 36.6.4; from Chapter 37 the objects and the target lattice (Definition 37.11.1); and the statements of Theorems A, B and C (Chapters 49, 39 and 67). The problems in Section 38.1 use no earlier chapter.

Theorems A and B supply many independent target forms, with upper bounds for the lengths of their untwisted lifts and for the index of the family; Theorem C bounds the covolume plus twice the index of the family of target forms from below by the main amount (Section 36.4), times the dimension, less a small error. How do the three statements give the theorem? Comparing the two bounds gives the main inequality for the total remainder \(\mathrm{rem}\). The identity of Theorem 3.2.1 then gives an upper bound for the prime-sum remainder \(R_{q_0}(x)\). We first do the comparison in a lattice, then state the main inequality, and finally pass to the one-sided bound and to the theorem. Parts VII, VIII and IX state and deduce Theorems B, A and C.

§38.1. The comparison in a lattice

Divisibility and size already meet in the plane.

Problem 38.1.1. The columns \((1, 1)\) and \((1, 7)\) are linearly independent and agree modulo \(2\) and modulo \(3\), so their determinant is a nonzero multiple of \(6\); the product of their lengths is \(10\). Hence the determinant is \(\pm 6\).

Let \(N \ge 1\) be an integer, and give \({\mathbb{C}}^d\) the Hermitian form \(\langle v, w \rangle := N\, \bar v^{\mathsf T} w\). For \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in {\mathbb{Q}}^d\) write \(\operatorname{Gram}(y) := \det(\langle y_i, y_j \rangle)_{i,j \le n}\), and let \(H := \log N\).

Problem 38.1.2. If \(\langle y_i, y_i \rangle \le e^{u}\) for \(i = 1, \dots, n\), then \(\operatorname{Gram}(y) \le e^{nu}\); if the \(y_i\) are linearly independent, \(\log \operatorname{Gram}(y) \le nu\).

The comparison itself is an inequality between real numbers. Suppose that a family of \(n\) vectors in a space of dimension \(d \ge 1\) satisfies a lower bound and two upper bounds, \[d(H - 3F) \le c + 2\delta , \qquad c \le n(H - \rho + F) , \qquad \delta \le dI ,\] for real numbers \(H, F, I, \rho, c, \delta\). The upper exponent per vector is \(H - \rho + F\), including the error \(F\).

Problem 38.1.3. If moreover \(H + F \ge 0\) and \(d/2 \le n \le d\), then \(n\rho \le d(4F + 2I)\), and \(\rho \le 2(4F + 2I)\) when \(4F + 2I \ge 0\).

Problem 38.1.4. In the comparison of real numbers above, after dropping the hypothesis \(H + F \ge 0\) one can satisfy all three bounds and \(d/2 \le n \le d\) while \(n\rho > d(4F + 2I)\). (Here \(H\) is any real number, not necessarily \(\log N\).)

In this lattice the main amount sits in the metric. In Chapter 67 the main amount enters through the height of the family \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\), where the twist \(\theta_\iota\) is chosen for each pair \((\ell, \iota)\) (Problem 67.2.5).

§38.2. The main inequality

Let \(\mathrm{rem}\) be the total remainder of Chapter 36, \(U = fa\log^3 b\) the unit of the errors and \(F := U\log b\). Theorems A and B (Chapters 49 and 39) concern the ramified source forms \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), the integral ones \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}}\), the finite group \(\bar G\), and the normalized translates \(A_s \colon S_{\mathrm{ram}} \to T\) of the Poincaré map, \(s \in \bar G\). For \(v \in T\) and \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\), \(\lVert v \rVert_\sigma^2\) is the squared length of Definition 37.11.1, computed from the \(\sigma\)-conjugate coordinates of the untwisted lift \(\Theta_1 v\). Theorems A, B and C are proved in Parts VIII, VII and IX (Chapters 49, 39 and 67); here we use only their statements, in the following form.

  • (Theorem 49.3.2(a), which writes \(\lVert \Theta_1 A_s\varphi \rVert_\sigma\) for the length used here and \(fa\log^4 b\) for \(F\).) \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) is spanned by the set \(S_{\mathrm{short}}\) of \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}}\) with \(\lVert A_s\varphi \rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp(h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ F)\) for every \(s \in \bar G\) and every \(\sigma\).

  • (Theorem 49.3.2(b), which writes \(2fa\log^3 b\) for \(2U\).) If \(J \subseteq \bar G\) is finite and every \(y_i\) lies in \(A_s(S_{\mathrm{ram}}^{\mathrm{int}})\) for some \(s \in J\), then \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) has index at most \(2\,\# J \dim S_{\mathrm{ram}} \cdot U\).

  • (Consequence of Theorem 39.4.2: there the maps \(e_0\rho(s)\mathcal{P}\) on \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) have the same ranges as the \(A_s\).) \(d_T > 0\), and there is a finite \(J \subseteq \bar G\) with \(\# J \dim S_{\mathrm{ram}} \le 5d_T\log(1 + 2b^2)\) and \(\dim \sum_{s \in J} A_s(S_{\mathrm{ram}}) \ge (1 - 4/b^2)d_T\).

Theorem C (Theorem 67.2.1), in full, is the lower bound: if \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) are linearly independent target forms with \((1 - 4/b^2)d_T \le n\), of index at most \(\delta\) and covolume at most \(c\), then \[d_T(h_{\mathrm{main}}- 3fa\log^4 b) \le c + 2\delta ;\] index and covolume are those of the target lattice (Definition 37.11.1).

The upper bound for the covolume is Hadamard’s inequality: if \(\lVert z_i \rVert_\sigma^2 \le e^u\) for \(i = 1, \dots, n\) and every \(\sigma\), then \(z_1, \dots, z_n\) has covolume at most \(nu\).

Problem 38.2.1. For \(J\) as in (B), the span of \(\bigcup_{s \in J} A_s(S_{\mathrm{short}})\) is \(\sum_{s \in J} A_s(S_{\mathrm{ram}})\). Hence that space has a basis \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) with each \(y_i \in A_s(S_{\mathrm{short}})\) for some \(s \in J\), and \((1 - 4/b^2)d_T \le n \le d_T\).

Problem 38.2.2. This family has covolume at most \(n(h_{\mathrm{main}}- m\,\mathrm{rem}+ F)\) and index at most \(d_T \cdot 10\log(1 + 2b^2)\, U\).

Problem 38.2.3. \(n\, m\,\mathrm{rem}\le d_T\bigl(4F + 20\log(1 + 2b^2)\,U\bigr)\).

Problem 38.2.4. \(n \ge d_T/2\), and \(8F + 40\log(1 + 2b^2)\, U \le fa\log^5 b\). Hence \(m\,\mathrm{rem}\le fa\log^5 b\).

Theorem 38.2.1 (Main inequality). \(m\,\mathrm{rem}\le fa\log^5 b\).

Used in: Chapter 3; Chapter 8

In the Lean code as first released, the inequality called \((M)\) bounds the excess of the prime-power sums over their main terms, summed over cells (as indexed in [Nas]) and \(m\) weight tables for each allowed translation. Here the single weight function of Chapter 3 has two cutoffs, and the same remainder is counted \(m\) times.

The errors per dimension are: \(F\) from the size in Theorem A, \(20\log(1 + 2b^2)\,U\) from the index in Theorems A and B (counted twice, as it enters as \(2\delta\)), and \(3F\) from Theorem C.

Keeping the factor \(d_T/n\) from Problems 38.2.1 and 38.2.3 gives \(m\,\mathrm{rem}\le (44 + o(1))fa(\log b)^4\) as \(b \to \infty\), so there is a logarithm to spare.

§38.3. From the main inequality to the theorem

Let \(q_0 \ge 1\) and \(d\) be the parameters of the data, with \(18480 \mid q_0\) and \(d\) prime. By Problem 36.1.1, \(d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \pmod{q_0}\), so \(d\) is odd and prime to \(q_0\). Recall that, for \(q \ge 1\) prime to \(d\), \(R_q(y) = \sum_{n \le y} \lambda(n)(1/n - 1/y) - \log y/\varphi(q) - c_q\), with \(\lambda(n) = \Lambda(n)(1 - (n/d))\) for \(n \equiv 1 \pmod q\) and \(\lambda(n) = 0\) otherwise, where \((n/d)\) is the Legendre symbol. Here \(c_q = c_{d,q}\) is as in Chapter 1, and Problem 1.4.7 gives \(R_q(y) \to 0\) as \(y \to \infty\). When \(d\) varies we write \(R_q^{(d)} = R_{d,q}\).

For an admissible datum, \(m > 0\) and \(b > 1\). In Chapter 36, Problem 36.2.2 gives \(\#\Phi_a = T(s)\), so Definition 36.3.1 gives \(\#\Phi_a \le \#\Phi_b \log b\).

Problem 38.3.1. For every admissible datum, the main inequality gives \[R_{q_0}(x) \le \frac{\varphi(q_0)(\log b)^6}{m} + \lvert R_{q_0}(x_1)\rvert + \log b\,\lvert R_1(x_1)\rvert .\]

Used in: Chapter 3

Two further inputs. By the prime number theorem in progressions [MV], for every prime \(d \ne 2\), every \(q \ge 1\) prime to \(d\) and every integer \(B \ge 0\), one has \(\lvert R_q(y) \rvert \le (\log y)^{-B}\) for all large \(y\) (Chapter 1). By Theorem 36.6.1, for \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/100\) and every large integer \(x\) there are data with this \(x\), with \(\log b \le 2x^\varepsilon\), \(m \ge \sqrt x\) and \(\log x_1 \ge x^{\varepsilon/12}\) (the bounds of Problem 36.6.4).

Problem 38.3.2. Let \(\varepsilon > 0\). For all large integers \(x\) and every real \(y\) with \(\log y \ge x^{\varepsilon/12}\), one has \(\lvert R_{q_0}(y) \rvert \le x^{-2}\) and \(\lvert R_1(y) \rvert \le x^{-2}\).

The three public versions of the Lean proof treat the cutoff \(x_1\) differently. The readable edition, followed here, bounds both absolute values at the prescribed \(x_1\) by the prime number theorem for a fixed modulus. The compact edition needs only an upper bound for the difference \(R_{q_0}(x_1) - (\#\Phi_a/\#\Phi_b) R_1(x_1)\) of Problem 3.3.1, at one \(x_1\). For every large \(t\) and every ratio in \([0,e^t]\), it finds \(\log x_1 \in [t,t^2]\) at which the difference is at most \(e^{-t}\), and takes \(t = 10^6\log x\). The two transforms continue across \(\operatorname{Re} s = 1\), since Dirichlet \(L\)-functions do not vanish there, and Cauchy’s estimate bounds their high moments. The level \(N\) is then chosen after the cutoff; the construction of the data still uses primes in a progression. The code as first released [Rel] instead assumes a bound on arbitrarily late windows \([Y,Y^{1+\delta}]\), and compares high derivatives of the transform with the contributions of its poles.

Problem 38.3.3. Let \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/100\). For all large integers \(x\), \(R_{q_0}(x) \le (64\varphi(q_0) + 3)\, x^{-1/2 + 6\varepsilon}\).

Problem 38.3.4. For \(q \ge 1\) prime to \(d\) and real \(y \ge 2\), \(R_q(y) - R_q(\lfloor y \rfloor) \le 3/y\). For \(-1 \le u \le 0\), \(\lfloor y \rfloor^{u} \le 2y^{u}\).

Problem 38.3.5. Let \(\varepsilon > 0\). For all large real \(y\), \(R_{q_0}(y) \le y^{-1/2 + \varepsilon}\).

Theorem 38.3.1 (One-sided bound). Let \(q_0 \ge 1\) be a multiple of \(18480\), let \(d \equiv q_0/2 - 1 \pmod{q_0}\) be a prime, and let \(\varepsilon > 0\). Then \(R_{q_0}(y) \le y^{-1/2 + \varepsilon}\) for all large real \(y\).

Used in: Chapter 3

The criterion of Chapter 1 reads: let \(q_0 \ge 1\) be a multiple of \(8\), let \(d_0 \ge 0\) be an integer with \(d_0 \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) and prime to \(q_0\), and let \(\beta > \frac12\) (in this section only, \(\beta\) denotes this exponent); if for every prime \(d \equiv d_0 \pmod{q_0}\) one has \(R^{(d)}_{q_0}(y) \le y^{\beta - 1}\) for all large \(y\), then \(L(\rho, \psi) \ne 0\) for every character \(\psi\) modulo \(q_0\) and every \(\rho \ne 1\) with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho > \beta\).

Problem 38.3.6. For \(q \ge 1\), \(q_0 := 18480q\) and \(d_0 := 9240q - 1\): \(d_0 \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), \(\gcd(d_0, q_0) = 1\), and \(d_0 = q_0/2 - 1\).

Problem 38.3.7. Let \(q \ge 1\), let \(\chi\) be a character modulo \(q\), and let \(\chi'\) be the character modulo \(q_0 = 18480q\) that it induces. If \(\chi\) is nonprincipal or \(s \ne 1\), then \(L(s, \chi') = L(s, \chi)\prod_{p \mid q_0}(1 - \chi(p)p^{-s})\), so \(L(s, \chi') \ne 0\) implies \(L(s, \chi) \ne 0\).

Problems 38.3.6 and 38.3.7, Theorem 38.3.1, the criterion and \(L(1, \chi) \ne 0\) for \(\chi\) nonprincipal prove the following.

Theorem 38.3.2. Let \(q \ge 1\), let \(\chi\) be a Dirichlet character modulo \(q\), and let \(s \in {\mathbb{C}}\) with \(\operatorname{Re} s > \frac12\), where \(s \ne 1\) if \(\chi\) is principal. Then \(L(s, \chi) \ne 0\).

Problem 38.3.8. Every zero \(s\) of a Dirichlet \(L\)-function with \(0 < \operatorname{Re} s < 1\) has \(\operatorname{Re} s = \frac12\).

Used in: Chapter Overview

Theorem 38.3.2 is the half-plane form of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions. Thus Theorems A, B and C give the main inequality, from which the one-sided bound and the criterion of Chapter 1 yield Theorem 38.3.2.
