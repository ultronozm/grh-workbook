Prerequisites.

Tate’s unfolding of the global zeta integral and its Euler product [CF, Ch. XV, §§4.4–4.5]; Poisson summation on \(\mathbb Z^2\); for Hecke \(L\)-series see also [Neu, Ch. VII, §8]. From Chapter 37: tame characters, their weight and tame part (Section 37.6), and the partial Hecke values \(L^S(z,\nu)\) (Definition 37.10.1). From Chapter 69: Problem 69.2.1; its theta integrals, and Chapter 12 (Fock models), are background only.

In the determinant of a chain (Chapter 68) each pair of lifts contributes ratios of Hecke \(L\)-values. They come from an identity \(I^\sharp = c\,I^\flat\) between a theta integral over a large unitary group and one over a small group, whose scalar has \(\lvert c\rvert^2 = C\mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu)\), with \(\mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu) = (\operatorname{Nm}S)^{h^2}\prod_{z=1}^h\lvert L^S(z,\nu)\rvert^2\) and \(C > 0\) independent of the character. Why this factor? Removing a hyperbolic plane lowers the index of the exchange factor by one, at the cost of one completed \(L\)-value, and a separate equal-rank calculation supplies the equal-rank part of the normalization. The instances are of matching, of \(\mathrm{ex}_h\) over \(\mathbb Q(\mathrm{i})\) and of one step; the identity itself is only specialized.

§71.1. Matched functions

Problem 71.1.1. Over \(\mathbb F_3\) let \(\chi\) be the quadratic character, \(\Psi(a,c) := \chi\) of the first nonzero entry of \((a,c)\), \(\Psi(0,0) := 0\), and \(\Phi(x,y) := \Psi(y,-x)\). Then \[\Phi(\text{last row of } k^{-1}) = \chi(\det k)\, \Psi(\text{first column of } k)\] for all \(48\) matrices \(k \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\), and the common value is \(+1\) for \(24\) of them.

Problem 71.1.2. Let \(A\) be a commutative ring, \(n = n^\flat+ n^\sharp\), and \(k \in \mathrm{GL}_n(A)\). For a set \(I\) of \(n^\flat\) rows, the minor of the first \(n^\flat\) columns of \(k\) on the rows \(I\) is \(\pm\det k\) times the minor of the last \(n^\sharp\) rows of \(k^{-1}\) on the columns not in \(I\). The last \(n^\sharp\) rows of \(k^{-1}\) times the first \(n^\flat\) columns of \(k\) is \(0\); over a field the row span of the former and the column span of the latter are each other’s annihilators.

Definition 71.1.3. Let \(A\) be a finite commutative ring, \(\chi\) a character of \(A^\times\), and \(\sigma \colon \mathcal B \sqcup \mathcal A \to \mathcal N\) a bijection of finite sets (in this sheet \(\mathcal N\), \(\mathcal A\), \(\mathcal B\) are coordinate sets), \(\#\mathcal B = n^\flat\), \(\#\mathcal A = n^\sharp\); for \(k \in \mathrm{GL}_{\mathcal N}(A)\) write \(k_{\mathcal B}\) for its columns indexed by \(\sigma(\mathcal B)\) and \((k^{-1})_{\mathcal A}\) for the rows of \(k^{-1}\) indexed by \(\sigma(\mathcal A)\). Functions \(\Phi\) on \(\mathcal A \times \mathcal N\) matrices and \(\Psi\) on \(\mathcal N \times \mathcal B\) matrices over \(A\) are matched for \((\sigma, \chi)\) if \(\Phi\) vanishes off the matrices \((k^{-1})_{\mathcal A}\), \(\Psi\) vanishes off the matrices \(k_{\mathcal B}\), and \(\Phi((k^{-1})_{\mathcal A}) = \chi(\det k)\, \Psi(k_{\mathcal B})\) for every \(k \in \mathrm{GL}_{\mathcal N}(A)\).

Used in: Chapter 73: Problem 73.2.4

Problem 71.1.1 gives a matched pair with \(n^\flat= n^\sharp= 1\).

§71.2. The two theta integrals

Let \(E\) be a CM field with a CM type and Weil data as in the proof (the field \(E\) of the data is one), \(\upsilon_E\) (in this sheet) the splitting character of the Weil data on the ideles of \(E\), and \(\upsilon\) its restriction to the norm-one ideles, as in Section 37.7. For a character \(\nu\) of the norm-one idele classes put \(\tilde\nu(x) := \nu(x/\bar x)\). As in Section 37.6, with \(\mu\) in place of \(0\), \(\nu\) has weight \(\mu\colon \{\text{infinite places}\} \to \mathbb Z\) (in this sheet \(\mu\) is a weight; the characters \(\mu^\flat\), \(\mu_j^0\) do not occur) and tame part \((\psi_w)\), characters of \(k_w^\times\) almost all trivial, if \(\nu(u) = \prod_\tau u_\tau^{\mu(\tau)}\prod_w \psi_w(u_w \bmod w)\) for every norm-one idele \(u\) with integral finite part.

Definition 71.2.1. A modulus is a nonzero ideal \(\mathfrak m \subseteq \mathcal O_E\) such that no prime \(w\) divides \(\mathfrak m\) together with its conjugate \(\bar w\); \(S_{\mathfrak m}\) is the set of primes dividing \(\mathfrak m\). A character \(\nu\) is tame for \((\mathfrak m, \mu, \chi)\), where \(\chi\) is a character of \((\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m)^\times\), if \(S_{\mathfrak m} \ne \varnothing\) and \(\nu\) has weight \(\mu\) and a tame part \((\psi_w)\) with \(\psi_w \ne 1\) exactly for \(w \in S_{\mathfrak m}\) and \(\chi(y) = \prod_{w \in S_{\mathfrak m}}\psi_w(y \bmod w)\).

Used in: Chapter 72; Chapter 78: Theorem 78.5.1; Chapter 79

Let \(V^0\), \(V^\sharp\), \(V^\flat\) be framed Hermitian spaces over \(E\) (Section 37.3) on coordinate sets \(\mathcal N\), \(\mathcal A\), \(\mathcal B\), of ranks \(n_0\), \(n^\sharp\), \(n^\flat\), with a bijection \(\sigma\colon \mathcal B \sqcup \mathcal A \to \mathcal N\) (so \(n_0 = n^\sharp+ n^\flat\)). Assume: the number \(q_-\) of negative signs of \(V^0\) is the sum of those of \(V^\sharp\) and \(V^\flat\); \(n^\sharp= n^\flat+ 2h\) with \(h \in \mathbb N\); \(2q_- + 2 \le n_0\); and \(n_0 \le 2\mu(\tau) + 2q_-\) for every infinite place \(\tau\), for an integer-valued \(\mu\). For a Schwartz vector \(x\) of the archimedean Fock model of \((V^0, V^\sharp)\) and a function \(\Phi\) on \(\mathcal A \times \mathcal N\) matrices over \(\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m\), \([\Phi, x]\) is the adelic datum equal to \(x\) at the infinite places, to \(\Phi\) read modulo \(\mathfrak m\) on the matrices integral at \(S_{\mathfrak m}\) (and \(0\) on the others) at the places of \(S_{\mathfrak m}\), and to the vacuum elsewhere; likewise \([\Psi, v]\) for \((V^\flat, V^0)\). Put \(\nu^0 := \nu^{-1}\upsilon^{n_0/2}\), a character for \(V^0\); characters \(\nu^\sharp\) for \(V^\sharp\) and \(\nu^\flat\) for \(V^\flat\) are compatible with \(\nu\) if \(\tilde\nu^\sharp\upsilon_E^{-n^\sharp}\cdot\tilde\nu^\flat\upsilon_E^{-n^\flat} = \tilde\nu^{-1}\), equivalently \(\nu^\sharp\nu^\flat = \nu^0\). The two functions of \(g \in \mathrm{U}(V^0)(\mathbb A)\) to be compared are \[I^\sharp(g) := \int_{[\mathrm{U}(V^\sharp)]}\theta^{V^0,V^\sharp}_{\nu^0,\nu^\sharp}[\Phi, x](g, h_1)\,dh_1, \quad I^\flat(g) := \int_{[\mathrm{U}(V^\flat)]}\theta^{V^\flat,V^0}_{\nu^\flat,\nu^0}[\Psi, v](h_2, g)\,dh_2,\] integrals of theta kernels over the quotients with their quotient measures. Theorem 71.5.1 gives, for each \(v\), an \(x\) making the two integrals proportional on matched data for every compatible pair of characters.

Problem 71.2.2. The last two hypotheses say \(\mu(\tau) \ge n_0/2 - q_- \ge 1\) for all \(\tau\). The ranks \((n_0, n^\sharp, n^\flat; q_-) = (2,1,1;0), (4,3,1;1), (6,5,1;2)\), with all \(q_-\) negative signs in \(V^\sharp\), satisfy the hypotheses for every \(\mu \ge 1\), with \(h = 0, 1, 2\), and \(n_0/2 - q_- = 1\) each time; these successive signatures can be realized by adjoining a hyperbolic plane to \(V^0\) and to \(V^\sharp\). For \(n^\flat= 0\) the smallest case with \(h \ge 1\) is \((2,2,0;0)\).

In the case \((2,2,0;0)\), \(V^\flat= 0\) and \(I^\flat\) is \(\operatorname{vol}[\mathrm{U}(0)]\) times the theta kernel of \((0, V^0)\); Theorem 71.5.1 says that \(I^\sharp\) is \(c\) times it, with \(\lvert c\rvert^2 = C\,\operatorname{Nm}S_{\mathfrak m}\,\lvert L^{S_{\mathfrak m}}(1,\nu)\rvert^2\).

For a finite set \(S\) of finite places put \(\operatorname{Nm}S := \prod_{w \in S} q_w\), \(q_w = \#k_w\). Recall (Definition 37.10.1) that for \(z > 1\), \(L^S(z,\nu)\) is the product of \((1-\tilde\nu_w(\varpi_w)q_w^{-z})^{-1}\) over \(w \notin S\cup\bar S\), at chosen uniformizers \(\varpi_w\), and at \(z = 1\) its limit from the right whenever that limit exists and is finite. For finite \(S\) and \(\nu\) with a tame part and a weight that is not identically zero, the values \(L^S(z,\nu)\), \(z \ge 1\) real, are finite and nonzero: for \(z > 1\) the Euler product converges absolutely, and at \(z = 1\) this is Hecke’s nonvanishing on the line \(\operatorname{Re} s = 1\) [La, Ch. XV]. Status: classical; the proof establishes it in this form.

Definition 71.2.3. For \(h \in \mathbb N\), and for real \(z \ge 1\), whenever the partial values in the formulas exist and are finite, define the exchange factor and the completed value by \[\mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu) := (\operatorname{Nm}S)^{h^2}\prod_{z'=1}^h\lvert L^S(z',\nu)\rvert^2, \qquad \hat L_S(z,\nu) := (\operatorname{Nm}S)^{z - 1/2}\lvert L^S(z,\nu)\rvert ,\] and \(\mathrm{ex}_{-h}(S,\nu) := \mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu)^{-1}\) when \(\mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu)\) is defined and nonzero.

Used in: Chapter 68; Chapter 72; Chapter 75: Definition 75.3.1; Chapter 89

Problem 71.2.4. \(\mathrm{ex}_0(S,\nu) = 1\). For \(h \in \mathbb N\), if the values \(L^S(j,\nu)\) are finite for the integers \(1 \le j \le h+1\) and nonzero for \(1 \le j \le h\), then \(\mathrm{ex}_{h+1}(S,\nu)/\mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu) = \hat L_S(h+1,\nu)^2\).

Problem 71.2.5. Assume \(L^S(j,\nu)\) is finite and nonzero for every integer \(j \ge 1\). For \(d \in \mathbb Z\) let \(z_d := d+1\) if \(d \ge 0\) and \(z_d := -d\) if \(d \le -1\). Then \(z_d \ge 1\) and \(\mathrm{ex}_{d+1}/\mathrm{ex}_d = \hat L_S(z_d,\nu)^2\) for every \(d \in \mathbb Z\).

§71.3. The factor over \(\mathbb Q(\mathrm{i})\)

In this section only, the field is \(\mathbb Q(\mathrm{i})\) and \(\alpha\) runs over \(\mathbb Z[\mathrm{i}] \smallsetminus 0\). The Hecke character with \(\tilde\nu((\alpha)) := (\alpha/\bar\alpha)^4\) is unramified at all finite places and trivial on the ideles of \(\mathbb Q\), so it has the form \(x \mapsto \nu(x/\bar x)\) for a character \(\nu\) of the norm-one ideles, as the notation says; its \(L\)-function is \(L(z) := \frac14\sum_\alpha \alpha^8\lvert\alpha\rvert^{-2z-8}\) for real \(z > 1\).

Problem 71.3.1. For \(y > 0\), \(F_8(y) := \sum_\alpha \alpha^8 e^{-\pi y\lvert\alpha\rvert^2}\) satisfies \(F_8(1/y) = y^9F_8(y)\), and with \(G_s(y_0) := \int_1^\infty e^{-y_0y}y^s\,dy/y\) for \(y_0 > 0\), \[L(z) = \frac{\pi^{z+4}}{4\,\Gamma(z+4)}\sum_\alpha \alpha^8\bigl[G_{z+4}(\pi\lvert\alpha\rvert^2) + G_{5-z}(\pi\lvert\alpha\rvert^2)\bigr]\] for \(z > 1\); with \(1/\Gamma\) read as an entire function, the right side is defined for all real \(z\). By machine, \(L(1) \approx 1.5012288658\) and \(L(2) \approx 1.2576665537\).

§71.4. Removing a plane

In the comparisons and equal-rank ratios of this section and the next, the indexing character \(\nu\) (briefly, a character) has the fixed weight \(\mu\) of Section 71.2 and a specified tame part whose nontrivial components form a nonempty finite set \(S\) disjoint from \(\bar S\). Here \(\mu \ge 1\) everywhere, and \(S = S_{\mathfrak m}\) for a triple of Definition 71.2.1. The kernel characters \(\nu^\sharp, \nu^\flat\) range over all pairs compatible with \(\nu\). The proof has three moves, which we describe in outline; the precise vectors, standard data and normalizations are those of the proof. Let \(\mathbb H\) be a hyperbolic plane, \(V^0_+ := \mathbb H \oplus V^0\), and \((V^\flat)^-\) the space \(V^\flat\) with the opposite form, as in Chapter 69. First, at the top the common space is \(\mathbb H^{n^\flat} \oplus V^0_+\) and the partners are \((V^\flat)^- \oplus V^0_+\) and \((V^\flat)^-\); from prescribed Schwartz vectors the proof builds vectors for the top pairs, and unfolding central Fourier coefficients and a vanishing criterion for singular forms give a fixed real \(\lambda\) and, for each character, \(c \in \mathbb C\) with \(T_{\mathrm L} = c\,T_{\mathrm S}\) on standard row and column data, where \(T_{\mathrm L}, T_{\mathrm S}\) are intermediate large and small theta integrals (both integrated in the partner variable), and \(\lambda\lvert c\rvert^2 = \mathrm{ex}_{n_0/2+1}(S,\nu)\,\mathrm{er}\), \(\mathrm{er}\) the equal-rank ratio of Section 71.5. The adapted \(n^\flat\)-frame is the basis of one of the two dual isotropic subspaces in the \(n^\flat\)-plane splitting of the common space. In the adelic coordinates of the common space, the splitting is adapted when its combined basis has orthonormal columns for the usual positive definite inner product at infinity and integral entries at the finite places. For the standard column of the adapted \(n^\flat\)-frame, the matrix Gauss sum reduces the comparison to central Fourier coefficients of \(T_{\mathrm L} - c T_{\mathrm S}\) with skew-Hermitian index \(T\) such that \(\delta^{-1}T\) is unimodular (integral with integral inverse) at \(S\), where \(\delta\) generates the local inverse differents. At standard lattice level off \(S \cup \bar S\), the criterion tests these coefficients at points acting on the frame by \(\gamma\) with \(\delta^{-1}\gamma^*T\gamma\) unimodular at every finite place and \(\gamma = 1\) at infinity and on \(S \cup \bar S\), provided the two partial means at points fixing the frame there are invariant under the left shears \(1 + ua\) at \(S\) with their unitary partners at \(\bar S\), where \(u\) generates either isotropic line of the extra adapted plane and \(au = 0\) for the corresponding mean. Second, the proof removes \(n^\flat+1\) planes, keeping \((V^\flat)^-\) fixed. At each step it takes both constant terms along an isotropic line of the common space and uses a large partner with the prescribed signs and an adapted splitting. The index decreases by one at each step, from \(n_0/2+1\) to \(h\) (Problem 71.4.2). The proof then passes to matched functions and to the adjoint model, lifts the data modulo \(\mathfrak m\), and twists both kernels, to obtain \(I^\sharp\) and \(I^\flat\) for every compatible pair. Third, a vector with nonzero constant \(\mathrm{er}\) is found (Section 71.5). (The letters \(\lambda\), \(\lambda'\), \(\lambda_i\), \(c_{\mathrm{pl}}\), \(C_{\mathrm{pl}}\), \(c_{\mathrm{sl}}\), the index \(d\), and the spaces \(W\), \(V\), \(Y\), \(J\) of this section and the next are local to this sheet.)

In the proof the small constant term is a sum over rational vectors of \((V^\flat)^-\), of which only the index zero survives.

Problem 71.4.1. Let \(E\) be any CM field, \(\nu\) a unitary character of its norm-one idele classes, \(S\) a finite set of finite places, \(z > 1\) real, and \(f = \otimes_w f_w\) Schwartz–Bruhat on \(\mathbb A_E\); the quotient measure is induced by \(d^\times y\) and counting measure. Then \[\int_{\mathbb A_E^\times/E^\times}\sum_{a \in E^\times} f(a y)\,\tilde\nu(y)\lvert y\rvert^z\,d^\times y = \int_{\mathbb A_E^\times} f(y)\,\tilde\nu(y)\lvert y\rvert^z\,d^\times y ,\] and if \(\tilde\nu\) is unramified and \(f_w = 1_{\mathcal O_w}\) at every finite \(w \notin S \cup \bar S\), the finite part of the right side is \(L^S(z,\nu)\) times the integrals of \(f_w\tilde\nu_w\lvert\cdot\rvert^z\) at \(w \in S \cup \bar S\) (Haar measures with \(\operatorname{vol}(\mathcal O_w^\times) = 1\)). In Problem 71.3.1, \(E = \mathbb Q(\mathrm{i})\), \(S = \varnothing\), \(f_\infty(t) = t^8e^{-\pi\lvert t\rvert^2}\), \(f_w = 1_{\mathcal O_w}\), and the finite factor is \(L(z)\).

On the large side the zero vector is killed by the datum and the surviving line orbit unfolds as in Problem 71.4.1. In outline, the one-plane formula reads as follows (the standard row and column data, built from an integral matrix at \(S\) and \(\tilde\nu^{-1}\) of a determinant, the adapted frames and the cone vectors are specified in the proof). Let the common space be \(\mathbb H \oplus W\) and the large partner \(\mathbb H \oplus V\), with adapted splittings, the small partner \((V^\flat)^-\) fixed, \(\operatorname{rk} W = \operatorname{rk} V + n^\flat\) even, and \(d := \operatorname{rk} V - \operatorname{rk} W/2\). There is \(C_{\mathrm{pl}} > 0\) independent of the character and, for each character, \(c_{\mathrm{pl}} \in \mathbb C\) with \(\lvert c_{\mathrm{pl}}\rvert = C_{\mathrm{pl}}\hat L_S(z_d, \nu)\), \(z_d\) as in Problem 71.2.5, such that on the standard row data, for every Schwartz vector \(x\) and \(g \in \mathrm{U}(W)(\mathbb A)\), the constant term of the large integral at the Levi point \((1, g)\) is \(c_{\mathrm{pl}}\) times the downstairs large integral at \(g\) of the cone vector of \(x\) (the dual cone if \(d \le -1\), where Poisson summation precedes evaluation, so that only values of \(L^S\) at points \(\ge 1\) occur). On the standard column data the small constant term at \((1, g)\) is \(c_{\mathrm{sl}}\) times the downstairs small integral of the zero slice, \(c_{\mathrm{sl}} \ne 0\) a fixed vacuum factor. Status: for cuspidal input Rallis proves the tower property for the orthogonal–symplectic pair [Ra84, Thm. I.1.1], and Wu gives the unitary tower property [Wu, Prop. 3.1]; the proof computes the contribution of the surviving line orbit for the character and the ramified standard data.

Problem 71.4.2. On the standard data of the one-plane formula, suppose \(T_{\mathrm L} = c\,T_{\mathrm S}\) upstairs with \(\lambda\lvert c\rvert^2 = \mathrm{ex}_{d+1}\,\mathrm{er}\). Then downstairs, for the cone vector and the zero slice, \(T_{\mathrm L} = c'\,T_{\mathrm S}\) with \(c' = c\,c_{\mathrm{sl}}/c_{\mathrm{pl}}\) and \(\lambda'\lvert c'\rvert^2 = \mathrm{ex}_d\,\mathrm{er}\), where \(\lambda' = \lambda C_{\mathrm{pl}}^2/\lvert c_{\mathrm{sl}}\rvert^2\) does not depend on the character (note \(c_{\mathrm{pl}} \ne 0\)).

§71.5. The constant of equal rank

After the planes are removed, \(\lambda\lvert c\rvert^2 = \mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu)\,\mathrm{er}\), where the equal-rank ratio of a Schwartz vector \(v_+\) for \((V^0_+, V^0_+)\) is \(\mathrm{er} = \lvert I_+(v_+)(1)\rvert^2/\mathrm{ex}_{n_0/2+1}\), with \(I_+(v_+)\) the large theta integral of \((V^0_+, V^0_+)\), with \(\nu^{-1}\upsilon^{n_0/2+1}\) in both slots, for \(v_+\) and the standard function equal to \(\tilde\nu^{-1}(\det)\) on \(\mathrm{GL}_{n_0+2}(\prod_{w \in S}\mathcal O_w)\) and \(0\) elsewhere. This integral is constant in the group variable, so \(\mathrm{er}\) is a number. More generally, for an even-rank space \(Y\) the ratio for \((Y,Y)\) uses the same construction with \(\operatorname{rk}Y\) in place of \(n_0+2\); for \(Y = \mathbb H \oplus J\) with \(\operatorname{rk}J = 2k_0\) its denominator is \(\mathrm{ex}_{k_0+1}(S,\nu)\). For the rest of this section fix \(k_0 \in \mathbb N\) with \(k_0 \le \mu(\tau)\) at every infinite place. The next two facts enter the proof that a suitable \(v_+\) has a nonzero constant ratio. Continuation: for a character with its set \(S\), the values \(L^S(z,\nu)\), \(z \ge 1\), extend real-analytically to \((-k_0,\infty)\), nonzero at every integer \(j > -k_0\); for integers \(-k_0 < j \le 0\) the functional equation in absolute values relates \(j\) to \(1-j \ge 1\), where the nonvanishing of Section 71.2 applies. For the classical continuation and functional equation see [CF, Ch. XV, §§4.4–4.5]. Descent of vanishing: let \(J\) be the standard positive definite space of rank \(2k_0\); if at one character the equal-rank ratio on \(\mathbb H \oplus J\) vanishes for every Schwartz vector, then the large integral for \((\mathbb H^{2k_0} \oplus \mathbb H \oplus J, J^- \oplus \mathbb H \oplus J)\) vanishes on every Schwartz vector and standard row datum, and by the continuation this descends through \(2k_0+1\) planes to \((J, 0)\); this is read from the proof and not developed.

Problem 71.5.1. For the twisted character of Problem 71.3.2 (ramified at \(\mathfrak p\) and \(\bar{\mathfrak p}\), so \(L^S\) is its full \(L\)-function), by machine the continuation has \(\lvert L^S(0)\rvert \approx 55.2242116195\) and \(\lvert L^S(-1)\rvert \approx 6198.83038564\).

By the functional equation [CF, Ch. XV, §4.4], if \(\tilde\nu\) has conductor \(\prod_{w \in S}w\bar w\), then \(\lvert L^S(1-z,\nu)\rvert = (\operatorname{Nm}S)^{2z-1}c_z\lvert L^S(z,\nu)\rvert\) (left side continued) for integers \(z \ge 1\) with \(z \le \mu(\tau)\) at every \(\tau\), with \(c_z > 0\) depending on \(z\), the weight and the field. For the character of Problem 71.3.2 it holds for \(1 \le z \le 4\) with \(c_z = \pi^{1-2z}\Gamma(z+4)/\Gamma(5-z)\). At a prime of conductor exponent one the local Fourier transform is a Gauss sum on a shifted unit group:

Problem 71.5.2. For a Schwartz vector for \((\mathbb H \oplus J, \mathbb H \oplus J)\), exchanging with \((J, J, 0)\) of index \(k_0\) and \((J, 0, J)\) of index \(-k_0\), the proof finds that \(\mathrm{er}^2\) does not depend on the character, the factors \(\mathrm{ex}_{\pm k_0}\) cancelling; with the descent of vanishing, some vector has \(\mathrm{er}\) equal to one positive constant for every character.

Now take \(k_0 = n_0/2 - q_-\), allowed by Problem 71.2.2. Each time a plane is added the proof finds a new Schwartz vector whose ratio is a positive multiple, independent of the character, of the preceding one; after \(q_-\) steps and a transfer to the space with the signs of \(V^0_+\) there is \(v_+\) with \(\mathrm{er} = C_0\) for every character, \(C_0 \ne 0\).

Problem 71.5.4. Fix this \(C_0\) and the real coefficient \(\lambda\) of the comparison, both independent of \((\mathfrak m, \nu, \chi)\), and suppose \(\lambda\lvert c\rvert^2 = \mathrm{ex}_h(S,\nu)C_0\) for every character. If a character exists then \(c \ne 0\), \(\lambda \ne 0\), and \(C := C_0/\lambda = \lvert c\rvert^2/\mathrm{ex}_h > 0\) does not depend on \((\mathfrak m, \nu, \chi)\).

Theorem 71.5.1. Under the hypotheses of Section 71.2, for every Schwartz vector \(v\) of the real Fock model of \((V^\flat, V^0)\) there are a real \(C > 0\) and a Schwartz vector \(x\) of the real Fock model of \((V^0, V^\sharp)\) such that for every modulus \(\mathfrak m\), every character \(\nu\) of the norm-one idele classes and every character \(\chi\) of \((\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m)^\times\) for which \(\nu\) is tame for \((\mathfrak m, \mu, \chi)\), there is \(c \in \mathbb C\) with \[\lvert c\rvert^2 = C\,\mathrm{ex}_h(S_{\mathfrak m}, \nu) \ne 0\] and \(I^\sharp(g) = c\,I^\flat(g)\) for all characters \(\nu^\sharp, \nu^\flat\) compatible with \(\nu\), all \((\Phi, \Psi)\) matched for \((\sigma, \chi)\) over \(\mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m\), and all \(g \in \mathrm{U}(V^0)(\mathbb A)\).

Used in: Chapter 68; Chapter 72: Problem 72.3.1; Chapter 73: Problem 73.2.3

Here \(x\) and \(C\) are chosen before \(\mathfrak m, \nu, \chi\); only \(c\) depends on them. The theorem is taken from the proof. Problems 71.2.4, 71.2.5, 71.4.2 and 71.5.4 give the scalar calculations, using the comparisons, the continuation, the descent of vanishing and the constructions of equal-rank vectors stated above. The proof adapts a criterion of Freitag for singular forms [Fr]; for singular forms and theta lifts see also [Li97]. Snitz [Sn] matches lifts of characters in a special case; for the trivial character see the regularized Siegel–Weil formulas [KR; Ic; GQT]. The statement above is read from the proof.

Problem 71.5.5. Let \(S\) and \(Q\) be disjoint finite sets of finite places, \(h \in \mathbb N\), and \(\nu, \nu'\) characters whose values \(L^S(z,\nu)\) and \(L^{S \cup Q}(z,\nu')\) are finite, with \(L^S(z,\nu) \ne 0\), for each integer \(1 \le z \le h\). Then \[\mathrm{ex}_h(S \cup Q, \nu') = \mathrm{ex}_h(S, \nu)\,(\operatorname{Nm}Q)^{h^2}\prod_{z=1}^h\frac{\lvert L^{S\cup Q}(z,\nu')\rvert^2}{\lvert L^S(z,\nu)\rvert^2} .\]

Used in: Chapter 72

So \(\lvert c\rvert^2 = C\prod_{j=1}^h\hat L_{S}(j,\nu)^2\): the index drops by one with each plane removed, and, when a character exists, the equal-rank value and the comparison coefficient give \(C = C_0/\lambda\). Chapter 72 applies Theorem 71.5.1 to the squares of a triangle of lifts, with \(S = \mathcal M \cup Q_\vartheta\), and uses Problem 71.5.5 to divide by the untwisted value.
