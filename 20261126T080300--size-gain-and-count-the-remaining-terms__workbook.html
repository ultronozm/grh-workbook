This sheet serves the second moment in the proof of Theorem 39.4.2 of Chapter 39, Theorem B: enough forms. Chapter 43 states two bounds. Theorem 43.3.1 bounds the sum of the squared norms of the blocks of truncated amplitudes for the terms of nonzero discriminant, taken separately for each link, each pair of classes and each reduction in the specified set. Theorem 43.3.2 bounds the square root of the full second moment of the terms off the Bessel subgroup over sets that avoid the scalar cosets. There the amplitude sums in each block agree with those of \(\Gamma^*\) by Theorem 43.2.1. What are they made of? For a stable set of remaining terms, the norm of a block of truncated amplitudes is at most \(\nu\) times the sum of the sizes of its near representatives. For two terms with cell ranks over one block with agreeing reduction, the congruence saving comes from the bound for the discriminant of their common lattice in terms of their calibres, which enter the sizes inversely. For pairs of near representatives of fixed corank and bounded discriminant, the count bounds the sum of the inverse \(k\)-th powers of the discriminants. For the data, we derive both bounds of Chapter 43 using the size, congruence saving, count and tail estimates of [GL], together with the stability and summation properties of the pieces in §44.5.

§44.1. The size at one place

The next four problems compute, at one place, a weighted Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the mean of a lattice sum, and a determinant integral.

Problem 44.1.1. For finitely many \(x_j,y_j\ge0\) and \(v_j>0\), \((\sum_jx_jy_j)^2\le(\sum_jx_j^2/v_j)(\sum_jy_j^2v_j)\). For \(x=(1,2)\), \(y=(3,1)\), \(v=(2,\tfrac12)\): \(25\le\tfrac{17}2\cdot\tfrac{37}2=\tfrac{629}4\).

Problem 44.1.2. Let \(L\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{k'}\) be a lattice of rank \(2k'\) and \(\mathsf h\ge0\) integrable. The mean, for the Haar probability measure on \({\mathbb{C}}^{k'}/L\), of \(\sum_{v\in L}\mathsf h(a+v)\) is \(\int\mathsf h/\operatorname{covol}(L)\). For \(\mathsf h(y)=(1+\lvert y\rvert^2)^{-k'-1}\) it is \(\pi^{k'}/(k'!\operatorname{covol}(L))\); for \(L=(2+\mathrm{i}){\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{i}]^2\) it is \(\pi^2/50\approx0.197\).

Problem 44.1.3. A lattice of rank \(n'\) in \(\mathbb R^{n'}\) whose inner products are integers has covolume at least \(1\).

Problem 44.1.4. Let \(\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}^\times\), \(y_0\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k'}\) a row, \(A\) a positive definite hermitian matrix of size \(k'\), and \(v:=\alpha y+y_0\) for rows \(y\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k'}\). Then \[\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{k'}}\det(A+v^*v)^{-k'-1}\,dy=\lvert\alpha\rvert^{-2k'}(\det A)^{-k'}\,\frac{\pi^{k'}}{k'!}.\] For \(k'=7\), \(\alpha=2\), \(A=\operatorname{diag}(3,1,\dots,1)\) the value is \(\pi^7/(7!\,2^{14}3^7)\approx1.672\cdot10^{-8}\).

For \(i'\) rows Hua’s bound holds: if \(k'\ge7\), \(\alpha\) is an invertible complex \(i'\times i'\) matrix, \(y_0\) a complex \(i'\times k'\) matrix and \(A\) positive definite of size \(k'\), then \(\int\lvert\det(A+v^*v)\rvert^{-k'-i'}\,dy\le\lvert\det\alpha\rvert^{-2k'}(\det A)^{-k'}\) with \(v:=\alpha y+y_0\), the integral over the complex \(i'\times k'\) matrices \(y\). Status: classical; see [Hua]. For one row, Problem 44.1.4 gives the exact integral for every \(k'\ge1\); its constant \(\pi^{k'}/k'!\) is at most \(1\) for \(k'\ge7\) (Problem 43.3.5).

§44.2. The size of a term

Throughout, \(F\) and \(E\) are the fields of an admissible filling, with \(E\) containing a primitive \(5040\)-th root of unity; the data are such a filling. For a term \(\gamma\) and classes \(c,c'\), write \(\tilde\gamma:=h_c^{-1}\gamma_fh_{c'}\); \(\gamma\) lies over the block \((c,s,c')\) if \(\tilde\gamma\) is integral with reduction \(s\).

Definition 44.2.1. A set \(\mathfrak S\) of terms is stable if \(u^{-1}\gamma u'\in\mathfrak S\iff\gamma\in\mathfrak S\) for all \(u,u'\in{\mathcal{U}}(F)\). An element \(s\in\bar G\) agrees if the character of \({\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb A_{F,f})\) of the definition of \(\Gamma^*\) in Chapter 43 takes the same value at any two integral \(u,u'\) in it whose reductions satisfy \(\bar us=s\bar u'\).

Used in: Chapter 43

Definition 44.2.2. A term \(\gamma\) has cell rank \(j\in\{0,\dots,\varrho+1\}\) if (i) for some \(\zeta\in E\) with \(\zeta\bar\zeta=1\) and every index \(a\) with \(j<a\le\varrho+1\), some vector \(\sum_{b\le a}x_bv_b=\gamma\sum_{b\le a}y_bv_b\) has \(x_a=\zeta\), \(y_a=1\); (ii) \(\langle\gamma\rangle_j\ne0\); (iii) \(j=\varrho+1\) if and only if \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\). This rank, when it exists, is unique [GL, BorderedDeterminant.lean, l. 550]. Write it as \(\operatorname{cr}(\gamma)\).

Definition 44.2.3. Let \({\mathcal{U}}^0_c\) be the group of the \(u\in{\mathcal{U}}(F)\) that fix every member of the Bessel tuple and have \(h_c^{-1}u_fh_c\in K_M\). The set \(\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak S;c,s,c')\) of near representatives of \(\mathfrak S\) over \((c,s,c')\) consists of the \(\gamma\in\mathfrak S\) over \((c,s,c')\) such that \(s\) agrees, \(\gamma\) has a cell rank, \(\gamma\) is the chosen representative of its double coset \({\mathcal{U}}^0_c\gamma\,{\mathcal{U}}^0_{c'}\) [GL, BorderedDeterminant.lean, l. 455], and \(\gamma\) is near: some archimedean point \(y\) has \(y\) and \(\gamma_\infty^{-1}y\) boxed. Use the domain \(D_0\) of [GL, Box.lean, ll. 145, 200], a measurable set of representatives of the tuples \((\mathsf w_\tau)_\tau\) of complex numbers modulo the diagonal image of \(M\mathcal O_E\), with \(\lvert\mathsf w_\tau\rvert\le b^{26}\) on \(D_0\); a point \(y\) is boxed if its Bessel length (Theorem 40.3.1(d)) is at most \(b^{6(\varrho+1)+2}\) at every infinite place and each entry above the diagonal of its Gram–Schmidt matrix in the rational Bessel tuple, as a tuple over the infinite places, lies in \(D_0\) [GL, Setup.lean, ll. 398–423]. A pair of near representatives over \(c,c'\) is a pair \((\gamma,\gamma')\) in \(\mathrm{Rep}(U(Y_0)(F);c,s,c')^2\) for some \(s\).

Used in: Chapter 43

Definition 44.2.4. Let \(\gamma\) lie over a block and have cell rank \(\operatorname{cr}\). Let \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E\) be the ring of integral finite adeles of \(E\). The pairing matrix of \(\tilde\gamma\) has entries given by the Bessel pairings \(h(\tilde\gamma v_b,v_a)\). Let \(I(\gamma)\) be the index, in the \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E\)-module spanned by rows \(a=1,\dots,\varrho\) of this matrix, of the submodule spanned by its first \(\min(\varrho,\operatorname{cr})\) rows, and \(I'(\gamma)\) the same index for \(\tilde\gamma^{-1}\); both are positive [GL, LinkLattice.lean, ll. 457, 464] and equal \(1\) if \(\operatorname{cr}\ge\varrho\), when all \(\varrho\) rows are taken. Define the calibre of \(\gamma\) by \[\operatorname{cal}(\gamma):=M^{2f\min(\varrho,\operatorname{cr})}\,\frac{\max\bigl(N^f\lvert\operatorname{Nm}_{E/\mathbb Q}\langle\gamma\rangle_{\operatorname{cr}}\rvert/N^{2f(\operatorname{cr}-\varrho)_+},\ \prod_\tau\lvert\tau(\operatorname{disc}\gamma)\rvert\bigr)}{I(\gamma)\,I'(\gamma)},\] where \(\tau\) runs over the \(f\) embeddings of the CM type. For this formula see [GL, Data.lean, l. 74 and BorderedDeterminant.lean, l. 768]. The pairing matrices used in \(I(\gamma)\) and \(I'(\gamma)\) are those of \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma^{-1}\) read in the finite adeles [GL, Terms.lean, l. 450]. Thus \(\operatorname{cal}(\gamma)\) is independent of \(c,c'\).

Definition 44.2.5. The tail scale is \(\mathrm{ts}:=b^{kf/4}\) and the pair scale \(\mathrm{pc}:=\#\bar R_0\cdot n_{\mathrm{cl}}/N^{kf}\), with \(n_{\mathrm{cl}}\) the number of classes. The cell moment \(D_{\mathrm{cell}}(\gamma)\) is \(D_{\mathrm{main}}\) if \(\operatorname{cr}(\gamma)=\varrho+1\) and \(D_{\mathrm{id}}/\mathrm{ts}^{16}\) otherwise, and the size of \(\gamma\) is \(\operatorname{sz}(\gamma):=\mathrm{ts}^2\bigl(D_{\mathrm{cell}}(\gamma)/(\mathrm{pc}\cdot\operatorname{cal}(\gamma)^k)\bigr)^{1/2}\).

Theorem 44.2.1 (size). For a stable \(\mathfrak S\subseteq\Gamma^*\), all classes \(c,c'\) and all \(s\in\bar G\), \[\lVert\mathrm{Amp}^\flat(\mathfrak S;c,s,c')\rVert\le\nu\sum_{\gamma\in\mathrm{Rep}(\mathfrak S;c,s,c')}\operatorname{sz}(\gamma),\] a sum of nonnegative terms, possibly infinite.

Used in: Chapter 43

Status: read from the proof; not proved here. For the Schur estimate on a double coset, see [GL, SchurOrbit.lean, l. 963].

§44.3. Congruence saving and count

Problem 44.3.1. For \(x_1,\dots,x_i,y_1,\dots,y_i\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n'}\), \[\lvert\det(\langle x_a,y_b\rangle)\rvert^2\le\det(\langle x_a,x_b\rangle)\det(\langle y_a,y_b\rangle).\] If all \(\langle x_a,y_b\rangle\) lie in \(M'{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{i}]\) for an integer \(M'\ge1\) and the determinant is not \(0\), the product of the two Gram determinants is at least \(M'^{2i}\).

The congruence saving uses the fact that two terms over one block have the same reduction.

Problem 44.3.2. Let \(M'\ge1\) be an integer, \(\zeta_1\in\mathbb Q(\mathrm{i})\), \(\zeta_1\bar\zeta_1=1\), \(\zeta_1\ne1\), and let \(D'\ge1\) be an integer with \(D'(1-\zeta_1)\in M'{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{i}]\). Then \(D'\ge M'/2\). For \(\zeta_1=(-12+5\mathrm{i})/13\), \(M'=5\), \(D'=13\): \(13(1-\zeta_1)=5(5-\mathrm{i})\), \(\lvert5-\mathrm{i}\rvert^2=26\), and \(\lvert1-\zeta_1\rvert\approx1.961\le2\).

Over \({\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{i}]\) let \(h:=\operatorname{diag}(1,-5)\) and \(\gamma:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g_1&5g_2\\g_2&g_1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(g_1+g_2\sqrt5=(9+4\sqrt5)^3\).

Problem 44.3.3. \(g_1+g_2\sqrt5=2889+1292\sqrt5\); \(\gamma\) is an isometry of \(h\) and \(\gamma\equiv-1\bmod17\); modulo each of the primes \(4\pm\mathrm{i}\) above \(17\) it fixes the line of \(e_2\). The determinant \(\det(e_2,\gamma e_2)=-6460\) lies in both primes; its norm \(6460^2=289\cdot144400\) is divisible by \(17\cdot17\), while \(6460\equiv102\bmod289\).

In Problem 44.3.3 the two places above \(17\) at which the line meets its image contribute \(17\cdot17\) to the norm of the determinant, not to the determinant.

Definition 44.3.1. Let \(\gamma,\gamma'\) lie over one block \((c,s,c')\), and let \(\mathrm{Lat}_c\) be the image under \(h_c\) of the lattice spanned by the lattice basis. The common link \(X_0(\gamma,\gamma')\subseteq X_0\) is the space of the \(x\in X_0\) for which some \(y\in X_0\) has \((x,y)\in\operatorname{lk}\gamma\cap\operatorname{lk}\gamma'\). Lattices are taken in the finite adelic points \(V(\mathbb A_{E,f})\) of \(E\)-spaces \(V\), and products in the lattice coordinates of \(c\). The term lattice \(L_\gamma\) is the set of \(x\in\mathrm{Lat}_c\cap X_0(\mathbb A_{E,f})\) whose pairings with the image of the Bessel tuple under \(\tilde\gamma\) are \(M\) times the products of some vector of \(\mathrm{Lat}_c\cap(Ev_1+\dots+Ev_\varrho)(\mathbb A_{E,f})\) with that image. The common lattice is \(L_{\gamma\gamma'}:=X_0(\gamma,\gamma')(\mathbb A_{E,f})\cap L_\gamma\cap L_{\gamma'}\). If it is spanned over \(\hat{\mathcal O}_E\) by the \(\delta_0:=\dim X_0(\gamma,\gamma')\) columns of a matrix \(\mathsf Q=\mathsf x\mathsf u\), where \(\mathsf x\) consists of \(\delta_0\) linearly independent vectors of \(X_0(\gamma,\gamma')\) and \(\mathsf u\) is an invertible \(\delta_0\times\delta_0\) matrix of finite adeles, its discriminant \(\operatorname{dsc}(\gamma,\gamma')\) is the square root of the inverse of the factor by which multiplication by \(\det(\mathsf Q^*\mathsf Q)\) scales Haar measure on the finite adeles, in orthonormal coordinates of \(X_0\); otherwise \(\operatorname{dsc}(\gamma,\gamma'):=0\). The corank is \(\operatorname{crk}(\gamma,\gamma'):=k-\dim X_0(\gamma,\gamma')\), and the link corank is \(\operatorname{lcr}(\gamma,\gamma'):=k+1-\dim(\operatorname{lk}\gamma\cap\operatorname{lk}\gamma')\), which vanishes if and only if \(\operatorname{lk}\gamma=\operatorname{lk}\gamma'\). Two terms are in the same link class if both discriminants are nonzero and their links are equal.

Theorem 44.3.1 (congruence saving). Let \(\gamma,\gamma'\) have cell ranks and lie over one block \((c,s,c')\) with \(s\) agreeing; put \(i:=\operatorname{lcr}(\gamma,\gamma')\), \(\ell:=\operatorname{crk}(\gamma,\gamma')\).

  1. For every integer \(t_0\) with \(0\le t_0\le\operatorname{mt}(s)\), let \(X:=t_0\) if \(i=0\) and \(\operatorname{cr}(\gamma)=\varrho+1\), and \(X:=1\) otherwise. Then \((\operatorname{dsc}\cdot X)^2\le(2/M)^{2if}\operatorname{cal}(\gamma)\operatorname{cal}(\gamma')\).

  2. \(\ell\le i+(\operatorname{cr}(\gamma)-\varrho)_+\).

  3. If \(i=0\), then \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)=0\) if and only if \(\operatorname{disc}(\gamma')=0\).

  4. \(\operatorname{dsc}\ge1\); and if \((\gamma,\gamma')\) is a pair of near representatives, then \(\ell\le(\varrho+1)^3\) and \(\operatorname{dsc}\le b^{(\varrho+1)^3f}\).

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 43

Theorem 44.3.2 (count). For every integer \(0\le\ell\le(\varrho+1)^3\), the sum of \(\operatorname{dsc}(\gamma,\gamma')^{-k}\) over all classes \(c,c'\) and the pairs of near representatives over \(c,c'\) of corank \(\ell\) with \(\operatorname{dsc}\le b^{(\varrho+1)^3f}\) is at most \(\mathrm{ts}^{\ell+2}\mathrm{pc}\).

Status: both read from the proof; not proved here. Problems 44.3.1–44.3.3 show over \({\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{i}]\), as Problem 43.3.2 does over \({\mathbb{Z}}\), how a congruence modulo the level and a meeting place make a nonzero determinant large. For a term \(\gamma\) with a cell rank over a block with \(s\) agreeing, \(\operatorname{cal}(\gamma)\ge1\).

§44.4. Pairs

In this section \(\eta>0\), \(D\) and \(B\ge0\) are real numbers, \((\gamma,\gamma')\) is a pair of near representatives over \(c,c'\), \(i:=\operatorname{lcr}(\gamma,\gamma')\) and \(\ell:=\operatorname{crk}(\gamma,\gamma')\).

Problem 44.4.1. Recall \(\operatorname{dsc}\ge1\) and \(\operatorname{cal}\ge1\). If \((\operatorname{dsc}\cdot\eta)^2\le\operatorname{cal}(\gamma)\operatorname{cal}(\gamma')\) and \(D_{\mathrm{cell}}(\gamma),D_{\mathrm{cell}}(\gamma')\le D\), then \(\operatorname{sz}(\gamma)\operatorname{sz}(\gamma')\le\mathrm{ts}^4D/(\mathrm{pc}\,\eta^k\operatorname{dsc}^k)\).

Used in: Problem 44.4.2, Problem 44.4.5; Chapter 43

Problem 44.4.2. For the data and all classes \(c,c'\), let \(\mathcal Q_{c,c'}\) be a set of pairs of near representatives over \(c,c'\), and suppose that every \((\gamma,\gamma')\in\mathcal Q_{c,c'}\) admits \(\eta,D\) as in Problem 44.4.1 with \(\mathrm{ts}^{\ell+6}D/\eta^k\le B\). Then \(\sum_{c,c'}\sum_{(\gamma,\gamma')\in\mathcal Q_{c,c'}}\operatorname{sz}(\gamma)\operatorname{sz}(\gamma')\le\mathrm{ts}\cdot B\).

Problem 44.4.3. For the data, \(D_{\mathrm{cell}}\le D_{\mathrm{main}}\). If \(i\ge1\) and \(\ell\le i+1\), then \(\mathrm{ts}^{\ell+6}D_{\mathrm{main}}(M/2)^{-ifk}\le\mathrm{ts}^{-10}D_{\mathrm{id}}\). At \(i=1\), \(\ell=2\) the left side is at most \(b^{-35kf/9}D_{\mathrm{id}}\), the right side \(b^{-10kf/4}D_{\mathrm{id}}\).

Problem 44.4.4. If \(\operatorname{lk}\gamma=\operatorname{lk}\gamma'\) and \(\gamma,\gamma'\) are not in the same link class, then \(\operatorname{disc}\gamma=\operatorname{disc}\gamma'=0\), \(\ell=0\), and \(\eta=1\), \(D=D_{\mathrm{id}}/\mathrm{ts}^{16}\) give \(\mathrm{ts}^{\ell+6}D/\eta^k=\mathrm{ts}^{-10}D_{\mathrm{id}}\).

Problem 44.4.5. If \(\gamma,\gamma'\) are in the same link class over \((c,s,c')\) and \(1\le z_0\le\operatorname{mt}(s)\) is an integer, then \(\ell\le1\), and \(\eta=z_0\), \(D=D_{\mathrm{main}}\) satisfy Problem 44.4.1 with \(\mathrm{ts}\cdot\mathrm{ts}^{\ell+6}D_{\mathrm{main}}/z_0^k\le(b^{2f}/z_0)^kD_{\mathrm{main}}\).

Used in: Chapter 43

Theorem 44.4.1 (pairs). For the data:

  1. the sum of \(\operatorname{sz}(\gamma)\operatorname{sz}(\gamma')\) over all classes \(c,c'\) and all pairs of near representatives over \(c,c'\) that are not in the same link class is at most \(b^{-kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}\);

  2. for every integer \(z_0\ge1\), the same sum over the pairs in the same link class that lie over some \((c,s,c')\) with \(z_0\le\operatorname{mt}(s)\) is at most \((b^{2f}/z_0)^kD_{\mathrm{main}}\).

§44.5. The bounds of Chapter 43

Partition \(\Gamma^*\) into pieces by labeling each term of nonzero discriminant by its link and giving all terms of discriminant \(0\) one further label. We use the stability of each piece before restricting to a block [GL, TermStructure.lean, l. 843 and Sum.lean, l. 512]. In each block the amplitude sum is the sum of the amplitude sums of the pieces [GL, Sum.lean, l. 498 and Size.lean, l. 709].

Problem 44.5.1. Four amplitudes \(2,-1,3,1\) with labels \(\Lambda,\Lambda,\Lambda',\varnothing\), where \(\Lambda\ne\Lambda'\) are links and \(\varnothing\) marks discriminant \(0\): the square of their sum is \(25\); the squares of the sums of the pieces \(\Lambda\), \(\Lambda'\) add to \(1+9=10\); the products \(\lvert a\rvert\lvert a'\rvert\) over the ordered pairs \((a,a')\) of amplitudes with different labels, or both labelled \(\varnothing\) (the amplitude \(1\) with itself), add to \(31\); and \(25\le10+31\).

Problem 44.5.2. For all \(c,s,c'\), \(\lVert\mathrm{Amp}^\flat(\Gamma^*;c,s,c')\rVert^2\) is at most \(\sum_\Lambda\lVert\mathrm{Amp}^\flat(\Gamma^*\cap\{\operatorname{disc}\ne0,\ \operatorname{lk}=\Lambda\};c,s,c')\rVert^2\) plus \(\nu^2\) times the sum of \(\operatorname{sz}(\gamma)\operatorname{sz}(\gamma')\) over the pairs in \(\mathrm{Rep}(\Gamma^*;c,s,c')^2\) that are not in the same link class.

Problem 44.5.3. For the data, let \(\mathcal B\subseteq\bar G\) and \(C_1\ge0\). If the link second moment of \(\{\gamma:\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\}\) over \(\mathcal B\) is at most \(C_1\nu^2\), then the second moment of \(\Gamma^*\) over \(\mathcal B\) formed with the Bessel truncation is at most \(C_1+b^{-kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}\).

Problem 44.5.4. For the data, let \(z_0\ge1\) be an integer and \(\mathcal B\subseteq\bar G\) with \(z_0\le\operatorname{mt}(s)\) for every \(s\in\mathcal B\). Then the link second moment of \(\{\gamma:\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\}\) over \(\mathcal B\) is at most \((b^{2f}/z_0)^kD_{\mathrm{main}}\,\nu^2\), which is Theorem 43.3.1 for the data.

Used in: Chapter 43

Theorem 44.5.1 (the tail). For every set \(\mathfrak S\) of terms and every \(\mathcal B\subseteq\bar G\), the second moment over \(\mathcal B\) of the differences \(\mathrm{Amp}(\mathfrak S;c,s,c')-\mathrm{Amp}^\flat(\mathfrak S;c,s,c')\) is at most \(b^{-40}D_{\mathrm{id}}\).

Status: read from the proof; not proved here.

Problem 44.5.5. For the data, let \(\mathcal B\) avoid the scalar cosets and \(C_0\ge0\), and let the link second moment of \(\{\gamma:\operatorname{disc}(\gamma)\ne0\}\) over \(\mathcal B\) be at most \(C_0\nu^2\). Then the second moment of \(U(Y_0)(F)\smallsetminus R(F)\) over \(\mathcal B\) has square root at most \(\sqrt{C_0+b^{-kf}D_{\mathrm{id}}}+b^{-20}\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{id}}}\le\sqrt{C_0}+b^{-15}\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{id}}}\). The bound by \(\sqrt{C_0}+b^{-15}\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{id}}}\) is Theorem 43.3.2 for the data.
