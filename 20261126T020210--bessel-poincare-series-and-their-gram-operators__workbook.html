§39.1. The Bessel subgroup

Let \(E/F\) be a CM extension, \(\psi_F\) a nontrivial character of \(F\backslash\mathbb A_F\), and let \(V\) be a hermitian space over \(E\) of rank \(r\) with a decomposition \[V=Ev_1\oplus\dots\oplus Ev_\varrho\oplus X\oplus Ez\oplus Ev'_1\oplus\dots\oplus Ev'_\varrho ,\] where \(h(v_a,v_c)=h(v'_a,v'_c)=0\), \(h(v_a,v'_c)=\delta_{ac}\), the space \(X\) of rank \(k\) is orthogonal to the \(v_a\), \(v'_a\) and \(z\), and \(z\) is orthogonal to every \(v_a\) and \(v'_a\), with \(h(z,z)=-N\) for a prime \(N\). So \(r=2\varrho+k+1\). This is the shape of \(Y_0\) in Chapter 36, with \(\varrho=m/2-1\), \(X=X_0\), and \(N\) the prime of the data.

Let \({\mathcal{U}}\) be the group of \(n\in U(V)\) such that \((n-1)v_a\) lies in the span of \(v_1,\dots,v_{a-1}\) for every \(a\), and \((n-1)y\) lies in the span of \(v_1,\dots,v_\varrho\) for every \(y\) orthogonal to all \(v_a\). In the displayed blocks the stabilizer of the isotropic flag \(Ev_1\subset Ev_1+Ev_2\subset\cdots\) is block upper triangular, and \({\mathcal{U}}\) is its subgroup with identity diagonal blocks, the unipotent radical. Embed \(U(X)\) in \(U(V)\) as the pointwise stabilizer of the \(v_a\), the \(v'_a\) and \(z\). Put \(v_{\varrho+1}:=z\) and call the ordered tuple \((v_1,\dots,v_{\varrho+1})\) the Bessel tuple. For \(n\in {\mathcal{U}}\), write \[\lambda(n):=\sum_{a=1}^\varrho h(nv_{a+1},v'_a),\qquad \psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(n):=\psi_F\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}\lambda(n)\bigr).\] Define the Bessel subgroup by \(R:={\mathcal{U}}\rtimes U(X)\). For scalar-valued automorphic forms \(y\) on \(U(V)\) and \(\phi\) on \(U(X)\) for which the integral converges absolutely, the Bessel period integrates \(y\) against \(\overline{\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}}\) over \({\mathcal{U}}(F)\backslash {\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb A_F)\), and the result against \(\bar\phi\) over \(U(X)(F)\backslash U(X)(\mathbb A_F)\) [GGP]. For \(\varrho=0\) the group \({\mathcal{U}}\) is trivial, and the Bessel period is the restriction period along \(U(X)\subset U(X\perp Ez)\).

Problem 39.1.1. For \(n\in {\mathcal{U}}\), \(\lambda(n)\) is the sum over \(a\le \varrho\) of the coefficient of \(v_a\) in \(nv_{a+1}\). The map \(\lambda\colon {\mathcal{U}}\to E\) is a homomorphism, and \(\lambda(gng^{-1})=\lambda(n)\) for \(g\in U(X)\). Hence \(\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}\) is a character of \({\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb A_F)\), trivial on \({\mathcal{U}}(F)\) and fixed by \(U(X)\).

Used in: Chapter 53

Problem 39.1.2. \(U(X)\) normalizes \({\mathcal{U}}\), and \(U(X)\cap {\mathcal{U}}=\{1\}\).

Used in: Chapter 53

Problem 39.1.3. As an algebraic group over \(F\), \({\mathcal{U}}\) has dimension \(\varrho(r+k)=2\varrho(r-2\varrho)+\varrho^2+\varrho(\varrho-1)\). For \((\varrho,k)=(1,0)\) it is the three-dimensional unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of \(U(V)\), \(V\) of rank \(3\).

§39.2. Poincaré series

The Poincaré series of the proof plays the role of that of Chapter 10: the Bessel subgroup replaces \(\Gamma_\infty\) in the period, and a Bessel function replaces \(e(mz)\). Take \(V=Y_0\), \(X=X_0\) and \(G:=U(Y_0)\), with the forms \(H(Y_0)\), a finite-dimensional space with the Petersson inner product, and the source forms \(S\), as in Chapter 37. On the Bessel subgroup of the data the proof fixes a generic character \(\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}\) and a quotient measure. The character is \(\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(n)=\psi_E\bigl(\tfrac12j_E\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{st}^*\mathrm{st}\,n)\bigr)\), with \(\psi_E\) the standard additive character of \(\mathbb A_E/E\), \(j_E\) the element given by the Weil data (paragraph Fillings below) and \(\mathrm{st}\colon Y_0\to Y_1\) the string map of Definition 53.2.3 for \(c_{Y_0}=-(j_EM\delta_E)^{-1}\), where \(M=p_0p_1\) is the product of the level primes and \(\delta_E\) is the chosen generator of the different (paragraph Fillings), as in Section 53.3 of Chapter 53; like the character of Section 39.1, it is read off the entries of \(n\) just above the diagonal of the string. At a real place \(\tau\), the local factor of this character is \(\exp(\sigma_R-\overline{\sigma_R})\), where \(\sigma_R\) is the sum of the entries just above the diagonal on the Bessel tuple after rescaling it at \(\tau\) (Section 42.3).

Definition 39.2.1 (Poincaré maps). Let \(\lambda\) be a linear form on the values of the source forms and \(g\) a point of the archimedean unitary group of \(Y_0\). (From here on \(\lambda\) is such a linear form; the homomorphism \(\lambda\colon{\mathcal{U}}\to E\) of Section 39.1 enters only through \(\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}\).) For \(\varphi\in S\), the Bessel function of \(\varphi\) formed with \(\lambda\) is \(\mathcal W_{\lambda,\varphi}(nh):=\psi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(n)\,\lambda\bigl(h_\infty^{-1}\varphi(h_f)\bigr)\) for \(n\in {\mathcal{U}}(\mathbb A_F)\) and \(h\in U(X_0)(\mathbb A_F)\), where \(h_\infty^{-1}\) acts on the values. By the Riesz representation theorem there is a unique \(\mathcal P_{g,\lambda}\varphi\in H(Y_0)\) with \[\langle \mathcal P_{g,\lambda}\varphi,y\rangle=\int_{R(F)\backslash R(\mathbb A_F)}\mathcal W_{\lambda,\varphi}(x)\,\overline{y(xg)}\,dx\qquad(y\in H(Y_0)).\] The linear map \(\mathcal P_{g,\lambda}\colon S\to H(Y_0)\) is the Poincaré map of \((g,\lambda)\): it represents a Bessel period of \(\varphi\), linear in \(\varphi\) and conjugate-linear in \(y\).

Used in: Chapter 52: Definition 52.4.1; Chapter 55

The proof shows that \((g,\lambda)\) can be chosen so that \(\mathcal P_{g,\lambda}\varphi\) is the series described below for every \(\varphi\). Write \(\mathcal P\) for the map of this pair.

At each infinite place \(\tau\), the Bessel system of Theorem 40.3.1 includes a full vector-valued function \(W_\tau\), its Bessel truncation \(W_\tau^\flat\) by length (part (d)), and a positive degree constant \(d_\tau\). The series here uses the full functions. The Poincaré series for \(\mathcal P\varphi\) is the sum over \(\gamma\in G(F)\) of the translates of a seed made of each coordinate of the archimedean Bessel function, multiplied by a cutoff at the infinite places and by the indicator of a translate of the level at the finite places, one for each class; the coefficients are the Bessel coordinates of the values of a central translate of \(\varphi\) at class representatives.

At a fixed level on a definite group with one class, the forms are the vectors invariant under a finite group, and the series becomes a finite sum. Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite group acting unitarily on a space \(V_\pi\), and let the forms on \(G\) be the vectors \(V_\pi^\Gamma\), with \(\lVert\psi\rVert^2:=\lvert\Gamma\rvert^{-1}\lVert\psi\rVert_{V_\pi}^2\), as in Chapter 21 for one class. Let \(\Gamma_X\subset\Gamma\) be a subgroup, \(W\) a unitary representation of \(\Gamma_X\), and \(\jmath\colon W\to V_\pi\) a \(\Gamma_X\)-equivariant linear map with \(\lVert\jmath\xi\rVert_{V_\pi}^2=c_0\lVert\xi\rVert_W^2\) for a constant \(c_0>0\). The forms on \(X\) are \(W^{\Gamma_X}\), with \(\lVert\xi\rVert^2:=\lvert\Gamma_X\rvert^{-1}\lVert\xi\rVert_W^2\). For \(\psi\in V_\pi^\Gamma\) and \(\xi\in W^{\Gamma_X}\) put \[\operatorname{Per}(\psi,\xi):=\lvert\Gamma_X\rvert^{-1}\langle\psi,\jmath\xi\rangle_{V_\pi},\qquad P\xi:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_X}\gamma\,\jmath\xi .\]

Problem 39.2.1. \(P\xi\) is well defined and lies in \(V_\pi^\Gamma\), and \(\langle\psi,P\xi\rangle=\operatorname{Per}(\psi,\xi)\) for every \(\psi\in V_\pi^\Gamma\), where the left side is the inner product of forms.

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 53

Problem 39.2.2. The adjoint of \(P\colon W^{\Gamma_X}\to V_\pi^\Gamma\) for the two norms of forms is \(P^*\psi=\jmath^*\psi\), where \(\jmath^*\) is the adjoint for the inner products of \(W\) and \(V_\pi\). In particular \(P\ne0\) if and only if \(\operatorname{Per}(\psi,\xi)\ne0\) for some \(\psi\) and \(\xi\).

Problem 39.2.3. Let \((W',\jmath')\) be a second pair as above, with Poincaré map \(P'\). For \(\xi\in W^{\Gamma_X}\) and \(\xi'\in W'^{\Gamma_X}\), \[\langle P\xi,P'\xi'\rangle=\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma_X\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_X}\frac{\langle\delta\,\jmath\xi,\jmath'\xi'\rangle_{V_\pi}}{\lvert\Gamma_X\cap\delta\Gamma_X\delta^{-1}\rvert} ,\] and if \((W',\jmath')=(W,\jmath)\) the term of \(\delta=1\) is \(c_0\langle\xi,\xi'\rangle\), the inner product of forms.

Used in: Problem 39.3.2; Chapter 40

So \(\mathcal K:=c_0^{-1}P^*P\) is the identity plus a sum over the nontrivial double cosets. This is the finite form of Petersson’s formula, Theorem 10.2.1, where the double cosets \(\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_\infty\) give the Kloosterman terms.

§39.3. The smallest case

Take \(\mathcal O={\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm i]\), \(\Gamma:=\Gamma_{I_2}\), of order \(32\) by Problem 21.1.2, and \(\mathrm{Cl}_2=\{I_2\}\) by Problem 21.1.4. Let \(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf e_2\) be the standard basis of \({\mathbb{C}}^2\), \(z:=\mathbf e_2\) and \(X:=\mathcal O\mathbf e_1\); here \(h(z,z)=1\), since everything is definite. The stabilizer of \(z\) is \(\Gamma_X=\{\operatorname{diag}(u,1):u\in\mu_4\}\). In this section, as in Chapter 21, \(a\ge0\) is the degree of a symmetric power (not the scale of Chapter 36), and \(0\le j,j'\le a\) are integers. Let \(V_\pi:=\operatorname{Sym}^a({\mathbb{C}}^2)\), \(W:=\operatorname{Sym}^j({\mathbb{C}}\mathbf e_1)\) and \(\jmath(\xi):=\xi\,\mathbf e_2^{a-j}\). When \(4\mid j\) and \(4\mid a-j\), \(\operatorname{Per}\) is the period \(P(\psi,\xi)\) of Section 23.4, and Problem 39.2.1 says that it is the inner product with \(P\xi\). For \(4\mid j\) let \(\xi_j\in W^{\Gamma_X}\) be the form with value \(\mathbf e_1^j\).

Problem 39.3.1. Let \(4\mid j\). Then \(P\xi_j=4\bigl(\mathbf e_1^j\mathbf e_2^{a-j}+\mathbf e_1^{a-j}\mathbf e_2^j\bigr)\) if \(4\mid a\), and \(P\xi_j=0\) otherwise. For \(a=8\): \(P\xi_0=4(\mathbf e_1^8+\mathbf e_2^8)\) and \(P\xi_4=8\mathbf e_1^4\mathbf e_2^4\).

Used in: Chapter 52

Problem 39.3.2. Here \(c_0=\binom aj^{-1}\). Let \(4\mid a\) and \(4\mid j,j'\), and put \(\mathcal K_{jj'}:=\bigl(c_0(j)c_0(j')\bigr)^{-1/2}\langle P\xi_j,P\xi_{j'}\rangle/(\lVert\xi_j\rVert\lVert\xi_{j'}\rVert)\). Then \[\mathcal K_{jj'}=4\bigl(\delta_{jj'}+\delta_{j',a-j}\bigr).\] In the expansion of Problem 39.2.3 the identity class contributes \(\delta_{jj'}\) to \(\mathcal K_{jj'}\), the three classes \(\operatorname{diag}(1,u)\) with \(u\ne1\) together contribute \(3\delta_{jj'}\), and the antidiagonal class contributes \(4\delta_{j',a-j}\).

Used in: Chapter 40: Problem 40.1.7

Problem 39.3.3. Let \(4\mid a\). The forms \(P\xi_j\) with \(4\mid j\) and \(2j\le a\) are an orthogonal basis of \(\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)\), their number is \(\lfloor a/8\rfloor+1\), and \(P\xi_{a-j}=P\xi_j\).

Here different indices can give the same series; in Problem 10.1.3 two Poincaré series are proportional.

So for \(4\mid a\) the series \(P\xi_j\) with \(4\mid j\) and \(0\le2j<a\) are orthogonal and span a subspace of codimension at most one, and \(\dim\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)=\lfloor a/8\rfloor+1\) exceeds \(D^*:=4\,\mathrm{mass}_2\dim\operatorname{Sym}^a({\mathbb{C}}^2)=(a+1)/8\) by at most \(7/8\), as \(\dim S_k\) is \((k-1)/12\) up to a bounded error for even \(k\ge4\) in Problem 10.5.2. But their diagonal Gram entries are \(4\), not \(1\): the elements \(\operatorname{diag}(1,u)\) with \(u\ne1\), which preserve the line \(Ez\) without fixing \(z\), each contribute as much as the identity. The toy has nothing to average over. (Problems 40.1.7 and 40.1.6 make this precise.) In the proof a finite level group acts on the forms of principal level and translates one Poincaré map.

§39.4. Translates of one Poincaré map

The proof gives three estimates for the scaled Poincaré map of the data: a second moment over the finite group (Theorem 47.3.1), the near isometry of its projection to the good forms (Theorem 48.2.2), and an upper bound for the dimension of the target (Theorem 48.3.1). We apply the two translate theorems of Chapter 9 to obtain Theorem 39.4.1 and, using also the dimension bound for \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\), Theorem 39.4.2. How small can the second moment of the Gram operators between a Poincaré map and its translates be, and how does its upper bound combine with near isometry of the projected map to give a spanning estimate?

Let \(\mathcal X\) and \(\mathcal Y\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, let \(\rho\) be a unitary representation of a finite group \(\bar G\) on \(\mathcal Y\), and let \(\mathcal P^\circ\colon\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) be linear. Put \(\mathcal K_s:=\mathcal P^{\circ*}\rho(s)\mathcal P^\circ\in\operatorname{End}\mathcal X\) for \(s\in\bar G\); it is the Gram operator between \(\mathcal P^\circ\) and its translate \(\rho(s)\mathcal P^\circ\). Recall two facts from Chapter 9. By Problem 9.2.2, \(\operatorname{Tr}\bigl((\rho(s)\mathcal P^\circ\mathcal P^{\circ*}\rho(s)^{-1})(\rho(t)\mathcal P^\circ\mathcal P^{\circ*}\rho(t)^{-1})\bigr)=\lVert\mathcal K_{s^{-1}t}\rVert^2\), so \(s\mapsto\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\) is of positive type: there are vectors \(u_s\), \(s\in\bar G\), of an inner product space with \(\langle u_s,u_t\rangle=\lVert\mathcal K_{s^{-1}t}\rVert^2\). By Problem 9.2.5, if \(p\) is the orthogonal projection onto a \(\bar G\)-stable subspace \(\mathcal Y_0\) and \(p\mathcal P^\circ\) is an isometry, then \(\dim\mathcal Y_0\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\ge\#\bar G\,(\dim\mathcal X)^2\). Chapter 40 computes the Gram operators of a Poincaré map on a finite group: there the lower bound is attained when the weight is irreducible (Problems 40.1.4 and 40.2.1), and the translates by a subgroup act on the source through a representation, as \(\bar R_0\) does below.

Definition 39.4.1 (the scaled Poincaré map). We use \(\mathcal M=\mathcal M_0\cup\mathcal M_1\), \(\bar G=\bar G_0\times\bar G_1\), \(\rho\) on \(H(Y_0)\), \(e_0\), \(T=e_0H(Y_0)\), \(d_T\), \(S\supseteq S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) and \(D_X=\dim S\) as in Chapter 37; for the data, \(b\ge1000\). A finite adelic element of \(U(Y_0)\) is integral if its matrix in the lattice basis of the proof, to which the vectors \(v_a\), \(z\), \(v'_a\) of Section 39.1 belong, is integral at every finite place; its reduction is then the family of its residues at the places \(w\in\mathcal M\), an element of \(\bar G\). The integral elements with reduction \(1\) form the principal level \(K_M\). In \(\operatorname{vol}(K_M)\) we take the volume of the corresponding principal level in orthonormal local bases, for the Haar measure giving the stabilizer of the standard lattice volume \(1\). The classes \(c\) are the double cosets of the finite adelic points of \(U(X_0)\) by the rational points and the level of the source forms; each has a representative \(h_c\), viewed in \(U(Y_0)\). A Bessel coordinate \(i=(i_\tau)_\tau\) is a tuple of coordinates of the archimedean Bessel functions, one at each infinite place. The coordinate space has an orthonormal basis \(e_{c,i}\) indexed by the pairs of a class and a Bessel coordinate. Define the coefficient map \(C\) of the series in Section 39.2 by translating a source form by the fixed central element and evaluating it at the class representatives in Bessel coordinates. The proof shows that \(C\) is an isomorphism. Give \(S\) the inner product transported by \(C\). Let \(\nu>0\) be the covolume, for the product of the local Bessel Haar measures on the real unipotent group, of the lattice of \(n\in{\mathcal{U}}(F)\) with \(h_c^{-1}n_fh_c\in K_M\). The covolume calculation in the proof gives the same \(\nu\) for every class. Define the scaled Poincaré map and its Gram operators by \[\mathcal P^\circ:=(\operatorname{vol}(K_M)\,\nu)^{-1/2}\,\mathcal P\circ C^{-1},\qquad \mathcal K_s:=\mathcal P^{\circ*}\rho(s)\mathcal P^\circ\quad(s\in\bar G),\] a map from the coordinate space to \(H(Y_0)\) and operators on the coordinate space. Further, \(G_\infty\) is the product over the infinite places of the real groups of \(Y_0\), in the rescaled bases of the proof, and \(dy\) is the product of the local Haar measures in the compact-dual normalization of Section 42.3; \(\gamma_\infty\) and \(\gamma_f\) are the components of a rational \(\gamma\in U(Y_0)(F)\) in \(G_\infty\) and in the finite adelic group; \(\deg\) is the product over the infinite places of the degree constants of the Bessel systems, which for the data are the formal degrees, and \(\mathrm{mass}_r\) is the mass \(\sum1/\#\mathrm{Aut}\) of the genus of the standard lattice of rank \(r\).

Definition 39.4.2 (the Bessel representation). Let \(\bar R_0\subseteq\bar G\) be the group of reductions of the integral points of the finite adelic Bessel subgroup. For \(s\in\bar G\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)\) is the operator on the coordinate space whose \(((c,i),(c',i'))\) entry is the complex conjugate of \(\nu^{-1}\) times the sum, over the \(\gamma\in R(F)\) with \(h_c^{-1}\gamma_fh_{c'}\) integral of reduction \(s\), of the integrals \[\int_{G_\infty}u_c(y)\,u_{c'}(\gamma_\infty^{-1}y)\,W_i(y)\,\overline{W_{i'}(\gamma_\infty^{-1}y)}\,dy .\] Here \(u_c\) is the class cutoff and \(W\) the full Bessel function; both are made precise in Chapter 40 (Theorem 40.3.1 and Definition 40.3.1), in whose notation the entry is the complex conjugate of \(\nu^{-1}\mathrm{Amp}(R(F);c,s,c')_{ii'}\). The proof shows that \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)=0\) for \(s\notin\bar R_0\) and that \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) is a unitary representation of \(\bar R_0\) on the coordinate space.

The same sums taken over all \(\gamma\in U(Y_0)(F)\) give the entries of \(\mathcal K_s\): this is the block identity, Theorem 40.3.2, the analogue of Problem 39.2.3. Fillings.

The strip bound, the second-moment bound and the near-isometry estimate for a pair of projectors have versions for admissible fillings (Definition 39.4.3).

Definition 39.4.3 (fillings). Among the data of a filling are a CM field \(E\) with a CM type, the ranks \(\varrho\) and \(k\) of Section 39.1 with \(X_0\) standard definite, a prime \(N\) and an integral finite idele \(\pi\) with \(\pi\bar\pi=N\), a purely imaginary generator \(\delta_E\) of the different of \(E\), and a real \(b\ge1000\). The two level primes \(p_i\) differ from \(N\), and above each there are \(f\) chosen places of norm \(p_i\), where \(f\) is the number of infinite places of \(E\). The chosen places are distinct and no conjugate of a chosen place is chosen. The filling includes a level in \(U(X_0)\) with trivial class stabilizers, a Bessel system at each infinite place, and further arithmetic and size conditions. It is admissible if it satisfies the standing hypotheses of the proof. For a filling, \(\bar G\), the classes, the coordinate space, \(\bar R_0\), \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\), \(\deg\) and \(\mathrm{mass}_r\) are defined using its groups and Bessel systems, \(D_X\) is the dimension of its coordinate space, and \(\nu\) is a positive number attached to it. Let \(\rho\) be a representation of \(\bar G\) on a complex inner product space \(\mathcal Y\). A linear map \(P\) from the coordinate space to \(\mathcal Y\) satisfies the block identity if \(\langle Pe_{c,i},\rho(s)Pe_{c',i'}\rangle=\nu^{-1}\mathrm{Amp}(U(F);c,s,c')_{ii'}\) for every \(s\in\bar G\), all classes \(c,c'\) and all Bessel coordinates \(i,i'\), where \(U(F)\) is the group of rational points of the unitary group of the filling and the amplitude sums are those of Definition 40.3.1, formed for the filling. The estimates below take \(\mathcal Y\) finite-dimensional. Weil data require \(E\) to contain a primitive \(5040\)-th root of unity, and consist of a splitting character, a purely imaginary element \(j_E\) and a finite idele, subject to further conditions.

Used in: Chapter 43; Chapter 44; Chapter 47; Chapter 48

The proof verifies that the data give an admissible filling and constructs Weil data for its field. For every filling, \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)=0\) for \(s\notin\bar R_0\). For an admissible filling over a field containing a primitive \(5040\)-th root of unity, \(\nu\) is the common covolume in Definition 39.4.1 and the restriction of \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) to \(\bar R_0\) is a unitary representation. The hypotheses for the estimates are given in Chapters 43–48; the reader may take the data throughout.

The second moment over the whole group is Theorem 47.3.1: with \(D_{\mathrm{main}}=D_X^2/(\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r)\) (Definition 40.3.2), \[\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\le(1+9b^{-4})\,D_{\mathrm{main}} .\] Chapter 47 proves the whole-group bound using the link bound (Definition 43.2.3 and Theorem 43.3.1), the truncation bound (Theorem 43.3.2, which passes from the link second moment formed with the \(W_\tau^\flat\) to the full second moment), and a sieve over ratios of successive leading minors of the pairing matrix, whose entries are the Bessel pairings \(h(\gamma v_b,v_a)\) for a rational term \(\gamma\) (Definition 43.2.1). In the proof [GL], Filling.sieve gives the sieve, Filling.linkSecondMoment_le_of_le_meetNorm gives the link bound, and Filling.sqrt_secondMoment_compl_besselS_le gives the truncation bound. For the data, Chapter 44 derives the link and truncation bounds from the size, count and tail estimates and the congruence saving from congruences between terms over the same block (Theorem 44.3.1). Chapter 46 derives the sieve recurrence for pairs of tuples with a common last entry. To bound the Gram operators near the Bessel subgroup, Chapter 43 uses its bound for links at meeting reductions and its truncation bound (Theorems 43.3.1 and 43.3.2). For \(j=0,1\), the sum of \(\lVert\mathcal K_s-\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}(s)\rVert^2\) over the \(s\in\bar G\) whose component in \(\bar G_j\) equals that of an element of \(\bar R_0\) is at most \(b^{-12}\,\#\bar R_0\,D_X\) (Theorem 43.3.3); hence \(\lVert\mathcal P^{\circ*}\mathcal P^\circ-1\rVert^2\le b^{-12}D_X\) and \(\lVert\mathcal K_1\rVert^2\le2D_X\) (Problem 43.3.9). For \(a\ge4\) divisible by \(4\), the first estimate fails in the toy of Section 39.3 with the source of Problem 40.1.7. In the finite model at principal level of Problem 40.1.6, the Gram operator at \(1\) is the identity.

The second moment is to be compared with the target. By Theorem 48.3.1, \[d_T\le\#\bar G\cdot\deg\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r .\] Its proof compares the good forms on \(Y_0\) with those on the definite space \(W_m\), which have the same dimension by Corollary 70.4.1 of Chapter 70 (Part IX); there the principal level is free, and the forms of the chosen weight have dimension exactly \(\#\bar G\cdot\mathrm{mass}_r\cdot\deg\). Section 39.3 shows the correction at a level with stabilizers. With Theorem 47.3.1 this gives (Problem 48.3.6) \[d_T\sum_{s\in\bar G}\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\le\#\bar G\,(1+9b^{-4})\,D_X^2 ,\] \(1+9b^{-4}\) times the lower bound of Problem 9.2.5 for an exactly isometric projected map, with \(\mathcal Y_0=T\).

Good forms.

Recall from Chapter 9 that \(L\colon\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\) if \(\dim Z-\varepsilon\dim\mathcal X\le\lVert L\,\mathrm{pr}_Z\rVert^2\) for every subspace \(Z\subset\mathcal X\), where \(\mathrm{pr}_Z\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(Z\), and that by Problem 9.3.1 this holds if \(\varepsilon\ge0\) and \(\lVert L^*L-1\rVert^2\le\varepsilon^2\dim\mathcal X\). By Theorem 48.2.2, \(e_0\mathcal P^\circ\) is a near isometry with error \(5b^{-4}\): \[\lVert e_0\mathcal P^\circ\,\mathrm{pr}_Z\rVert^2\ge\dim Z-5b^{-4}D_X\qquad\text{for every subspace \(Z\) of the coordinate space.}\] Its proof writes the action of \(e_0\) as a product of two commuting projections, one for each level prime. For each prime the removed part is estimated using a trace against \(\rho_{\mathrm{Bes}}\) and a remainder controlled by the strip estimate.

Few translates.

Recall Theorems 9.3.1 and 9.4.1. One translate: let \(\rho\) be unitary, \(p\) the orthogonal projection onto a \(\bar G\)-stable subspace \(\mathcal Y_0\), \(\varepsilon\ge0\), \(p\mathcal P^\circ\) a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\), and \(\dim\mathcal Y_0\sum_s\lVert\mathcal K_s\rVert^2\le\#\bar G\,(1+\varepsilon)(\dim\mathcal X)^2\). Then for every subspace \(\mathcal F\subset\mathcal Y_0\), with \(\omega:=\dim\mathcal F/\dim\mathcal Y_0\) if \(\mathcal Y_0\ne0\) and \(\omega:=0\) otherwise, some \(s\in\bar G\) has \(\dim\{x\in\mathcal X:p\rho(s)\mathcal P^\circ x\in\mathcal F\}\le\bigl(\frac{1+\omega}2+\frac32\varepsilon\bigr)\dim\mathcal X\). Few translates: let \((B_s\colon\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y_0)_{s\in\mathcal I}\) be a family of linear maps between finite-dimensional complex spaces, indexed by a set \(\mathcal I\), with \(\mathcal X\ne0\ne\mathcal Y_0\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1/16\), such that for every subspace \(\mathcal F\subset\mathcal Y_0\) some \(s\) has \(\dim B_s^{-1}(\mathcal F)\le\bigl(\frac{1+\omega}2+\frac32\varepsilon\bigr)\dim\mathcal X\). Then there is a finite set \(J\subset\mathcal I\) with \[\#J\cdot\dim\mathcal X\le\tfrac52\bigl(1+\log\tfrac1{16\varepsilon}\bigr)\dim\mathcal Y_0,\qquad \dim\sum_{s\in J}B_s(\mathcal X)\ge(1-16\varepsilon)\dim\mathcal Y_0 .\]

Problem 39.4.1. Let \(\varepsilon:=(8b^2)^{-1}\). (a) If \(b\ge9\), then \(5b^{-4}\le\varepsilon\) and \(9b^{-4}\le\varepsilon\). (b) If \(b^2\ge2\), then \(16\varepsilon=2b^{-2}\) and \(\frac52\bigl(1+\log(b^2/2)\bigr)\le5\log(1+2b^2)\); if \(b^2>2\), then \(0<\varepsilon<1/16\).
