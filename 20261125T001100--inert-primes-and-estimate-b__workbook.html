Prerequisites.

Chapter 1, From zeros to a one-sided bound, Sections 1.1, 1.2 and 1.4: the functions \(G\) and \(G_\chi\) of its equations (1) and (3), the transform \(F\) of (2), Landau’s theorem and the argument of Problem 1.2.4. Quadratic reciprocity with its supplements [SeA, Ch. I, §3], the Jacobi symbol and its reciprocity law [IR, Ch. 5], and the splitting of primes in an imaginary quadratic field [Ne, Ch. I, §8]. From Davenport [Dav]: Dirichlet’s theorem in the form that \(\sum \log p/p\) over the primes of an invertible class diverges (Ch. 4), primitive characters (Ch. 5), the theta functions of a primitive character and their transformation formula (Ch. 9), and the Pólya–Vinogradov inequality (Ch. 23). The three-lines theorem [Ru, Ch. 12].

The criterion of Theorem 1.4.1 assumes a one-sided bound for a remainder of a sum with coefficients \(\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_d(n))\) over the \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}\). We first study the unrestricted sum with these coefficients, which are supported on odd powers of primes inert in an imaginary quadratic field and on powers of the ramified prime. How does its centered value compare with \(G\), and what information does it give about the zeros of \(\zeta\)?

§2.1. Inert primes and a quadratic character Tag 223A

Let \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) be a prime, let \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), and let \(\chi_d(n) := \bigl(\frac{n}{d}\bigr)\) be the Legendre symbol.

Tag 223B

Problem 2.1.1. (i) For every prime \(p\), \(\chi_d(p)\) is \(1\), \(-1\) or \(0\) according as \(p\) splits, is inert, or ramifies in \(K\). (ii) For a prime power \(r = p^h\), \(1 - \chi_d(r)\) is \(2\) if \(p\) is inert and \(h\) is odd, \(1\) if \(p = d\), and \(0\) otherwise.

Used in: Chapter 17; Chapter 36

The ramp sum over odd powers of inert primes, with weight \(2\), and over powers of \(d\), with weight \(1\), is therefore \[S_d(x) := \sum_r \Lambda(r) \bigl(1 - \chi_d(r)\bigr) \Bigl(\frac{x}{r} - 1\Bigr)_+ .\] For a primitive nonprincipal character \(\chi\) let \(\Psi_\chi(t) := \sum_{n \le t} \Lambda(n) \chi(n)\), and let \(c_\chi\) and \(G_\chi\) be as in (3).

Tag 223C

Problem 2.1.2. \(G_\chi(x) = -\sum_n \Lambda(n) \chi(n) \min(1, x/n) = -x \int_x^\infty \Psi_\chi(t) \, t^{-2} \, d t\).

Tag 223D

Problem 2.1.3. \(S_d(x) - x \log x + (1 + \gamma - c_{\chi_d}) x = G(x) - G_{\chi_d}(x)\).

An upper bound for \(S_d\) minus its main term is thus an upper bound for \(G - G_{\chi_d}\), not for \(G\). How much does such a bound say about the zeros of \(\zeta\)?

Tag 223E

Problem 2.1.4. Let \(0 < \theta < 1\). Suppose that \(L(s, \chi_d)\) has no real zero in \((\theta, 1)\), and that \(G(x) - G_{\chi_d}(x) \le x^\theta\) for all large \(x\). Then every zero of \(\zeta(s)\) or of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) in \(\operatorname{Re}s > \theta\) is a zero of both, with the same multiplicity.

Problem 2.1.4 leaves the possibility of a shared zero of equal multiplicity. We next consider a region free of zeros of \(L(s, \chi_d)\). For \(\beta, t_0 \in \mathbb{R}\) let \[\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0) := \{ s \in \mathbb{C}: \operatorname{Re}s \ge \beta,\ \lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le t_0 \},\] which is empty for \(t_0 < 0\) and unbounded to the right otherwise.

Tag 22Z9

Problem 2.1.5. Let \(0 < \theta < \beta\) and \(t_0 \ge 0\). Suppose that \(L(s, \chi_d) \ne 0\) on \(\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0)\), and that \(G(x) - G_{\chi_d}(x) \le x^\theta\) for all large \(x\). Then \(\zeta\) has no zero in \(\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0)\).

Fix \(\theta > 0\). If the bound \(G - G_{\chi_d} \le x^\theta\) holds for every prime \(d\) of some class, and every box \(\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0)\) with \(\beta > \theta\) is free of zeros of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) for at least one \(d\) of the class, then \(\zeta\) has no zero in \(\operatorname{Re}s > \theta\). The choice of \(d\) depends on the box. Section 2.2 shows that the box is free of zeros of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) for some prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) when \(\theta \ge \frac12\). This class is used for the following reason.

Tag 223F

Problem 2.1.6. Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\). Then \(\chi_d(2) = 1\) and \(\chi_d(-1) = -1\). Hence \(2\) splits in \(K\), \(1 - \chi_d(r)\) vanishes at every power of \(2\), and \(\chi_d\) is an odd character.

In the proof the weight is restricted further, to prime powers \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}\), and the characters that occur are all \(\psi\) modulo \(q_0\) and their twists \(\psi\chi_d\). Its criterion, Theorem 1.4.1, takes \(q_0\) a positive multiple of \(8\), a nonnegative integer \(d_0 \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) prime to \(q_0\), and \(\beta > \frac12\), and assumes, for every prime \(d \equiv d_0 \pmod{q_0}\), that the remainder \(R_{d, q_0}(x)\) of (4) is at most \(x^{\beta - 1}\) for all sufficiently large \(x\). Given \(\rho \ne 1\) with \(\operatorname{Re}\rho > \beta\), its proof uses one such \(d\), given by Theorem 2.2.2 below at the same \(\beta\), with \(N = q_0\), \(a = d_0\) and \(t_0 = \lvert \operatorname{Im}\rho \rvert\).

§2.2. The choice of \(d\) Tag 22ZA

Is there a prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) for which \(L(s, \chi_d)\) has no zero in a given box \(\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0)\) with \(\beta > \frac12\)? Problem 2.2.13 bounds the number of exceptional primes \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) up to \(X\). The quantity that detects a zero is built from theta series.

For \(j \in \{0, 1\}\) let \(w_j(z) := z^j e^{-\pi z^2}\) for \(z \in \mathbb{R}\), and let \[\Gamma_j(s) := \int_0^\infty w_j(z)\, z^{s} \, \frac{d z}{z} \qquad (\operatorname{Re}s > 0) .\] For a sequence \(c \colon \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1} \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(\lvert c(n) \rvert \le 1\) let \[\Theta_c(u) := u^{1/2} \sum_{n \ge 1} c(n)\, w_j(n u) \qquad (u > 0),\] and write \(c\mu\) for the sequence \(n \mapsto c(n)\mu(n)\), with \(\mu\) the Möbius function. The index \(j\) is fixed in each argument and is dropped from \(\Theta_c\). A Dirichlet character \(\chi\) modulo \(q\) is a sequence of this kind, and has parity \(j\) if \(\chi(-1) = (-1)^j\).

Tag 22ZB

Problem 2.2.1. \(\Gamma_j(s) = \frac12 \pi^{-(s+j)/2}\, \Gamma\bigl(\frac{s+j}{2}\bigr)\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s > 0\). In particular \(\Gamma_1(1) = 1/(2\pi)\).

Tag 22ZC

Problem 2.2.2. (i) Let \(\chi\) be a nonprincipal character modulo \(q\) of parity \(j\). For \(\operatorname{Re}s > 0\) the integral \(\int_0^\infty \Theta_\chi(u)\, u^{s - 1/2}\, du/u\) converges absolutely and equals \(\Gamma_j(s) L(s, \chi)\). For \(\chi = \chi_7\), \(j = 1\) and \(s = 1\) its value is \(1/(2\sqrt 7) = 0.18898\ldots\) (ii) For every character \(\chi\) and \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1\), \(\int_0^\infty \Theta_{\chi\mu}(u)\, u^{s - 1/2}\, du/u = \Gamma_j(s)/L(s, \chi)\).

Tag 22ZD

Problem 2.2.3. Let \(\chi\) be a primitive nonprincipal character modulo \(q\) of parity \(j\). Then \(\lvert \Theta_\chi(1/(q u)) \rvert = \lvert \Theta_\chi(u) \rvert\) for \(u > 0\). Hence the integrals of \(\lvert \Theta_\chi(u) \rvert\, du/u\) over \(u < q^{-1/2}\) and over \(u > q^{-1/2}\) are equal.

For \(T > 0\) define the tail integral of a sequence \(c\) and the mollifier of a character \(\chi\): \[J(c, T) := \int_{1/T}^\infty \bigl( \lvert \Theta_c(u) \rvert + \lvert \Theta_{c\mu}(u) \rvert \bigr) \frac{du}{u}, \qquad \mathcal{M}_T(s) := \int_{1/T}^\infty \Theta_{\chi\mu}(u)\, u^{s - 1/2} \, \frac{du}{u} .\] By Problem 2.2.2(ii), for \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1\), \(\mathcal{M}_T(s)\) is the integral for \(\Gamma_j(s)/L(s, \chi)\) cut at \(u = 1/T\). For \(\operatorname{Re}z > 0\) where \(L(z, \chi)\) is finite, let \(\Phi(z) := \Gamma_j(z) L(z, \chi) \mathcal{M}_T(z) - \Gamma_j(z)^2\).

Tag 22ZE

Problem 2.2.4. (i) \(\sum_{n \ge 1} \lvert w_j(n u) \rvert \le 3 e^{-u}/u\) for \(u > 0\). (ii) For every character \(\chi\), every \(T > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re}z = 2\): \(\lvert \Gamma_j(z)/L(z, \chi) - \mathcal{M}_T(z) \rvert \le 3/T\) and \(\lvert \Gamma_j(z) L(z, \chi) \rvert \le 3\), so \(\lvert \Phi(z) \rvert \le 9/T\).

Tag 22ZF

Problem 2.2.5. Let \(\chi\) be a primitive nonprincipal character modulo \(q\) of parity \(j\), and \(T > 0\) with \(q \le T^2\). On \(\operatorname{Re}z = \frac12\): \(\lvert \Gamma_j(z) L(z, \chi) \rvert \le 2 J(\chi, T)\) and \(\lvert \mathcal{M}_T(z) \rvert \le J(\chi, T)\).

Tag 22ZG

Problem 2.2.6. There is a constant \(C_j\) with the following property. Let \(\delta > 0\) and \(T > 0\), and let \(\chi\) be a primitive nonprincipal character modulo \(q\) of parity \(j\), with \(q \le T^2\) and \(J(\chi, T) \le T^{2\delta}\). If \(L(s, \chi) = 0\) and \(\frac12 + 9\delta \le \operatorname{Re}s \le 1\), then \(\lvert \Gamma_j(s) \rvert^2 \le C_j T^{-2\delta}\).

Tag 22ZH

Problem 2.2.7. Let \(\delta > 0\) and \(t_0 \in \mathbb{R}\). For all \(T\) large in terms of \(\delta\) and \(t_0\) the following holds for every \(q\) and every primitive nonprincipal character \(\chi\) modulo \(q\) of parity \(j\) with \(q \le T^2\): if \(L(s, \chi)\) vanishes somewhere on \(\mathcal{B}(\frac12 + 9\delta, t_0)\), then \(J(\chi, T) > T^{2\delta}\).

A zero in the box therefore forces a large tail integral. It remains to show that the tail integrals of the characters \(\chi_d\) are small on average over \(d\). Write \(n^\circ := n/2^{v_2(n)}\) for the odd part of \(n \ge 1\), and \(\bigl(\tfrac{b}{m}\bigr)\) for the Jacobi symbol, \(m \ge 1\) odd.

Tag 22ZJ

Problem 2.2.8. Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be prime. For every \(n \ge 1\), \(\bigl(\tfrac{n}{d}\bigr) = \bigl(\tfrac{-d}{n^\circ}\bigr)\). For instance \(\bigl(\tfrac{12}{7}\bigr) = \bigl(\tfrac{-7}{3}\bigr) = -1\).

For a sequence \(\alpha\) with \(\lvert \alpha(n) \rvert \le 1\) and \(v \ge 1\) let \(c_v(n) := \alpha(n) \bigl(\tfrac{-v}{n^\circ}\bigr)\). The mean over \(1 \le v \le T^2\) below runs over all integers \(v\). The Pólya–Vinogradov inequality [Dav, Ch. 23] states that \(\lvert \sum_{v \le X} \omega(v) \rvert \le C \sqrt{m} \log m\) for every nonprincipal character \(\omega\) modulo \(m \ge 2\) and every \(X\), with \(C\) absolute.

Tag 22ZK

Problem 2.2.9. Let \(\delta > 0\) and \(n \ge 1\), and suppose that \(n^\circ\) is not a square. Then \(v \mapsto \bigl(\tfrac{-v}{n^\circ}\bigr)\) is \(\pm 1\) times a nonprincipal character modulo \(n^\circ\), and \(\bigl\lvert \sum_{1 \le v \le X} \bigl(\tfrac{-v}{n^\circ}\bigr) \bigr\rvert \le C_\delta\, n^{1/2 + \delta/2}\) for every \(X\), with \(C_\delta\) depending only on \(\delta\).

Tag 22ZL

Problem 2.2.10. Let \(\delta > 0\). For \(u > 0\), \(\sum \lvert w_j(n u)\, w_j(n' u) \rvert \le C_\delta\, u^{-1 - 2\delta}\), the sum over the pairs \(n, n' \ge 1\) with \((n n')^\circ\) a square.

Tag 22ZM

Problem 2.2.11. Let \(\delta > 0\), \(T > 0\) and \(u \ge 1/T\). Then \(\sum_{1 \le v \le T^2} \lvert \Theta_{c_v}(u) \rvert^2 \le C_\delta\, T^2 u^{-2\delta}\), with \(C_\delta\) depending only on \(\delta\) and \(j\), not on \(\alpha\), \(T\) or \(u\).

Tag 22ZN

Problem 2.2.12. Let \(\delta > 0\). For \(T > 0\) and every \(\alpha\) with \(\lvert \alpha(n) \rvert \le 1\), \(\sum_{1 \le v \le T^2} J(c_v, T) \le C_\delta\, T^{2 + \delta}\), with \(C_\delta\) depending only on \(\delta\) and \(j\).

We return to the primes. For a prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), \(\chi_d\) is nonprincipal modulo the prime \(d\), hence primitive, and Problem 2.1.6 gives its odd parity.

Tag 22ZP

Problem 2.2.13. Let \(\delta > 0\) and \(t_0 \ge 0\). For all large \(X\), at most \(C X^{1 - \delta/2}\) primes \(d \le X\) with \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) have a zero of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) on \(\mathcal{B}(\frac12 + 9\delta, t_0)\); here \(C\) depends on \(\delta\) and \(t_0\).

Tag 22ZQ

Problem 2.2.14. For every \(\beta > \frac12\) and \(t_0 \ge 0\) there are infinitely many primes \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) with \(L(s, \chi_d) \ne 0\) on \(\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0)\).

Problem 2.2.13 gives a power saving of \(X^{-\delta/2}\) relative to the trivial bound \(X\). Density estimates for quadratic twists far stronger than Problem 2.2.13 are known; they rest on mean values of real character sums [Ju], [HB].

Now let \(\xi\) run over the characters modulo \(N = q_0\), as at the end of Section 2.1. Here \(\xi\chi_d\) denotes the character \(n \mapsto \xi(n)\bigl(\tfrac{n}{d}\bigr)\) modulo \(Nd\); for an odd prime \(d \nmid N\) it is not principal, so \(L(s, \xi\chi_d)\) is entire. The smallest case is \(N = 8\) and the class \(a = 7\).

Tag 22ZR

Problem 2.2.15. Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be prime, \(\xi\) a character modulo \(8\), and \(\xi^*\) the primitive character inducing \(\xi\), of conductor \(f \in \{1, 4, 8\}\). Then \(\xi^*\chi_d\) is primitive modulo \(fd\); \(L(s, \xi\chi_d) = (1 - 2^{-s}) L(s, \chi_d)\) if \(\xi\) is principal, and \(L(s, \xi\chi_d) = L(s, \xi^*\chi_d)\) otherwise. Hence for \(\beta > 0\) the four twists have no zero on \(\mathcal{B}(\beta, t_0)\) exactly when the four \(L(s, \xi^*\chi_d)\) have none.

For \(d = 7\), each of the four \(L(s, \xi\chi_7)\) has positive real part on \(3/4 \le \operatorname{Re}s \le 1\), \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le 1/10\), and has no zero in \(\mathcal{B}(3/4, 1/10)\); this is a finite computation.

For general \(N\), a character \(\xi\) modulo \(N\) is induced by a primitive character \(\xi^*\) of conductor \(M \mid N\). For a prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) with \(d \nmid N\), the twist \(\xi^*\chi_d\) is primitive modulo \(Md\) and has parity opposite to that of \(\xi^*\). The functions \(L(s, \xi\chi_d)\) and \(L(s, \xi^*\chi_d)\) have the same zeros in \(\operatorname{Re}s > 0\), since the Euler factors removed on passing to level \(Nd\) are nonzero there. The following theorems give the exceptional count and the simultaneous choice of \(d\).

Tag 22ZS

Theorem 2.2.1. Let \(N \ge 1\), \(\delta > 0\) and \(t_0 \in \mathbb{R}\), and let \(a \in \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) be a class such that every prime \(p \equiv a \pmod N\) satisfies \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) and \(\gcd(N, p) = 1\). Call a prime \(d \equiv a \pmod N\) bad if for some character \(\xi\) modulo \(N\) the function \(L(s, \xi\chi_d)\) vanishes at some \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s \ge \frac12 + 9\delta\) and \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le t_0\). Then the number of bad primes \(d \le X\) is \(O(X^{1 - \delta/2})\) as \(X \to \infty\) through the positive integers.

Tag 22ZT

Theorem 2.2.2. Let \(N \ge 1\), \(\beta > \frac12\) and \(t_0 \in \mathbb{R}\), and let \(a\) be a unit of \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) such that every prime \(p \equiv a \pmod N\) satisfies \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) and \(\gcd(N, p) = 1\). Then there is a prime \(d \equiv a \pmod N\) such that for every character \(\xi\) modulo \(N\) and every \(s \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s \ge \beta\) and \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le t_0\), \(L(s, \xi\chi_d) \ne 0\).

Used in: Chapter 1

The hypothesis on the class holds when \(8 \mid N\) and \(a \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) is a unit; for \(1 \le N < 8\) it holds for no unit class. One \(d\) serves all characters \(\xi\) and all points of the box at once, and the characters \(\xi\) and the class \(a\) have the same modulus \(N\).

§2.1

2.1.1

For odd \(p \ne d\) compare \(\bigl(\frac{-d}{p}\bigr)\) with \(\bigl(\frac{p}{d}\bigr)\) by reciprocity. For \(p = 2\) use \(\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[(1 + \sqrt{-d})/2]\).

2.1.2

Use \(\sum_n \Lambda(n)\chi(n)/n = -c_\chi\), convergent by the prime number theorem for \(\chi\).

2.1.3

Write \(S_d\) as the ramp sum with coefficient \(1\) minus the one with coefficient \(\chi_d\), and use (1) and (3).

2.1.4

The Mellin transform of \(G - G_{\chi_d}\) is \(F(s) + \frac{L'}{L}(s, \chi_d)/(s(s-1)) - c_{\chi_d}/(s-1)\), with \(F\) as in (2). Follow Problem 1.2.4: a real zero of \(\zeta\) gives the residue sign of that problem, but a real zero of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) gives the opposite sign.

2.1.5

As in Problem 2.1.4, with \(A(x) := x^\theta - G(x) + G_{\chi_d}(x)\). Show that the abscissa \(\sigma_c\) is less than \(\beta\): a real singularity \(\sigma_c \ge \beta\) would be a real zero of \(\zeta\) that is not a zero of \(L(s, \chi_d)\).

2.1.6

The supplementary laws.

§2.2

2.2.2

Integrate term by term where the sum converges absolutely. For (i) with \(0 < \operatorname{Re}s \le 1\), bound \(\sum_n \chi(n) w_j(nu)\) for small \(u\) by partial summation, using that the partial sums of \(\chi\) are bounded. For the value, \(L(1, \chi_7) = \pi/\sqrt 7\) by the class number formula.

2.2.3

The transformation formula for the theta function of a primitive character [Dav, Ch. 9]; then substitute \(u \mapsto 1/(qu)\).

2.2.4

(i) Compare the sum with an integral; \(w_j\) is unimodal on \((0, \infty)\). (ii) The difference is the integral over \(0 < u \le 1/T\); use (i) on both integrals.

2.2.5

On \(\operatorname{Re}z = \frac12\) the factor \(u^{z - 1/2}\) has modulus \(1\). Use Problem 2.2.3 and \(q^{-1/2} \ge 1/T\).

2.2.6

Evaluate \(\Phi\) at the given zero \(s\). Apply the three-lines theorem to \(\Phi\) on \(\frac12 \le \operatorname{Re}z \le 2\), with Problems 2.2.4 and 2.2.5 on the two edges. The weight of the right edge at \(\operatorname{Re}s\) is \((\operatorname{Re}s - \frac12)/\frac32 \ge 6\delta\).

2.2.7

For \(t_0 < 0\) the box is empty. For \(t_0 \ge 0\), use \(L(s, \chi) \ne 0\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s \ge 1\) and the positive minimum of \(\lvert \Gamma_j \rvert\) on the compact rectangle \(\frac12 \le \operatorname{Re}s \le 1\), \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le t_0\).

2.2.8

Factor \(n = 2^k n^\circ\), use \(\bigl(\tfrac{2}{d}\bigr) = 1\), and apply reciprocity for the Jacobi symbol with \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\).

2.2.9

\(\bigl(\tfrac{-v}{m}\bigr) = \bigl(\tfrac{-1}{m}\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac{v}{m}\bigr)\). For nontriviality choose \(v\) by the Chinese remainder theorem at a prime dividing \(m\) to an odd power.

2.2.10

Write \(n = 2^a g k^2\) and \(n' = 2^b g k'^2\) with \(g\) odd and squarefree; the terms with \(n\) or \(n'\) much larger than \(1/u\) are negligible.

2.2.11

Expand the square, so that the inner sum over \(v\) is \(\sum_v \bigl(\tfrac{-v}{(nn')^\circ}\bigr)\). Bound it by \(T^2\) for the pairs of Problem 2.2.10 and by Problem 2.2.9 for the others, and use \(u^{-2} \le T^2\).

2.2.12

2.2.13

2.2.14

Reduce to \(\beta < 1\) and put \(\delta := (\beta - \frac12)/9\). If all large primes of the class were bad, partial summation and Problem 2.2.13 would make \(\sum \log p/p\) over the class converge.
