§76.1. A seesaw with a twist

Problem 76.1.1. Let \(A\), \(T\) and \(V_\vartheta\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, with \(\vartheta\) in a set containing \(1\); let \(u_\vartheta\colon A\to V_\vartheta\) and \(g_\vartheta\colon T\to V_\vartheta\) be linear, \(n>0\), and \(c'_\vartheta\ne0\) numbers such that \(c'_\vartheta g_\vartheta^*u_\vartheta= \mathrm{per}\) for every \(\vartheta\), with \(\mathrm{per}\colon A\to T\) injective and independent of \(\vartheta\). Then \(\Upsilon_\vartheta:= n\,g_\vartheta g_\vartheta^*\) satisfies \[\langle u_\vartheta y', \Upsilon_\vartheta u_\vartheta y\rangle = \rho_\vartheta\,\langle y', \Upsilon^\circ y\rangle, \qquad \rho_\vartheta= \frac{\lvert c'_1\rvert^2}{\lvert c'_\vartheta\rvert^2},\quad \Upsilon^\circ = \frac{n}{\lvert c'_1\rvert^2}\,\mathrm{per}^*\mathrm{per},\] every \(u_\vartheta\) is injective, and \(\langle y,\Upsilon^\circ y\rangle>0\) for \(y\ne0\): the hypotheses of Problem 75.1.2.

Write \({\mathcal{O}}:={\mathbb{Z}}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(\mathfrak q:=(3+2\mathrm{i})\), so \({\mathcal{O}}/\mathfrak q\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{13}\) by \(\mathrm{i}\mapsto5\). Take \(\psi\) identically \(1\) on \({\mathcal{O}}\), or the cubic character modulo \(\mathfrak q\) with \(\psi(2)=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\), extended by zero on \(\mathfrak q\). It is trivial on units. Write \(c_a(\psi):=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}}\psi(\alpha)\alpha^a e^{-\pi|\alpha|^2}\), with \(c_a(1)=c_a\) as in Chapter 23.

On \(A:={\mathcal{A}}_{(8)}(1)={\mathbb{C}}\) take \(\langle y',y\rangle=\overline{y'}y/4\). On \(\operatorname{Sym}^8({\mathbb{C}}^2)\) let \(\langle v,w\rangle_0\) be the conjugate of the pairing of Problem 21.3.2, linear in \(w\). The target pairing is \(\langle v,w\rangle_0/32\), giving the norm of \({\mathcal{A}}_{(8)}(2)\) on invariant vectors. Define \(u_\psi,g\colon A\to\operatorname{Sym}^8({\mathbb{C}}^2)\) by \[u_\psi(\varphi) := \frac{\varphi}{4}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{O}}^2}\psi(x_2)\,(x_1e_1+x_2e_2)^8 e^{-\pi\lvert x\rvert^2}, \qquad g(\zeta) := 8\zeta\, e_1^4e_2^4 .\]

Problem 76.1.2. \(u_\psi(\varphi) = \frac{\varphi}{4}\bigl(c_8c_0(\psi)\,e_1^8 + 70\,c_4c_4(\psi)\,e_1^4e_2^4 + c_0c_8(\psi)\,e_2^8\bigr)\), and \(g^*v = \langle e_1^4e_2^4, v\rangle_0\).

Problem 76.1.3. \(g^*u_\psi(\varphi) = c_4c_4(\psi)\varphi/4\). Hence, with \(c'_\psi := c_4/c_4(\psi)\) (when \(c_4(\psi)\ne0\)), \(c'_\psi\,g^*u_\psi = \mathrm{per}\) is multiplication by \(c_4^2/4\) for both \(\psi\). By machine: \(c_4(\psi) \approx 0.187713 + 0.026069\,\mathrm{i}\) for \(\psi\ne1\), against \(c_4 \approx 0.143191\); so \(\rho_\psi := \lvert c'_1\rvert^2/\lvert c'_\psi\rvert^2 = \lvert c_4(\psi)\rvert^2/c_4^2 \approx 1.75167\).

Problem 76.1.4. Let \(\Upsilon_\psi := gg^*\), \(Z(\psi) := u_\psi^*u_\psi\) and \(D_\psi\) the compression determinant of \(\Upsilon_\psi\) on the line \(u_\psi({\mathbb{C}})\). Then \(\Upsilon^\circ = c_4^4/16\), \(Z(\psi)D_\psi = (c_4^4/16)\rho_\psi\), and \(Z(1)/Z(\psi) = (D_\psi/D_1)\rho_\psi^{-1}\). At the trivial twist \(D_1 = 1/81\). By machine, for \(\psi\ne1\), \(Z(\psi)\approx 6.6676\cdot10^{-4}\) and \(D_\psi \approx 0.069029\).

With toy Hecke value \(|c_4(\psi)|^2\), these identities model clauses (1) and (2).

Problem 76.1.5. \({\mathcal{A}}_{(8)}(2)\) is spanned by \(e_1^8+e_2^8\) and \(e_1^4e_2^4\) (Problem 21.3.5); \(\phi_{\mathrm f}:= e_1^8 - 34e_1^4e_2^4 + e_2^8\) is orthogonal to \(u_1({\mathbb{C}})\), and \(g^*\phi_{\mathrm f}= -17/35\). §76.2. Gluing by a congruence

In the period of the gluing map, a sum over a congruence group enforces a matrix congruence.

Problem 76.2.1. Modulo \(9\), let \(\Gamma := \{Y \equiv 1 \pmod 3\} = \{1,4,7\}\). For \(v\in{\mathbb{Z}}/9\), the equation \(vY=1\) has a solution \(Y\in\Gamma\) if and only if \(v\equiv1\pmod 3\), and then exactly one. Hence \(\mathbf 1_{v\equiv1\,(3)} = \sum_{Y\in\Gamma}\mathbf 1_{vY=1}\).

Problem 76.2.2. Let a finite group \(\Gamma\) act on a finite set \(X\) on the right, and let \(S, N\subseteq X\) be such that for \(x\in S\) exactly one \(Y\in\Gamma\) has \(xY\in N\), and \(xY\in N\) implies \(x\in S\). Then \(\mathbf 1_S(x) = \sum_{Y}\mathbf 1_N(xY)\), and for every \(\Gamma\)-invariant \(f\), \(\sum_{x}\mathbf 1_S(x)f(x) = \#\Gamma\sum_x \mathbf 1_N(x)f(x)\).

In the proof, \(X\) consists of matrices modulo \(\pi_M^2\), where \((\pi_M)\) is the product of the chosen primes (Section 37.1 and [GL, ExchangeData, line 80]). Invertible \(r\times r\) matrices congruent to \(1\) modulo \(\pi_M\) act on the right. Here \(S\) is given by the gluing congruence and \(N\) by block normalization. The group order occurs in \(c^\circ_i\) below [GL, GluedLattices, lines 490–535 and GluingMaps, line 999].

§76.3. The general statement

Fix \(1\le j\le m\). Write \(V_j=V''\oplus V^\circ\) with definite summands of ranks \(r+9m+1\) and \(t-9m-1\). A choice \(i\) specifies a \(3m\times(r+9m+1)\) matrix modulo \(\pi_M^2\), a class of the standard definite space of rank \(6m+1\) at principal level \(\pi_M^2\), and at each infinite place \(r^8-(t+1)/2\) lists of \(2m\) row indices of that space. Let \(c\) range over the classes of \(V^\circ\) at principal level \(\pi_M^2\). Write \(\mu^-\) for the minus diagram of the untwisted \(j\)-th descent pair and \(h\) for its hook number. Tuples \(\zeta=(\zeta_{i,c})\) carry one Weyl module of weight \(\mu^-\) per slot \((i,c)\), with the sum of the Gram pairings [GL, ExchangeData, lines 169–193, 294, 444–449 and GluingMaps, lines 58–88].

Definition 76.3.1. For a twist \(\vartheta\), define the gluing map from tuples \(\zeta\) to the target of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) by \[g_\vartheta\zeta=M^{l_1}\sum_{i,c,d''}\delta_{\operatorname{block}(d'',c)\gamma_i} \bigl(\phi''_{\vartheta,i}(d'')\mathbin{\odot}\zeta_{i,c}\bigr).\] Here \(\mathbin{\odot}\) denotes the Cartan product, \(d''\) runs over class representatives of \(V''\) at the row-fixing level of \(i,\vartheta\), and \(c\) in the subscript denotes its chosen representative. The class form satisfies \(\langle\delta_x(v),\phi\rangle=\langle v,\phi(x)\rangle\). The matrix \(\gamma_i\) is a glued lattice basis chosen at the trivial twist and \(l_1\) the denominator exponent of the splitting. The first factor \(\phi''_{\vartheta,i}=\overline{c''L_{\vartheta,i}}\) comes from a vector-valued theta series \(L_{\vartheta,i}\) on \(\mathrm U(V'')(\mathbb A_f)\), with \(c''\ne0\) depending only on the data and \(j\) [GL, TestedForms, line 138, GluedLattices, line 550, GluedForms, line 450, LineCoordinates, line 331 and GluingMaps, lines 190–199, 211–247, 350–354].

§76.4. Periods of false lifts

For clause (3) we need \(g_\vartheta\mathrm{per}_jy\) orthogonal to the false lifts (Problem 76.3.4).

Problem 76.4.1. \(\sum_U q^{\binom{\dim U}{2}}x^{\dim U} = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1+q^kx)\), the sum over the subspaces \(U\) of \({\mathbb{F}}_q^n\). For \(q=2\), \(n=2\): \(1+3x+2x^2\).

Problem 76.4.2. Let \(q\) be a prime power, \(F\) a bounded function on a group \(G\), elements \(g_U\in G\) indexed by the subspaces of \({\mathbb{F}}_q^n\), and \(\sum_U a_U F(gg_U) = 0\) for all \(g\), with \(a_{\{0\}} = 1\) and \(\lvert a_U\rvert = q^{\binom{\dim U}2}\lvert X\rvert^{\dim U}\). Taking \(n_{\mathbb H}=\operatorname{rk}V''\) gives \(\Theta^\circ((g_\vartheta^*\phi_{\mathrm f})_i)(g)=0\) for all choices \(i\) and adelic \(g\). §76.5. Tight frames, integrality and size

Definition 76.5.1. Let \(G\) be a positive definite Hermitian \(d\times d\) matrix and \(h>0\). A tight frame for \((G,h)\) is a finite family \((\eta_a)\) in \({\mathbb{C}}^d\) with \(\sum_a(\eta_a^*G\xi)\,\eta_a = h^2\xi\) for every \(\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^d\).

A frame expresses \(g_\vartheta g_\vartheta^*\) as a sum of operators of rank at most one.

Problem 76.5.1. Let \(h>0\) be an integer, \(F\) an integer matrix with \(d\) independent columns (indexed by a basis of a Weyl module) and rows indexed by tensors \(T\), \(G := F^tF\), and let an integer matrix \(H\) satisfy \(GH = hI\). Then \(\eta_T := HF^te_T\in{\mathbb{Z}}^d\) satisfies \(G\eta_T = hF^te_T\), the \(\eta_T\) form a tight frame for \((G,h)\), and \(\eta_T^*G\eta_T\le h^2\).

For the Weyl modules of the proof, \(G\) is the integral Gram matrix and \(H\) the matrix of Fock pairings of the basis polynomials. The identity \(GH=hI\) and the integral frame are read from the code [GL, GramMatrix, lines 101, 447].

Problem 76.5.2. For \(\operatorname{Sym}^a({\mathbb{C}}^n)\) with \(n\ge2\), let \(H\) be the matrix of Fock pairings of the monomials \(z^\alpha\), and take the basis \(m_\alpha\), \(\lvert\alpha\rvert = a\), with \(m_\alpha\) the sum of the distinct tensors \(e_{w_1}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{w_a}\) of content \(\alpha\) (so \(m_\alpha = (a!/\alpha!)e^\alpha\) in the notation of Chapter 21), and \(F\) the inclusion into tensors. Then \(G = \operatorname{diag}(a!/\alpha!)\), \(H = \operatorname{diag}(\alpha!)\) and \(h = a!\). For \(\operatorname{Sym}^2{\mathbb{C}}^2\) (basis \(11,12,22\)): \(G = \operatorname{diag}(1,2,1)\), \(h = 2\), the frame is \(2e_{11}, e_{12}, e_{12}, 2e_{22}\), with squared \(G\)-norms \(4,2,2,4\), and \(\sum_T\eta_T\eta_T^tG = 4I\).

In each slot \(\rho(\delta_c)\overline{f_a(i,c)}\) is integral in the Weyl basis, and \(N_\sigma(f_a)\le h^2\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{C}})\).
