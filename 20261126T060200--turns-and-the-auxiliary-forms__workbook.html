The proof of Theorem C (Chapter 67) extends a linearly independent family of at least \((1 - 4/b^2)\dim_{\mathbb C} T\) target forms to a basis by auxiliary forms. Their twisted lifts, after multiplication by one scalar \(\gamma(\vartheta)\) for each twist, are integral away from \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\), and their untwisted lifts are short in the sense of Section 73.4. Theorem 73.4.1 expresses \(\lvert\gamma(\vartheta)\rvert\) as a power of \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\) times \(m^2\) turn ratios (Section 73.2), each a product of \(t\) ratios of Hecke values; the determinant formula of Chapter 68 has \(m(m-1)/2\) pairwise factors. Why \(m^2\)? The \(m^2\) factors correspond to the grid squares (unit squares) crossed in transferring a period through two grids of Hermitian spaces, each crossing using one exchange square. Sections 73.1–73.2 work out the paths and check one turn (the interchange \(LU\mapsto UL\)); Section 73.3 states the transfer; Section 73.4 derives Theorem 73.4.1 from the statement on short lifts and input 3 of Section 49.3.

§73.1. Two grids and their paths

In this sheet \(m\) is even, as for the data; the small values of \(m\) in Problems 73.1.2 and 73.1.5 concern only the combinatorics of paths. A node is a pair \((i, j)\) with \(0 \le i, j \le m\), and there are two grids of Hermitian spaces over \(E\) on the nodes, with the ranks and signatures specified below and positive signs on all added blocks; the signs refer to the archimedean components of the adelic frames of Section 37.3. The space \(Y^i_j\) of the first grid has rank \(r + (i + j)t\) and \(\lfloor (m-j)/2\rfloor\) negative signs, and \(Y^0_j = Y_j\). The space \(\widetilde Y^i_j\) of the second grid has rank \(r + 1 + i(t-1) + j(t+1)\) and \(\lfloor (m-i+1)/2 \rfloor\) negative signs, \(\widetilde Y^0_0 = Y_0 \oplus\) a positive line, and on the diagonal, after a choice of basis identification, \(\widetilde Y^j_j = Y^j_j \oplus\) a line of sign \((-1)^j\). Call the step \((i, j) \to (i, j+1)\) up and the step \((i+1, j) \to (i, j)\) left. The matrix of an edge is directed from the end with the smaller coordinate to the end with the larger one, with columns at the former and rows at the latter; its entries are in \(\mathfrak R_\vartheta := \mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m_\vartheta\) (in this sheet), where \[\mathfrak m_\vartheta := (\pi_M)^b\prod_{w \in Q_\vartheta} w .\] Each edge carries a theta lift or a transposed theta lift with the point mass at its matrix as finite datum. At an end of rank \(d\), the character of an up edge between \(j\) and \(j+1\) is \(\mu^0_{j+1}\vartheta^{j+1}\upsilon^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\), and that of a left edge between \(i+1\) and \(i\) is \(\mu^0_{i+1}\upsilon^{\lceil d/2\rceil}\), without twist. Status: read from the proof.

A path (in this sheet) goes from \((m, 0)\) to \((0, m)\) by \(m\) steps left (\(L\)) and \(m\) steps up (\(U\)); we write it as a word in \(L, U\). Its nodes with \(i > j\) carry the spaces \(\widetilde Y^i_j\), those with \(i < j\) the spaces \(Y^i_j\), and its node \((l, l)\) on the diagonal both \(\widetilde Y^l_l\) and \(Y^l_l\): the part of the path before it is its half in the second grid, the part after it its half in the first. For periods the two halves are read from their outer ends \(\widetilde Y^m_0\) and \(Y_m\) towards the diagonal. In the first grid the maps are then transposed theta lifts along up edges and theta lifts along left edges, acting on conjugate forms; in the second grid they are theta lifts along up edges and transposed theta lifts along left edges. Throughout, forms at a node have the archimedean type fixed by the proof: conjugate forms in the first grid and forms in the second. The first path is \(L^mU^m\), through \((0,0)\); the last is \(U^mL^m\), through \((m,m)\). The grid squares of a path are the unit squares \((i, j)\) (named by their lower left corner, \(0 \le i, j < m\)) lying below it. If a path contains \(LU\) with the node \((i, j)\) between the two steps, replacing \(LU\) by \(UL\) is the turn at \((i, j)\); it replaces the node \((i, j)\) by \((i+1, j+1)\).

Problem 73.1.1. Formally substituting \(m = 4\) in the signature formulas gives \(2, 1, 1, 0, 0\) negative signs for \(Y^0_0, \dots, Y^0_4\), as in Problem 36.3.4, and \(0, 1, 1, 2, 2\) for \(\widetilde Y^4_0, \widetilde Y^3_0, \dots, \widetilde Y^0_0\). In general all spaces on the last path, \(\widetilde Y^m_j\) and \(Y^i_m\), are positive definite, and for \(0 \le j < m\), \(\widetilde Y^{j+1}_j\) and \(Y^{j+1}_j\) have the same rank and the same number of negative signs.

Problem 73.1.2. There are \(\binom{2m}{m}\) paths (\(2, 6, 20\) for \(m = 1, 2, 3\)), and each meets the diagonal \(i = j\) in exactly one node \((l, l)\). The grid squares of a path form a Young diagram: the column \(i\) contains the grid squares \((i, 0), \dots, (i, h_i - 1)\), with \(h_0 \ge h_1 \ge \dots \ge h_{m-1}\).

Problem 73.1.3. A turn at \((i, j)\) adds exactly the grid square \((i, j)\) to the grid squares of the path. It is diagonal if \(i = j\); then the node \((j, j)\) on the diagonal is replaced by \((j+1, j+1)\).

Problem 73.1.4. Every path other than the last has a turn. Any sequence of turns from the first path to the last has exactly \(m^2\) turns, one at each \((i, j)\) with \(0 \le i, j < m\), and \(m\) of them are diagonal.

Problem 73.1.5. Replacing at each stage the first \(LU\) of the word by \(UL\), the turns from the first path to the last are at \((0,0)\) for \(m = 1\); at \((0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1)\) for \(m = 2\); and at \((0,0), (1,0), (2,0), (0,1), (1,1), (2,1), (0,2), (1,2), (2,2)\) for \(m = 3\). The numbers of sequences of turns from the first path to the last are \(1, 2, 42\) for \(m = 1, 2, 3\).

So the paths count \(m^2\) grid squares, against the \(m(m-1)/2\) squares of the triangle of Chapter 72 (Problem 72.2.7): \(1, 4, 9\) against \(0, 1, 3\) for \(m = 1, 2, 3\).

§73.2. One turn

For a turn at \((i, j)\), call \((i,j)\) the lost node, \((i+1,j+1)\) the gained node, and \((i, j+1)\) and \((i+1, j)\) the kept nodes. Off the diagonal the square uses the first grid if \(i < j\) and the second if \(i > j\), with the copy of a kept node on the diagonal from that grid; a diagonal turn uses the square at \((j, j)\) in the first grid, \(Y^{j+1}_j\) having the rank and signature of \(\widetilde Y^{j+1}_j\) (Problem 73.1.1).

Definition 73.2.1. For nonnegative integers \(i, j\), set \(\nu := \mu^0_{i+1}\mu^0_{j+1}\). For \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) define the turn ratio by \[\rho'_{ij}(\vartheta) := \prod_{z=1}^{t} \frac{\lvert L^{\mathcal M\cup Q_\vartheta}\bigl(z, (\nu\vartheta^{j+1})^{-1}\bigr)\rvert}{\lvert L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu^{-1})\rvert} ,\] a quotient by \(0\) being \(0\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 68

Here \(\vartheta^{j+1}\) uses the second index. We use the ratios for \(0 \le i, j < m\).

Problem 73.2.2. Let \(0 \le i, j < m\). The character \(\mu^0_{i+1}\mu^0_{j+1}\) has weight \(-(\kappa_{i+1} + \kappa_{j+1})\), with \(t \le \kappa_{i+1}(\tau) + \kappa_{j+1}(\tau) \le 4b\) for every \(\tau\), and tame part \((\omega_w^{i+j+2})\), nontrivial exactly at the \(w \in \mathcal M\). So each \(P_z := (\mu^0_{i+1}\mu^0_{j+1}, z)\), \(1 \le z \le t\), is a ramified critical pair, \(\rho'_{ij}(\vartheta) = \prod_{z=1}^t R_{P_z}(\vartheta^{j+1}, 1)\), and \(\rho'_{ij}(1) = 1\).

Used in: Chapter 68

Problem 73.2.3. For \(0 \le i, j < m\), consider the square at \((i, j)\) in either grid, with its lost, gained and kept nodes as in this section. The gained node has rank \(2t\) more than the lost one; the ranks of the two kept nodes add up to those of the lost and the gained node, and so do their numbers of negative signs; if \(n_0\) is the sum of the ranks of the kept nodes and \(q_-\) the sum of their negative signs, then \(2q_- + 2 \le n_0 \le 2(\kappa_{i+1}(\tau) + \kappa_{j+1}(\tau)) + 2q_-\) for every \(\tau\). These are the rank and signature conditions of Theorem 71.5.1 for \(V^0\) the sum of the kept nodes, \(V^\sharp\) the gained and \(V^\flat\) the lost node, \(n^\sharp- n^\flat= 2t\), \(h = t\) and \(\mu = \kappa_{i+1} + \kappa_{j+1}\).

Let \(\nu = \mu^0_{i+1}\mu^0_{j+1}\). The characters of the two edges at the lost node, taken at any two end ranks with sum \(n_0\), multiply to \(\nu\vartheta^{j+1}\upsilon^{n_0/2}\). Let \(\chi\) be the inverse of the product of the two characters modulo \(\mathfrak m_\vartheta\) given by their tame parts. The exchange character \((\nu\vartheta^{j+1})^{-1}\) has weight \(\kappa_{i+1} + \kappa_{j+1}\) and induces \(\chi\).

The square law of a turn (read from the proof). Fix \(0 \le i, j < m\) and one of the two grids. Take the source kept node to be \((i, j+1)\) in the first grid and \((i+1, j)\) in the second, and the other kept node as target. There are \(C_\square \in \mathbb C\) (possibly \(0\)), a bijection \(\sigma\) from the coordinates of the lost and gained nodes to those of the source and target, and \(c\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb C^\times\) with \(c \doteq \rho'_{ij}\), with the following property. For every \(\vartheta\), every form \(f\) at the source and every linear functional \(f^\vee\) on the forms at the target, let \(L'(\mathbf y)\) be \(f^\vee\) of the transposed lift and then the lift of \(f\) through the lost node, using the two row blocks of \(\mathbf y\), and \(L(\mathbf x)\) be \(f^\vee\) of the lift and then the transposed lift of \(f\) through the gained node, using the two column blocks of \(\mathbf x\); the matrices have entries in \(\mathfrak R_\vartheta\), \(\mathbf y\) with rows at the kept nodes and columns at the lost node, \(\mathbf x\) with rows at the gained node and columns at the kept nodes. Then \(L'(\mathbf y k) = \chi(\det k)L'(\mathbf y)\) and \(L(h\mathbf x) = \chi(\det h)L(\mathbf x)\) for invertible \(k\), \(h\), and \[\sum_{\mathbf y} \Psi(\mathbf y)L'(\mathbf y) = \frac{C_\square}{c(\vartheta)}\sum_{\mathbf x} \Phi(\mathbf x)L(\mathbf x)\] for every pair \((\Phi, \Psi)\) matched for \((\sigma, \chi)\). The square law uses Theorem 71.5.1, with the conditions of Problem 73.2.3, the critical pair of Problem 73.2.2, and the four edges of the square.

Problem 73.2.4. Let \(A\) be a finite commutative ring, \(\chi\) a character of \(A^\times\), \(n = n^\flat+ n^\sharp\), and \(\Psi\) a complex-valued function on \(n \times n^\flat\) matrices over \(A\) that vanishes off the first \(n^\flat\) columns \(k_{\mathcal B}\) of the \(k \in \mathrm{GL}_n(A)\) and satisfies \(\chi(\det h)\Psi(\mathbf y h) = \Psi(\mathbf y)\) for \(h \in \mathrm{GL}_{n^\flat}(A)\). Then \(\chi(\det k)\Psi(k_{\mathcal B})\) depends only on the last \(n^\sharp\) rows of \(k^{-1}\), and the function \(\Phi\) equal to it there and to \(0\) elsewhere is matched with \(\Psi\) for \((\sigma, \chi)\) (Definition 71.1.3), where \(\sigma\) identifies the blocks of sizes \(n^\flat\), \(n^\sharp\) with the first and the last coordinates of \(A^n\).

Let \(\mathbf e\) be an additive character of a finite commutative ring \(\mathfrak R\) such that \(x \mapsto \mathbf e(ax)\) is nontrivial for every \(a \ne 0\); such characters exist on \(\mathfrak R_\vartheta\), the product of the rings \(\mathcal O_E/w^b\), \(w \in \mathcal M\), and \(\mathcal O_E/w\), \(w \in Q_\vartheta\). For a function \(F\) on columns \(v \in \mathfrak R^u\) put \(\hat F(v') := \sum_{v \in \mathfrak R^u}\mathbf e(v'v)F(v)\), for rows \(v' \in \mathfrak R^{1\times u}\).

The line of a diagonal turn (read from the proof). At a diagonal turn at \((j, j)\), the old column \(v\) is on the added line of \(\widetilde Y^j_j\) and the new row \(v'\) on the added line of \(\widetilde Y^{j+1}_{j+1}\); put \(u := r + (2j+1)t\), the rank of \(Y^{j+1}_j\). Let \(\mathfrak a_\vartheta\) be the product of a chosen finite idele generating \(\mathfrak m_\vartheta\) and a fixed finite idele of the Weil data, and \(\mathfrak u_\vartheta := \bar{\mathfrak a}_\vartheta/\mathfrak a_\vartheta\). The proof writes the theta kernel of the old line, with column \(v\) and archimedean polynomial \(\mathsf P\) (and the finite translation \((1, \mathfrak u_\vartheta^j)\) on the line), as \(c_{\mathrm{line}}(\vartheta)\sum_{v' \in \mathfrak R_\vartheta^{1\times u}}\mathbf e_\vartheta(v'v)\) times the kernel of the new line with row \(v'\) and polynomial \(\mathsf T\mathsf P\) (and \((1, \mathfrak u_\vartheta^{j+1})\) on the line), the sum being taken over these products; here \(\mathsf T\) is a map of archimedean polynomials common to all twists, \(\mathbf e_\vartheta\) an additive character of \(\mathfrak R_\vartheta\), and \(\lvert c_{\mathrm{line}}(\vartheta)\rvert^2(\#\mathfrak R_\vartheta)^u = 1\). The denominator in the transfer of a diagonal turn is \(c/c_{\mathrm{line}}\), with \(c\) from the square law in the first grid at \((j, j)\).

Problem 73.2.5. Over \(\mathbb F_3\) with \(\mathbf e(x) = e^{2\pi \mathrm i x/3}\) and \(u = 1\), the transform of the indicator \(F\) of \(\{1\}\) is \(\hat F(v') = \mathbf e(v')\), with \(\sum\lvert\hat F\rvert^2 = 3\), and transforming back, \(\sum_{v'}\mathbf e(v'v)\hat F(v') = 3F(-v)\), for \(v, v' \in \mathbb F_3\). For a finite commutative ring \(\mathfrak R\) with an additive character \(\mathbf e\) as above, every integer \(u \ge 0\) and every \(F\colon \mathfrak R^u \to \mathbb C\), \(\sum_{v' \in \mathfrak R^{1\times u}}\lvert\hat F(v')\rvert^2 = (\#\mathfrak R)^u\sum_{v \in \mathfrak R^u}\lvert F(v)\rvert^2\).

Problem 73.2.6. \(\#\mathfrak R_\vartheta = \bigl(\prod_{w \in \mathcal M} q_w\bigr)^b\,\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\) for every \(\vartheta\). Fix \(0 \le j < m\), with \(c\) and \(c_{\mathrm{line}}\) as in the line of a diagonal turn. Then \(c_{\mathrm{line}}^{-1} \doteq 1\) and \(c/c_{\mathrm{line}} \doteq \rho'_{jj}\).

Problem 73.2.7. Let \(u, \mathcal C\) be nonnegative integers, \(\mathfrak R\) and \(\mathbf e\) as above, and \(\Psi'(v)\) be a complex-valued function of \(v \in \mathfrak R^u\). If each value is a sum of at most \(\mathcal C\) roots of unity, then for each row \(v'\) and each root of unity \(\zeta\), \(\zeta\sum_{v \in \mathfrak R^u}\mathbf e(v'v)\Psi'(v)\) is a sum of at most \((\#\mathfrak R)^u\mathcal C\) roots of unity. For a diagonal turn, \(u = r + (2j+1)t \le 2n_m\).

§73.3. The transfer from the first path to the last

Fix \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\). A tuple on a path assigns to each of its edges a matrix over \(\mathfrak R_\vartheta\) of the size of the edge, directed as in Section 73.1. A family \(\mathbf g\) of invertible matrices over \(\mathfrak R_\vartheta\), one at each node, is admissible if it lies in the level of the node at the two ends \(Y^0_m = Y_m\) and \(\widetilde Y^m_0\) (trivial modulo \(\mathfrak m_\vartheta\) at \(Y_m\), the kernel of reduction modulo \((\pi_M)^b\) at \(\widetilde Y^m_0\)), and if \(\mathbf g\) at \(\widetilde Y^a_a\) is \(\mathbf g\) at \(Y^a_a\) extended by \(1\) on the line. These families act on the right on tuples by \((S\cdot\mathbf g)_e := \mathbf g_{e^+}^{-1}S_e\mathbf g_{e^-}\), where \(e^-\) and \(e^+\) are the ends of \(e\) with the smaller and the larger coordinate. For \(\mathcal C \in \mathbb N\), a path datum of bound \(\mathcal C\) (in this sheet) is a function \(\mathfrak d\) of the tuples with: \(\mathfrak d(S\cdot\mathbf g)\prod_e \chi_e(\det \mathbf g_{e^+}/\det \mathbf g_{e^-}) = \mathfrak d(S)\) for admissible \(\mathbf g\), \(\chi_e\) the character of the edge modulo \(\mathfrak m_\vartheta\); \(\mathfrak d(S) = 0\) unless the matrices on the up edges have left inverses; and every value is a sum of at most \(\mathcal C\) roots of unity.

In this sheet \(\Gamma\) denotes a path. For a family \(\mathfrak d= (\mathfrak d_\vartheta)\) of complex-valued functions of the tuples on \(\Gamma\), with diagonal node \((l, l)\), and a real point \(g\) of \(U(\widetilde Y^l_l)\), define the period to be the following function of triples \((\vartheta, \varphi', \varphi)\), where \(\varphi'\) is a conjugate form on \(Y_m\) and \(\varphi\) a form on \(\widetilde Y^m_0\), of arbitrary finite levels. For each tuple \(S\), the maps along the two halves give \(F_S\) on \(Y^l_l\) and \(\widetilde F_S\) on \(\widetilde Y^l_l\); the period is the sum over \(S\) of \(\mathfrak d_\vartheta(S)\) times the integral over the adelic quotient of \(U(Y^l_l)\) of \(F_S(x)\,\widetilde F_S\bigl(\mathrm{diag}(x, \mathfrak u_\vartheta^l)(g, 1)\bigr)\), the second entry acting on the added line at the finite places. For a function \(\mathcal X\) of triples (in this sheet) we write \(\mathfrak d\propto_\Gamma \mathcal X\) if for every \(g\) the period is \(\mathfrak c\,\mathcal X\) for some \(\mathfrak c \in \mathbb C\) independent of the triple.

For \(k\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb N\) and \(c\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb C^\times\), we say that \(\Gamma'\) transfers to \(\Gamma\) with factors \((k, c)\) if for every bound \(\mathcal C\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb N\) and all path data \(\mathfrak d'_\vartheta\) on \(\Gamma'\) of bound \(\mathcal C(\vartheta)\) there are path data \(\mathfrak d_\vartheta\) on \(\Gamma\) of bound \(k(\vartheta)\mathcal C(\vartheta)\) such that for every \(\mathcal X\), \(\mathfrak d\propto_\Gamma \mathcal X\) implies \(\mathfrak d' \propto_{\Gamma'} \mathcal X/c\).

One turn (read from the proof). For \(0 \le i, j < m\) there is \(c_{ij}\colon \mathrm{Tw}\to \mathbb C^\times\) with \(c_{ij} \doteq \rho'_{ij}\) such that every turn at \((i, j)\) from \(\Gamma'\) to \(\Gamma\) transfers \(\Gamma'\) to \(\Gamma\) with factors \(((\#\mathfrak R_\vartheta)^{2n_m}, c_{ij})\). With the matrices on the preserved edges fixed, the new datum is matched (Problem 73.2.4), for the bijection and character of the square, to the Fourier transform of the old datum in the column of the added line, at each new row; off the diagonal the line is absent and the transform is the identity. Off the diagonal the square law expresses the old sum as \(C_\square/c(\vartheta)\) times the new sum; on the diagonal the comparison of the column and row periods uses \(\mathsf P\) and \(\mathsf T\mathsf P\) and has the factor \(c_{\mathrm{line}}(\vartheta)C_\square/c(\vartheta)\).

Problem 73.3.1. If \(\Gamma_1\) transfers to \(\Gamma_2\) with factors \((k, c)\) and \(\Gamma_2\) to \(\Gamma_3\) with \((k', c')\), then \(\Gamma_1\) transfers to \(\Gamma_3\) with \((kk', cc')\). Hence the first path transfers to the last with factors \(\bigl((\#\mathfrak R_\vartheta)^{2n_m m^2}, c\bigr)\), where \(c := \prod_{0 \le i, j < m}c_{ij} \doteq \prod_{0 \le i,j < m}\rho'_{ij}\).

The two ends (read from the proof). There are path data on the first path, a real point, a constant and a linear map \(\mathcal E\) (in this sheet) from the forms on \(\widetilde Y^m_0\) of principal level \((\pi_M)^b\) onto \(H(Y_0)\), chosen before the twist and the forms, such that the integral of a conjugate form \(\varphi'\) on \(Y_m\) against the lower chain of \(\mathcal E\varphi\) at the twist \(\vartheta\), before the passage to algebraic forms, is the constant times the period of these data. On the last path every space is definite (Problem 73.1.1), and the proof expresses its period through a finite sum, over the tuples, of class integrals of a pairing of two chains of algebraic forms. Transferring by Problem 73.3.1, bounding the denominators and the size of that finite sum, and reading the values of \(\Theta_\vartheta\) from such periods by pairing with forms supported on one class, the proof obtains the following statement.

Short lifts (read from the proof). There is \(c\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb C^\times\) with \(c \doteq \prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}\) such that \(H(Y_0)\) is spanned by the \(z\) with: (a) for every \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\), every prime \(p \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb C\to\mathbb C_p\), \((c(\vartheta)/c(1))\,\Theta_\vartheta z\) is integral at \(\eta\); (b) \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1z)(g)\rvert^2 \le \exp(x^{202}b^2)\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)\) and every \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\).

§73.4. Auxiliary forms

Definition 73.4.1. Let \(\gamma\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb C\). A form \(y \in T\) is auxiliary for \(\gamma\) if (i) for every prime \(\ell\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) and every \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) with \(\gcd(\ell, \operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta) = 1\), the form \(\gamma(\vartheta)\Theta_\vartheta y\) lies in \(\mathfrak L_{\vartheta,\iota}\), that is, is integral at \(\iota\); and (ii) \(\lVert y\rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp(2x_1^2b^2)\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)\), with \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_\sigma\) the length of Definition 37.11.1, computed from \(\Theta_1 y\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 68

In the rest of this section \(c\) is the function of the statement on short lifts, and \(\gamma := c/c(1)\).

Problem 73.4.2. \(\gamma(\vartheta) \ne 0\) for all \(\vartheta\), \(\gamma(1) = 1\), and there is \(e_\gamma \in \mathbb Z\) with \(\lvert\gamma(\vartheta)\rvert = (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e_\gamma/2}\prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}(\vartheta)\) for all \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\).

Problem 73.4.3. The map \(z \mapsto \#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)z\) sends \(H(Y_0)\) onto \(T\). The image of a spanning set of \(H(Y_0)\) spans \(T\).

Problem 73.4.4. \(x^{99} + x^{202}b^2 \le 2x_1^2b^2\).

Problem 73.4.5. If \(z\) satisfies (a) and (b) of the statement on short lifts, then \(\#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)z\) is auxiliary for \(\gamma\).

Problems 73.4.2–73.4.5, the statement on short lifts and input 3 of Section 49.3 prove the following.

Theorem 73.4.1. There are an integer \(e_\gamma\) and a function \(\gamma\colon\mathrm{Tw}\to\mathbb C\), with \(\gamma(\vartheta) \ne 0\) for every \(\vartheta\), \(\gamma(1) = 1\) and \[\lvert\gamma(\vartheta)\rvert = (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e_\gamma/2}\prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}(\vartheta) \qquad (\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}),\] such that \(T\) is spanned over \(\mathbb C\) by the forms that are auxiliary for \(\gamma\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 68

By Problem 73.2.2, \(\prod_{i,j<m}\rho'_{ij}\) is a product of \(m^2t\) ratios \(R_P(\vartheta^n, 1)\) of ramified critical pairs with \(1 \le n \le m\); from this Theorem 68.5.1 bounds, for a family \(\theta\) with small ratios, the height of \(\iota \mapsto \lvert\gamma(\theta_\iota)\rvert^{-1}\), which Chapter 67 uses. We have proved the bookkeeping of the turns: the path count gives one turn ratio for each of the \(m^2\) grid squares, and each turn uses an exchange square with \(h = t\); the bound on the number of roots of unity in a value of the datum is at most multiplied by \((\#\mathfrak R_\vartheta)^{2n_m}\) at each turn. The formulas for the numbers of negative signs. For the last statement compare \(r + 1 + (j+1)(t-1) + j(t+1)\) with \(r + (2j+1)t\).

A path is determined by the positions of its \(m\) letters \(L\). For the grid squares, read the height of the path above the column \(i\).

Only the height of one column changes.

A word other than \(U^mL^m\) contains \(LU\). Count the grid squares of the first and of the last path.

For the count, a sequence of turns adds the grid squares of the \(m \times m\) square one by one, keeping a Young diagram; use the hook length formula.
