§51.1. Central idempotents

In this section \(G\) is a finite group, and an element \(e = \sum_u e(u)\,u\) of \(\mathbb{C}[G]\) has coefficients \(e(u)\). The central idempotents are the sums of the idempotents \((\dim\mathsf V/\#G)\sum_u \operatorname{tr}(u^{-1}\mid\mathsf V)\,u\) of distinct irreducible representations \(\mathsf V\) [Se]. Let \(S_3\) act on \(\mathbb{C}^3\) by permuting coordinates, let \(\mathbb{C}^3_0 \subset \mathbb{C}^3\) be the plane of vectors with coordinate sum zero, and let \(e\) be the idempotent of \(\mathbb{C}^3_0\). It is a central idempotent, not the \(e_0\) of any data.

Problem 51.1.1. \(6e\) has coefficient \(4\) at the identity, \(0\) at the transpositions and \(-2\) at the \(3\)-cycles. One has \(e(1, 0, 0) = (\tfrac23, -\tfrac13, -\tfrac13)\) and \(6e(1, 0, 0) = (4, -2, -2)\), of squared length \(24\). The largest \(3\)-adic absolute value of a coefficient of \(e\) is \(3\); that of \(6e\) is \(1\).

Used in: Problem 51.1.2, Problem 51.2.4

Problem 51.1.2. For a central idempotent \(e\) of \(\mathbb{C}[G]\), the function \(u \mapsto \#G\,e(u)\) is the character of a representation of \(G\) of dimension at most \(\#G\). In Problem 51.1.1 it is the character of \(\mathbb{C}^3_0 \oplus \mathbb{C}^3_0\), of dimension \(4\).

Problem 51.1.3. Let \(e\) be a central idempotent of \(\mathbb{C}[G]\) and \(u \in G\). Then \(\lvert\eta(\#G\,e(u))\rvert_p \le 1\) for every prime \(p\) and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\), and \(\lvert\sigma(e(u))\rvert \le 1\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\).

§51.2. The good translate

Definition 51.2.1. For \(s \in \bar G\) define the good translate to be the linear map \(\mathsf p_s:= \#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)\colon H(Y_0) \to T\).

By Definition 49.3.1, the normalized translate satisfies \(A_s\varphi = c_B\,c_{\mathrm{ss}}^{-1}\,\mathsf p_s(\mathcal P\varphi)\) for every \(\varphi \in S_{\mathrm{ram}}\). The proof relates the lower chain of \(\mathsf p_sz\) to that of \(z\) through the action of \(\bar G\). For a tame character \(\xi\) and \(s \in \bar G\) it fixes an element \(\tilde s_\xi\) of \(K^\circ(Y_m)\) that reduces to \(\operatorname{diag}(s_w, 1)\) at each chosen place \(w\), where \(s = (s_w)_{w \in \mathcal M}\), and to \(1\) at every \(w \in Q_\xi\), and shows that for all \(y \in H(Y_0)\) and \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\) \[\Theta_\xi(\rho(s)y)(g) = (\Theta_\xi y)(g\,\tilde s_\xi) .\] Status: read from the proof [GL]. In this chapter \(u\) denotes an element of \(\bar G\), and for every element of \(\bar G\), such as \(us\), the element fixed in this way is written with a tilde and the subscript \(\xi\).

Problem 51.2.1. For every tame character \(\xi\), \(z \in H(Y_0)\), \(s \in \bar G\) and \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\), \[(\Theta_\xi\,\mathsf p_sz)(g) = \sum_{u \in \bar G} \#\bar G\,e_0(u)\,(\Theta_\xi z)(g\,\widetilde{us}_\xi) .\]

Integrality is measured by the gauge. We use the following classical fact. Integral Weyl modules. The Weyl module of a highest weight has a basis, dual to the products of minors indexed by semistandard tableaux, in which an invertible matrix \(h\) with entries in a commutative ring \(A\) and \(\det h \in A^\times\) acts by a matrix with entries in \(A\) [DEP], [Fu]. Status: classical. The proof takes the coordinates of Definition 37.5.1 dual to a chosen integral basis of products of minors, in which the action of such matrices is again integral [GL].

Problem 51.2.2. Fix a prime \(p\), a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\), a framed space \(V\) and a point \(g\) of its finite adelic group. For Weyl vectors \(\omega, \omega'\) and \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\): \(\lVert\lambda\omega\rVert_g = \lvert\eta(\lambda)\rvert_p\lVert\omega\rVert_g\), \(\lVert\omega + \omega'\rVert_g \le \max(\lVert\omega\rVert_g, \lVert\omega'\rVert_g)\), and \(\lVert\omega\rVert_{gh} = \lVert\omega\rVert_g\) for every \(h \in K^\circ(V)\).

Problem 51.2.3. Let \(p\) be a prime, \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) a ring homomorphism, \(\xi\) a tame character, \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\), \(z \in H(Y_0)\) and \(s \in \bar G\). If \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi z\) is integral at \(\eta\), so is \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi\,\mathsf p_sz\).

In Problem 51.1.1 the denominator \(3\) of \(e\) is cleared by \(\#G = 6\): this is the role of the factor \(\#\bar G\). The price is paid in size.

Problem 51.2.4. Let \(z \in H(Y_0)\), \(s \in \bar G\), \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) and \(c \in \mathbb{R}\). If \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1 z)(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for every \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\), then \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1\,\mathsf p_sz)(g)\rvert^2 \le (\#\bar G)^4e^c\) for every \(g\). In the model of Problem 51.1.1, with the identity in place of \(\Theta_1\), \(z = (1, 0, 0)\) and \(c = 0\), for every \(s \in S_3\) the coordinates of \(6e\,s(1, 0, 0)\) have absolute value at most \(\sum_u \lvert 6e(u)\rvert = 8 \le (\#G)^2 = 36\), and the largest is \(4\).

Recall from Chapter 66 the class count, read from the proof: for the data, let \(G_f\) be the finite adelic unitary group of the standard definite hermitian space of rank at most \(n_m\) and \(\Gamma\) its rational points; if a level \(K\) contains the principal congruence subgroup of \(\pi_M\pi_N\), then \(\#(\Gamma\backslash G_f/K) \le e^{x^{30}}\).

Problem 51.2.5. The final level \(K_{\mathrm{last}}(1)\) of Definition 37.9.1 contains the principal congruence subgroup of \(\pi_M\pi_N\) in \(K^\circ(Y_m)\), and \(Y_m\) has rank at most \(n_m\). Hence, for \(y \in H(Y_0)\), \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) and \(c \in \mathbb{R}\): if \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1 y)(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for all \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\), then \(\lVert\Theta_1 y\rVert_\sigma^2 \le e^{x^{30} + c}\).

Problem 51.2.6. \(\#\bar G\) divides \(\mathcal N\), and \(x^{30} + 4\log\mathcal N\le x^{99}\).

Problems 51.2.4–51.2.6 give the first estimate and Problems 51.2.1–51.2.3 the second. This is input 3 of Section 49.3, word for word (\(\#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z = \mathsf p_sz\)).

Theorem 51.2.2 (the good part). Let \(z \in H(Y_0)\) and \(s \in \bar G\). If \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1 z)(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for all \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\), with \(c \in \mathbb{R}\) and a fixed \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), then \[\lVert\Theta_1(\#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z)\rVert_\sigma^2 \le e^{x^{99} + c}.\] For every prime \(p\), every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\), every tame character \(\xi\) and every \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}\), if \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi z\) is integral at \(\eta\), so is \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi(\#\bar G\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z)\).

§51.3. The ramified forms

For \(w \mid \pi_N\) put \(\mathrm{Av}_w := \sum_{i=0}^{N-2}T_w^i\) (in this chapter). Then \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}= \bigcap_w \ker\mathrm{Av}_w\), and by Problem 52.5.3, \(S_{\mathrm{ram}}\) is the image of \(\prod_{w \mid \pi_N}(1 - T_w)\). We first count dimensions in two finite models, in which the source is replaced by the functions on a finite set and \(T_w\) by translations.

Problem 51.3.1. Let \(F_1\) be the operator \((F_1v)(y) = v(2y)\) on complex-valued functions \(v\) on \(\mathbb{F}_5^\times\), so \(F_1^4 = 1\). Then \(\ker(1 + F_1 + F_1^2 + F_1^3)\) is the space of vectors of coordinate sum zero, it equals \(\operatorname{im}(1 - F_1)\), and its dimension is \(3 = (1 - \tfrac14)\cdot 4\); the fixed vectors of \(F_1\) are the constants. Moreover, for \(F\) in a ring with identity and an integer \(n \ge 0\), \(n - \sum_{i<n}F^i = (1 - F)\sum_{i<n}\sum_{j<i}F^j\); for \(n = 4\) the last factor is \(3 + 2F + F^2\).

Problem 51.3.2. On complex-valued functions on \((\mathbb{Z}/4)^2\) let \(F_1, F_2\) be the translations by \((1, 0)\) and \((0, 1)\). Then \(\bigcap_k\ker(1 + F_k + F_k^2 + F_k^3)\) has dimension \(9\), \(\ker(1 - F_1)\) has dimension \(4\), and \(\ker\bigl((1 - F_1)(1 - F_2)\bigr)\) has dimension \(7 \le 4 + 4\). With two operators in place of the \(f\) places and order \(4\) in place of \(N - 1\), the bound \((1 - f/(N - 1))D_X\) of Theorem 51.3.1 becomes \((1 - \tfrac24)\cdot16 = 8 \le 9\).

Problem 51.3.3. For endomorphisms \(F, F'\) of a finite-dimensional vector space, \(\dim\ker FF' \le \dim\ker F + \dim\ker F'\).

Write \(K^X_0\langle z_w\rangle\) for the subgroup generated by \(K^X_0\) and the scalar matrix \(z_w\).

Problem 51.3.4. Let \(w \mid \pi_N\) and \(i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\). The scalar \(z_w^i\) lies in \(K^X_0\) if and only if \(N - 1\) divides \(i\). Hence \(T_w^{N-1} = 1\) on \(S\), and \([K^X_0\langle z_w\rangle : K^X_0] = N - 1\). Formally, for \(N = 5\) (no data have \(N = 5\)) and \(z_w\) reducing to \(2\) at \(w\), the least \(i \ge 1\) with \(z_w^i \in K^X_0\) is \(4\).

For the standard definite hermitian spaces over \(E\), recall from Chapter 50 that a level \(K\) is free if \(\Gamma \cap gKg^{-1} = 1\) for every finite adelic \(g\), \(\Gamma\) the rational points; that, for the data, every level of rank at most \(4n_m\) contained in the integral matrices that reduce to the identity at every \(w \in \mathcal M\) is free (Problem 50.1.4); and that for free levels \(K \subseteq K'\) the forms of level \(K\) of a given weight have \([K' : K]\) times the dimension of those of level \(K'\) (Problem 50.1.5).

Problem 51.3.5. For \(w \mid \pi_N\), the source forms fixed by \(T_w\) are exactly the forms of the weight of \(S\) and level \(K^X_0\langle z_w\rangle\); this level and \(K^X_0\) are free; and \(\dim\ker(1 - T_w) = D_X/(N - 1)\).

Problem 51.3.6. \(\dim\ker\prod_{w \mid \pi_N}(1 - T_w) \le fD_X/(N - 1)\), and \(\dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}\ge (1 - f/(N - 1))D_X\).

Problem 51.3.7. For data, \(f \le b\) and \(N - 1 \ge b^9\); hence \(f/(N - 1) \le b^{-8}\).

Problems 51.3.4–51.3.7, with Problem 51.3.3, prove input 5 of Section 49.3.

Theorem 51.3.1 (the ramified forms). \(\dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}\ge (1 - f/(N - 1))D_X\), and \(f/(N - 1) \le b^{-8}\).

Used in: Problem 51.3.2; Chapter 39; Chapter 49

In the proof of Theorem 39.4.2, the chosen finite set \(J \subseteq \bar G\) satisfies \(\#J\,D_X \le 5d_T\log(1 + 2b^2)\), where \(d_T = \dim_\mathbb{C}T\); for this \(J\), Theorem 51.3.1 and Problem 39.4.4 give \(\#J\,(D_X - \dim S_{\mathrm{ram}}) \le 2b^{-2}d_T\).

We have proved Theorem A’s inputs 3 and 5. 51.1.2

Write \(e\) as a sum of idempotents of irreducible representations; which representation has character \(\dim\mathsf V\cdot\operatorname{tr}(u^{-1}\mid\mathsf V)\)? Count dimensions with \(\sum_{\mathsf V}(\dim\mathsf V)^2 = \#G\).

51.1.3

Character values are sums of roots of unity, and \(\lvert\operatorname{tr}(u\mid\mathsf V)\rvert \le \dim\mathsf V\). Applying \(\sigma\) to the coefficients of \(e\) gives a central idempotent.
