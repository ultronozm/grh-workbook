§13.1. Complex multiplication Tag 22DL

Which tori have endomorphisms other than \(u \mapsto nu\)? Every holomorphic map \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda \to \mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) that fixes \(0\) is \(u \mapsto \alpha u\) with \(\alpha\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\) [Si1, Thm. VI.4.1]. So if \(E\) corresponds to \(\Lambda\), then \(\operatorname{End}(E) = \{\alpha \in \mathbb{C}: \alpha\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\}\), and we write \([\alpha]\) for \(u \mapsto \alpha u\).

Tag 22DM

Problem 13.1.1. If \(\operatorname{End}(E) \ne \mathbb{Z}\), then \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\) is imaginary quadratic for every \(\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E) \smallsetminus \mathbb{Z}\), \(\operatorname{End}(E)\) is an order in \(K\), and \(\Lambda\) is homothetic to a lattice contained in \(K\).

We then say that \(E\) has complex multiplication by that order.

Tag 22DN

Problem 13.1.2. \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) = \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[2\mathrm{i}]) = \mathbb{Z}[2\mathrm{i}]\), and \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[\rho]) = \mathbb{Z}[\rho]\) with \(\rho = e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\).

Tag 22DP

Problem 13.1.3. For \(\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E)\), \(\alpha \ne 0\), the kernel of \([\alpha]\) on \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) has \(\lvert\alpha\rvert^2\) elements.

For each nonzero \(\alpha\in\operatorname{End}(E)\), the function \(\wp(\alpha u)\) is even and elliptic for \(\Lambda\), so it is a rational function of \(\wp(u)\), and \([\alpha]\) is a rational map of the curve.

Tag 22DQ

Problem 13.1.4. For the lemniscatic lattice, \(\wp((1+\mathrm{i})u) = \dfrac{\wp(u)^2 - 1}{2\mathrm{i}\, \wp(u)}\).

Tag 22DR

Problem 13.1.5. On \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\), \([\mathrm{i}](x, y) = (-x, \mathrm{i}y)\). For every \(\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E)\), \([\alpha]^*(dx/2y) = \alpha\, dx/2y\).

Tag 22DS

Problem 13.1.6. For \(a, b \in \mathbb{C}^\times\), the curve \(y^2 = x^3 + ax\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), and \(y^2 = x^3 + b\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\rho]\).

The endomorphisms of \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\) extend to its elliptic curve model over \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i},\tfrac12]\), so they can be reduced at each prime of this ring above an odd prime \(p\). What do they look like near the origin? In a local parameter at the origin, addition gives a formal group law, and each endomorphism acts on it by a power series.

§13.2. The formal group Tag 22DT

Let \(E\) be given by a Weierstrass equation over a ring \(R\), and let \(z := -x/y\). The group law, written in \(z\), is a formal group law \(F(z_1, z_2) \in R[[z_1, z_2]]\), and every endomorphism defined over \(R\) becomes a series \([\alpha](z) \in R[[z]]\) [Si1]. The invariant differential becomes \(\omega(z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} c_n z^{n-1}\, d z\) with \(c_1 = 1\) and \(c_n \in R\). If \(R\) is torsion-free, then over \(R \otimes_\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Q}\) the logarithm \(\ell(z) := \sum_{n \ge 1} c_n z^n/n\) satisfies \(F(z_1, z_2) = \ell^{-1}(\ell(z_1) + \ell(z_2))\) [Si1]. Over \(\mathbb{C}\), take the uniformizing coordinate \(u\) for which \(\omega\) pulls back to \(d u\). Then \(\ell(z(u)) = u\) near the origin. Define the invariant derivation by \(D := \ell'(z)^{-1}\, d/dz\), where \(\ell'(z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} c_n z^{n-1}\) has the coefficients of \(\omega\); over \(\mathbb{C}\), \(D = d/du\).

For a one-parameter formal group over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), either \([p](z) \equiv 0 \pmod p\) or \([p](z) \equiv g(z^{p^h}) \pmod p\) with \(g'(0) \not\equiv 0\); we call the integer \(h\) the height [Si1]. For an elliptic curve with good reduction at \(p\), \(h \in \{1, 2\}\), and \(h = 2\) exactly when the reduction is supersingular [Si1, Thm. V.3.1]. The two extreme models are \(\hat G_a(X, Y) = X + Y\) and \(\hat G_m(X, Y) = X + Y + XY\).

Tag 22DU

Problem 13.2.1. For \(\hat G_m\), \(\ell(X) = \log(1+X)\), \([p](X) \equiv X^p \pmod p\), and the height is \(1\).

Tag 22DV

Problem 13.2.2. As operators on \(\mathbb{Z}_p[[X]]\) modulo \(p\): for \(\hat G_a\), \(D^p \equiv 0\); for \(\hat G_m\), \(D = (1+X)\,d/dX\) and \(D^p \equiv D\).

For a one-parameter formal group over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), its normalized invariant derivation satisfies \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\) for some \(\lambda \in \mathbb{F}_p\). Problems 15.3.1 and 15.3.2 of Chapter 15 give the argument for the lemniscate, and the same argument applies to every one-parameter formal group over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\). Which constant is \(\lambda\)?

Tag 22DW

Problem 13.2.3. Let \(\hat G\) be the formal group of a Weierstrass equation over \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\), with \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\). Then \((D^n z)(0) = n!\, [u^n]\, \ell^{-1}(u)\) for every \(n\), and \(\lambda \equiv c_p \pmod p\).

Used in: Problem 13.2.4; Chapter 15
Tag 22DX

Problem 13.2.4. For \(\hat G\) as in Problem 13.2.3, the coefficient of \(z^p\) in \([p](z)\) is congruent to \(c_p\) modulo \(p\). Hence the height is \(1\) exactly when \(p \nmid c_p\).

Now we compute \(c_n\) for \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\). Let \(w := -1/y\).

Tag 22DY

Problem 13.2.5. For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), \(w + z w^2 = z^3\), and \(\omega(z) = d z / \sqrt{1 + 4z^4}\).

Tag 22DZ

Problem 13.2.6. For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), \(\ell(z) = \sum_{m \ge 0} (-1)^m \binom{2m}{m} z^{4m+1}/(4m+1)\). For odd \(n\), \(c_n\) is the coefficient of \(x^{n-1}\) in \((x^3 - x)^{(n-1)/2}\).

For odd \(p\) and a curve \(y^2 = f(x)\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) with \(f\) a cubic with distinct roots over \(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p\), the coefficient \(A_p\) of \(x^{p-1}\) in \(f^{(p-1)/2}\) is the Hasse invariant, and the curve is supersingular exactly when \(A_p = 0\) [Si1, Thm. V.4.1(a)]. By Problem 13.2.6, \(A_p\) is the reduction of \(c_p\) modulo \(p\) for \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), and Problems 13.2.3 and 13.2.4 recover the criterion for this curve. When \(p \mid c_p\), the first term of \([p](z)\) modulo \(p\) lies beyond \(z^p\). What is its first nonzero term?

§13.3. Reduction at an inert prime Tag 22E2

Which primes are supersingular for a curve with complex multiplication? Deuring’s answer [Deu]: if \(E\) has complex multiplication by an order in \(K\) and good reduction at a prime above \(p\), the reduction is supersingular if and only if \(p\) does not split in \(K\). For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) these are the \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). We check this by hand.

Tag 22E3

Problem 13.3.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). Then \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1\), that is, \(a_p := p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = 0\).

Tag 22E4

Problem 13.3.2. For \(p = 4m+1\), \(A_p = (-1)^m \binom{2m}{m} \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\). For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(A_p = 0\).

For \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) one has \(a_p = \gamma + \bar\gamma\), where \(\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) has \(\gamma\bar\gamma = p\) and \(\gamma \equiv 1 \pmod{2 + 2\mathrm{i}}\) [IR, Ch. 18].

Tag 22E5

Problem 13.3.3. \(a_5 = -2\) and \(a_{13} = 6\), in agreement with \(\gamma = -1 + 2\mathrm{i}\) and \(\gamma = 3 + 2\mathrm{i}\).

Now let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), let \(\phi(x, y) := (x^p, y^p)\) be the Frobenius endomorphism of \(E\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), and reduce \([\mathrm{i}]\) modulo \(p\) into \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\).

Tag 22E6

Problem 13.3.4. \(\phi^2 = [-p]\) and \(\phi \circ [\mathrm{i}] = [-\mathrm{i}] \circ \phi\). Hence \(\operatorname{End}(E_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p})\) is not commutative.

Tag 22E7

Problem 13.3.5. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). In the parameter \(z = -x/y\), \([p](z) \equiv -z^{p^2} \pmod p\), and \([\mathrm{i}](z) = \mathrm{i}z\).

For instance \([3](z) \equiv -z^9 \pmod 3\). To describe the formal group at these primes, we use a theorem of Lubin and Tate [LT]. Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be the ring of integers of a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), with uniformizer \(\pi\) and residue field of order \(q\). Lubin and Tate prove: (i) each \(f \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) with \(f(X) \equiv \pi X \bmod X^2\) and \(f(X) \equiv X^q \bmod \pi\) is an endomorphism of a unique formal group \(F_f\) over \(\mathcal{O}\); (ii) each \(a \in \mathcal{O}\) gives a unique endomorphism \([a]_f\) of \(F_f\) with linear term \(aX\), and \(a \mapsto [a]_f\) is a ring homomorphism; (iii) for two such \(f\), \(g\) there is a unique isomorphism \(\theta \colon F_f \to F_g\) over \(\mathcal{O}\) with linear term \(X\), and it commutes with the \([a]\). By Hazewinkel’s functional-equation lemma [Ha], the model logarithm \(\ell_\pi(X) := X + X^q/\pi + X^{q^2}/\pi^2 + \dotsb\) is the logarithm of such an \(F_f\).

Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}]\), so that \(\pi := -p\) is a uniformizer and \(q = p^2\).

Tag 22E8

Problem 13.3.6. The series \(f := [-p]\) satisfies the hypotheses of (i), the formal group \(\hat E\) of \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) is \(F_f\), and \([a]_f\) extends \(\alpha \mapsto [\alpha]\) from \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) to \(\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22E9

Problem 13.3.7. There is \(\theta \in z + z^2\mathcal{O}[[z]]\) with \(\ell = \ell_{-p} \circ \theta\). Consequently the coefficients of \(\ell\) in degrees less than \(p^2\) are \(p\)-integral, and \(p \cdot [z^{p^2}]\, \ell \equiv -1 \pmod p\).

Used in: Chapter 17

For \(p = 3\): \([z^9]\,\ell = \tfrac23\), and \(3 \cdot \tfrac23 \equiv -1 \pmod 3\). Chapter 15 proves the same congruence for the lemniscate with binomial coefficients (Problem 15.4.2).

In the model logarithm the denominators occur only in the degrees \(q, q^2, \dotsc\), and there they are \(p, p^2, \dotsc\). For the lemniscate, Theorem 15.4.1 of Chapter 15 gives a divisibility bound for the invariant derivatives of series integral in the formal parameter. Problem 15.5.3 of that chapter gives a theta function whose fourth derivative at the origin is \(4\) when \(p=3\). Chapter 14 uses \(D^p\) in a single determinant; theta functions are the subject of Chapters 15 and 16. These four chapters prepare the study of derivatives of theta functions at primes that do not split in the imaginary quadratic field of the proof. The corresponding statement in the Lean formalization is Parameters.norm_jet_minorProd_jetSeries_le; its proof uses CMLattice.exists_exponent_tsum.
