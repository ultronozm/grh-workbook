\title{The comparison operator}

\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
Complex inner product spaces and Gaussian binomial coefficients \cite[Ch.~1]{St}.  For lifts, norms and Gaussian moments over \(\Z[\ii]\), see Chapters~\ref{dt-chap} and~\ref{tn-chap}.  We use the data and spaces of Chapters~\ref{da-chap} and~\ref{ob-chap}, the descent pair of Definition~\ref{dp-def:descent-pair} and the definitions of Chapter~\ref{l1-chap}.

\medskip
The cost bound in Theorem~\ref{col-thm:main} of Chapter~\ref{col-chap} uses a comparison operator \cite[LiftVolumeHeight, line 729]{GL}.  How is it constructed?  Clause (2) of Definition~\ref{l1-def:comparison} asks that all the pullbacks \(u_{\vt,j}^*\Upsilon_\vt u_{\vt,j}\) equal a common positive definite operator times \(\Hc_j(\vt)/\Hc_j(1)\).  A seesaw over \(\Z[\ii]\) illustrates how rescaling a period makes it independent of the twist.

\section{A seesaw with a twist}\label{sec:toy}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:scalar}
Let \(A\), \(T\) and \(V_\vt\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, with \(\vt\) in a set containing \(1\); let \(u_\vt\colon A\to V_\vt\) and \(g_\vt\colon T\to V_\vt\) be linear, \(n>0\), and \(c'_\vt\ne0\) numbers such that \(c'_\vt g_\vt^*u_\vt = \per\) for every \(\vt\), with \(\per\colon A\to T\) injective and independent of \(\vt\).  Then \(\Upsilon_\vt := n\,g_\vt g_\vt^*\) satisfies
\[
  \langle u_\vt y', \Upsilon_\vt u_\vt y\rangle = \rho_\vt\,\langle y', \Upsilon^\circ y\rangle,
  \qquad \rho_\vt = \frac{\lvert c'_1\rvert^2}{\lvert c'_\vt\rvert^2},\quad \Upsilon^\circ = \frac{n}{\lvert c'_1\rvert^2}\,\per^*\per ,
\]
every \(u_\vt\) is injective, and \(\langle y,\Upsilon^\circ y\rangle>0\) for \(y\ne0\): the hypotheses of Problem~\ref{l1-pr:comparison}.
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:scalar

Write \(\Oc:=\Z[\ii]\) and \(\mathfrak q:=(3+2\ii)\), so \(\Oc/\mathfrak q\cong\F_{13}\) by \(\ii\mapsto5\).  Take \(\psi\) identically \(1\) on \(\Oc\), or the cubic character modulo \(\mathfrak q\) with \(\psi(2)=e^{2\pi\ii/3}\), extended by zero on \(\mathfrak q\).  It is trivial on units.  Write \(c_a(\psi):=\sum_{\alpha\in\Oc}\psi(\alpha)\alpha^a e^{-\pi|\alpha|^2}\), with \(c_a(1)=c_a\) as in Chapter~\ref{tn-chap}.

On \(A:=\Ac_{(8)}(1)=\C\) take \(\langle y',y\rangle=\overline{y'}y/4\).  On \(\Sym^8(\C^2)\) let \(\langle v,w\rangle_0\) be the conjugate of the pairing of Problem~\ref{dt-pr:sym-norm}, linear in \(w\).  The target pairing is \(\langle v,w\rangle_0/32\), giving the norm of \(\Ac_{(8)}(2)\) on invariant vectors.  Define \(u_\psi,g\colon A\to\Sym^8(\C^2)\) by
\[
  u_\psi(\varphi) := \frac{\varphi}{4}\sum_{x\in\Oc^2}\psi(x_2)\,(x_1e_1+x_2e_2)^8 e^{-\pi\lvert x\rvert^2},
  \qquad g(\zeta) := 8\zeta\, e_1^4e_2^4 .

\section{Gluing by a congruence}\label{sec:glue}

In the period of the gluing map, a sum over a congruence group enforces a matrix congruence.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:glue-mod9}
Modulo \(9\), let \(\Gamma := \{Y \equiv 1 \pmod 3\} = \{1,4,7\}\).  For \(v\in\Z/9\), the equation \(vY=1\) has a solution \(Y\in\Gamma\) if and only if \(v\equiv1\pmod 3\), and then exactly one.  Hence \(\mathbf 1_{v\equiv1\,(3)} = \sum_{Y\in\Gamma}\mathbf 1_{vY=1}\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:glue-mod9

\begin{problem}\label{pr:glue-sum}
Let a finite group \(\Gamma\) act on a finite set \(X\) on the right, and let \(S, N\subseteq X\) be such that for \(x\in S\) exactly one \(Y\in\Gamma\) has \(xY\in N\), and \(xY\in N\) implies \(x\in S\).  Then \(\mathbf 1_S(x) = \sum_{Y}\mathbf 1_N(xY)\), and for every \(\Gamma\)-invariant \(f\), \(\sum_{x}\mathbf 1_S(x)f(x) = \#\Gamma\sum_x \mathbf 1_N(x)f(x)\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:glue-sum

In the proof, \(X\) consists of matrices modulo \(\pi_M^2\), where \((\pi_M)\) is the product of the chosen primes (Section~\ref{ob-sec:chosen} and \cite[ExchangeData, line 80]{GL}).  Invertible \(r\times r\) matrices congruent to \(1\) modulo \(\pi_M\) act on the right.  Here \(S\) is given by the gluing congruence and \(N\) by block normalization.  The group order occurs in \(c^\circ_i\) below \cite[GluedLattices, lines 490--535 and GluingMaps, line 999]{GL}.

\section{The general statement}\label{sec:general}

Fix \(1\le j\le m\).  Write \(V_j=V''\oplus V^\circ\) with definite summands of ranks \(r+9m+1\) and \(t-9m-1\).  A choice \(i\) specifies a \(3m\times(r+9m+1)\) matrix modulo \(\pi_M^2\), a class of the standard definite space of rank \(6m+1\) at principal level \(\pi_M^2\), and at each infinite place \(r^8-(t+1)/2\) lists of \(2m\) row indices of that space.  Let \(c\) range over the classes of \(V^\circ\) at principal level \(\pi_M^2\).  Write \(\mu^-\) for the minus diagram of the untwisted \(j\)-th descent pair and \(h\) for its hook number.  Tuples \(\zeta=(\zeta_{i,c})\) carry one Weyl module of weight \(\mu^-\) per slot \((i,c)\), with the sum of the Gram pairings \cite[ExchangeData, lines 169--193, 294, 444--449 and GluingMaps, lines 58--88]{GL}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:glue}
For a twist \(\vt\), define the \emph{gluing map} from tuples \(\zeta\) to the target of \(u_{\vt,j}\) by
\[
  g_\vt\zeta=M^{l_1}\sum_{i,c,d''}\delta_{\operatorname{block}(d'',c)\gamma_i}
     \bigl(\phi''_{\vt,i}(d'')\mathbin{\odot}\zeta_{i,c}\bigr).
\]
Here \(\mathbin{\odot}\) denotes the Cartan product, \(d''\) runs over class representatives of \(V''\) at the row-fixing level of \(i,\vt\), and \(c\) in the subscript denotes its chosen representative.  The class form satisfies \(\langle\delta_x(v),\phi\rangle=\langle v,\phi(x)\rangle\).  The matrix \(\gamma_i\) is a glued lattice basis chosen at the trivial twist and \(l_1\) the denominator exponent of the splitting.  The first factor \(\phi''_{\vt,i}=\overline{c''L_{\vt,i}}\) comes from a vector-valued theta series \(L_{\vt,i}\) on \(\mathrm U(V'')(\mathbb A_f)\), with \(c''\ne0\) depending only on the data and \(j\) \cite[TestedForms, line 138, GluedLattices, line 550, GluedForms, line 450, LineCoordinates, line 331 and GluingMaps, lines 190--199, 211--247, 350--354]{GL}.
\end{definition}

First, write \(\mathsf S_{\vt,i}(g)\) for the pairing, conjugate-linear in \(\phi''_{\vt,i}\), with the theta kernel for \((W_j,V'')\), summed over the row-fixing classes.  Its finite datum tests the rows of the choice matrix stacked over the level generator times the identity modulo \(\pi_M^2\).  It evaluates the residue character, with local factors \(\chi_{M,w}^{\,j}\vt_w\), on the determinant of the distinguished rows modulo the product of \((\pi_M)^2\) and the prime ideals at the places in \(Q_\vartheta\).  Here \(\vt_w\) is the local tame part \cite[LevelAndTwists, line 336, GluedLattices, lines 272--278, 422--425 and GluingMaps, lines 249--255]{GL}.  In the last of three applications of the exchange identity of Chapter~\ref{c3-chap}, the first two applications having produced a carrier form of signature \((2m,m)\), one obtains functions \(F_i\) on \(\mathrm U(W_j)(\mathbb A)\) independent of the twist.  There is a scalar \(c'_\vt\) per twist such that \(c'_\vt\mathsf S_{\vt,i}(g)=c''F_i(g)\) for all \(i,g\), and
\[
  \lvert c'_\vt\rvert^2 = C/\Hc_j(\vt)\qquad\text{for every twist }\vt,
\]
with one \(C\in\C\) and \(1/0:=0\) in this identity.  The three exchange factors give \(\Hc_j\) of Definition~\ref{l1-def:hecke-product}.  Second, for every good form \(y\) on \(W_j\) and tuple \(\zeta\),
\[
  c'_\vt\,\langle\zeta, g_\vt^*u_{\vt,j}y\rangle = \sum_i c^\circ_i\int y\,F_i\,\Theta^\circ(\zeta_i),
\]
integrated over the adelic unitary group of \(W_j\) modulo its rational points.  Here \(c^\circ_i\ne0\) is independent of \(\vt\), and \(\Theta^\circ(\zeta_i)\) pairs the slice \((\zeta_{i,c})_c\) conjugate-linearly with the untwisted theta kernel for \((W_j,V^\circ)\) at principal level \(\pi_M^2\).  \emph{Status}: read from the code \cite[OneExchange, line 93, ThreeExchanges, lines 181, 284, 748 and GluingMaps, lines 293--319, 999--1048]{GL}.  The right side is independent of \(\vt\), so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:period-lift}
  c'_\vt\, g_\vt^*u_{\vt,j} = \per_j := c'_1\, g_1^*u_{1,j} .
\end{equation}
Third, \(\per_j\) is injective on \(H_g(W_j)\).  The proof shows that if a good form \(y\ne0\) had \(\int yF_i\Theta^\circ(\zeta_i)=0\) for all \(i,\zeta_i\), a function \(F_0\) with \(F_0(1)\ne0\) would satisfy \(|F_0(1)|\le 2(4b_*+1)b_*^{-19}|F_0(1)|\), where \(b_*:=b^{2ft^3}>10\).  \emph{Status}: read from the code \cite[ProductsSpan, lines 717, 855--907]{GL}.  For stable-range nonvanishing compare \cite{Li92}.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:peak}
For \(b_*>10\), \(2(4b_*+1)<b_*^2\le b_*^{19}\).  At \(b_*=2\) the factor \(2(4b_*+1)b_*^{-19}\) is about \(3.4\cdot10^{-5}\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:peak

\begin{problem}\label{pr:nonzero}
If \eqref{eq:period-lift} holds for every twist, \(\per_j\) is injective and \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T > 0\) (Corollary~\ref{c2-cor:equal}; positivity by \cite[SpanningTheorem, line 307]{GL}), then \(c'_\vt\ne0\) for every twist.
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:nonzero

\begin{problem}\label{pr:ratio}
Let \(c'_\vt\ne0\) and \(\lvert c'_\vt\rvert^2 = C/\Hc_j(\vt)\) for every twist, with one \(C\in\C\), where \(1/0\) is read as \(0\).  Then \(C\ne0\), every \(\Hc_j(\vt)\ne0\), and \(\Hc_j(\vt)/\Hc_j(1) = \lvert c'_1\rvert^2/\lvert c'_\vt\rvert^2 \ne 0\): clause (1).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:ratio

\begin{problem}\label{pr:clause2}
Let \(h>0\), \(\per_j\) be injective, and for every twist let \eqref{eq:period-lift} hold with \(c'_\vt\ne0\) and \(\Hc_j(\vt)/\Hc_j(1)=\lvert c'_1\rvert^2/\lvert c'_\vt\rvert^2\) (Problem~\ref{pr:ratio}).  Then the family \(\Upsilon_\vt := \Nc h^2 g_\vt g_\vt^*\) satisfies clause (2) with \(\Upsilon^\circ = \Nc h^2\lvert c'_1\rvert^{-2}\per_j^*\per_j\), and \(\Upsilon_\vt u_{\vt,j}y = \Nc h^2 c'_\vt{}^{-1}\, g_\vt\per_j y\) for every twist and good form \(y\).
\end{problem}

\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:small-roots}
Let \(q\) be a prime power, \(F\) a bounded function on a group \(G\), elements \(g_U\in G\) indexed by the subspaces of \(\F_q^n\), and \(\sum_U a_U F(gg_U) = 0\) for all \(g\), with \(a_{\{0\}} = 1\) and \(\lvert a_U\rvert = q^{\binom{\dim U}2}\lvert X\rvert^{\dim U}\). \section{Tight frames, integrality and size}\label{sec:frames}

\begin{definition}\label{def:frame}
Let \(G\) be a positive definite Hermitian \(d\times d\) matrix and \(h>0\).  A \emph{tight frame} for \((G,h)\) is a finite family \((\eta_a)\) in \(\C^d\) with \(\sum_a(\eta_a^*G\xi)\,\eta_a = h^2\xi\) for every \(\xi\in\C^d\).
\end{definition}

A frame expresses \(g_\vt g_\vt^*\) as a sum of operators of rank at most one.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:frame}
Let \(h>0\) be an integer, \(F\) an integer matrix with \(d\) independent columns (indexed by a basis of a Weyl module) and rows indexed by tensors \(T\), \(G := F^tF\), and let an integer matrix \(H\) satisfy \(GH = hI\).  Then \(\eta_T := HF^te_T\in\Z^d\) satisfies \(G\eta_T = hF^te_T\), the \(\eta_T\) form a tight frame for \((G,h)\), and \(\eta_T^*G\eta_T\le h^2\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:frame

For the Weyl modules of the proof, \(G\) is the integral Gram matrix and \(H\) the matrix of Fock pairings of the basis polynomials.  The identity \(GH=hI\) and the integral frame are read from the code \cite[GramMatrix, lines 101, 447]{GL}.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:sym-frame}
For \(\Sym^a(\C^n)\) with \(n\ge2\), let \(H\) be the matrix of Fock pairings of the monomials \(z^\alpha\), and take the basis \(m_\alpha\), \(\lvert\alpha\rvert = a\), with \(m_\alpha\) the sum of the distinct tensors \(e_{w_1}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{w_a}\) of content \(\alpha\) (so \(m_\alpha = (a!/\alpha!)e^\alpha\) in the notation of Chapter~\ref{dt-chap}), and \(F\) the inclusion into tensors.  Then \(G = \operatorname{diag}(a!/\alpha!)\), \(H = \operatorname{diag}(\alpha!)\) and \(h = a!\).  For \(\Sym^2\C^2\) (basis \(11,12,22\)): \(G = \operatorname{diag}(1,2,1)\), \(h = 2\), the frame is \(2e_{11}, e_{12}, e_{12}, 2e_{22}\), with squared \(G\)-norms \(4,2,2,4\), and \(\sum_T\eta_T\eta_T^tG = 4I\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T060300--the-comparison-operator__workbook.tex#pr:sym-frame

Problems~\ref{pr:nonzero}, \ref{pr:ratio}, \ref{pr:clause2}, \ref{pr:clause3}, \ref{pr:rank-one-sum}, \ref{pr:clause4} and~\ref{pr:clause5}, with the inputs of Sections~\ref{sec:general}--\ref{sec:frames}, prove the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:operator}
For every \(1\le j\le m\) the operators \(\Upsilon_\vt := \Nc h^2\,g_\vt g_\vt^*\), one for each twist \(\vt\), form a comparison operator in the sense of Definition~\ref{l1-def:comparison}.  Here \(g_\vt\) is the gluing map of Definition~\ref{def:glue}, from tuples of forms on the definite space \(V^\circ\) of rank \(t-9m-1\) to the target space of \(u_{\vt,j}\), \(h\) is the hook number of the weight \(\mu^-\) of those forms, and \(\Nc = \prod_{w\in\Mch}\#\mathrm{GL}_{rt}(k_w)\).
\end{theorem}

The inputs are the scalar and period identities, injectivity, \(d_T>0\) and hook positivity \cite[WeylKernel, line 57]{GL}, vanishing for false lifts, the frame, the gluing and pairing bounds and the index counts.  For the ratio and pullback see \cite[LiftVolumeHeight, lines 207--243]{GL}.  Chapter~\ref{l3-chap} treats the factors of the inverse ratio \(\Hc_j(1)/\Hc_j(\vt)\).

\begin{leanremark}
\raggedright In \cite{GL}, \tl{LiftVolumeHeight.lean}, Theorem~\ref{thm:operator} is \tl{New.Data.isComparisonOperator\_comparisonOp}.  Problem~\ref{pr:rank-one-sum} applies \tl{comparisonOp\_eq\_sum} to the frame above.  In \tl{GluingMaps.lean}, Definition~\ref{def:glue} gives \tl{New.Rows.glue}, the period integral is \tl{twistFactor\_mul\_inner\_adjoint\_glue}, and line 1068 gives injectivity.  At the bottom of the tower, \tl{normalizedDescent\_eq\_zero\_of\_mem\_orthogonal} gives vanishing of the normalized descent, \tl{hyperplaneSum\_apply\_eq\_zero} gives annihilation by the hyperplane sums, and \tl{tamePart\_twistPlace\_ne\_one} gives nontriviality of the tame part at each twist place.  The proof of \tl{descentsVanish\_hyperbolicSum} obtains vanishing for all the prescribed Schwartz data at the highest vector by \tl{lift\_polyVector\_blockDatum\_eq\_zero} and \tl{descentsVanish\_of\_hyperplaneSumsAnnihilate}.
\end{leanremark}
