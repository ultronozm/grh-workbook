Prerequisites.

Finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces: adjoints, orthogonal projections, the spectral theorem for self-adjoint maps, and the trace. No earlier chapter is used.

In the proof, Theorem B bounds how many translates of a map between spaces of automorphic forms are needed for their images to span almost all of the target. The translates are indexed by a finite group. When do a few translates of one map span? Section 9.1 bounds the rank of a sum of images from below by a second moment. The rest of the chapter turns a second moment over the group into a choice of few translates, by Cauchy–Schwarz and a greedy choice. All spaces are finite-dimensional, and inner products are linear in the second variable.

§9.1. Gram matrices Tag 22A6

For a linear map \(A\) between inner product spaces, \(\lVert A \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 := \operatorname{tr}(A^*A)\) is the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a complex inner product space of dimension \(D' > 0\). Let \(B_i \colon X_i \to \mathcal{H}\), \(i \in I\), be a finite family of linear maps from inner product spaces of dimensions \(r_i\), with \(R := \sum_i r_i > 0\), let \(\gamma_i > 0\), and let \(K_i := \gamma_i B_i^* B_i\) and \(Q := \sum_i \gamma_i B_i B_i^*\). Let \(X := \bigoplus_i X_i\) and \(\Phi \colon X \to \mathcal{H}\), \(\Phi((x_i)_i) := \sum_i \sqrt{\gamma_i} B_i x_i\), so that \(Q = \Phi\Phi^*\). Define the Gram matrix of the family by \(G := \Phi^*\Phi\). Its blocks are \(G_{ij} = \sqrt{\gamma_i\gamma_j}\, B_i^* B_j \colon X_j \to X_i\), and \(G_{ii} = K_i\). When every \(X_i\) is a line spanned by a unit vector \(\xi_i\), \(G\) is the Gram matrix of the vectors \(\sqrt{\gamma_i} B_i \xi_i\).

Tag 234A

Problem 9.1.1. If \(Q \ne 0\), then \(\operatorname{rank}Q \ge (\operatorname{tr}Q)^2/\operatorname{tr}Q^2\), with equality exactly when \(Q\) is a multiple of an orthogonal projection.

Tag 22A7

Problem 9.1.2. \(Q(\mathcal{H}) = \sum_i B_i(X_i)\).

Tag 22A8

Problem 9.1.3. \(Q\) and \(G\) have the same nonzero eigenvalues, with multiplicities. Hence \(\operatorname{tr}Q = \sum_i \operatorname{tr}K_i\) and \(\operatorname{tr}Q^2 = \sum_{i,j} \lVert G_{ij} \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\).

Problems 9.1.1–9.1.3 prove the following.

Tag 22A9

Theorem 9.1.1. If some \(B_i\) is nonzero, then \[\dim \sum_i B_i(X_i) \ge \frac{\bigl( \sum_i \operatorname{tr}K_i \bigr)^2}{\sum_{i,j} \lVert G_{ij} \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2} .\]

Used in: Problem 9.1.4

For lines this is a form of Welch’s bound [We]. Unlike the left side, the right side changes little when the \(B_i\) are perturbed.

Tag 22AA

Problem 9.1.4. Let \(v, w \in \mathbb{C}^2\) be unit vectors, \(X_1 = X_2 = \mathbb{C}\), \(B_1(1) = v\), \(B_2(1) = w\) and \(\gamma_1 = \gamma_2 = 1\). The right side of Theorem 9.1.1 is \(2/(1 + \lvert \langle v, w \rangle \rvert^2)\).

So two nearly parallel lines count as about one direction. In the proof a finite group acts on the target of one map \(B\). The moment hypothesis bounds the dimension of the projected target times the sum of the squared overlaps \(\lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\) over all group elements, including the identity (Theorem 9.3.1 below).

§9.2. Translates and the second moment Tag 234B

From now on \(S\) and \(T\) are finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, \(\Gamma\) is a finite group, and \(\rho\) is a representation of \(\Gamma\) on \(T\) with \(\langle \rho(s)x, \rho(s)y \rangle = \langle x, y \rangle\) for all \(s \in \Gamma\) and \(x, y \in T\). Let \(B \colon S \to T\) be linear. For a subspace \(W \subseteq T\) let \(P_W\) be the orthogonal projection of \(T\) onto \(W\), as a map \(T \to T\). We define the second moment of \(B\) to be \(\sum_{s \in \Gamma} \lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\). Write \(\langle A, A' \rangle_\mathrm{HS}:= \operatorname{tr}(A^*A')\) for linear maps \(A, A'\) with the same source and target.

Tag 234C

Problem 9.2.1. For every orthonormal basis \((e_k)\) of \(S\) and all \(A, A' \colon S \to T\), \(\langle A, A' \rangle_\mathrm{HS}= \sum_k \langle Ae_k, A'e_k \rangle\). For every subspace \(W \subseteq T\), \(\lVert P_W \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \dim W\) and \(\lVert P_W A \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \langle P_W, AA^* \rangle_\mathrm{HS}\).

Used in: Chapter 40

Consider the family of all translates, \(I = \Gamma\), \(X_s = S\), \(B_s = \rho(s)B\) and \(\gamma_s = 1\), in the notation of Section 9.1.

Tag 234D

Problem 9.2.2. \(G_{st} = B^*\rho(s^{-1}t)B\), and \(\langle \rho(s)BB^*\rho(s)^*, \rho(t)BB^*\rho(t)^* \rangle_\mathrm{HS}= \lVert B^*\rho(s^{-1}t)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\). Hence \[\Bigl\lVert \sum_{s \in \Gamma} \rho(s)BB^*\rho(s)^* \Bigr\rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \sum_{s, t \in \Gamma} \lVert G_{st} \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \#\Gamma \sum_{s \in \Gamma} \lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 .\]

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 40; Chapter 47
Tag 234E

Problem 9.2.3. Let \(S \ne 0\), let \(B\) be an isometry, and let \(\varepsilon \ge 0\). If \(\dim T \cdot \sum_s \lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le \#\Gamma\,(1 + \varepsilon)(\dim S)^2\), then the images \(\rho(s)B(S)\), \(s \in \Gamma\), together span a subspace of dimension at least \(\dim T/(1 + \varepsilon)\).

Problem 9.2.3 uses all \(\#\Gamma\) translates. For \(0<\varepsilon<1/16\), Theorem 9.4.1 will give a span of at least \((1-16\varepsilon)\dim T\) dimensions in this setting, using at most \(\frac52(1+\log(1/(16\varepsilon)))\dim T/\dim S\) translates. The first step is a bound for one subspace at a time.

Tag 234F

Problem 9.2.4. For every subspace \(W \subseteq T\), \[\Bigl( \sum_{s \in \Gamma} \lVert P_W\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \Bigr)^2 \le \dim W \cdot \#\Gamma \sum_{s \in \Gamma} \lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 .\]

An isometry condition on the projected map gives a lower bound for the second moment. Let \(p \colon T \to T\) be a linear map with \(p^2 = p\) and \(p^* = p\), commuting with every \(\rho(s)\), and let \(T_0 := \operatorname{im}p\).

Tag 234G

Problem 9.2.5. \(T_0\) and \(T_0^\perp\) are stable under \(\Gamma\), and \(p = P_{T_0}\). If \(pB\) is an isometry, then \(\#\Gamma\,(\dim S)^2 \le \dim T_0 \cdot \sum_s \lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\).

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 40: Problem 40.2.1

When \(pB\) is an isometry and \(T_0\ne0\), Problem 9.2.5 says that the mean of \(\lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\) over \(s \in \Gamma\) is at least \((\dim S)^2/\dim T_0\). The hypothesis below requires it to be at most \(1+\varepsilon\) times this value.

§9.3. One translate Tag 234H

Let \(A \colon S \to T\) be linear and \(\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}\). We say that \(A\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\) if \(\dim U - \varepsilon \dim S \le \lVert A P_U \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\) for every subspace \(U \subseteq S\), where now \(P_U \colon S \to S\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(U\). This is a lower bound in mean square only.

Tag 234J

Problem 9.3.1. If \(\varepsilon \ge 0\) and \(\lVert A^*A - 1 \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le \varepsilon^2 \dim S\), then \(A\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\).

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 48

Let \(\Theta\) be a set, let \(V\) and \(Y\) be finite-dimensional complex vector spaces, and let \(C_\theta \colon V \to Y\), \(\theta \in \Theta\), be linear maps. For \(\eta \in \mathbb{R}\), we say that the family \((C_\theta)\) spreads with error \(\eta\) if for every subspace \(F \subseteq Y\), with \(\alpha := \dim F/\dim Y\) (and \(\alpha := 0\) if \(Y = 0\)), there is \(\theta \in \Theta\) with \[\dim C_\theta^{-1}(F) \le \Bigl( \frac{1 + \alpha}{2} + \eta \Bigr) \dim V .\] The index \(\theta\) may depend on \(F\).

Now return to Section 9.2, and let \(C_s := p\rho(s)B\), viewed as a map \(S \to T_0\). Assume that \(\varepsilon \ge 0\), that \(pB\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\), and that \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:moment}\tag{9.3.1} \dim T_0 \cdot \sum_{s \in \Gamma} \lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le \#\Gamma\,(1 + \varepsilon)(\dim S)^2 . \end{equation}\] Let \(W \subseteq T_0\) be a subspace and let \(\alpha := \dim W/\dim T_0\), with \(\alpha := 0\) if \(T_0=0\).

Tag 234K

Problem 9.3.2. Some \(s \in \Gamma\) has \(\lVert P_W\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le \frac12(1 + \alpha + \varepsilon)\dim S\).

Used in: Problem 9.3.3
Tag 234L

Problem 9.3.3. For every \(s \in \Gamma\), let \(U := C_s^{-1}(W) \subseteq S\). Then \(\lVert pBP_U \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le \lVert P_W\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\). For \(s\) as in Problem 9.3.2, \(\dim U \le \bigl(\frac{1+\alpha}{2} + \frac32\varepsilon\bigr)\dim S\).

Problems 9.2.4, 9.3.2 and 9.3.3 prove the following, which is a step of the proof.

Tag 234M

Theorem 9.3.1. Let \(S\) and \(T\) be finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, \(\Gamma\) a finite group, and \(\rho\) a representation of \(\Gamma\) on \(T\) with \(\langle \rho(s)x, \rho(s)y \rangle = \langle x, y \rangle\) for all \(s \in \Gamma\) and \(x, y \in T\). Let \(p \colon T \to T\) be linear with \(p^2 = p\), \(p^* = p\) and \(p\rho(s) = \rho(s)p\) for every \(s \in \Gamma\). Let \(B \colon S \to T\) be linear and \(\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}\), with \(\varepsilon \ge 0\), such that \(pB\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\) and (9.3.1) holds with \(T_0 := \operatorname{im}p\). Then the maps \(p\rho(s)B \colon S \to T_0\), \(s \in \Gamma\), spread with error \(\frac32\varepsilon\).

Used in: Chapter 10; Chapter 39; Chapter 47; Chapter 48: Problem 48.3.6

Here \(T_0 = 0\) and \(\varepsilon = 0\) are allowed.

§9.4. The greedy choice Tag 234N

Return to maps \(C_\theta \colon V \to Y\), \(\theta \in \Theta\), as in Section 9.3. For a subspace \(F \subseteq Y\) let \(c(F) := \dim Y - \dim F\) be its codimension. In Problems 9.4.2–9.4.4 assume that \(\dim Y > 0\), that \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/16\), and that the family spreads with error \(\frac32\varepsilon\), and put \(c_0 := 2\dim V/(5\dim Y)\).

Tag 234P

Problem 9.4.1. For every linear \(A \colon V \to Y\) and every subspace \(F \subseteq Y\), \(\dim(F + A(V)) + \dim A^{-1}(F) = \dim F + \dim V\).

Tag 234Q

Problem 9.4.2. If \(c(F) > 16\varepsilon\dim Y\), then some \(\theta\) has \(c(F + C_\theta(V)) \le (1 - c_0)\,c(F)\).

Tag 234R

Problem 9.4.3. For every integer \(\ell \ge 0\) there is a set \(t \subseteq \Theta\) with \(\#t \le \ell\) and \(c\bigl(\sum_{\theta \in t} C_\theta(V)\bigr) \le \max\bigl(16\varepsilon, e^{-\ell c_0}\bigr)\dim Y\).

Tag 234S

Problem 9.4.4. If \(V \ne 0\), then \(0 < c_0 \le 1\).

Used in: Problem 9.4.5
Tag 234T

Problem 9.4.5. Under the hypotheses of Problem 9.4.4, with \(V\ne0\), there is an integer \(\ell\ge0\) such that \(e^{-\ell c_0}\le16\varepsilon\) and \(\ell c_0\le1+\log(1/(16\varepsilon))\), where \(\log\) is the natural logarithm.

Problems 9.4.3 and 9.4.5 give the following theorem, used in the proof.

Tag 234U

Theorem 9.4.1. Let \(\Theta\) be a set, let \(V\) and \(Y\) be finite-dimensional complex vector spaces, let \(C_\theta \colon V \to Y\), \(\theta \in \Theta\), be linear maps, and let \(\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}\). Assume that \(V \ne 0\), that the family spreads with error \(\frac32\varepsilon\), that \(\dim Y > 0\), and that \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/16\). Then there is a finite set \(t \subseteq \Theta\) with \[\#t \cdot \dim V \le \frac52 \Bigl( 1 + \log \frac{1}{16\varepsilon} \Bigr) \dim Y \quad\text{and}\quad (1 - 16\varepsilon)\dim Y \le \dim \sum_{\theta \in t} C_\theta(V) .\]

Used in: Problem 9.5.2; Chapter 10; Chapter 39

Applied with \(\Theta = \Gamma\), \(V = S\), \(Y = T_0\) and \(C_s = p\rho(s)B\), Theorems 9.3.1 and 9.4.1 say: if \(S \ne 0\), \(T_0 \ne 0\), \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/16\), \(pB\) is a near isometry with error \(\varepsilon\) and (9.3.1) holds, then the images of at most \(\frac52(1 + \log(1/(16\varepsilon)))\dim T_0/\dim S\) of the maps \(p\rho(s)B\) span at least \((1 - 16\varepsilon)\dim T_0\) dimensions of \(T_0\). This is the step that Theorem B of the proof needs. There (Chapter 39) \(\Gamma\) is the finite group \(\bar G\), \(p\) is the action of the good idempotent \(e_0\), \(T_0\) is the space \(T\) of target forms, of dimension \(d_T\), and the theorems are used with \(\varepsilon=1/(8b^2)\), where \(b\) is the weight at the \(b\)-places (Chapter 36). The resulting statement, after restriction to the ramified source forms, is Theorem 39.4.2. For a related logarithmic bound in greedy submodular covering, see Wolsey [Wo].

§9.5. Translations of a cyclic group Tag 234V

Let \(\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\), \(T = \mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}\) with \(\langle f, g \rangle = \sum_x \overline{f(x)} g(x)\), \((\rho(s)f)(x) = f(x - s)\), and \(1 \le M \le N\). Let \(S = \mathbb{C}^M\) with standard basis \(e_0, \dots, e_{M-1}\), and \(I := \{0, 1, \dots, M-1\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\).

Tag 234W

Problem 9.5.1. Let \(Be_j := \delta_j\), the indicator function of \(\{j\}\). Then \(\lVert B^*\rho(s)B \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \#(I \cap (I + s))\), and the second moment is \(M^2\). Hence, with \(p = 1\), (9.3.1) holds with \(\varepsilon = 0\), with equality.

Used in: Problem 9.5.2
Tag 234X

Problem 9.5.2. In Problem 9.5.1, for \(0 < \varepsilon < 1/16\), the least number of translates \(\rho(s)B\) whose images span at least \((1 - 16\varepsilon)N\) dimensions is \(\lceil (1 - 16\varepsilon)N/M \rceil\). For \(N = 12\), \(M = 3\), \(\varepsilon = 1/128\) it is \(4\), while the bound of Theorem 9.4.1 is \(10(1 + \log 8) \approx 30.79\).

Every translate of \(B(S)\) is orthogonal to any \(\Gamma\)-stable subspace orthogonal to \(B(S)\).

Tag 234Y

Problem 9.5.3. Let \(Be_j(x) := N^{-1/2}e^{2\pi \mathrm{i} jx/N}\), \(0 \le j < M\). Then \(B\) is an isometry, every \(B^*\rho(s)B\) is diagonal and unitary, the second moment is \(NM\), and the images of any nonempty set of translates span exactly \(B(S)\). With \(p = 1\), (9.3.1) holds exactly when \(N \le (1 + \varepsilon)M\). The subspace \(B(S)\) is \(\Gamma\)-stable, and with \(p = P_{B(S)}\), (9.3.1) holds with \(\varepsilon = 0\).

Tag 234Z

Problem 9.5.4. In the setting of Section 9.2, let \(S \ne 0\), let \(B\) be an isometry, and let \(T_1 \subseteq T\) be a \(\Gamma\)-stable subspace orthogonal to \(B(S)\). If (9.3.1) holds with \(p = 1\), then \(\dim T_1 \le \varepsilon\dim T/(1 + \varepsilon)\).

Thus a stable subspace orthogonal to \(B(S)\) gives a lower bound for the second moment. In general the theorems bound the dimension spanned inside the chosen target \(T_0=\operatorname{im}p\).
