Prerequisites.

Jensen’s formula; Gauss’s lemma for polynomials over a factorial ring; binary forms and their discriminants; Bézout’s theorem for plane curves. Mahler measures [BG] and Chow forms [GKZ, Chs. 3–4] are recalled in the text. Hadamard’s inequality \(\lvert\det(v_1, \dots, v_r)\rvert \le \prod_j \lVert v_j \rVert\) for vectors of a Hermitian space of dimension \(r\) (for integer matrices, Problem 6.1.3). From Chapter 6, The slope method: Section 6.3 (degrees; over \(\mathbb{Q}\), \(\deg L = -\log\operatorname{covol}L\)). From Chapter 26, Covolumes of lattices of sections: the lattice \(E_D\) and Problems 26.1.1 and 26.1.5, and Theorem 26.1.1, of Section 26.1, and Theorem 26.2.1 and formula (1) of Section 26.2.

In Chapter 26 the covolume of \(E_D = H^0(\mathbb{P}^1_\mathbb{Z}, \mathcal O(D))\) was computed exactly because the monomials are orthogonal. On a curve \(C \subset \mathbb{P}^2\) no orthogonal basis of sections is known, and the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem gives \(\deg H^0(C, \mathcal O(D))\) only up to \(o(D^2)\), with an error that is not explicit. How can one bound this degree from below with every constant explicit? The reconstruction [Adr, proof dependency map, item HSAt] describes the covolume bound of the argument as a Chow-form determinant argument in the style of Nesterenko and Philippon [NP], with Mahler heights, Hadamard’s inequality and a monomial count. This chapter treats the ingredients: Mahler measures, Chow forms as heights, the exact covolume for points on \(\mathbb{P}^1\), and a determinant of sections of \(\mathbb{P}^1\), where one sees what Hadamard’s inequality loses.

§27.1. Mahler measures Tag 22R4

For a nonzero \(P \in \mathbb{C}[x_1, \dots, x_N]\) let \[m(P) := \int_{[0,1]^N} \log\bigl\lvert P(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_1}, \dots, e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta_N})\bigr\rvert \, d\theta , \qquad M(P) := e^{m(P)} ,\] the (logarithmic) Mahler measure [BG]. For \(G\) homogeneous on \(\mathbb{C}^N\), or multihomogeneous on \(\mathbb{C}^{N_1} \times \dots \times \mathbb{C}^{N_k}\), let \(m_{S}(G)\) be the mean of \(\log\lvert G \rvert\) over the product of the unit spheres \(S^{2N_j - 1}\), each with its invariant probability measure. Write \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_2\) for the Euclidean norm and \(H_k := \sum_{j=1}^{k} 1/j\), with \(H_0 = 0\).

Tag 22R5

Problem 27.1.1. \(M(a \prod_i (x - \alpha_i)) = \lvert a \rvert \prod_i \max(1, \lvert\alpha_i\rvert)\), and \(M(PQ) = M(P)M(Q)\). Hence if a binary form factors as \(F = \prod_i (\beta_i x - \alpha_i y)\), then \(M(F(x,1)) = \prod_i \max(\lvert\alpha_i\rvert, \lvert\beta_i\rvert)\).

Tag 22R6

Problem 27.1.2. Order the monomials of \(P \ne 0\) lexicographically with \(x_N\) most significant: compare the exponents of \(x_N\) first, then those of \(x_{N-1}\), and so on. Let \(a\) be the coefficient of the largest monomial of \(P\). Then \(m(P) \ge \log\lvert a \rvert\). In particular \(M(P) \ge 1\) for every nonzero \(P \in \mathbb{Z}[x_1, \dots, x_N]\).

Tag 22R7

Problem 27.1.3. Let \(z\) be uniform on the unit sphere of \(\mathbb{C}^N\), \(N \ge 2\). Then \(\lvert z_1 \rvert^2\) has density \((N-1)(1-t)^{N-2}\) on \([0,1]\), and \(\mathbb E \log\lvert z_1 \rvert^2 = -H_{N-1}\).

The measure \(m_{S}\) on spheres was introduced by Philippon [Ph1]. Lelong [Le] proved that \(0 \le m(G) - m_{S}(G) \le \frac12 H_{N-1}\, k\) for every \(G \in \mathbb{C}[u_1, \dots, u_N]\) homogeneous of degree \(k\); see [KPS]. We need the lower bound in the following form.

Tag 22R8

Problem 27.1.4. If \(G \in \mathbb{Z}[u_1, \dots, u_N]\) is nonzero and homogeneous of degree \(k\), then \(m_{S}(G) \ge -\tfrac12 H_{N-1}\, k\).

Tag 22R9

Problem 27.1.5. For \(p \in \mathbb{C}^N \setminus 0\), \(m_{S}(u_1p_1 + \dots + u_Np_N) = \log\lvert p \rvert_2 - \tfrac12 H_{N-1}\).

§27.2. Points on the projective line Tag 22RA

The smallest case is a hypersurface in \(\mathbb{P}^1\), a finite set of points, where the covolume is exact. Let \(F \in \mathbb{Z}[x, y]\) be a primitive binary form of degree \(\delta \ge 1\) with \(\operatorname{disc}F \ne 0\), and factor \(F = \prod_{i=1}^{\delta} (\omega_{i1}x - \omega_{i0}y)\) with \(\omega_i \in \mathbb{C}^2\). The \(\omega_i\) are determined up to \(\omega_i \mapsto t_i\omega_i\) with \(\prod t_i = 1\), and the points \(X = \{(\omega_{i0} : \omega_{i1})\}\) are the zeros of \(F\). The polynomial \[f_X(u_0, u_1) := \prod_i (u_0\omega_{i0} + u_1\omega_{i1}) = F(u_1, -u_0)\] vanishes exactly when the hyperplane \(u_0x + u_1y = 0\) meets \(X\); it is the Chow form of \(X\).

Tag 22RB

Problem 27.2.1. We have \[m_{S}(f_X) = \sum_i \log\lvert\omega_i\rvert_2 - \tfrac12\delta , \qquad m(F) = \sum_i \log\max(\lvert\omega_{i0}\rvert, \lvert\omega_{i1}\rvert) ,\] so that \(m(F) \le m_{S}(f_X) + \tfrac12\delta \le m(F) + \tfrac12\delta\log 2\). For \(\delta = 1\) and \(F = qx - py\), \(m(F)\) is the Weil height of \((p : q)\).

For \(D \ge \delta - 1\) let \(\Lambda_D := \mathbb{Z}[x,y]_D / F\,\mathbb{Z}[x,y]_{D-\delta}\), the lattice of sections of \(\mathcal O(D)\) on \(X\), with \[\lVert g \rVert^2 := \sum_{i=1}^{\delta} \frac{\lvert g(\omega_i) \rvert^2}{\lvert\omega_i\rvert_2^{2D}} .\]

Tag 22RC

Problem 27.2.2. \(F\,\mathbb{Z}[x,y]_{D-\delta} = \mathbb{Z}[x,y]_D \cap F\,\mathbb{Q}[x,y]_{D-\delta}\), so \(\Lambda_D\) is free of rank \(\delta\), and \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) is a Euclidean norm on \(\Lambda_D \otimes \mathbb{R}\). For linearly independent \(g_1, \dots, g_\delta \in \Lambda_D\), \[\lvert\det(g_j(\omega_i))\rvert \big/ \textstyle\prod_i \lvert\omega_i\rvert_2^{D}\] is the covolume of the lattice they span.

Let \(a\) be the coefficient of \(x^\delta\) in \(F\), and suppose \(a \ne 0\). Let \(V := \prod_{i < i'} (\omega_{i'0}\omega_{i1} - \omega_{i0}\omega_{i'1})\).

Tag 22RD

Problem 27.2.3. The images of \(x^jy^{D-j}\), \(0 \le j < \delta\), span a sublattice of index \(\lvert a \rvert^{D-\delta+1}\) in \(\Lambda_D\).

Tag 22RE

Problem 27.2.4. \(\det\bigl(\omega_{i0}^{j}\omega_{i1}^{D-j}\bigr)_{1 \le i \le \delta,\, 0 \le j < \delta} = \pm a^{D-\delta+1} V\), and \(V^2 = \operatorname{disc}F\), where \(\operatorname{disc}F = a^{2\delta-2}\prod_{i<i'}(\alpha_i - \alpha_{i'})^2\) for \(F = a\prod_i (x - \alpha_i y)\).

So for a basis \(g_j\) of \(\Lambda_D\) the determinant \(\det(g_j(\omega_i))\) is \(\pm V\): the index of Problem 27.2.3 cancels the power of \(a\) in Problem 27.2.4. When \(a = 0\) one first applies a substitution in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), which changes \(\det(g_j(\omega_i))\) and \(V\) at most by a sign. With Problems 27.2.1 and 27.2.2 this proves the following.

Tag 22RF

Theorem 27.2.1. For \(D \ge \delta - 1\), \[\deg \Lambda_D = D \sum_i \log\lvert\omega_i\rvert_2 - \tfrac12\log\lvert\operatorname{disc}F\rvert = D\bigl(m_{S}(f_X) + \tfrac12\delta\bigr) - \tfrac12\log\lvert\operatorname{disc}F\rvert .\]

Tag 22RG

Problem 27.2.5. For \(F = x^2 + y^2\), \(\deg\Lambda_D = (D-1)\log 2\). For \(F = x^2 - 2y^2\), \(\deg\Lambda_D = D\log 3 - \tfrac32\log 2\); for \(D = 1\) this is \(0.0588\ldots\)

The slope grows linearly in \(D\) with coefficient \(\frac1\delta\bigl(m_{S}(f_X) + \frac12\delta\bigr)\), a Mahler measure of the Chow form plus a constant. For a curve there is no such closed form.

§27.3. Chow forms Tag 22RH

Let \(X \subset \mathbb{P}^n_\mathbb{Q}\) be irreducible of dimension \(d\) and degree \(\delta\). The \((d+1)\)-tuples of hyperplanes \(u^{(0)}, \dots, u^{(d)}\) with a common zero on \(X(\mathbb{C})\) form a hypersurface defined over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and irreducible over \(\mathbb{Q}\). Its equation \(f_X \in \mathbb{Z}[u^{(0)}, \dots, u^{(d)}]\), taken primitive, is irreducible over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and multihomogeneous of degree \(\delta\) in each block; it is the Chow form of \(X\), unique up to sign [GKZ, Ch. 3, §2 and Ch. 4, §1]. For a hypersurface \(X = V(F)\), \(f_X\) is \(F\) evaluated at the vector of signed \(n \times n\) minors of \((u^{(0)}, \dots, u^{(n-1)})\), the common zero of the \(n\) hyperplanes. Philippon used Mahler measures of \(f_X\) as heights [Ph1]. He also proved that the Fubini–Study height of Bost, Gillet and Soulé [BGS], the arithmetic self-intersection of \(\bar{\mathcal O}(1)\) on \(X\), is \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:philippon}\tag{27.1} h_{\mathrm{FS}}(X) = m_{S}(f_X) + \tfrac12 (d+1)\,\delta\, H_n \end{equation}\] [Ph3]. By Problem 27.2.1, Theorem 27.2.1 reads \(\deg\Lambda_D = Dh_{\mathrm{FS}}(X) - \frac12\log\lvert\operatorname{disc}F\rvert\).

From now on \(C = V(F) \subset \mathbb{P}^2\), with \(F \in \mathbb{Z}[x_0, x_1, x_2]\) primitive and irreducible of degree \(\delta\). Lines are \(s(x) = s_0x_0 + s_1x_1 + s_2x_2\), and \(s \times l\) is the cross product.

Tag 22RJ

Problem 27.3.1. \(f_C(s, l) = F(s \times l)\). If the line \(s = 0\) meets \(C\) in \(\delta\) distinct points \(P_1, \dots, P_\delta \in \mathbb{C}^3\), then \(f_C(s, l) = c(s)\prod_j l(P_j)\) with \(c(s) \ne 0\).

The hypothesis holds for \(s\) outside a proper Zariski-closed set: through a point \(Q \notin C\) only finitely many lines are tangent to \(C\) or pass through a singular point, by Bézout applied to \(C\) and the polar curve \(\sum_k Q_k\,\partial F/\partial x_k = 0\).

Tag 22RK

Problem 27.3.2. For the line \(x_2 = 0\) in \(\mathbb{P}^2\), \(f = s_0l_1 - s_1l_0\) and \(m_{S}(f) = -1\) on \(S^5 \times S^5\). For \(\mathbb{P}^1\) itself, with \(f = s_0l_1 - s_1l_0\) on \(S^3 \times S^3\), \(m_{S}(f) = -\frac12\). In both cases (27.1) gives \(\frac12\), the value of formula (1) of Chapter 26 for \(N = 1\).

Now let the line move, so that the model changes while the curve over \(\mathbb{C}\) stays a projective line. For an integer \(N \ge 0\) let \(C_N \subset \mathbb{P}^2\) be the line \(x_2 = Nx_0\); thus \(C_0\) is the line of Problem 27.3.2. Let \(H^0(C_N, \mathcal O(T))\) be the lattice of restrictions of \(\mathbb{Z}[x_0, x_1, x_2]_T\) to \(C_N\), with the norm \[\lVert g \rVert^2 := \int_{C_N(\mathbb{C})} \frac{\lvert g(z) \rvert^2}{\lvert z \rvert_2^{2T}} \, d\mu_N(z) ,\] where \(\mu_N\) is the probability measure of the metric induced by the Fubini–Study metric of \(\mathbb{P}^2\). For \(N = 0\) this is the lattice \(E_T\) of Chapter 26.

Tag 22RL

Problem 27.3.3. The restrictions of \(x_0^ix_1^{T-i}\), \(0 \le i \le T\), form an orthogonal \(\mathbb{Z}\)-basis of \(H^0(C_N, \mathcal O(T))\), and \[\deg H^0(C_N, \mathcal O(T)) - \deg H^0(C_0, \mathcal O(T)) = \tfrac14 T(T+1)\log(1 + N^2) .\]

Tag 22RM

Problem 27.3.4. \(f_{C_N}(s, l) = (s \times l)_2 - N(s \times l)_0\), which is \(\det(p, s, l)\) for \(p = (-N, 0, 1)\), and \(h_{\mathrm{FS}}(C_N) - h_{\mathrm{FS}}(C_0) = \frac12\log(1 + N^2)\).

By Theorem 26.2.1 with \(d = 1\), the coefficient of \(T^2\) in \(\deg H^0(C_N, \mathcal O(T))\) is \(\frac12h_{\mathrm{FS}}(C_N)\). The change of height in Problem 27.3.4 therefore predicts a change \(\frac14\log(1 + N^2)\) in that coefficient, which is the coefficient of \(T^2\) in Problem 27.3.3. The height determines the leading term exactly; the term \(\frac14 T\log(1 + N^2)\) is invisible to it.

§27.4. A determinant of sections of the projective line Tag 22RN

What does Hadamard’s inequality lose when the sections are not orthogonal? Take \(X = \mathbb{P}^1\), so that \(\Lambda_D = E_D\) with the norm of Chapter 26. For \(s, l \in \mathbb{C}^2\) write also \(s = s_0x + s_1y\) and \(l = l_0x + l_1y\), and consider the sections \(s^al^{D-a}\), \(0 \le a \le D\).

Tag 22RP

Problem 27.4.1. In the monomial basis, \(\det\bigl(s^al^{D-a}\bigr)_{0 \le a \le D} = \pm(s_0l_1 - s_1l_0)^{D(D+1)/2}\).

Tag 22RQ

Problem 27.4.2. If \(s\) and \(l\) are orthonormal, the columns are orthogonal in \(E_D \otimes \mathbb{C}\), Hadamard’s inequality is an equality, and it gives Problem 26.1.1 again.

Tag 22RR

Problem 27.4.3. Bounding the norm of each section \(s^al^{D-a}\) by \(1\) for unit vectors \(s, l\), and averaging over \(s, l\) in the unit spheres, gives \(\deg E_D \ge -\frac14 D(D+1)\). For \(D = 10\) this is \(-27.5\), against \(\deg E_{10} = 32.1969\ldots\)

So at an orthonormal pair nothing is lost, and the main term \(\frac14D^2\) of Theorem 26.1.1 comes from the small norms of the columns, about \(\binom{D}{a}^{-1/2}\). Averaging over \(s\) and \(l\) loses \(\frac12 D^2\), because it spreads the pair over pairs whose sections are far from orthogonal. For a curve \(C \subset \mathbb{P}^2\) no orthogonal family of this kind is known, and the argument described in [Adr, proof dependency map, item HSAt] uses Chow forms and a determinant estimate. The argument needs a lower bound of this kind, with explicit constants, on varieties of growing dimension, where Section 26.4 recorded that such bounds lose a factor exponential in the dimension.
