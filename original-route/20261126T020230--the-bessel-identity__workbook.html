Prerequisites.

From Chapter 35: the lattices and the period maps \(B_u\) of Section 35.5, and the coefficient field \(L\) with its fixed embedding \(\sigma\colon L\to\mathbb C\). From Chapter 43: Bessel realizations (1), Theorems 43.2.1, 43.2.2 and 43.3.1, and Problems 43.4.2 and 43.4.3. From Chapter 44: the Poincaré series \(P_u\), the spaces \(C_u\), the toy of Section 44.3 and Theorem 44.4.1. From Chapter 45: Problems 45.1.2, 45.1.3, 45.1.4 and 45.3.2, and Theorem 45.5.1. From Chapter 23: the period of Section 23.4. Inner products on complex Hilbert spaces are conjugate-linear in the first variable, as in Chapter 43.

Condition (d) of Theorem 43.3.1 asks that the period maps have a Bessel realization: each \(B_u\) is, up to unitary changes of variable and a scalar, an inner product with a Poincaré series. Chapter 45 moved the pairing at the top of the chain to a Bessel period at the bottom, and Chapter 44 wrote Bessel periods as inner products with Poincaré series. This chapter assembles these into the identity that the Lean formalization [Lean] proves, and says what it gives.

§46.1. The statement

For one record, let \(\mathcal H\) be the space of automorphic forms of Chapter 44, of dimension \(D\). Let \(\mathcal E_0:=(R\otimes L)\otimes_{L,\sigma}\mathbb C\) be the complexification of the row space of Chapter 35, of dimension \(D_{\rm r}\le D\), with the inner product given by the norm of \(R\) at \(\sigma\), and let \(s\colon\mathcal E_0\to\mathcal H\) be a conjugate-linear isometry, the row embedding. For each word \(u\) let \(M_u\subset C_u\) be the complexification of the forms of level \(0\) of \(u\) ramified at \(T_u\), with the inner product of \(C_u\), and \(P_u\colon C_u\to\mathcal H\) the Bessel–Poincaré map. The period map gives a \(\mathbb C\)-bilinear pairing \(B_u\colon M_u\times\mathcal E_0\to\mathbb C\); on vectors coming from the \(L\)-structures it is defined, as in (B) of Section 35.5, by the pairing at the end of the chain, and \(B_u^{\rm alg}\) denotes the corresponding \(L\)-valued pairing of the lattices.

Theorem 46.1.1 (Bessel identity). For every record of the family at a large scale:

  1. \(B_u(\varphi,F)=\sigma\bigl(B_u^{\rm alg}(\varphi,F)\bigr)\) for \(\varphi\) and \(F\) in the \(L\)-structures;

  2. there are a unitary operator \(V\) of \(\mathcal E_0\) and, for each word \(u\), a number \(a_u\ne0\) and a unitary operator \(U_u\) of \(M_u\) with \[B_u(\varphi,F)=a_u\,\langle sVF,\ P_uU_u\varphi\rangle_\mathcal H\qquad(\varphi\in M_u,\ F\in\mathcal E_0).\]

Status: proved in the formalization, for the family it constructs; the proof is the subject of this chapter, and we say at each step how far we have followed it. In [Adr] the statement is Theorem 43.4.3 of Chapter 43, derived there from hypotheses assumed there, among them a seesaw relation for an abstract period map, which the formalization does not state.

Problem 46.1.1. The right side of (ii) is \(\mathbb C\)-linear in \(\varphi\) and in \(F\). If \(s\) were \(\mathbb C\)-linear and (ii) held with \(B_u\) bilinear, then \(B_u=0\).

Problem 46.1.2. By Theorem 46.1.1(i), \(B_u\) is the \(\mathbb C\)-bilinear extension of \(\sigma\circ B_u^{\rm alg}\), that is, the map \(B_{u,v}\) of Chapter 43 at the place \(v\) of \(\sigma\); so Theorem 46.1.1(i) and (ii) give a Bessel realization (1) of the maps \(B_u\), with \(Y_v=\mathcal E_0\), \(V_{u,v}=M_u\), \(\iota_0=s\), \(U=V\), \(\alpha_u=a_u\), \(\iota_u\) the inclusion \(M_u\subset C_u\), and the same \(U_u\).

In the toy of Section 44.3, let \(\mathcal E_0:=\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)\), with \(s(F)\) the form whose coefficients in the basis \(e^\nu\) are the complex conjugates of those of \(F\), let \(M_j=C_j:=\mathcal A_{(j)}(1)\), and put \(B_j(\xi,F):=B(sF,\xi)\), with the period \(B\) of Section 44.2 read with the inner product of this chapter, so that \(B(\psi,\xi)\) is conjugate-linear in \(\psi\); it is the complex conjugate of the period \(P(\psi,\xi)\) of Chapter 23 when the latter is read as linear in \(\psi\).

Problem 46.1.3. \(s\) is a conjugate-linear isometry of \(\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)\), \(B_j\) is bilinear, and \(B_j(\xi,F)=\langle sF,P\xi\rangle\): Theorem 46.1.1(ii) holds with \(a_j=1\) and \(U_j=V=1\). For \(a=8\): \(B_0(\xi_0,e_1^8+e_2^8)=1/4\) and \(B_4(\xi_4,e_1^4e_2^4)=1/280\).

Problem 45.3.2 of Chapter 45 adds the chain: for \(F=\Theta_{(a)}\varphi\) with \(\varphi\) real, the period at the top equals \(B_j(\xi,F)\).

§46.2. From the kernel identity to the Bessel identity

In the formalization the row space is reached from below: there are a finite-dimensional inner product space \(V_0\) of forms and a linear map \(\iota\colon V_0\to\mathcal E_0\) with \(\iota(V_0)=\mathcal E_0\), and \(B_u(\varphi,\iota q)=\mathrm{per}_u(\varphi,q)\) for \(q\in(\ker\iota)^\perp\), where \(\mathrm{per}_u\) is the pairing at the end of the chain of \(u\) at the base twist. The row dictionary is an isometry \(j\) of \(\mathcal E_0\) into \(L^2\) of the automorphic quotient with \(s(F)=\overline{j(F)}\). The column dictionary is a unitary operator \(W_u\) of \(M_u\): the column of the definite side attached to \(\psi\in M_u\) is the element \(W_u\psi\) of \(M_u\subset C_u\). In Sections 46.3 and 46.4, \(P_u\psi\) for \(\psi\in M_u\) means \(P_uW_u\psi\).

Theorem 46.2.1 (kernel identity). There are a unitary operator \(V\) of \(\mathcal E_0\) and, for each word \(u\), a number \(a_u\ne0\) and a unitary operator \(U_u\) of \(M_u\) such that \[\mathrm{per}_u(\varphi,q)=a_u\,\bigl\langle\overline{j(V\iota q)},\ P_uW_uU_u\varphi\bigr\rangle\] for all \(\varphi\in M_u\) and \(q\in V_0\).

Status: proved in the formalization from Theorem 46.3.1 below by the argument of Section 46.3.

Problem 46.2.1. Let \(\iota\colon V_0\to\mathcal E_0\) be a linear map of finite-dimensional inner product spaces with \(\iota(V_0)=\mathcal E_0\). For every \(x\in\mathcal E_0\) there is \(q\in(\ker\iota)^\perp\) with \(\iota q=x\).

Problem 46.2.2. Theorem 46.2.1, the two dictionaries and \(B_u(\varphi,\iota q)=\mathrm{per}_u(\varphi,q)\) imply Theorem 46.1.1(ii), with \(U_u\) replaced by \(W_uU_u\).

§46.3. Bilinear forms on bases

The step behind Theorem 46.2.1 compares two bilinear forms on coordinate vectors. Fix an \(L\)-basis \(m_1,\dots,m_d\) of the \(L\)-structure of \(M_u\), and write \(\varphi\in L^d\) also for \(\sum_l\varphi_lm_l\in M_u\). The pairing \(\mathrm{per}_u\) is \(\mathbb C\)-bilinear on \(M_u\times V_0\), and \(V_0\) has an \(L\)-structure with a basis \(r_1,\dots,r_n\), which is a \(\mathbb C\)-basis of \(V_0\); for \(F\in L^n\) put \(\mathrm{row}(F):=\sum_kF_kr_k\in V_0\). For each twist \(t\) there are row coordinates \(F\in L^{n_t}\) (with \(n_{t_0}=n\)), a \(\mathbb C\)-linear map \(\Theta_t\) from \(\mathbb C^{n_t}\) to \(\mathcal E_0\), and an \(L\)-bilinear period \(\operatorname{Pt}_{u,t}\colon L^d\times L^{n_t}\to L\) of the definite side; the period map at the twist \(t\) on coordinates is \(B_{u,t}(\varphi,F)=\sigma\operatorname{Pt}_{u,t}(\varphi,F)\), and at the base twist \(\mathrm{per}_u(\varphi,\mathrm{row}(F))=\sigma\operatorname{Pt}_{u,t_0}(\varphi,F)\) (the chain tie). Finally \(\iota(\mathrm{row}(F))=\lambda^{-1}\Theta_{t_0}(F)\) for a number \(\lambda\ne0\) (the row tie).

Theorem 46.3.1 (multiplicity identity). There are a unitary operator \(V\) of \(\mathcal E_0\) and, for each word \(u\), numbers \(a_u\ne0\), a unitary operator \(U_u\) of \(M_u\), and numbers \(\zeta(u,t)\ne0\) with \(\zeta(u,t_0)=1\), such that \[\zeta(u,t)\,\sigma\bigl(\operatorname{Pt}_{u,t}(\varphi,F)\bigr)=a_u\,\bigl\langle sV\Theta_t(F),\ P_uU_u\varphi\bigr\rangle\] for all twists \(t\) and all coordinate vectors \(\varphi\in L^d\), \(F\in L^{n_t}\). Moreover, if \(\zeta(u,t)=\sigma(a)\) with \(a\in L\), then \(v_w(a)=0\) at every finite place \(w\) of \(L\) above no prime of the set \(S_t\) of Chapter 45.

Problem 46.3.1. Let \(\beta\colon L^d\times L^n\to L\) be \(L\)-bilinear and \(\Phi\colon\mathbb C^d\times\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C\) be \(\mathbb C\)-bilinear, with \(\Phi(e_i,e_k)=\sigma\beta(e_i,e_k)\) on the standard basis vectors. Then \(\Phi(\sigma x,\sigma y)=\sigma\beta(x,y)\) for all \(x\in L^d\), \(y\in L^n\), and \(\Phi\) is determined by \(\beta\).

Problem 46.3.2. Assume Theorem 46.3.1, the chain tie and the row tie. Then the two \(\mathbb C\)-bilinear forms \((\varphi,q)\mapsto\mathrm{per}_u(\varphi,q)\) and \((\varphi,q)\mapsto a_u\lambda\langle sV\iota q,P_uU_u\varphi\rangle\) on \(M_u\times V_0\) agree on \((m_l,r_k)\), hence everywhere; this is Theorem 46.2.1 with \(a_u\lambda\) in place of \(a_u\).

In the formalization the embedding in the chain tie may be the complex conjugate of \(\sigma\), and then the scalars are conjugated accordingly; we suppress this.

§46.4. The multiplicity identity

Each \(e\in\mathcal E_0\) has a theta lift \(\mathsf R_e\), an automorphic form on the indefinite group at the top, whose restriction to the base is a row form \(T_e\) in the sense of Chapter 45; every row form at the base is some \(T_e\), and \(\mathrm{row}_t(F)\) of Chapter 45 is \(T_{\Theta_t(F)}\). Recall that \(P_ux\) means \(P_uW_ux\). Theorem 46.3.1 comes from five inputs.

  • There are a vector \(v\) of the archimedean discrete series and an isometric bijection \(J\) from the space \(\{\mathsf R_e:e\in\mathcal E_0\}\) onto \(\mathcal H\), such that \(J(\mathsf R_e)\) is almost everywhere the complex conjugate of the evaluation of \(\mathsf R_e\) at \(v\). Status: proved in the formalization from a proposition stating a classification of discrete series of unitary groups [HC]; we have not traced how that proposition is supplied.

  • There are a unitary operator \(V\) of \(\mathcal E_0\), induced by translation by a central element, and \(\kappa\ne0\) such that \(\langle sVe,P_ux\rangle=\kappa\,b(e,x)\) for \(e\in\mathcal E_0\) and \(x\in M_u\), where \(b(e,x):=\langle J(\mathsf R_e),P_ux\rangle_\mathcal H\) is the integral of the evaluated theta lift of \(e\) against the Poincaré series of \(x\), without complex conjugation. Status: proved in the formalization; we have read only its statement.

  • Theorem 45.5.1 of Chapter 45, with its unitary operator \(U\) of \(M_u\), its constants \(c_t\) and, for each row form \(T\), its function \(p\).

  • There is a unitary operator \(R_u\) of \(M_u\) such that for all \(e,e'\in\mathcal E_0\), \(x,x'\in M_u\) and \(\alpha,\alpha'\in\mathbb C\): if \(\alpha\operatorname{Bper}(x,T_e)+\alpha'\operatorname{Bper}(x',T_{e'})=0\), then \(\alpha b(e,R_ux)+\alpha'b(e',R_ux')=0\). Status: proved in the formalization; by our reading it is the unfolding of Chapter 44, but we have not read its proof.

  • For every word \(u\) there is \(\varphi\in M_u\) with \(P_u\varphi\ne0\). Status: an assumption of the step of the formalization that proves Theorem 46.3.1; we have not traced where it is proved.

Problem 46.4.1. Assume the second inequality of Theorem 44.4.1 with \(e_0\le\frac12\), and \(\dim M_u>\frac12\dim C_u\). Then (NZ) holds.

Problem 46.4.2. (E) and (NZ) give, for every word \(u\), vectors \(e^\circ\in\mathcal E_0\) and \(x^\circ\in M_u\) with \(b(e^\circ,x^\circ)\ne0\).

Problem 46.4.3. Assume (J), (Bo) and the conclusion of Problem 46.4.2, and put \(b(t,\varphi,F):=b(\Theta_t(F),R_uU\varphi)\). There are numbers \(p_0\ne0\) and \(b_0\ne0\), depending on \(u\), such that \(c_t\,B_{u,t}(\varphi,F)\,b_0=p_0\,b(t,\varphi,F)\) for all \(t\), \(\varphi\), \(F\).

Problem 46.4.4. Assume Problem 46.4.3 and (Pa). Then the identity of Theorem 46.3.1 holds with \(\zeta(u,t)=c_t/c_{t_0}\), \(a_u=p_0/(\kappa b_0c_{t_0})\) and \(U_u:=R_uU\).

Problem 46.4.5. In Problem 46.4.4, \(\zeta(u,t_0)=1\) and \(\zeta(u,t)\ne0\); and if \(\zeta(u,t)=\sigma(a)\) with \(a\in L\), then \(v_w(a)=0\) at every finite place \(w\) of \(L\) above no prime of the set \(S_t\) of Chapter 45.

Problems 46.4.2–46.4.5 prove Theorem 46.3.1 from (E), (Pa), (J), (Bo) and (NZ); Problems 46.3.1 and 46.3.2 give Theorem 46.2.1; and Problems 46.2.1 and 46.2.2 give Theorem 46.1.1(ii). Part (i) is proved in the formalization from the same multiplicity identity and the chain tie, by an argument we have not followed.

§46.5. What the identity gives

Fix orthonormal bases \((b_k)\) of \(\mathcal E_0\), \((e_l)\) of \(M_u\) and \((f_i)\) of \(\mathcal H\). In the bases of the \(L\)-structures the matrix of \(B_u\) differs from the one below by invertible matrices, the \(Y_i\) and \(Y'\) of Theorem 43.2.1.

Problem 46.5.1. Let \(\mathsf S_{ki}:=\langle sVb_k,f_i\rangle\) and \(\Pi_{il}:=\langle f_i,P_uU_ue_l\rangle\). Then \(B_u(e_l,b_k)=a_u(\mathsf S\Pi)_{kl}\), and \(\mathsf S\) is a \(D_{\rm r}\times D\) matrix with orthonormal rows.

Complete \(\mathsf S\) to a unitary \(D\times D\) matrix \(\hat{\mathsf S}\), and put \(A_u:=\hat{\mathsf S}\Pi\), whose first \(D_{\rm r}\) rows are \(a_u^{-1}\) times the matrix of \(B_u\).

Problem 46.5.2. With \(\gamma_u:=c_0(u)^{-1}\) and \(K_{uu}\) as in Chapter 44, \(\gamma_uA_u^*A_u-1=U_u^*(\iota_u^*K_{uu}\iota_u-1)U_u\), where \(\iota_u\colon M_u\to C_u\) is the inclusion; and \(Q:=\sum_u\gamma_uA_uA_u^*\) is unitarily equivalent to \(\sum_u\gamma_uP_u\iota_u\iota_u^*P_u^*\). So the hypotheses of Theorem 43.2.2 for the matrices \(\hat{\mathsf S}P_u\) and \(V_u:=\iota_uU_u\) are those for \(P_u\) and \(\iota_uU_u\).

Problem 46.5.3. Assume the second inequality of Theorem 44.4.1. Then the rank of \(B_u\) is at least \(\dim M_u-e_0\dim C_u-(D-D_{\rm r})\).

With Theorem 44.4.1 Problem 46.5.2 gives conditions (v) and (vi) of Theorem 43.2.1, through Theorem 43.2.2, and Problem 46.1.2 gives (d) of Theorem 43.3.1.
