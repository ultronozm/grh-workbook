Prerequisites.

Chapter 52, Section 52.4: window pairs, Theorem 52.4.1, Theorem 52.4.2 and Problem 52.4.1; there the page exponent is written \(e\) and the twist \(i\). Chapter 30: the lengths \(h_{\ell,e}(F)\) and the sum \(V(E,t)\) of Section 30.3. Chapter 54: layers, Lemma 54.3.2, Theorems 54.3.1, 54.4.1 and 54.4.2, with the constant \(C_H\), and Problems 54.1.5 and 54.4.1. Chapter 55: the fields \(E(\psi,a,q,d)\), the two signs, and Theorem 55.2.3. Chapter 56: the bad primes \(\mathcal{B}_\psi\) and Theorem 56.4.1.

Throughout, \(\psi\) is a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor \(N_\psi\) and order \(o\), \(0<\varepsilon<1/52\), \(b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)\), and \(a(x)\) is a page exponent: an integer with \(x^{2+8\varepsilon}\le2^{a(x)}\le2x^{2+8\varepsilon}\) for \(x\ge1\). We write \(E_x=E(\psi,a(x),q,d)\), and \(N_x\) for its modulus \(\operatorname{lcm}(2^{a(x)+2},N_\psi)qd\).

§57.1. The statement

Chapter 52 states the bound that the argument needs as Theorem 52.4.1; in [Adr] it is a hypothesis. In the Lean formalization it is a theorem, and this chapter outlines its proof.

Theorem 57.1.1 (Selmer bound on windows). For \(\psi\), \(\varepsilon\) and \(a\) as above there are \(J\ge1\) and \(X_0\) such that for every \(X\ge X_0\) and every window pair \((q,d)\) at \(X^{1/J}\) there is \(Y\) with \(X^{1/J}\le Y\) and \(Y^2\le X\) such that, for every \(x\in[Y,Y^2]\) and \(t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor\), all groups \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E_x,e)\) with \(1\le e<t\) are finite and \(V(E_x,t)\le\sqrt b\).

The auxiliary primes \(q\), \(d\) are fixed through the window while the field \(E_x\) changes with \(x\). By Problem 55.1.1, \[V(E_x,t)=B_2\log2+B_-+B_+,\] with \[B_2=\sum_{1\le e<t}h_{2,e}(E_x),\qquad B_\pm=\sum_{\ell\ \mathrm{odd}}\log\ell\sum_{1\le e<t}h^\pm_{\ell,e}(E_x).\] Theorem 55.2.3 bounds \(B_-\) at every scale. Theorem 56.4.1 bounds \(B_+\), given a bound on the layers at the bad primes. The prime \(2\) and those layers are where the window is needed.

Problem 57.1.1. For \(x\ge1\), \(a(x)+2\le4(1+\log x)\).

Problem 57.1.2. If \((q,d)\) is a window pair at \(Y_0\), then \(\log(qd)\le Y_0^{\varepsilon/4}\) and, for each fixed \(J\ge1\), \(q,d>2Y_0^{401J}+1\) for all large \(Y_0\), uniformly in the pair.

§57.2. The prime two and the bad primes

At \(\ell=2\) the field \(E_x\) is compared with a layer over a base field that does not depend on \(x\). Let \(L_0=A_\psi(i,\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})\).

Problem 57.2.1. \(i\in L_0\), \([L_0:\mathbb{Q}]\le8o\), \(L_0\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{4N_\psi qd})\), and \(E_x\subseteq L_0(\zeta_{2^{a(x)+2}})\).

Lemma 57.2.1 (descent at two). For every \(a\ge2\) there is a layer \(L\) of degree \(2^m\) over \(L_0\), with \(m\le a+2\), such that \(h_{2,e}(E(\psi,a,q,d))\le1+h_{2,e}(L)\) for every \(e\ge1\).

Lemma 57.2.2 (uniform towers). There is \(R_2\), depending only on \(\psi\), such that for every pair of primes \(q\equiv3\pmod8\), \(d\equiv7\pmod{16}\) prime to \(N_\psi\) the hypothesis of Lemma 54.3.2 holds over \(L_0\) with \(\ell=2\) and \(R=R_2\). For each odd \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) there is \(R_\ell\) such that it holds over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell N_\psi})\) with \(R=R_\ell\).

Status: both lemmas are proved in the Lean formalization, the second from Theorem 54.3.1; we have read their statements only. We have not followed how the formalization makes \(R_2\) independent of \(q\) and \(d\); the congruences on \(q\) and \(d\) are among its hypotheses.

Problem 57.2.2. Let \(j_0=\lfloor\log_2R_2\rfloor+3\) and \(L'\) the layer of degree \(2^{j_0}\) over \(L_0\). Then \([L':\mathbb{Q}]\le8o\,2^{j_0}\), and \(\log(2\lvert\Delta_{L'}\rvert)\le c_1(1+\log(qd))\) with \(c_1\) depending only on \(\psi\).

§57.3. The pigeonhole on scales

By Theorem 54.4.2, for \(\ell=2\) over \(L_0\), and for each \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell N_\psi})\), the twists \(e\le Z\) split into at most \(R_\ell\) exceptional ones and the rest. At the exceptional twists only Theorem 54.4.1 is available, and by Problem 54.4.1 it is affordable only for \(e\le x^\eta\), with \(\eta=\varepsilon/(2C_H)\). The twists that matter at scale \(x\) are those with \(e\le x^{C_0}\). So one needs scales at which no exceptional twist lies in \((x^\eta,x^{C_0}]\).

Problem 57.3.1. Let \(R\ge0\), \(B\ge1\), \(U>0\), and \(T\) a set of at most \(R\) real numbers. There is \(y\) with \(U/(2^{R+2}B^R)\le y\le U/2\) such that for every \(\alpha\in T\) and \(u\in[y,2y]\), \(\alpha\le u\) implies \(B\alpha\le u\).

Problem 57.3.2. Let \(0<\eta\le C_0\), \(R\ge0\), \(X>1\), \(\mathcal{E}\) a set of at most \(R\) positive integers, and \(J=2^{R+2}(C_0/\eta)^R\). There is \(Y\) with \(X^{1/J}\le Y\) and \(Y^2\le X\) such that for every \(x\in[Y,Y^2]\) and \(e\in\mathcal{E}\), \(e\le x^{C_0}\) implies \(e\le x^\eta\).

Problem 57.3.3. In Problem 57.3.2 let \(L=\log X\) and \(u_i=(L/2)(\eta/(2C_0))^i\) for \(0\le i\le R\). The intervals \((\eta u_i,2C_0u_i]\) are disjoint, one of them contains no \(\log e\) with \(e\in\mathcal{E}\), and \(Y=e^{u_i}\) for that \(i\) satisfies the conclusion with \(J\) replaced by \(J/2\).

Problem 57.3.4. For \(R=1\), \(\mathcal{E}=\{1000\}\), \(C_0=2\), \(\eta=1/2\) and \(X=e^{20}\), the proof of Problem 57.3.1 by induction on \(R\) gives \(Y=e^{5/4}\); here \(J=32\), and \(x^2\le e^5<1000\) for every \(x\in[Y,Y^2]\).

Problem 57.3.5. One window serves all the primes at once: if \(\mathcal{E}_2\) and \(\mathcal{E}_\ell\), \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\), have at most \(R_2\) and \(R_\ell\) elements, Problem 57.3.2 applies to their union with \(R=R_2+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi}R_\ell\). With \(\eta=\varepsilon/(2C_H)\) the exponent is \(J=2^{R+2}(2C_HC_0/\varepsilon)^R\).

§57.4. The bounds on a window

Fix \(C_0\ge1\) and a function \(t\) with \(2\le t(x)\le x^{C_0}\) for \(x\ge2\). Let \(X\) be large, \(q\equiv3\pmod8\) and \(d\equiv7\pmod{16}\) primes prime to \(N_\psi\) with \(\log(qd)\le X^{\varepsilon/(4J)}\), \(Z=\lfloor X^{C_0}\rfloor\), and \(Y\) as in Problem 57.3.5 for the exceptional sets of Theorem 54.4.2 with this \(Z\). Let \(x\in[Y,Y^2]\).

Problem 57.4.1. Let \(e\le t(x)\) be non-exceptional for \(\ell=2\), and \(L\) a layer of degree \(2^m\) over \(L_0\) with \(m\le a(x)+2\). Then \(h_{2,e}(L)\le C(1+\log x)\), with \(C\) independent of \(x\), \(X\), \(q\) and \(d\).

Problem 57.4.2. Let \(e\le t(x)\) be exceptional for \(\ell=2\), and \(L\) as in Problem 57.4.1. Then \(h_{2,e}(L)\le C(1+\log x)+2\exp(Cx^{3\varepsilon/4})\).

Problem 57.4.3. For every \(C\) and all large \(x\), \(x^{C_0}\bigl(1+C(1+\log x)+2\exp(Cx^{3\varepsilon/4})\bigr)\le\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\).

The same bounds hold, more easily, at \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) over the fixed layer of degree \(\ell^{j_\ell}\), \(j_\ell=\lfloor\log_\ell R_\ell\rfloor+3\), over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell N_\psi})\), whose discriminant does not depend on \(q\) and \(d\). Problems 57.1.1, 57.2.2 and 57.3.1–57.4.3, Lemmas 57.2.1 and 57.2.2, and Theorem 54.4.2 prove the following.

Theorem 57.4.1 (windows at two and at the bad primes). Let \(C_0\ge1\), \(A_1>0\), \(R=R_2+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi}R_\ell\) and \(J=2^{R+2}(2C_HC_0/\varepsilon)^R\). There is \(X_0\) such that for all functions \(a\), \(t\) with \(2\le a(x)\), \(2^{a(x)}\le x^{A_1}\) and \(2\le t(x)\le x^{C_0}\) for \(x\ge2\), every \(X\ge X_0\) and all primes \(q\equiv3\pmod8\), \(d\equiv7\pmod{16}\) prime to \(N_\psi\) with \(\log(qd)\le X^{\varepsilon/(4J)}\), there is \(Y\) with \(X^{1/J}\le Y\) and \(Y^2\le X\) such that for every \(x\in[Y,Y^2]\) \[\sum_{1\le e<t(x)}h_{2,e}(E_x)\le\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5}),\] and the same bound holds for \(\sum_{1\le e<t(x)}h_{\ell,e}\) of the layer of degree \(\ell^{j_\ell}\) over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell N_\psi})\), for every \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\).

Status: proved in the Lean formalization, as just described. Problem 57.1.1 uses the page exponent; for general \(a\) the bound \(m\le a+2\le C(1+\log x)\) follows from \(2^{a(x)}\le x^{A_1}\) in the same way. Zhang states the result as [Zh, Prop. 5.3], with the window lemma [Zh, Lemma 5.5].

§57.5. The assembly

Let \(t=\lfloor x^{401}\rfloor\), and let \(x\) lie in the window of Theorem 57.4.1 with \(C_0=401\) and \(A_1=4\); these are admissible since \(2x^{2+8\varepsilon}\le x^4\) for \(x\ge2\). By Problem 52.4.1, \(\log(N_xt)\le2x^{\varepsilon/4}\) for large \(x\).

Problem 57.5.1. The right side of Theorem 55.2.3 for \(E_x\) and \(t\) is at most \(\exp(Cx^{\varepsilon/4})\), at every large scale \(x\).

Problem 57.5.2. Theorem 56.4.1 applies on the window, with \(B=\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\), and gives \(B_+\le\exp(2Cx^{\varepsilon/4})+C\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\).

Problem 57.5.3. On the window, all \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E_x,e)\), \(1\le e<t\), are finite and \(V(E_x,t)\le\sqrt b\) for all large \(x\).

Problems 57.1.2, 57.5.1–57.5.3 and Theorem 57.4.1 prove Theorem 57.1.1. Status: this is how the Lean formalization proves Theorem 57.1.1; the inputs it uses are those listed in Chapters 54, 55 and 56, with their statuses there. In [Adr] the statement is assumed as a hypothesis, and Chapter 52 records it as such.

Why windows.

The minus part and the plus part away from \(\mathcal{B}_\psi\) are bounded at every scale. At \(\ell=2\) and at the finitely many bad primes, the vanishing of \(\mu\) makes the lengths along a cyclotomic tower linear in the layer, except at boundedly many twists \(\ell\)-adically close to fixed centres (Theorem 54.4.2). At those twists only the doubly exponential bound for \(H^2\) is known, which is affordable only for \(e\le x^{\varepsilon/(2C_H)}\) (Problems 54.4.1 and 54.4.2). The exceptional twists do not move with \(x\), so the scale is moved instead: a pigeonhole on logarithmic scales finds \([Y,Y^2]\) free of them, at the price of the exponent \(J\), which grows like \((C/\varepsilon)^R\). Chapter 52 then passes from \([Y,Y^2]\) to ranges \([Y,Y^{3/2}]\) (Theorem 52.4.3); in the formalization a similar passage, to families of scales with parameter \(3/2\), is made by a step we have not followed.
