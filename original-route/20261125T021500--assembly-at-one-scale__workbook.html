Prerequisites.

From Chapter 1: the functions \(G\) and \(G_\chi\) of (1) and (3), Theorem 1.2.3, and Sections 1.4 and 1.5. From Chapter 2: Problem 2.1.7 and Estimate B (Section 2.2). From Chapter 4: the notation \(q, d, n, H, \Phi\), \(P_{t,w}(a)\), \(M_w(a)\), \(c_{\chi_d}\), \(\delta = \lvert T_2\rvert/n\), \(R_{q,d}\), Theorems 4.2.1 and 4.4.1, and Section 4.5. From Chapter 5: the bound (1), Theorem 5.2.1 and Problem 5.3.1. From Chapter 8: Theorem 8.4.2 and Section 8.5. Mertens’ estimate \(\sum_{n \le x}\Lambda(n)/n = \log x + O(1)\). From Chapters 36, 37, 43, 47 and 52: the deliverable stated at the end of each, which Sections 58.2 and 58.5 match with the inputs, and the problems cited below.

Chapter 8 balanced an error \(x/m + m\) for a model chain whose inputs were given. What are the inputs in Alpöge’s argument [Alp], what error must each achieve, and which of them pays for each term of the final bound? We state what the Lean formalization [Lean] proves. The expositions of Adriazola [Adr], Zhang [Zh and 6] and Rubinstein [Rub] are cited as pointers; they agree with it except where noted. Chapter 59 records the formalization’s accounting in full. Throughout, \(0 < \varepsilon < \frac1{52}\), the range in which [Adr, Thms. 5.10 and 5.12] construct the auxiliary primes of Section 58.5; no problem uses this bound. Let \[y := x^\varepsilon , \qquad b := e^{y} , \qquad a := xb .\] We treat \(\zeta\), so that the averaging of Chapter 4 is over all of \(H\); for \(L(s,\psi)\) see Section 4.5 there and [Adr].

§58.1. The accounting

The data are: \(m\) lifts; a set \(T \subset H\) of \(N_T\) tops with weights \(a_t > 0\), and \(A := \sum_t a_t\); nodes \(w_j \colon \Phi \to (0,\infty)\) for \(1 \le j \le m\); a finite set \(\mathcal{W}\) of words, and for \(u \in \mathcal{W}\) a source lattice \(N_u\) of rank \(d_u\), a base lattice \(M_u\), an auxiliary prime \(T_u\), and a bound \(h_u\) for the height of the period map \(B_u \colon N_u \to R^\vee\), where \(R^\vee\) is the target. These objects are defined in Chapter 35. All lattices are hermitian lattices over the ring of integers of a coefficient field \(L \supset E\) (over \(\mathcal{O}_E\) in [Adr, Def. 18.9]), with the normalized slopes of Chapter 6. Let \(S := \sum_{t,j} P_{t,w_j}(a_t)\), let \(\Gamma\) be the total gain of the lifts at the inert primes, net of the colength of the Hecke image, \(\mathcal{L}\) their common local terms and \(E_u\) their local terms at the level and twisted places. Let \(\varphi(t) := t\log t - t\), \(v := \frac12 \operatorname{rd}_E\), \(\log\Omega := \gamma + \log(\pi/v) - c_{\chi_d}\) (for \(E = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), with \(\chi_{-4}\) in place of \(\chi_d\), \(v = 1\) and \(\Omega\) is the \(\Omega\) of Problem 12.3.7), let \(g := \log(g_0g_1)\) for two level primes \(g_0, g_1\), and write \(\langle X \rangle := \sum_u d_uX_u / \sum_u d_u\). The inputs are seven inequalities, for every \(u \in \mathcal{W}\), with numbers \(e_1, \dots, e_7 \ge 0\): \[\begin{align} S &\le \Gamma + e_1 , \tag{A1}\label{A1}\\ \mu(M_u) + \Gamma + \mathcal{L}- E_u - e_2 &\le \mu(N_u) , \tag{A2}\label{A2}\\ \langle \mu(N) - h \rangle - e_3 &\le \mu(R^\vee) , \tag{A3}\label{A3}\\ \mu(R^\vee) + h_u - \mathcal{L}&\le \textstyle\sum_{t} \bigl( m\varphi(a_t) - m a_t \log\Omega - a_t\log T_u \bigr) + e_4 , \tag{A4}\label{A4}\\ E_u &\le mAg + e_5 , \tag{A5}\label{A5}\\ \log T_u &\ge \textstyle\sum_{j} \frac{1}{f}\sum_{\rho\in\Phi} \log\bigl(w_j(\rho)v/\pi\bigr) + mg - e_6 , \tag{A6}\label{A6}\\ \mu(M_u) &\ge -e_7 , \tag{A7}\label{A7} \end{align}\] where \(f := \lvert\Phi\rvert = 2n\). In the formalization \(\Gamma\) is a weighted sum, over the inert places, of the gains \(\sum_j \mathrm{gain}_j(p)\) less the exponent \(\tau(p)\) of the Hecke image (Theorem 42.4.1 and Chapter 41). So \(\Gamma\) already deducts a benchmark colength \(\tau(p)\), with the gain set to zero at the primes that are dropped. The actual colength is bounded in terms of \(\tau(p) + \mathrm{io}(p)\) (Theorem 41.4.1(b)); its excess over the benchmark is the number \(I\) of Chapter 59, which the formalization bounds as it bounds \(e_1\), and which enters \(e_2\).

Problem 58.1.1. \(\Gamma \le \mu(N_u) - \mathcal{L}+ E_u + e_2 + e_7\) for every \(u\).

Problem 58.1.2. \(\Gamma \le \mu(R^\vee) - \mathcal{L}+ \langle h + E \rangle + e_2 + e_3 + e_7\).

Problem 58.1.3. Let \(\Sigma_u\) be the right side of (A4). Then \(\Gamma \le \langle \Sigma + E \rangle + e_2 + e_3 + e_7\): the heights cancel.

Problem 58.1.4. \[\Sigma_u + E_u \le \sum_{j}\sum_t a_t\bigl(\log a_t - 1 - \gamma + c_{\chi_d} - \langle\log w_j\rangle_\Phi\bigr) + e_4 + e_5 + Ae_6 ,\] where \(\langle\cdot\rangle_\Phi\) is the mean over \(\Phi\): the level terms \(mAg\) cancel, and so do \(\log v\) and \(\log\pi\).

The sum in Problem 58.1.4 is \(\sum_{t,j} M_{w_j}(a_t)\). Problems 58.1.1–58.1.4 and (A1) prove the following [Adr, Prop. 3.1]; with Theorem 4.2.1 it bounds a sum of the functions \(G_\theta\) of Chapter 4.

Theorem 58.1.1 (accounting). Under (A1)–(A7), \[\sum_{t \in T}\sum_{j=1}^m \bigl( P_{t,w_j}(a_t) - M_{w_j}(a_t) \bigr) \le e := e_1 + e_2 + e_3 + e_4 + e_5 + Ae_6 + e_7 .\]

Three cancellations occur, as in Stepanov’s count (Chapter 8): the heights between (A3) and (A4), the level terms between (A5) and (A6), and the linear terms. Euler’s constant and \(c_{\chi_d}\) enter \(M_w\) through the prime sum, and \(\log\Omega\) through the period, by the Chowla–Selberg formula (Theorem 20.3.1); Problem 58.1.4 is where they meet.

§58.2. The inputs and their errors

What must each \(e_i\) be? Put \(N_2 := \lvert T_2\rvert = \delta n\) and \[B_0 := 2nmby , \qquad N_c := \frac{N_2\,k\,b\,y^2}{n^2} ,\] where \(k\) is the dimension of the first hermitian space of the chain [Zh]. In the following, \(C \ge 1\) and \(C_H \ge 0\) depend on \(\varepsilon\) but not on \(x\). The data satisfy \(N_T \ge 1\), \(1 \le m \le k \le b\), \(mb \le a\) and \(a \le a_t \le a + b\) for \(t \in T\) [Adr, Def. 18.5, Lemma 18.15] (the bound \(k \le b\) holds once \(x\) is large, since \(k\) is at most a power of \(x\) and \(b = e^y\)), and \(1 \le \lvert\mathcal{W}\rvert \le 2^{k-3}\) [Adr, Def. 18.9(a)]; so \(a_t \le 2a\) and \(\lvert a_t/b - x\rvert \le 1\). Let \[U := 8Cy\bigl(N_Ta(1 + m^2/k) + mB_0\bigr) ;\] Problem 58.2.2 shows that it bounds the spread defined below. The errors are those of [Adr, Def. 18.9 and Prop. 18.14], and the earlier chapters of this part deliver them:

  • (A1) and (A2), with \(e_1 \le CN_Tmb\) and \(e_2 \le Cy\bigl(N_Tmb + N_Ta\,m^2/k + a\bigr)\): Theorems 37.5.1 and 37.5.2, with Problems 37.5.4 and 37.5.5;

  • (A3), with \(e_3 = \log\lvert\mathcal{W}\rvert + 8\log(1 + x^{C_H})(U + b) + 12b\): Theorem 43.5.1 and Problem 43.5.3;

  • (A4), with \(e_4 \le Cy^2\bigl(N_Ta + m(B_0 + N_c)\bigr)\): Theorem 47.2.2, where the term \(mN_c\) is the cost of the congruence defects, Theorem 52.4.5;

  • (A5), with \(e_5 \le CmB_0\): Theorem 52.1.1 and Problem 52.1.3;

  • (A6), with \(e_6 \le Cy(1 + m^2/k)\), and (A7), with \(e_7 \le CyN_Ta(1 + m/k)\): Theorems 47.3.1 and 47.4.1, with Problem 47.4.2.

Section 58.7 records the status of each.

The formalization assumes more than (A1)–(A7). For all \(u, u' \in \mathcal{W}\) it also has \[\begin{align} \mu_{\max}(N_u) &\le \mu_{\max}(M_u) + \Gamma + \mathcal{L}- E_u + e_2^+ , \tag{A2\(^+\)}\label{A2p}\\ E_u &\ge mAg - e_5 , \tag{A5\(^-\)}\label{A5m}\\ \mu_{\max}(M_u) &\le e_7^+ , \tag{A7\(^+\)}\label{A7p}\\ \bigl\lvert (h_u + A\log T_u) - (h_{u'} + A\log T_{u'}) \bigr\rvert &\le e_H , \tag{H}\label{H}\\ \lvert \log T_u - \log T_{u'} \rvert &\le e_T , \tag{A6\('\)}\label{A6v} \end{align}\] with \(e_2^+ \le CmB_0\), \(e_7^+ \le CyN_Ta\), \(e_H \le Cy(N_Ta + mB_0)\) and \(e_T \le C\log k\), and an upper bound for \(\log T_u\) (Chapter 59). These are further clauses of the statements that give (A2), (A4), (A5), (A6) and (A7); (A5\(^-\)) is the other half of Theorem 52.1.1. With them the spread \[u_F := \max_u\bigl(\mu_{\max}(N_u) - h_u\bigr) - \langle\mu(N) - h\rangle ,\] which enters (A3) through \(e_3\), is bounded by a proved lemma; [Adr, Lemma 18.13] states the bound.

Problem 58.2.1. Assume (A2), (A5), (A7) and (A2\(^+\))–(A6\('\)), and \(\mu(N_u) \le \mu_{\max}(N_u)\) for every \(u\). Then \(0 \le u_F \le e_2 + e_2^+ + 2e_5 + e_7 + e_7^+ + e_H + Ae_T\).

Problem 58.2.2. With the errors of this section and \(y \ge 1\), \(u_F \le U\).

Problem 58.2.3. If \(\mathcal{W}\) is nonempty, \(\lvert\mathcal{W}\rvert \le 2^{k-3}\) and \(k \le b\), then \(e_3 \le 8(1 + C_H)\,y\,(U + b) + 13b\) for large \(x\).

Problem 58.2.4. Let \(\mathcal{B}:= N_Ta(1 + m^2/k) + m(B_0 + N_c)\), and suppose that the data satisfy the conditions stated at the start of this section and that \(y \ge 1\). Then \(e \le C_1y^2\mathcal{B}\) for large \(x\), with \(C_1\) depending on \(C\) and \(C_H\) only.

Theorem 58.2.1. Under the conditions on the data and the inputs (A1)–(A7) and (A2\(^+\))–(A6\('\)) with the errors of this section, for large \(x\), \(\sum_{t,j}\bigl(P_{t,w_j}(a_t) - M_{w_j}(a_t)\bigr) \le x^{(e_D + e_L + 3)\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{B}\), where \(e_D := 2\) and \(e_L := 0\).

This is the bound the formalization proves at each translate. The numbers \(e_D\) and \(e_L\) are the powers of \(y\) in the errors of (A4), on the term \(m(B_0 + N_c)\), and of (A1); the formalization fixes them before \(\varepsilon\) (Section 58.5). Problem 58.2.4 gives more, \(x^{3\varepsilon}\mathcal{B}\) for large \(x\); we keep the formalization’s exponent, so that the exponents below are the ones it proves. The bound is [Zh], [Adr, Thm. A], and [Rub] before normalization. Reading Problem 58.2.4 backwards shows which input pays for which term of \(\mathcal{B}\). The term \(N_Ta\) collects costs incurred once for each top, not once for each lift: the base lattice (\(e_7\)), the precision \(\log(a\operatorname{rd}_E) \le 2y\) of the linear term of the norms (\(e_4\)), and the auxiliary prime (\(e_6\)). The term \(N_Ta\,m^2/k\) is the colength and size of the auxiliary prime (\(e_2\), \(e_6\)). The term \(mB_0\) collects the local losses of the \(m\) lifts (\(e_5\), and \(e_4\)); each is of size \(B_0\), which grows with \(m\) because the nodes of the \(j\)th lift grow with \(j\), as the cost of the \(k\)th identity did in Chapter 8. The term \(mN_c\) comes only from the congruence defects, through \(e_4\). The spread \(U\) and hence \(e_3\) contain all but the last.

§58.3. Normalization

The averaging of Chapter 4 is over translates. We write \(\tau \in H\) for the translate index, called \(u\) there, since \(u\) indexes words here; so the nodes of the \(j\)th lift at the translate \(\tau\) are \(w_{\tau,j} \colon \Phi \to (0,\infty)\). Before averaging we need, for one \(x\):

  1. for every \(\tau \in H\), data as in Section 58.2 with the nodes \(w_{\tau,1}, \dots, w_{\tau,m}\), and with the same \(m, k, n, T, T_2\) and \(a_t\) for all \(\tau\), satisfying the conditions stated there, (A1)–(A7) and (A2\(^+\))–(A6\('\)) with the errors stated there, with the same \(C\), \(C_H\) and threshold for “large \(x\)”; the words \(\mathcal{W}\) and the lattices may depend on \(\tau\);

  2. for every \(t \in T\) and every \(j\), all hypotheses of Theorem 4.4.1 for \(a = a_t\) and the family \((w_{\tau,j})_{\tau \in H}\): \(\lvert a_t/b - x\rvert \le 1\), the thick and thin conditions (2) with numbers \(\Delta\) and \(X\), and the thin-scale bound \(\lvert G_\theta(y)\rvert \le \vartheta y\) for all \(\theta\) and \(y \ge X\);

  3. \(2f(x+1)\vartheta \le 1/x\) and \(6f(x+1)(\Delta + 1) \le b/x\).

(V3) is the form of the cost conditions in the formalization. We use Chebyshev’s bound \(\sum_{r \le y}\Lambda(r) \le 3y\) for \(y \ge 0\) [RS].

Problem 58.3.1. Under (V3), the last two terms of Theorem 4.4.1 add up to at most \(4/(\delta x)\).

Let \(Q := N_Tmb\): \(N_T\) tops, \(m\) lifts, weight \(b\).

Problem 58.3.2. Under (V1)–(V3), for large \(x\), \(Q\,\frac{\delta}{2}\bigl(R_{q,d}(x) - \mathcal{K}(x)\bigr) \le x^{(e_D+e_L+3)\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{B}+ 2Q/x\).

The factor \(Q\delta/2\) counts the gain: \(N_T\) tops, \(m\) lifts, weight \(b\), at a proportion \(\delta/2\) of the places. What remains is to divide \(\mathcal{B}\) by it. The formalization does not form the quotient. It bounds the four terms of \(\mathcal{B}\) one at a time by \(Q\) times a power of \(x\), and uses only the inequalities \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:S}\tag{S} \begin{gathered} a \le 2xb, \quad N_T \ge \tfrac12 x^{-\varepsilon/8}n, \quad x^{-\varepsilon} \le \delta, \quad N_2 \le n, \\ x^{1/2-\varepsilon} \le m \le x^{1/2+\varepsilon/2}, \quad xm \le k \le n^2x^{1/2-\varepsilon} . \end{gathered} \end{equation}\] In Problems 58.3.3–58.3.5 assume (S), \(x \ge 1\), \(\varepsilon > 0\), and let \(Y := x^{1/2+13\varepsilon/8}\).

Problem 58.3.3. \(N_Ta \le 2YQ\) and \(N_Ta\,m^2/k \le 2Q\).

Problem 58.3.4. \(mB_0 \le 4YQ\).

Problem 58.3.5. \(mN_c \le 2YQ\). Hence \(\mathcal{B}\le 10YQ\).

Theorem 58.3.1 (normalization). Let \(x \ge 3\), \(\varepsilon > 0\), \(C \ge 0\) and \(x^{3\varepsilon/8} \ge 26\). Let \(n, N_T, N_2, m\) be positive integers and \(b > 0\), and assume (S). If a real number \(G\) satisfies \(Q\,\frac{\delta}{2}\bigl(G - 4x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\bigr) \le x^{C\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{B}+ 2Q/x\), then \(G \le x^{1/2+(C+3)\varepsilon}\).

Problem 58.3.6. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 58.3.1, \(\delta(G - 4x^{1/2+\varepsilon}) \le 22x^{C\varepsilon}Y\), and \(G \le 26x^{1/2+(C+21/8)\varepsilon}\). This proves the theorem.

Theorem 58.3.1 is [Zh, Lemma 6.1], with the same constants, as the formalization proves it. Why the inequalities (S)? Dividing \(\mathcal{B}\) by the gain shows which input pays for which term. This is how [Adr] and [Rub, and §I.6, Thm. A] state the normalization; the arithmetic is correct, but it is not how the formalization argues.

Problem 58.3.7. \(\displaystyle \frac{\mathcal{B}}{N_Tmb\delta/2} = \frac{2x}{\delta}\Bigl(\frac1m + \frac mk\Bigr) + \frac{4nmy}{N_T\delta} + \frac{2ky^2}{nN_T}\).

Problem 58.3.8. If \(\delta \ge x^{-\varepsilon}\) and \(N_T \ge \frac12 x^{-\varepsilon/8}n\), the quotient of Problem 58.3.7 is at most \(8x^{17\varepsilon/8}\bigl(x/m + xm/k + m + k/n^2\bigr)\).

So \(x/m\) is paid at the ends, \(xm/k\) at the auxiliary primes, \(m\) by the local losses of the steps, and \(k/n^2\) by the congruence defects: the four bounds of Problems 58.3.3–58.3.5, in order. Adriazola writes the fourth term as \(kx^{c\varepsilon}/(nN_T)\) [Adr], the same up to \(n/N_T \le 2x^{\varepsilon/8}\). Rubinstein [Rub] describes \(x/m\) and \(m\) in the same way, and calls \(xm/k\) and \(k/n^2\) the costs of the auxiliary parameters \(k\) and \(n\); for the last term we follow [Adr]. The hypothesis \(\delta \ge x^{-\varepsilon}\) is (1), which Behrend’s set of columns satisfies (Theorem 5.2.1, and Problem 36.4.1 at the scale of the argument). The hypothesis \(N_T \ge \frac12 x^{-\varepsilon/8}n\) holds with high probability for the random tops of density \(\delta_1 = x^{-\varepsilon/8}\) (Problem 5.3.1, and Theorem 36.4.2(iv)).

Why must the constants in (A4) be exact, when a long chain absorbs so much else? Change one input and follow it.

Problem 58.3.9. Let every \(a_t = xb\), and keep all inputs as they are except that the right side of (A4) carries an additional \(\eta mA\), \(\eta > 0\), as it would if \(\log\Omega\) were known only to within \(\eta\). (i) The bound of Theorem 58.1.1 increases by \(\eta mA\), and the bound for \(R_{q,d}(x) - \mathcal{K}(x)\) given by Problem 58.3.2 by \(2\eta x/\delta\), whatever \(m\) is; this is at most \(C\sqrt x\) exactly when \(\eta \le \frac12 C\delta x^{-1/2}\). (ii) If instead the additional term is \(\eta A\), a cost paid once for each top rather than once for each lift, that bound increases by \(2\eta x/(m\delta)\).

So a long chain divides a cost paid at its ends by \(m\), but an error in the linear term is paid by every lift, and dividing by the \(m\) lifts that pay it gains nothing. Since \(x^{-\varepsilon} \le \delta \le 1\), an error \(\eta\) in \(\log\Omega\) is harmless if \(\eta \le \frac12 Cx^{-1/2-\varepsilon}\), and only if \(\eta \le \frac12 Cx^{-1/2}\). This is why \(\log\Omega\) must cancel exactly against \(\gamma\) and \(c_{\chi_d}\) in Problem 58.1.4, and why the Chowla–Selberg formula, not an estimate of the period, is used.

§58.4. The exponents

The normalization needs of \(m\), \(k\) and \(n\) only the inequalities (S). The formalization chooses them when it builds the family of data, as [Adr, Prop. 5.15] does. Why \(m \approx x^{1/2}\), \(k \approx x^{9/2}\) and \(n \approx x^2\)? The four terms of Problem 58.3.8 explain it. Put \(\delta_1 := x^{-\varepsilon/8}\).

Problem 58.4.1. If \(\sqrt{x\delta_1} \le m \le 2\sqrt{x\delta_1}\), then \(x/m \le x^{1/2 + \varepsilon/16}\) and \(m \le 2x^{1/2 - \varepsilon/16}\). If instead \(m = x^{1/2 + \kappa}\), then \(x/m + m \ge x^{1/2 + \lvert\kappa\rvert}\).

Thus \(m\) balances \(x/m\) against \(m\). The rank hypothesis behind (A3) makes \(k\) at least about \(m^5n\) (Problem 43.4.5), and \(k/n^2 \le x^{1/2}\) then makes \(n\) about \(x^2\); Chapter 59 works this out, and checks that the choices of [Adr, Prop. 5.15], which the formalization makes, satisfy (S). The terms \(xm/k\) and \(k/n^2\) never compete with the balance. It remains to bound the constant \(\mathcal{K}(x)\) of Problem 58.3.2, which contains \(\lvert c_\chi\rvert\) for \(\chi = \chi_q, \chi_d, \chi_q\chi_d\), uniformly in \(q\) and \(d\).

Problem 58.4.2. Let \(\chi\) be a primitive nonprincipal character with \(\lvert G_\chi(x)\rvert \le x\). Then \(\lvert c_\chi\rvert \le 1 + \sum_{n \le x}\Lambda(n)/n\). Hence, if Estimate B ((W1) below) holds at \(x\), then \(\mathcal{K}(x) \le C''\log x\) for \(x \ge 3\), with \(C''\) absolute.

In the formalization the bound \(\lvert L'/L(1,\chi)\rvert \le C_L\log x\) is one of the conditions on the data at \(x\), not derived from Estimate B as here. By Problems 2.1.7, 58.3.2 and 58.4.2, and Theorem 58.3.1 with \(C = e_D + e_L + 3\):

Theorem 58.4.1 (Estimate A). Assume (V1)–(V3), (S), and (W1) of Section 58.5. For large \(x\), \(G(x) \le x^{1/2 + (e_D + e_L + 6)\varepsilon}\).

The formalization proves the bound \(x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon} = x^{1/2+9\varepsilon}\) for the positive combination of \(G\) and the \(G_{\psi'}\) attached to a character \(\psi\) of order \(o\) (Section 58.5), one \(\varepsilon\) more. After averaging over the subgroup \(A_0\), the characters not trivial on \(A_0\) contribute zero, by orthogonality, and \(4o\) characters remain. Those that induce the level give the positive combination; Estimate B bounds the others by \(4o\,x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) in total, and this is what the last \(\varepsilon\) pays for (Chapter 59).

If the local loss of the \(j\)th lift grew like \(j^2\) instead of \(j\), the term \(m\) would become \(m^2\), and the balance \(x/m + m^2\) would give only the exponent \(\frac23\) (Problem 8.5.4; [Alp, step 14]).

§58.5. The window, and the zeros

Theorem 1.2.3 needs \(G(x) \le x^\theta\) at every point of \([Y, Y^{3/2}]\), for arbitrarily large \(Y\). What must hold uniformly as \(x\) runs through such a window? The form of [Adr] is this. For every small \(\varepsilon\) there is \(J = J(\varepsilon) \ge 2\) such that for arbitrarily large \(Y\) there are \(Y_0 \le Y\) with \(Y^2 \le Y_0^J\), and primes \(q \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), \(d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}\), \(q \mid d + 1\), chosen at the scale \(Y_0\) with \(\log q \le Y_0^{\varepsilon/5}\) and \(\log d \le Y_0^{\varepsilon/5}\log^2Y_0\), such that at every \(x \in [Y, Y^{3/2}]\):

  1. Estimate B: \(\lvert G_\chi(x)\rvert \le x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) for \(\chi = \chi_q, \chi_d, \chi_q\chi_d\);

  2. the thin-scale bound \(\lvert G_\theta(y')\rvert \le y'\exp(-c_6x^{\varepsilon/8})\), with \(c_6 > 0\) independent of \(x\), for the \(4n\) characters \(\theta\) of Chapter 4 and all \(y' \ge \frac13\exp(x^{\varepsilon/3})\);

  3. \(\sum_{1 \le i < \lfloor x^{400}\rfloor} \sum_\ell h_{\ell,i}(E)\log\ell \le \exp(\frac12x^\varepsilon)\) for the field \(E\) of Chapter 4 at \(x\), where \(h_{\ell,i}(E)\) is the length of \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E,i)\) as in Chapter 30 (there the twist is called \(e\); here \(e\) is the error of Theorem 58.1.1);

Problem 58.5.1. If \(Y_0 \le Y\) and \(Y^2 \le Y_0^J\), then \([Y, Y^{3/2}] \subset [Y_0, Y_0^J]\). Problem 58.5.2. Under (W1)–(W4), \(G(x) \le x^{1/2+(e_D+e_L+6)\varepsilon}\) for every \(x\) in the window, once \(Y\) is large.

Problem 58.5.3. Let \(0 < c(\varepsilon) < \frac12\) for small \(\varepsilon > 0\), and suppose that for every small \(\varepsilon\) there are arbitrarily large \(Y\) with \(G(x) \le x^{1/2+c(\varepsilon)}\) for all \(x \in [Y, Y^{3/2}]\). Then \(\zeta(s) \ne 0\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s > \frac12 + \liminf_{\varepsilon\to0} c(\varepsilon)\).

By Problem 58.5.2, \(c(\varepsilon) = (e_D + e_L + 6)\varepsilon\) for large \(Y\); the formalization’s statement has \(c(\varepsilon) = (e_D + e_L + 7)\varepsilon = 9\varepsilon\). Problem 58.5.3 then gives the Riemann hypothesis. This is why \(e_D\) and \(e_L\) must not depend on \(\varepsilon\), while \(J\), \(C\), \(C_H\) and the threshold for \(x\) may. The formalization chooses in this order: first \(e_D = 2\), \(e_L = 0\) and a bound \(\varepsilon_1\); then, for each \(\varepsilon < \varepsilon_1\), a single family of data; then the constants of the record and a threshold \(x_0\). In the proof a design threshold is fixed before the family, which is chosen large relative to it; the scalar constants of the record are found after the family. At every \(x \ge x_0\) in the set of scales of that family it proves (W4). That set contains \([Y, Y^{3/2}]\) for arbitrarily large \(Y\). Given \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s > \frac12\), it takes \(\varepsilon\) with \((e_D + e_L + 7)\varepsilon \le \frac12\min(\operatorname{Re}s - \frac12, \frac12)\).

Theorem 58.5.1. Assume (W1)–(W4). Then \(\zeta(s) \ne 0\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s > \frac12\).

Problem 58.6.1. Suppose that for every small \(\varepsilon > 0\) there are arbitrarily large \(Y\) such that, at every \(x \in [Y, Y^{3/2}]\), \(R^\psi(x) \le x^{1/2+c\varepsilon}\), with \(c\) independent of \(\varepsilon\), and the six characters of Problem 36.5.3 satisfy Estimate B. §58.7. The status of the inputs

The Lean formalization [Lean] proves the final theorem. Its intermediate statements are organized differently from those of [Adr], and we have not checked that they are the hypotheses of [Adr] named below; where a difference is known, from a comparison of the statements, the ledger records it. Each input is classical, with a reference; proved in [Adr]; or a hypothesis of [Adr]: the formalization proves the final theorem, and the corresponding step in [Adr] is the hypothesis named, assumed there, with no published human-readable proof. Where a chapter of this part develops the proof that the formalization gives for an input, the ledger names it and the gaps it records; these accounts follow the formalization, and [Adr] is cited for comparison. The accounting, the normalization, the exponents and the window are proved in this chapter, on the models of Chapters 6, 8, 9 and 15. Of these, the normalization (Theorem 58.3.1), the per-translate bound (Theorem 58.2.1) and the order of choices are as the formalization proves them. The accounting from (A1)–(A7), with the seven errors of [Adr], is expository: the formalization assumes the eleven inequalities of its record (Chapter 59). The bound \(8\varepsilon\) for \(\zeta\) is our deduction; the formalization’s statement is \(9\varepsilon\) for the positive combination. For each input we name the deliverable that supplies it.

(A1), (A2)

Theorems 37.5.1 and 37.5.2, for the lifts of Theorem 37.3.3. The formalization proves the final theorem by a different arrangement at this step (Chapter 42). Its counterpart of (A1) is an inequality between explicit numbers at each inert prime, Theorem 42.4.1, proved with the chain of lifts replaced by the zero chain. The divisibility of the lifts on the Hecke image is Theorem 42.2.1, proved on a separate branch from an input on the lifts, and the two meet only in the source lattices \(N_u\), whose slopes give (A2). On the divisibility branch the formalization proves conditions (a)–(c) of Theorem 37.3.3: the normalized kernel law, for kernels built from one theta function of the CM curve normalized to take the value \(1\) at the origin (Chapter 38), and the unit scalars and the dual frames at the odd positions (Chapter 39). The Hecke image is controlled by a nondegeneracy condition at one edge (Chapter 40) and its colength bound (Chapter 41). These chapters give a human-readable account, with these gaps: the proofs of the nondegeneracy theorems for small primes and for primes of class I are large and not developed (Chapter 40); we have not followed the covering lemma behind the colength bound (Chapter 41), the proof of the input on the lifts, part (ii) of Theorem 42.4.1, or how the size conditions give the bound for the rejected primes (Chapter 42); nor the proof that the values at the origin entering the one-copy step are \(p\)-integral, the computation with coset averages for lattices standard at \(p\), or the passage to the torus-averaged exponent (Chapter 38); and we have not matched the formalization’s description of the lattices at the odd positions with the dual model weights of [Adr], nor compared its scalars with those of [Adr, Def. 11.23] (Chapter 39). [Adr, Thms. 12.27 and 12.21] derive (A1) and (A2) from [Adr, Hyps. 11.22, 11.38, 11.42 and 12.12], assumed there; [Adr, Prop. 11.27] proves [Adr, Hyp. 11.22] for its own step normalization. The three engines of [Adr, Hyp. 12.12] are not separate statements in the formalization. In the formalization \(\Gamma\) is net of the colength of the Hecke image, and (A2) and (A2\(^+\)) come from the source lattices by an exact computation (Chapter 59). (W3)

Theorem 52.4.3, from the Selmer bound on windows, Theorem 52.4.1. The deliverables of Chapters 37–52 at every \(x\) of the window, with constants independent of \(x\), \(Y\), \(Y_0\), \(q\), \(d\).
