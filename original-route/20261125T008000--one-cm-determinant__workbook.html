Prerequisites.

Legendre’s formula \(v_p(r!) = \sum_{e \ge 1} \lfloor r/p^e \rfloor\), Stirling’s formula, Chebyshev’s bound (Problem 6.1.1 in Chapter 6), and the arithmetic of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\). The invariant differential and complex uniformization of an elliptic curve [Si1, Ch. VI], and supersingular reduction [Si1]. The Jacobi theta function \(\theta_1\) [DLMF], Cauchy’s inequality and the argument principle. Section 15.6 also uses the prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions [MV]. From Chapter 12, Complex tori, theta functions and periods: Jacobi’s function \(\vartheta\) (Section 12.2). From Chapter 14, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime: the congruence \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\) for a formal group (Section 14.2) and the supersingular reduction of \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) at the primes inert in \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) (Section 14.3). Chapter 16, The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime, treats the derivation \(D^p\) for the lemniscate.

§15.1. Mertens from the factorial Tag 22EB

Let \(\theta(X) := \sum_{p \le X} \log p\). How much of \(\log r!\) comes from each prime? Chebyshev’s bound \(\theta(2n) - \theta(n) \le n \log 4\) is Problem 6.1.1 of Chapter 6.

Tag 22EC

Problem 15.1.1. \(\theta(X) < 2X\log 4\) for \(X \ge 1\).

Tag 22ED

Problem 15.1.2. For \(r \ge 1\), \(-\theta(r) \le \log r! - r \sum_{p \le r} \frac{\log p}{p} \le r \sum_p \frac{\log p}{p(p-1)}\).

With Stirling’s formula, Problems 15.1.1 and 15.1.2 prove the following [Mer].

Tag 22EE

Theorem 15.1.1 (Mertens). \(\sum_{p \le X} \log p/p = \log X + O(1)\) for \(X \ge 2\).

How much of \(\log r!\) comes from each odd class modulo \(4\)? Let \[S_a(X) := \sum_{p \le X,\ p \equiv a \ (4)} \frac{\log p}{p}.\]

Tag 22EF

Theorem 15.1.2. \(S_a(X) = \frac12 \log X + O(1)\) for \(a = 1, 3\) and \(X \ge 2\).

This is classical, and the usual proof rests on \(L(1, \chi_{-4}) \ne 0\) [MV, Ch. 4]. Since \(S_1 + S_3 = \log X + O(1)\), two upper bounds suffice. We replace the factorial by one determinant for each class and take \(r = \lfloor X \rfloor\).

§15.2. The Gaussian Vandermonde Tag 22EG

Let \(z_1, \dots, z_r\) be distinct Gaussian integers with \(0 \le \operatorname{Re} z, \operatorname{Im} z < \lceil \sqrt r \rceil\), and \(V := \prod_{j < k} (z_k - z_j)\).

Tag 22EH

Problem 15.2.1. \(\lvert V \rvert^2\) is a positive integer, and \(\log \lvert V \rvert^2 \le \binom r2 \log(2r)\).

Tag 22EJ

Problem 15.2.2. If \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\), then \(v_p(\lvert V \rvert^2) \ge r^2/p - r\).

Summing Problem 15.2.2 with weight \(\log p\) over \(p \le r\) gives \(r^2 S_1(r) \le \log \lvert V \rvert^2 + r\theta(r)\). With Problems 15.1.1 and 15.2.1 this proves the following. The counting of coincident pairs follows [PV].

Tag 22EK

Theorem 15.2.1. \(S_1(X) \le \frac12 \log X + O(1)\).

Tag 22EL

Problem 15.2.3. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(v_p(\lvert V \rvert^2) \ge r^2/p^2 - r\).

The weights \(\log p/p^2\) have a convergent sum, so Problem 15.2.3 says nothing about \(S_3\). An inert prime has residue field \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\), and Gaussian integers coincide modulo \(p\) only with frequency \(1/p^2\). To see an inert prime with frequency \(1/p\) we use the curve with complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\), which is supersingular exactly at these primes. Its invariant derivation \(D\) satisfies \(D^p \equiv 0 \pmod p\), so every block of \(p\) derivatives gains a factor \(p\), as every block of \(p\) consecutive integers does in \(r!\).

§15.3. The derivation at an inert prime Tag 22EM

Let \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\), \(A := \mathbb{Z}[x, y]/(y^2 - x^3 + x)\), \(\omega := dx/(2y)\), and \(P := (0, 0)\). The derivation \(D\) of \(A\) dual to \(\omega\) is \[Dx = 2y, \qquad Dy = 3x^2 - 1.\] The automorphism \([i](x, y) := (-x, iy)\) fixes \(P\) and pulls \(\omega\) back to \(i\omega\). Let \(d_k := (D^k x)(P)\).

Tag 22EN

Problem 15.3.1. The \(d_k\) are integers, and \(d_k = 0\) unless \(k \equiv 2 \pmod 4\). Moreover \(d_2 = -2\), \(d_6 = 144\), \(d_{10} = -96768\).

Let \(s = 1 + \frac14 t^4 + \dotsb\) be the root of \(s^4 = 1 + t^4\) with constant term \(1\). For odd \(p\) it lies in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[t]]\).

Tag 22EP

Problem 15.3.2. The point \(x = -t^2/s^2\), \(y = -t/s^3\) lies on \(E\), \(t\) is a parameter at \(P\), and \[\omega = \frac{dt}{s^3} = \sum_{h \ge 0} (-1)^h b_h \, t^{4h} \, dt, \qquad b_h := \frac{\prod_{j=1}^h (4j - 1)}{4^h h!}.\]

Thus \(E\) is a quotient of the Fermat quartic \(s^4 = 1 + t^4\), and its differential is a series in \(t^4\).

Tag 22EQ

Problem 15.3.3. Let \(p\) be an odd prime. On \(A/pA\) the operator \(D^p\) is a derivation, and \(D^p = cD\) for a constant \(c \in \mathbb{F}_p\).

On the formal group of \(E\) this is the congruence \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\) of Section 14.2, with \(\lambda \equiv c\).

Tag 22ER

Problem 15.3.4. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(c = 0\).

This is the vanishing of the Hasse invariant at a supersingular prime [Si1, Thm. V.4.1(a)].

Tag 22ES

Problem 15.3.5. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(p^{\lfloor k/p \rfloor}\) divides \((D^k f)(P)\) for all \(f \in A\) and \(k \ge 0\). For instance \(3^2 \mid d_6\) and \(7^2 \mid d_{14} = 268240896\).

§15.4. The determinant of derivatives Tag 22ET

Write \(E(\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) with \(dz = \omega\) [Si1, Ch. VI], and let \(w(z)\) be the \(x\)-coordinate of \(P + z\). Then \(w(z) = \wp(z + u_0)\) for a lift \(u_0 \notin \Lambda\) of \(P\), \(D = d/dz\), \(d_k = w^{(k)}(0)\), and \[(w')^2 = 4(w^3 - w), \qquad w(0) = w'(0) = 0, \qquad w''(0) = -2.\] For \(r \ge 1\), let \(M\) be the matrix whose row of index \(k \ge 0\) lists the \(k\)th derivatives at \(0\) of \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:columns}\tag{1} \frac{z^j}{j!} \quad (0 \le j < r), \qquad \frac{z^j}{j!}\, w(z) \quad (0 \le j < r). \end{equation}\] The first \(r\) columns are the identity followed by zeros.

Tag 22EU

Problem 15.4.1. The entry of \(M\) in row \(k\) and column \(z^j w/j!\) is \(\binom kj d_{k - j}\) for \(k \ge j\), and \(0\) otherwise.

A relation among the columns, restricted to rows \(0, \dots, 4r\), is a pair of polynomials \(a, b\) of degree \(< r\), not both zero, with \(a + bw = O(z^{4r+1})\).

Tag 22EV

Problem 15.4.2. Let \(Q(a, b) := (a'b - ab')^2 + 4ab(a^2 - b^2)\). Then \(Q(a, b) = b^4\bigl((h')^2 - 4(h^3 - h)\bigr)\) with \(h = -a/b\), when \(b \ne 0\). If \(a + bw = O(z^{4r+1})\) with \(\deg a, \deg b < r\), then \(Q(a, b) = 0\).

Tag 22EW

Problem 15.4.3. No nonconstant rational function \(h \in \mathbb{C}(z)\) satisfies \((h')^2 = 4(h^3 - h)\).

Tag 22EX

Problem 15.4.4. If \(h \in \{0, \pm1\}\) and \(b \ne 0\) has degree \(< r\), then \(b(w - h)\) vanishes at \(0\) to order at most \(r + 1\).

Problems 15.4.2–15.4.4 prove the following.

Tag 22EY

Theorem 15.4.1. The rows of \(M\) of index \(0, \dots, 4r\) have rank \(2r\).

In order of increasing \(k\), keep each row of \(M\) that is independent of its predecessors, until \(2r\) rows are kept. Their indices are \(I = \{0, \dots, r-1, k_1, \dots, k_r\}\) with \(r \le k_1 < \dots < k_r \le 4r\). Expanding along the identity block, \[N_r := \det M_I = \det\Bigl[\tbinom{k_i}{j} d_{k_i - j}\Bigr]_{1 \le i \le r,\ 0 \le j < r}\] is a nonzero integer. Let \(L := \sum_i k_i - \binom r2\), the total order \(\sum_i (k_i - j_i)\) of the derivatives in each term of this determinant.

Tag 22EZ

Problem 15.4.5. For \(r = 1, 2, 3\) the new rows are \(\{2\}\), \(\{2, 3\}\), \(\{3, 4, 6\}\), and \(N_r = -2\), \(12\), \(2^7 3^4\).

Tag 22F2

Problem 15.4.6. \(r^2 \le L \le 4r^2\), and \(v_p(N_r) \ge L/p - r\) for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\).

§15.5. The size of the determinant Tag 22F3

The entries \(d_k\) grow like \(k!\) times a power of the distance from \(0\) to the nearest pole of \(w\). To bound \(N_r\) we first remove the poles.

Tag 22F4

Problem 15.5.1. Let \(H\) be holomorphic near \(0\) with \(H(0) = 1\). Multiplying all the functions (1) by \(H\) does not change \(\det M_I = N_r\).

Write \(\Lambda = \lambda(\mathbb{Z}+ \tau\mathbb{Z})\) with \(\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0\), and let \[\theta_\tau(v) := 2\sum_{n \ge 0} (-1)^n e^{\pi i\tau(n + 1/2)^2} \sin\bigl((2n + 1)\pi v\bigr),\] so that \(\theta_\tau(v) = \theta_1(\pi v \mid \tau)\) [DLMF]. In terms of Jacobi’s function \(\vartheta\) of Section 12.2, \(\theta_\tau(v) = -i\, e^{\pi i\tau/4 + \pi i v}\, \vartheta\bigl(v + \tfrac{1+\tau}{2}\bigr)\). It is odd, \(\theta_\tau(v + 1) = -\theta_\tau(v)\), and \(\theta_\tau(v + \tau) = -e^{-\pi i\tau - 2\pi i v}\theta_\tau(v)\).

Tag 22F5

Problem 15.5.2. The zeros of \(\theta_\tau\) are the points of \(\mathbb{Z}+ \tau\mathbb{Z}\), and they are simple.

Let \(H(z) := \theta_\tau\bigl((z + u_0)/\lambda\bigr)^2 / \theta_\tau(u_0/\lambda)^2\).

Tag 22F6

Problem 15.5.3. \(H\) and \(Hw\) are entire, and \(\lvert H(z) \rvert, \lvert H(z) w(z) \rvert \le e^{C(1 + \lvert z \rvert^2)}\) with \(C\) independent of \(z\).

Tag 22F7

Problem 15.5.4. For every \(R > 0\), \(\lvert N_r \rvert \le (2r)!\,(4r/R)^L e^{2Cr(1 + R^2)}\). Hence \(\log \lvert N_r \rvert \le \frac12 L \log r + O(r^2)\).

Summing Problem 15.4.6 with weight \(\log p\) over \(p \le r\) gives \(L S_3(r) \le \log\lvert N_r \rvert + r\theta(r)\). By Problems 15.1.1 and 15.5.4, and \(L \ge r^2\), this proves the following.

Tag 22F8

Theorem 15.5.1. \(S_3(X) \le \frac12 \log X + O(1)\).

Theorems 15.1.1, 15.2.1 and 15.5.1 prove Theorem 15.1.2. The proof never uses \(L(1, \chi_{-4}) \ne 0\).

§15.6. The linear term Tag 22F9

Both determinants lose \(O(r^2)\): the floors \(\lfloor k/p \rfloor\) cost \(r\theta(r)\), and the bounds for \(\lvert V \rvert\) and \(\lvert N_r \rvert\) are crude at order \(r^2\). So they give \(S_a\) up to \(O(1)\) and say nothing of the constant term. For \(\chi = \chi_{-4}\) that term involves \[c_\chi := \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi) = \gamma + \log\frac{\pi^2}{2\varpi^2} = 0.2456\ldots, \qquad \varpi := 2\int_0^1 \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1 - t^4}},\] where the second expression follows from Kronecker’s limit formula and the value of \(\varpi\) in terms of \(\Gamma(\frac14)\) (Theorem 12.3.1); it is the case \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) of the formula of Chowla and Selberg [CS]. How close can a determinant come to \(c_\chi\)?

On the Vandermonde side the floors can be kept exactly. For a prime power \(q\) let \(w_q(k) := \sum_{e \ge 1} \lfloor k/q^e \rfloor\), and let \(k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} := \prod_{\mathfrak p} \mathfrak p^{w_{N\mathfrak p}(k)}\), the \(k\)th factorial of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) in the sense of Pólya and Bhargava. For any \(n\) Gaussian integers the Vandermonde product is divisible by \(\prod_{k < n} k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]}\) [Bh]. Write \(\lvert k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} \rvert\) for the absolute value of a generator.

Tag 22FA

Problem 15.6.1. \(\log \dfrac{k!}{\lvert k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} \rvert} = \frac12 w_2(k) \log 2 + \sum_{p \equiv 3 \ (4)} \log p \sum_{e \text{ odd}} \Bigl\lfloor \dfrac{k}{p^e} \Bigr\rfloor\). For \(k = 6\) both sides are \(\log 36\).

Let \(\beta(n) := \sum_{k < n} \log\lvert k!_{\mathbb{Z}[i]} \rvert\).

Tag 22FB

Problem 15.6.2. \(\beta(n) = \frac12 \sum_{N < n} (n - N) \bigl( \log N + \sum_{p^e \mid N} \chi(p^e) \log p \bigr)\), the inner sum over prime powers \(p^e > 1\).

The prime number theorem for \(\chi\) [MV] gives \(\sum_{p^e \le x} \chi(p^e) \log p = o(x)\) and \(\sum_{p^e \le x} \chi(p^e) \log p / p^e = -c_\chi + o(1)\).

Tag 22FC

Problem 15.6.3. \(\beta(n) = \frac{n^2}4 \log n - \frac{n^2}{4}\bigl(\frac32 + c_\chi\bigr) + o(n^2)\).

We take the points in a disc.

Tag 22FD

Problem 15.6.4. Let \(\mu\) be the uniform probability measure on the disc \(\lvert z \rvert \le R\). For \(\lvert z \rvert \le R\), \(\int \log\lvert z - u \rvert \, d\mu(u) = \log R - (R^2 - \lvert z \rvert^2)/(2R^2)\), and hence \(\iint \log\lvert z - u \rvert \, d\mu \, d\mu = \log R - \frac14\).

Tag 22FE

Problem 15.6.5. Let \(V_n\) be the Vandermonde product of the \(n\) Gaussian integers nearest \(0\). Then \(\log\lvert V_n \rvert = \frac{n^2}{4} \log\frac n\pi - \frac{n^2}8 + o(n^2)\).

Tag 22FF

Problem 15.6.6. \(\beta(n) \le \log\lvert V_n \rvert\) implies \(c_\chi \ge \log\pi - 1 = 0.1447\ldots\).

So even with exact divisibility the Vandermonde loses \(c_\chi - \log\pi + 1 = 0.1008\ldots\) in the linear term. On the side of derivatives one may build Padé-type determinants from the Taylor coefficients of the lemniscatic sine, whose poles are the points of \(\varpi\bigl(\frac{1+i}2 + \mathbb{Z}[i]\bigr)\). We expect, from computation, that they give \(c_\chi \le \log(4\pi/\varpi^2) = 0.6031\ldots\), short by the absolute constant \(\log(8/\pi) - \gamma = 0.3574\ldots\); we do not know a proof of the size estimate this needs. In both directions the loss comes from a disc of Gaussian integers, as nodes on one side and as poles on the other. An error \(O(1)\) in \(S_a\) is Chebyshev’s strength. We expect that one determinant is not enough to control the constant term, let alone the error terms that the Riemann hypothesis requires.
