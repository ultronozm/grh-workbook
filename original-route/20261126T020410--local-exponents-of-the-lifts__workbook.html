Prerequisites.

We use the notation of Chapter 52, Section 52.1: the scale \(x\), \(y=x^\varepsilon\) with \(0<\varepsilon<1/52\), \(b=e^y\), \(a=xb\), the CM type \(\Phi\) of \(E\) with \(f=2n\) elements, the field \(L\) with its places \(w\), weights \(\ell_w\) and ramification indices \(e_w\), the level primes \(g_\iota=\pi_\iota\bar\pi_\iota\), the terminal prime \(T_u=\varpi_u\bar\varpi_u\), the total weight \(A=\sum_ta_t\) of the tops \(t\in\mathcal T\), and \(B_0=fmby\). Recall \(\sum_{w\mid p}\ell_we_w=\log p\), and Problem 52.1.1: if \(\pi\in\mathcal O_K\) and \(\pi\bar\pi=g\) is prime, then \(\sum_{w\mid g}\ell_wv_w(\tau\bar\pi)=\tfrac12\log g\) for every automorphism \(\tau\) of \(L\). Section 53.4 uses Legendre’s formula for the exponent of \(p\) in \(n!\) and Chebyshev’s bound \(\sum_{p\le n}\log p\le n\log4\) [AZ, Ch. 2].

Chapter 52 states the bound for the local terms, Theorem 52.1.1, in the form this chapter proves. In [Adr, Thm. 16.6] its inputs are size bounds for the numbers \(\kappa_j\), \(\delta_j\), \(\varepsilon^T_u\), \(\varepsilon^d_j\) and \(\varepsilon^{\rm in}_{j,p}\) [Adr, Hyps. 16.13–16.14]. In the Lean formalization [Lean] these numbers are given by formulas. What are the formulas, and why are the bounds true? Almost everything turns out to be elementary: a congruence at the terminal prime, a count of rows at the level primes, Minkowski’s bound for finite groups, and, at the inert primes, a linear programme along Frobenius orbits summed with Mertens’ estimate.

§53.1. The exponent at each place

Besides the scale, the data have integer ranks \(k\ge2\), \(m\ge1\), \(t\ge0\) with \(N_i=k+i(t+1)\), weights \(w_j(\rho)\ge0\) for \(1\le j\le m\) and \(\rho\in\Phi\), a width \(\Theta\) of the windows in which these weights lie, and a size parameter \(\sigma\). Put \(X'=\exp(x^{\varepsilon/3})\), \(b'=a/X'\) and \(X''=xnX'\). In the formalization the elements of \(\Phi\) restrict to the identity on \(K\). The lifts gain only at primes of two classes, called class I and class II below, both consisting of primes inert in \(K\).

The exponent \(E_{u,j}(w)\) at a place \(w\) with residue characteristic \(p\) is defined as follows. If \(p=T_u\), it is \(\varepsilon^T_u\) when \(v_w(\varpi_u)>0\) and \(0\) otherwise, with \[\varepsilon^T_u=2\sum_{\rho\in\Phi}W_u(\rho)+fN_m+\tfrac14\lvert\mathcal T\rvert(t+1)(m+1).\] Here \(W_u(\rho)\) is the largest of the numbers \(\lvert\lambda-\lfloor i(t+1)/2\rfloor\rvert\) over the entries \(\lambda\) at \(\rho\) of the highest weights of the chain \(u\) at the levels \(i\le m\), the top entry at the top places excepted. If \(p\ne T_u\), then \(E_{u,j}(w)=v_p(D_\Gamma)+E'\), where

  • \(E'=-\tfrac12\Delta_j+e_w^{-1}\sum_\rho n_{u,j}(\rho)v_w(\tau_\rho\bar\pi_\iota)+\kappa_j\) with \(\kappa_j=c_gf(N_m^2+m^2\Theta)\) if \(p=g_\iota\);

  • \(E'=\varepsilon^d_j\) if \(p=d\);

  • \(E'=\varepsilon^{\rm cl}_{j,p}=3fN_m^2\log_pa+(p^2-1)^{-1}\sum_\rho w_j(\rho)\) if \(p\le X''\) is of class I and its Frobenius label \(h_p\) of Section 53.4 vanishes;

  • \(E'=\varepsilon^{\rm orb}_{j,p}\), defined in Section 53.4, for the other \(p\le X''\) of classes I and II;

  • \(E'=0\) otherwise.

Here \(\Delta_j\) is the Fock degree, \(n_{u,j}(\rho)\) the exponent of \(\bar\pi_\iota\) at \(\rho\) in the \(j\)-th lift, \(c_g\ge10\) a constant, and \(\varepsilon^d_j\) an explicit expression in the weights at the two places of \(\Phi\) above each column. This is the shape of [Adr, Def. 16.5], with its free numbers now given by formulas.

Sizes.

The family of data satisfies size conditions. Those we use are:

  • \(0\le w_j(\rho)\le3b\) and \(W_u(\rho)\le5b\);

  • \(N_m^2\le\Theta\), \(4N_m\le\Theta\), \(1\le y\le\Theta\), \(f\Theta\le b\), \(X''\Theta\le b\) and \(X''N_m^2\le b\);

  • \(2\le m\le f\), \(\lvert\mathcal T\rvert\le f\), \((t+1)(m+1)\le b\), \(kb'\le b\) and \(k(b'+\Theta)\le mb\);

  • \(\log g_\iota\le C_Yy\) and \(\log d\le y\);

  • \(\log T_u\le C_Tmy\), and \(D_\Gamma\) divides Minkowski’s number \(M(2fN_m)\), where \(M(r)=\prod_pp^{\mu_p(r)}\) with \(\mu_p(r)=\sum_{i\ge1}\lfloor r/(p^{i-1}(p-1))\rfloor\).

Minkowski proved that the order of every finite subgroup of \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\) divides \(M(r)\) [Se]. Status: in the Lean formalization (S1)–(S4) follow from the size conditions imposed on the family, and (S5) is part of the definition of the class of families to which the main argument is applied; we have not traced how the constructed family is shown to satisfy them. Consequently \(X''\le b\le a\) and \(fN_m\le b\).

Problem 53.1.1. Let \(\varpi\in\mathcal O_K\) with \(\varpi\equiv1\pmod{4\mathcal O_K}\), and suppose \(T=\varpi\bar\varpi\) is prime. Then \(T\equiv1\pmod 4\), the trace \(\varpi+\bar\varpi\) is \(\equiv2\pmod4\), and \(T\nmid\varpi+\bar\varpi\). For \(K=\mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\) and \(\varpi=1+4\mathrm i\) one has \(T=17\) and trace \(2\).

Problem 53.1.2. In Problem 53.1.1, at each place \(w\mid T\) of \(L\) exactly one of \(v_w(\varpi)\), \(v_w(\bar\varpi)\) is positive, and it equals \(e_w\). For \(\varpi=1+\mathrm i\), which is not \(\equiv1\pmod4\), both are positive at the place above \(2\).

In the construction \(\varpi_u\equiv1\pmod{Q_Sqd(g_0g_1)^2}\) [Adr, Rem. 16.8], where \(Q_S\) is the part of the conductor of \(E\) prime to \(qd\); it is divisible by \(4\), since in the formalization \(2^{e+2}\mid Q_S\) for an exponent \(e\ge2\) of the \(2\)-power tower. So Problems 53.1.1 and 53.1.2 apply to \(T_u\).

Problem 53.1.3. Let \(S_u\) be the set of places \(w\mid T_u\) with \(v_w(\varpi_u)>0\). Then \(\sum_{w\in S_u}\ell_we_w=\frac12\log T_u\), and the places above \(T_u\) contribute exactly \(\varepsilon^T_u\log T_u\) to \(\sum_w2\ell_we_wE_{u,j}(w)\).

[Adr, Thm. 16.6] bounds this contribution by \(2\varepsilon^T_u\log T_u\); the congruence halves it.

Problem 53.1.4. Under (S1), (S3) and \(N_m\le b\), one has \(\varepsilon^T_u\le12fb\). With (S5), the places above \(T_u\) contribute at most \(12C_TB_0\).

§53.2. The level primes

By Problem 52.1.2, the places above \(g_\iota\) contribute \((2v_{g_\iota}(D_\Gamma)-\Delta_j+2\kappa_j+n_{u,j})\log g_\iota\), where \(n_{u,j}=\sum_\rho n_{u,j}(\rho)\). The main term \(A\log g_\iota\) appears once we know that \(n_{u,j}-\Delta_j\) is close to \(A\).

The exponent \(n_{u,j}(\rho)\) is a sum of \(N_{j-1}\) row exponents, read off from the highest weights of the \(j\)-th lift. There are \(k-2\) middle rows, each with exponent within \(\sigma+N_m\) of \(w_j(\rho)\); \(N_{j-1}-k\) block rows, each with exponent in \((0,2\Theta]\); and two end rows. The two end rows together have exponent close to \(w_j(\rho)+a(\rho)+w'_j(\rho)\). Here \(a(\rho)=a_t\) and \(w'_j(\rho)=0\) if \(\rho\) is the top place of \(t\in\mathcal T\), and \(a(\rho)=0\), \(w'_j(\rho)=w_j(\rho)\) if \(\rho\) is not a top place. Each top \(t\) has exactly one top place, and distinct tops have distinct top places. On the other side \(\Delta_j=\sum_\rho\Delta_j(\rho)\), where \(\Delta_j(\rho)\) is a sum of \(k+m-1\) absolute values, \(k-1\) of them close to \(w_j(\rho)\) and the others small.

Lemma 53.2.1 (degree identity). For every \(u\) and \(1\le j\le m\), \[\lvert n_{u,j}-\Delta_j-A\rvert\le\delta_j,\qquad \delta_j=2\sum_\rho w_j(\rho)+40\bigl(f(k\sigma+mt\Theta+kN_m)+\lvert\mathcal T\rvert b'/x\bigr),\] and \(\delta_j\le166fb\) under the size conditions.

Status: proved in the Lean formalization by the row-by-row comparison just described. The row exponents and the Fock degree are given by formulas in the construction of the lifts, which we have not re-derived. The corresponding step in [Adr] is the degree part of [Adr, Hyp. 16.14], assumed there.

Problem 53.2.1. Suppose that for every \(\rho\) \[\bigl\lvert n_{u,j}(\rho)-(k-1)w_j(\rho)-a(\rho)-w'_j(\rho)\bigr\rvert\le e_1(\rho),\qquad \bigl\lvert\Delta_j(\rho)-(k-1)w_j(\rho)\bigr\rvert\le e_2(\rho).\] Then \(\lvert n_{u,j}-\Delta_j-A\rvert\le\sum_\rho\bigl(w_j(\rho)+e_1(\rho)+e_2(\rho)\bigr)\).

Problem 53.2.2. Assume (S2)–(S4) and Lemma 53.2.1. Then \(\kappa_j\log g_\iota\le2c_gC_YB_0\), and \(\delta_j\log g_\iota\le166C_YB_0\).

Problem 53.2.3. For \(\iota=0,1\), \[\Bigl\lvert\sum_{w\mid g_\iota}2\ell_we_wE_{u,j}(w)-A\log g_\iota\Bigr\rvert\le(\delta_j+2\kappa_j+2v_{g_\iota}(D_\Gamma))\log g_\iota .\]

§53.3. The other places

Problem 53.3.1. For every \(r\ge1\), \(M(r)\) divides \((2r)!\), so \(\log M(r)\le2r\log(2r)\). For instance \(M(1)=2\) and \(M(2)=24\).

Problem 53.3.2. Assume (S3), (S5) and \(fN_m\le b\). Then \(\log D_\Gamma\le12fN_m\log(fN_m)\) and \(2\log D_\Gamma\le24B_0\).

Lemma 53.3.1. Under the size conditions, \(\lvert\varepsilon^d_j\rvert\le\lvert\mathcal T\rvert b+fkb'\).

Status: proved in the Lean formalization, from the windows for the weights at the two places above each column.

Problem 53.3.3. Under (S3), (S4) and Lemma 53.3.1, \(2\log d\,\lvert\varepsilon^d_j\rvert\le4B_0\).

Write \(P^{\rm in}\) for the primes \(p\le X''\) of classes I and II, and \(\varepsilon^{\rm in}_{j,p}\) for \(\varepsilon^{\rm cl}_{j,p}\) or \(\varepsilon^{\rm orb}_{j,p}\), as in Section 53.1.

Problem 53.3.4. For every \(u\) and \(j\), \[\sum_{w\nmid g_0g_1}2\ell_we_w\lvert E_{u,j}(w)\rvert\le \varepsilon^T_u\log T_u+2\log D_\Gamma+2\log d\,\lvert\varepsilon^d_j\rvert+\sum_{p\in P^{\rm in}}2\log p\,\lvert\varepsilon^{\rm in}_{j,p}\rvert .\]

§53.4. Orbit losses at inert primes

It remains to bound the last sum in Problem 53.3.4; this is [Adr, Hyp. 16.13] together with [Adr, Lemma 16.12]. The \(f=2n\) places of \(\Phi\) are labelled by pairs \(z=(\xi,h)\in\{0,1\}\times H\), where \(H\) is an abelian group of order \(n\) indexing the columns; we write \(w_j(z)\) for the weight at the place labelled \(z\). The tops are elements of \(H\), and the top places are the labels \((0,t)\) with \(t\in\mathcal T\). For \(p\) of class I or II there is an element \(h_p\in H\), read off from the Frobenius at \(p\), and Frobenius acts on labels by \[\phi_p(\xi,h)=(\xi+\xi_p,\ h+h_p),\qquad \xi_p=0\text{ for class I},\quad \xi_p=1\text{ for class II},\] with \(\xi\) taken modulo \(2\). Let \(n_p\) be the order of \(\phi_p\).

Problem 53.4.1. For \(p\) of class I, \(n_p\) is the order of \(h_p\); for \(p\) of class II, \(n_p=2\operatorname{ord}(2h_p)=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}h_p)\). Every orbit of \(\phi_p\) on labels has exactly \(n_p\) elements.

The loss along an orbit is the defect of a linear programme. For an integer \(l\ge2\), an integer \(d\ge1\) and numbers \(D_s\ge0\) indexed by \(s\in\mathbb Z/d\), let \(V(l,d,D)\) be the supremum of \(\sum_{s,i}x_{s,i}\) over the real \(x_{s,i}\ge0\), with \(s\in\mathbb Z/d\) and \(i\) odd, \(0<i<d\), subject to \[\sum_il^ix_{s,i}+\sum_ix_{s-i,i}\le D_s\qquad\text{for every }s\in\mathbb Z/d .\] Put \(F(l,d,D)=\frac{l}{l^2-1}\sum_sD_s-V(l,d,D)\). The factor \(l/(l^2-1)\) is the gain per unit weight at an inert prime \(l\), Chapter 18.

Problem 53.4.2. \(V(l,d,D)\le(l+1)^{-1}\sum_sD_s\), hence \(0\le F(l,d,D)\le\frac{l}{l^2-1}\sum_sD_s\).

Problem 53.4.3. If \(d=1\), then \(F(l,1,D)=lD_0/(l^2-1)\). If \(d=2\) and \(D_0=D_1=D\), then \(V=2D/(l+1)\) and \(F(l,2,D)=2D/(l^2-1)\).

For a label \(z\) put \(F_{j,p}(z)=F(p,n_p,D)\) with \(D_s=(k-1)w_j(\phi_p^sz)\). Let \(Z_p\) be the set of labels with \(\xi=0\) if \(p\) is of class I, and \(Z_p=\emptyset\) if \(p\) is of class II; let \(Z'_p\) be its complement and \(Z'^{\rm top}_p\) the top places in \(Z'_p\). Let \(P^{\rm odd}\) be the set of primes of class I whose \(h_p\ne0\) has odd order, and \(\gamma_p=p/(p^2-1)\). Then \[\begin{multline*} \varepsilon^{\rm orb}_{j,p}=\max\Bigl(0,\frac1{n_p}\sum_{z\in Z_p}F_{j,p}(z)\Bigr) +\gamma_p\lvert Z'^{\rm top}_p\rvert(b+\Theta+1+3\sigma)+\frac{12}p\sum_{z\in Z'_p}w_j(z)\\ +\lvert Z'_p\rvert N_{j-1}N_j\bigl(\log_p(4n_pN_ma)+1\bigr) +5\lvert Z'_p\rvert N_m^2\log_p(2a)+4\lvert Z'^{\rm top}_p\rvert(\log_p2a)^2+\varepsilon^{\rm odd}_{j,p}, \end{multline*}\] where \(\varepsilon^{\rm odd}_{j,p}=(k-1)p^{-3}\sum_{z\in Z'_p}w_j(z)+12fN_m^2\log_pa\) for \(p\in P^{\rm odd}\), and \(\varepsilon^{\rm odd}_{j,p}=0\) otherwise. The first term is the orbit loss; the others are errors.

Lemma 53.4.1. Under the size conditions, for every prime \(p\le b\) of class I or II, \[\lvert\varepsilon^{\rm orb}_{j,p}-\varepsilon^{\rm odd}_{j,p}\rvert\log p\le126fmb\frac{\log p}p+46f\Theta y,\qquad \lvert\varepsilon^{\rm odd}_{j,p}\rvert\log p\le9fbk\frac{\log p}{p^3}+24f\Theta y,\] and \(k\sum_{p\in P\cap P^{\rm odd}}(\log p)/p^3\le2\) for every finite set \(P\) of primes.

Status: proved in the Lean formalization. The first bound treats the seven terms one at a time; its main term is Problem 53.4.4 below. The last bound rests on a congruence \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod{2^e}\) for the primes of \(P^{\rm odd}\), with \(2^e\) large compared to \(k\); we have not followed its proof.

Problem 53.4.4. Suppose \(w_j(z)\le3(b'+\Theta)\) whenever \(z\) has \(\xi=0\), and assume (S3). Then for \(p\ge2\) of class I or II, \[\max\Bigl(0,\frac1{n_p}\sum_{z\in Z_p}F_{j,p}(z)\Bigr)\le\frac{6fmb}{p}.\]

Problem 53.4.5. For every integer \(N\ge1\), \(\sum_{p\le N}(\log p)/p\le\log N+\log4\).

Problem 53.4.6. Assume (S2), (S3), Lemma 53.4.1 and \(X''\le b\). For every set \(P\) of primes \(p\le X''\) of classes I and II, \(\sum_{p\in P}2\log p\,\lvert\varepsilon^{\rm orb}_{j,p}\rvert\le848B_0\).

Problem 53.4.7. For \(x\ge1\), \(\log a\le(1/\varepsilon+1)y\).

Problem 53.4.8. Assume (S1) and (S2), and \(X''\le a\). For every set \(P\) of primes \(p\le X''\), \(\sum_{p\in P}2\log p\,\varepsilon^{\rm cl}_{j,p}\le18(1/\varepsilon+1)B_0\).

Problems 53.4.6 and 53.4.8 bound the last sum of Problem 53.3.4 by \((848+18/\varepsilon+18)B_0\). The constant depends on \(\varepsilon\), as in [Adr, Rem. 16.9].

§53.5. The theorem

Problems 53.1.3–53.2.3, 53.3.2, 53.3.3, 53.3.4, 53.4.6 and 53.4.8 prove the following.

Theorem 53.5.1 (local terms). Let \(0<\varepsilon<1/52\), and consider a family of data satisfying the size conditions, Lemmas 53.2.1, 53.3.1 and 53.4.1. There are \(C\) and \(x_0\) such that for every datum at a scale \(x\ge x_0\), every \(u\) and every \(1\le j\le m\), \[\Bigl\lvert\sum_w2\ell_we_wE_{u,j}(w)-A\log(g_0g_1)\Bigr\rvert\le CB_0,\qquad \sum_{w\nmid g_0g_1}2\ell_we_w\lvert E_{u,j}(w)\rvert\le CB_0 .\]

Status: proved in the Lean formalization, for the family it constructs. The first inequality is the bound of [Adr, Thm. 16.6] for these exponents. The corresponding steps in [Adr] are [Adr, Hyps. 16.13 and 16.14], assumed there; here Lemmas 53.2.1, 53.3.1 and 53.4.1 take their place. The second inequality is not stated in [Adr]. Summing over \(j\) gives (A5), \(E_u\le mAg+e_5\), with \(e_5=CmB_0\), as in Problem 52.1.3.

In the Lean formalization 1. In [Lean] the exponent is Eval, with the pieces EatT, EatG, EatD, EatC, EatI, EatX, the orbit loss FOrb and the programme lpF. The two inequalities of Theorem 53.5.1 are LocalQuadraticParts.adm11; the first is a clause of LinkDIV_at, supplied by linkDIV_at_words. They are assembled by secondComplement_of_split from C2atG_of (Problem 53.2.3) and C2off_of (Problem 53.3.4). The family version is familySecondComplementInputs_of_tuSize_of_dGamSize, whose hypotheses TuSize and DGamSize are (S5). Its inputs are: typePrime_valuationSum_eq_logGap_all (Problem 52.1.1); not_dvd_trace, coprime_conj, sum_over_eq, ETpi_le and admissible_off_type_level_budget (Problems 53.1.1–53.1.4); admissibleDegree_count_at_parameter_const, via degree_fine, nuAbs_place_fine and letters_place (Lemma 53.2.1); typePrime_sizes_le_budget_single (Problem 53.2.2); dGamSize_of_dvd and log_minkM_le_two (Problems 53.3.1 and 53.3.2); abs_EatD_le and offDiagonalRamified_bound_const_four (Lemma 53.3.1, Problem 53.3.3); factLpF (Problem 53.4.2), first_term, eatI_bound, eatX_bound, sum_cube_le, prime_log_sum_bound and sum_eatI (Lemma 53.4.1, Problems 53.4.4–53.4.6); bound_one and sum_primes_le (Problem 53.4.8). They are combined in inertPrimes_loss_log_sum_admissible.

In the formalization the orbit losses \(F_{j,p}(z)\) also enter other local terms of the lifts, and a separate loss term enters the gain inequality at inert primes. We have not traced how these are related to the orbit losses of this chapter.
