§17.1. The isotropic plane Tag 22GF

The function \(F\) does not gain by itself (Problem 16.5.3). Why should \(\Theta\) along the directions \(e_\pm = (1, \pm\zeta)\) do better? They are the eigenlines of an action of \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\) on \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]^2\). Let \[\rho(\zeta) := \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ \mathrm{i}& 0 \end{pmatrix},\] let \(\sigma_+\) be the identity of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\) and \(\sigma_-\) the automorphism \(\zeta \mapsto -\zeta\), and let \(H(Z, W) := Z_1 \bar W_1 + Z_2 \bar W_2\).

Tag 22GG

Problem 17.1.1. \(\rho\) extends to a ring homomorphism \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta] \to M_2(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}])\), and \(\rho(\bar a) = \rho(a)^*\), the conjugate transpose.

Tag 22GH

Problem 17.1.2. \(\rho(a) e_\pm = \sigma_\pm(a) e_\pm\), and \(H(e_+, e_-) = 0\).

Now let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). The Frobenius \(\zeta \mapsto \zeta^3\) generates the decomposition group of \(p\) in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\), with fixed field \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})\), \(\sqrt{-2} := \zeta + \zeta^3\). Since \(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]\) is a principal ideal domain, \(p = x^2 + 2y^2\) for integers \(x\), \(y\). We regard \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\) as a subring of \(\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22GJ

Problem 17.1.3. After replacing \(y\) by \(-y\) if necessary, \(a := x + y\sqrt{-2}\) lies in \(p\mathcal{O}\), while \(\bar a = x - y\sqrt{-2}\) is a unit of \(\mathcal{O}\). Moreover \(\sigma_-(a) = \bar a\). (For \(p = 3\): \(a = 1 \pm \sqrt{-2}\).)

Tag 22GK

Problem 17.1.4. If \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), then \(v_p(a) = v_p(\bar a)\) for every \(a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\).

Complex conjugation on \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) reduces modulo \(p\) to the Frobenius \(z \mapsto z^p\) of \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\), so \(H\) reduces to the hermitian form \(h(z, w) := z_1 w_1^p + z_2 w_2^p\) on \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^2\). Let \(\bar e_\pm\) be the reductions of \(e_\pm \in \mathcal{O}^2\). The Frobenius sends \(\zeta\) to \(\zeta^p\), which is not \(\bar\zeta\) when \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\); so \(h(\bar e_+, \bar e_-)\) need not vanish although \(H(e_+, e_-) = 0\).

Tag 22GL

Problem 17.1.5. \(h(\bar e_+, \bar e_+) = h(\bar e_-, \bar e_-) = 1 + \zeta^{p+1}\) and \(h(\bar e_+, \bar e_-) = 1 - \zeta^{p+1}\). So for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) both lines are isotropic and \(h\) pairs them, while for \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) they are anisotropic and orthogonal.

We call \(e_-\) the partner of \(e_+\). In terms of embeddings, \(\sigma_- = c \circ \operatorname{Frob}\circ \sigma_+\), where \(c\) is complex conjugation and \(\operatorname{Frob}(\zeta) = \zeta^p\); for \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), by contrast, \(c \circ \operatorname{Frob}\circ \sigma_+ = \sigma_+\). The derivatives along \(e_+\) will gain, and those along the partner \(e_-\) will pay for it.

§17.2. Integrality on an eigenline Tag 22GM

Let \(P(s) := \Theta(s e_+) = F(s)\, F(\zeta s)\). Since \(F\) is even, \(\Theta(t e_-) = P(t)\), and \(t(n, 0) = P^{(n)}(0)\). Write \(P(s) = \hat P(\operatorname{sl}s)\) with \(\hat P \in \mathbb{Q}[[X]]\). We show that \(\hat P\) is \(p\)-integral, unlike \(F\).

Let \(L\) be the period lattice of \(\operatorname{sl}\) and \(\eta\) the quasi-period map of \(\Lambda\). Since \(\eta(\mathrm{i}\omega) = -\mathrm{i}\eta(\omega)\), Legendre’s relation gives \(\eta(\omega) = (\pi/\operatorname{vol}\Lambda)\, \bar\omega\), and with the law \(\sigma(u + \omega) = \pm e^{\eta(\omega)(u + \omega/2)}\sigma(u)\) [Si2], \[F(u + \omega) = \exp\bigl(c\, \bar\omega u + \tfrac{c}{2} \lvert\omega\rvert^2\bigr) F(u), \qquad \omega \in \Lambda,\quad c := \frac{2\pi}{\operatorname{vol}\Lambda} .\]

Tag 22GN

Problem 17.2.1. For \(\gamma \in L^2\), \(\Theta(Z + \gamma) = \exp\bigl(c H(Z, \gamma) + \tfrac{c}{2}H(\gamma, \gamma)\bigr)\Theta(Z)\). For \(a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\), \(a \ne 0\), the function \[F_a(Z) := \frac{\Theta(\rho(a) Z)}{\Theta(\rho(\bar a) Z)}\] is \(L^2\)-periodic with \(F_a(0) = 1\), hence a rational function on \(E \times E\).

Tag 22GP

Problem 17.2.2. On \(\mathbb{C}/L = E(\mathbb{C})\), the zeros of \(F\) are the two points at infinity of \(y^2 = 1 - X^4\), each with multiplicity two.

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be \(E \times E\) over \(\mathcal{O}\), an abelian scheme on which \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\) acts through \(\rho\), and write \(\hat F_a(X_1, X_2)\) for the expansion of \(F_a\) in \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}Z_1\), \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}Z_2\).

Tag 22GQ

Problem 17.2.3. \(\hat F_a \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\).

Now let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), and let \(a\) be as in Problem 17.1.3.

Tag 22GR

Problem 17.2.4. \(\hat P([a]X) = \hat F_a(X, [\zeta]X)\, \hat P([\bar a]X)\).

Tag 22GS

Problem 17.2.5. The coefficients of \(\hat P\) lie in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\).

Problems 17.2.1–17.2.5 and Theorem 16.4.1 prove the following.

Tag 22GT

Theorem 17.2.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). Then \(\hat P \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[X]]\), and \(v_p(t(n, 0)) = v_p(P^{(n)}(0)) \ge E_p(n)\) for every \(n \ge 0\).

The induction in Problem 17.2.5 is the argument Bannai and Kobayashi use for the Kronecker theta function at ordinary primes [BK, Prop. 2.14], where one element of the CM order serves for all coordinates at once. Here \(\mathcal{A}\) is supersingular, but on the line \(\mathbb{C}e_+\) the element \(a\) acts by a non-unit and \(\bar a\) by a unit. At \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) there is no such element (Problem 17.1.4), and at \(p = 7\) the conclusion fails.

Tag 22GU

Problem 17.2.6. \(\hat P(X) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{70}X^8 + O(X^{12})\). So \(\hat P\) is not \(7\)-integral, and \(t(8,0) = -576\).

§17.3. The cross term Tag 22GV

Theorem 17.2.1 accounts for the derivatives along each axis. What remains is a factor equal to \(1\) on both axes. Throughout, \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}s\), \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}t\), and \(P_n := P^{(n)}(0)\).

Tag 22GW

Problem 17.3.1. \(J(s,t) := \partial_s\partial_t \log\Theta(s e_+ + t e_-) = 2\operatorname{sl}^2(s + t) - 2\mathrm{i}\, \operatorname{sl}^2\bigl(\zeta(s - t)\bigr)\), and \(J \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\).

Let \(M(s,t) := \int_0^s \int_0^t J\), and \(M_{\alpha\beta} := \partial_s^\alpha \partial_t^\beta M(0,0)\).

Tag 22GX

Problem 17.3.2. \(\Theta(s e_+ + t e_-) = P(s)\, P(t)\, e^{M(s,t)}\). The numbers \(M_{\alpha\beta}\) vanish unless \(\alpha, \beta \ge 1\), and then \(M_{\alpha\beta} \in p^{E_p(\alpha - 1)}\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22GY

Problem 17.3.3. Let \(S\) and \(T\) be sets with \(a\) and \(w\) elements. Then \[t(a,w) = \sum P_{\lvert S_0 \rvert}\, P_{\lvert T_0 \rvert} \prod_{B \in \pi} M_{\lvert B \cap S \rvert, \lvert B \cap T \rvert},\] the sum over subsets \(S_0 \subseteq S\), \(T_0 \subseteq T\), and partitions \(\pi\) of \((S \smallsetminus S_0) \sqcup (T \smallsetminus T_0)\) into blocks each meeting both \(S\) and \(T\).

Tag 22GZ

Problem 17.3.4. Each term in Problem 17.3.3 has at most \(w\) blocks and valuation at least \(\lfloor a/p \rfloor - w\).

So \(v_p(t(a,w)) \ge \lfloor a/p \rfloor - w\). For the higher odd levels one must also use the multiplicities of the terms. Group the terms of Problem 17.3.3 by \(a_0 = \lvert S_0 \rvert\), \(w_0 = \lvert T_0 \rvert\), and the number \(m_{\alpha\beta}\) of blocks with \(\lvert B \cap S \rvert = \alpha\) and \(\lvert B \cap T \rvert = \beta\).

Tag 22H2

Problem 17.3.5. The number of terms with given \(a_0\), \(w_0\) and \((m_{\alpha\beta})\) is \(N \cdot a!/\bigl(a_0! \prod_{\alpha,\beta} (\alpha!)^{m_{\alpha\beta}}\bigr)\) with \(N\) a positive integer.

Tag 22H3

Problem 17.3.6. Let \(\alpha_1, \dotsc, \alpha_k \ge 1\) and \(a_0 \ge 0\) have sum \(a\), and let \(n_i := \#\{b : p^i \mid \alpha_b\}\). Then \[v_p\Bigl(\frac{a!}{a_0!\, \alpha_1! \dotsm \alpha_k!}\Bigr) + E_p(a_0) + \sum_{b=1}^k E_p(\alpha_b - 1) \ \ge\ \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{a}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor - n_i\Bigr)_+ ,\] where \(x_+ := \max(x, 0)\).

Since \(n_i \le k \le w\), Problems 17.3.2–17.3.6 and Theorem 17.2.1 bound every group of terms. With the symmetry \(t(a,w) = t(w,a)\) of Problem 16.1.5, this proves the following.

Tag 22H4

Theorem 17.3.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). For all integers \(a, w \ge 0\), \[v_p\bigl(t(a,w)\bigr) \ \ge\ \max\Biggl( \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{a}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor - w\Bigr)_+ ,\ \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{w}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor - a\Bigr)_+ \Biggr) .\]

In the table of Section 16.1 the bound is an equality whenever it is positive. It is not always sharp: \(t(11,1) = -2^9 \cdot 3^6\), while the bound at \(p = 3\) is \(2\).

Exercise

If \(M = s^p t/p\), then \(\partial_s^{pw}\partial_t^w e^{M}(0) = (pw)!/p^w\), of valuation \(v_p(w!)\). For \(w < p\) this is \(0\), whereas a series integral in \(X_1\), \(X_2\) would give at least \(w\) by Problem 16.3.4: each block \(s^p t/p\) loses one factor of \(p\).

§17.4. Questions Tag 22H5

Theorem 17.3.1 does not extend to \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\): it fails at \(p = 7\) (Problem 17.2.6). What fails is the choice of directions: \(e_\pm\) are global eigenlines of \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\), and they are isotropic modulo \(p\) only for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) (Problem 17.1.5). For every \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the form \(H\) on \(\mathcal{O}^2\) has a basis \(f, f'\) with \(H(f, f) = H(f', f') = 0\) and \(H(f, f') = 1\), which need not come from any element of the CM order. Adriazola [Adr and Thm. 8.21] writes theta functions of hermitian lattices in such local hyperbolic coordinates over \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}\), using the action of all of \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}\) on the formal group [Adr, Lemma 8.13], and obtains the gain at the odd levels at every inert \(p \ge 5\), for the imaginary quadratic fields used there, with no condition modulo \(8\) [Adr, Thm. 11.45]. That result assumes that the constant in the kernel law is integral; in [Adr] this is [Adr, Hyp. 11.42, (N3)]; see the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58. The Lean formalization [Lean] does not assume it: it proves an integral kernel law of this shape for the kernels it uses, by a different route, in which the exponent is a torus average of a coboundary of the logarithm of the sigma function of one CM curve, and the integrality comes from a factorization of the fourth tensor power of the kernel into sigma functions. So the restriction \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) belongs to the eigenline model of this chapter, not to the argument.

Let \(A\) be an abelian variety with complex multiplication by the ring of integers of a CM field \(K\), with CM type \(\Phi\), and let \(\Theta\) be a normalized theta function on its universal cover. The eigenlines \(\mathbb{C}e_\phi\) (\(\phi \in \Phi\)) replace \(e_\pm\); the case above is \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\), \(\Phi = \{\sigma_+, \sigma_-\}\). The argument of Section 17.2 on \(\mathbb{C}e_\phi\) needs an element \(a\) of the CM order with \(\phi(a)\) a non-unit and \(\phi(\bar a)\) a unit, and such an element exists exactly when complex conjugation does not fix the prime of \(K\) determined by \(\phi\). Theorem 17.3.1 subtracts at each odd level \(i\) the order of differentiation along \(c \circ \operatorname{Frob}^i \circ \sigma_+ = \sigma_-\). We do not know whether in general the weight subtracted at level \(i\) from the order along \(e_\phi\) is the order along \(c \circ \operatorname{Frob}^i \circ \phi\), at the levels with \(\operatorname{Frob}^i \circ \phi \notin \Phi\).

For \(E \times E\) the eigenline argument fails only at \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), and there the hyperbolic coordinates above are the way around it. The eigenline argument fails at every supersingular prime for the Jacobian of \(y^2 = x^5 - 1\), with CM by \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)\). For odd \(p \not\equiv 0, 1 \pmod 5\), complex conjugation lies in the decomposition group of every prime above \(p\), and these are the primes at which the Jacobian is supersingular. We do not know whether the restriction of \(\Theta\) to an eigenline is \(p\)-integral in the formal parameter at these primes, nor what lower bound holds for its derivatives at the origin.

Find its divisor on \(\mathcal{A}\) near the origin of the special fiber, using Problem 17.2.2 and the fact that the points at infinity of \(E\) do not meet the origin modulo \(p\). Since \(a\) divides the nonzero integer \(N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\), both \(\rho(a)\) and \(\rho(\bar a)\) are isogenies on the special fiber, so pulling back the closure of the zero divisor of \(\Theta\) adds no vertical component. The condition \(F_a(0) = 1\) excludes the special fiber itself.
