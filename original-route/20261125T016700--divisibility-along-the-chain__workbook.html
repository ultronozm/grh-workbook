Prerequisites.

Formal power series over a commutative ring. Finite extensions of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), their valuations, and lattices over their rings of integers; the length of a module of finite length. Gaussian binomial coefficients \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q}\) [Mac, Ch. I, §2, Example 3]. From Chapter 6, The slope method: Theorem 6.5.1. From Chapter 8, The chain and the square root: Section 8.4. From Chapter 19, Hecke nondegeneracy on the tree: the loss, defined in Section 19.1. From Chapter 24, Hecke operators and theta lifts: Section 24.5, in particular Problems 24.5.3 and 24.5.4.

Theorem 8.4.2 adds gains that hold on the whole of each lattice. In the argument a lift gains more only on a sublattice spanned by images of a Hecke operator, and Chapter 19 measures the colength of such a sublattice. How does a gain on a sublattice at the start of a chain become a gain of the composite? The Hecke operator has to be moved along the chain, by Problem 24.5.4, and the coefficients that appear are complete symmetric functions. Their denominators are the price of the move. We follow the reconstruction of Adriazola [Adr], with one Hecke operator in place of several.

§25.1. Elementary and complete symmetric functions Tag 22PF

Let \(x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)\) be a finite family in a commutative ring. The elementary and complete symmetric functions \(e_l(x)\) and \(h_l(x)\) are the sums of the products \(x_{i_1} \cdots x_{i_l}\) over \(i_1 < \dots < i_l\) and over \(i_1 \le \dots \le i_l\) respectively, with \(e_0 = h_0 = 1\). Their generating series are \[E_x(X) := \prod_{i} (1 + x_i X) = \sum_{l \ge 0} e_l(x) X^l , \qquad H_x(X) := \prod_{i} (1 - x_i X)^{-1} = \sum_{l \ge 0} h_l(x) X^l .\]

Tag 22PG

Problem 25.1.1. \(E_x(X) H_x(-X) = 1\), that is, \(\sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l e_{r-l}(x)\, h_l(x) = 0\) for \(r \ge 1\). For \(x = (a, b)\), \(h_2(x) = a^2 + ab + b^2\).

This is [Mac, Ch. I, (2.6)]. For families \(x\) and \(y\) write \(x \sqcup y\) for the concatenated family.

Tag 22PH

Problem 25.1.2. \[e_r(x \sqcup y) = \sum_{l=0}^r e_l(x)\, e_{r-l}(y) , \qquad h_r(x \sqcup y) = \sum_{l=0}^r h_l(x)\, h_{r-l}(y) .\]

The families that occur are geometric progressions.

Tag 22PJ

Problem 25.1.3. For \(t \ge 0\), \[e_l(1, Q, \dots, Q^t) = Q^{l(l-1)/2} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{t+1}{l}_{Q} , \qquad h_l(1, Q, \dots, Q^t) = \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{t+l}{l}_{Q} .\] For instance \(h_2(1, Q) = 1 + Q + Q^2\).

These are [Mac, Ch. I, §2, Example 3]. Let \(q\) be invertible, \(Q := q^2\), \(g \ge 1\) and \(t := g - 1\). A block is a family \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:block}\tag{1} x = m\,(q^t, q^{t-2}, \dots, q^{-t}) \end{equation}\] of \(g\) terms with ratio \(Q^{-1}\). These are the families of Problem 24.5.3, where \(q^t\) is written \(Q^{t/2}\).

Tag 22PK

Problem 25.1.4. For the block (1), \(e_l(x) = m^l q^{-l(g-l)} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q}\) and \(h_l(x) = m^l q^{-lt} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{t+l}{l}_{Q}\).

The first formula is Problem 24.5.3.

§25.2. Valuations Tag 22PL

Let \(k\) be a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), with ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}\) and valuation \(v\) normalized by \(v(p) = 1\).

Tag 22PM

Problem 25.2.1. \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q}\) is a polynomial in \(Q\) with integer coefficients and constant term \(1\), for \(0 \le l \le n\). Hence \(v\bigl(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{n}{l}_{Q}\bigr) = 0\) if \(Q \in k\) and \(v(Q) > 0\).

Tag 22PN

Problem 25.2.2. Let \(a \in k^\times\) and \(Q \in k\) with \(v(Q) = \nu > 0\). Then \[\begin{align*} v\bigl(e_l(a, aQ, \dots, aQ^t)\bigr) &= l\,v(a) + \nu\,\frac{l(l-1)}{2} && (0 \le l \le t+1) , \\ v\bigl(h_l(a, aQ, \dots, aQ^t)\bigr) &= l\,v(a) && (l \ge 0) . \end{align*}\]

So the valuation of \(e_l\) exceeds that of \(h_l\) by \(\nu\,l(l-1)/2\).

Tag 22PP

Problem 25.2.3. Let \(x\) be the block (1) with \(m, q \in k^\times\) and \(v(q) = \nu > 0\). Then \(v(h_l(x)) = l\,(v(m) - t\nu)\) and \(v(e_l(x)) = l\,(v(m) - t\nu) + \nu\, l(l-1)\) for \(0 \le l \le g\). In particular every \(h_l(x)\) lies in \(\mathcal{O}\) if and only if \(v(m) \ge t\nu\).

For a union of blocks only a bound survives.

Tag 22PQ

Problem 25.2.4. Let \(Z\) be a finite family in \(k\) whose least valuation is \(\mu\). Then \(v(h_l(Z)) \ge l\mu\), with equality if exactly one member of \(Z\) has valuation \(\mu\). For \(Z = (1, 1)\) and \(Z = (1, -1)\) one has \(h_l(Z) = l + 1\) and \(h_l(Z) = (1 + (-1)^l)/2\).

Tag 22PR

Problem 25.2.5. Let \(Z = x_1 \sqcup x_2\), where \(x_i = m_i\,(q, q^{-1})\) are blocks with \(g = 2\), and let \(v(q) = \nu > 0\) and \(v(m_1) = v(m_2) = 0\). Then \(h_1(Z) = (m_1 + m_2)(q + q^{-1})\), so \(v(h_1(Z)) = v(m_1 + m_2) - \nu\), which exceeds \(-\nu\) exactly when \(m_1 \equiv -m_2\) modulo the maximal ideal of \(\mathcal{O}\).

§25.3. Moving a Hecke operator along a chain Tag 22PS

We use the setting of Problem 24.5.4. Let \(F_0, \dots, F_m\) be finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces, let \(K_j \colon F_j \to F_{j+1}\) be linear maps, and let \(\mathcal{K}_j := K_{j-1} \cdots K_0\), with \(\mathcal{K}_0 := 1\). Fix \(r \ge 1\). For each \(j\) let \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_0 = 1, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_1, \dots, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_r\) be operators on \(F_j\), and let \(x_1, \dots, x_m\) be finite families in \(k\) with \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:R}\tag{2} \mathcal{E}^{(j+1)}_i\, K_j = K_j \sum_{l=0}^i e_l(x_{j+1})\, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_{i-l} \qquad (0 \le i \le r,\ 0 \le j < m) . \end{equation}\] For lifts with split kernels, Section 24.5 gives (2) with \(x_{j+1}\) a block (1), up to a unit that we take to be \(1\) [Adr, Props. 10.8 and 10.13]. Let \(Z_j := x_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup x_j\). In (2) the operators at the target are expressed through those at the source. We need the converse.

Tag 22PT

Problem 25.3.1. Let \(r = 1\). Then \(K_0 \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_1 = \bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_1 - e_1(x_1)\bigr) K_0\), and \(\mathcal{K}_2 \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_1 = \bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(2)}_1 - h_1(Z_2)\bigr) \mathcal{K}_2\).

Tag 22PU

Problem 25.3.2. Let \(r = 2\). Then \(K_0 \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_2 = \bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_2 - h_1(x_1)\, \mathcal{E}^{(1)}_1 + h_2(x_1)\bigr) K_0\).

Tag 22PV

Problem 25.3.3. \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i\, \mathcal{K}_j = \mathcal{K}_j \sum_{l=0}^i e_l(Z_j)\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_{i-l}\) for \(0 \le i \le r\).

Problems 25.3.3 and 25.1.1 give the following, which is Problem 24.5.4.

Tag 22PW

Theorem 25.3.1. For \(1 \le j \le m\), \(\displaystyle \mathcal{K}_j\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r = \sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l h_l(Z_j)\, \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_{r-l}\, \mathcal{K}_j\).

The relation for one lift has the elementary functions of a block, whose valuations exceed those of the complete functions by \(\nu\,l(l-1)\) (Problem 25.2.3). The inverse relation has the complete functions, which may have denominators. The operators \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i\) are normalized forms of the sums \(T^{(j)}_i\) over edges of type \(i\) of Section 24.5: when \(F_j\) consists of functions of lattices in a space of dimension \(N_j\), with \(N_0 < N_1 < \dots < N_m\), one takes \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i = q^{-i(N_j - i)}\, T^{(j)}_i\) [Adr].

Tag 22PX

Problem 25.3.4. With this normalization, Theorem 25.3.1 reads \[\sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l h_l(Z_j)\, q^{l(N_j - 2r + l)}\, T^{(j)}_{r-l}\, \mathcal{K}_j = q^{r(N_j - N_0)}\, \mathcal{K}_j\, T^{(0)}_r .\]

Tag 22PY

Problem 25.3.5. Suppose that \(Z_j\) is a union of blocks (1) with the same \(t\), that \(v(q) > 0\), that \(v(m) \ge 0\) for each block, and that \(N_j \ge 2r + t - 1\). Then every coefficient \(h_l(Z_j)\, q^{l(N_j - 2r + l)}\) in Problem 25.3.4 lies in \(\mathcal{O}\).

§25.4. A gain on a sublattice Tag 22PZ

For each \(j\) let \(\mathrm{Int}_j \subset F_j\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-lattice of full rank. For \(w \in F_j\) let \(\operatorname{ord}(w)\) be the least valuation of a coordinate of \(w\) in a basis of \(\mathrm{Int}_j\), with \(\operatorname{ord}(0) = \infty\). For a linear map \(A \colon F_i \to F_j\) let \(\operatorname{gain}(A)\) be the least value of \(\operatorname{ord}(Ab)\) over the members \(b\) of a basis of \(\mathrm{Int}_i\). For \(k = \mathbb{Q}_p\), \(\operatorname{gain}(A)\) is the largest integer \(g\) with \(A(\mathrm{Int}_i) \subseteq p^g \mathrm{Int}_j\); so \(\operatorname{gain}(A) \log p\) plays at \(p\) the role of the divisibility \(-h_{\mathrm{fin}}\) of Section 8.4.

Tag 22Q2

Problem 25.4.1. \(\operatorname{ord}\) and \(\operatorname{gain}\) do not depend on the bases. \(\operatorname{ord}(Aw) \ge \operatorname{ord}(w) + \operatorname{gain}(A)\), \(\operatorname{gain}(AB) \ge \operatorname{gain}(A) + \operatorname{gain}(B)\), \(\operatorname{gain}(A + B) \ge \min(\operatorname{gain}(A), \operatorname{gain}(B))\), and \(\operatorname{gain}(cA) = v(c) + \operatorname{gain}(A)\) for \(c \in k^\times\).

Assume (2). For \(1 \le j \le m\) and \(0 \le i \le r\) let \[\gamma_j(i) := \operatorname{gain}\bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i K_{j-1}\bigr) ,\] so that \(\gamma_j(0) = \operatorname{gain}(K_{j-1})\) is the gain of the \(j\)th step on the whole lattice, and \(\gamma_j(r)\) its gain after the Hecke operator \(\mathcal{E}^{(j)}_r\) at the target. Let \(c \in k^\times\) be such that \(Y := c\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r\) maps \(\mathrm{Int}_0\) into itself, and let \(A := v(c)\). The gain of the \(j\)th step on the image of \(Y\) is not \(A + \gamma_j(r)\). By Theorem 25.3.1 it is controlled by \[\delta_j := \min_{0 \le l \le r} \bigl( v(h_l(Z_j)) + \gamma_j(r - l) \bigr) , \qquad \mathrm{Pr}_j := \gamma_j(r) - \delta_j .\] When \(\gamma_j(r) < \infty\), we call \(\mathrm{Pr}_j\) the price of the \(j\)th step; the term \(l = 0\) shows that \(\mathrm{Pr}_j \ge 0\), and \(\delta_j = \gamma_j(r) - \mathrm{Pr}_j\).

Tag 22Q3

Problem 25.4.2. For \(w \in F_0\) and \(1 \le j \le m\), \(\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_j\, \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r w\bigr) \ge \operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_{j-1} w\bigr) + \delta_j\).

Tag 22Q4

Problem 25.4.3. Let \(S \subseteq \{1, \dots, m\}\), and let \(n_j\) be the number of elements of \(S\) that are at most \(j\). For \(G \in \mathrm{Int}_0\) and \(0 \le j \le m\), \[\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_j\, Y^{n_j} G\bigr) \ge \sum_{i \le j,\ i \in S} \bigl(A + \delta_i\bigr) + \sum_{i \le j,\ i \notin S} \gamma_i(0) .\]

Take \(S\) to be the set of \(j\) with \(A + \delta_j > \gamma_j(0)\), and \(G := Y^{m - n_m} F\), which lies in \(\mathrm{Int}_0\) because \(Y\) preserves \(\mathrm{Int}_0\). Problem 25.4.3 with \(j = m\) gives the following.

Tag 22Q5

Theorem 25.4.1. For every \(F \in \mathrm{Int}_0\), \[\operatorname{ord}\bigl(\mathcal{K}_m\, Y^m F\bigr) \ge \sum_{j=1}^m \max\bigl(\gamma_j(0),\ A + \delta_j\bigr) .\]

This is the case of one orbit of [Adr, Thm. 10.7], in which the estimates at several primes are combined. A step uses a factor of \(Y\) only when the factor pays for its price; the other factors are applied at the start, where they cost nothing. The gain holds on the image \(Y^m \mathrm{Int}_0\). When \(Y\) is injective this is a sublattice of full rank, and in Theorem 6.5.1 the gain on such a sublattice is charged its colength, divided by the rank.

Tag 22Q6

Problem 25.4.4. If \(Y\) is injective, then the length of \(\mathrm{Int}_0/Y^m\mathrm{Int}_0\) is \(m\) times the length of \(\mathrm{Int}_0/Y\,\mathrm{Int}_0\).

When \(\mathrm{Int}_0\) consists of integral forms and \(Y\) is the normalized Hecke operator of Chapter 19, the values of \(Y\) at one lattice \(L\) span the module \(S(L)\) there, and the loss of Chapter 19 is its colength in \(H(L)\).

§25.5. Stepwise maxima overcount Tag 22Q7

Theorem 25.4.1, like [Adr, Thm. 10.7], charges each step a price; in [Adr] an upper bound for it is subtracted from the gain of the \(j\)th lift. Can the price be ignored, so that each step gains \(\max(\gamma_j(0), A + \gamma_j(r))\) on \(Y^m\mathrm{Int}_0\)? We compare two chains with the same numbers \(\gamma_j(i)\). Let \(p\) be any prime, \(k = \mathbb{Q}_p\), \(m = 2\), \(r = 1\), \(F_j = \mathbb{Q}_p^2\) and \(\mathrm{Int}_j = \mathbb{Z}_p^2\). Let \(K_0 = K_1 = K\) be a diagonal matrix, let \(x_1 = (a_1)\) and \(x_2 = (a_2)\) be families of one element, and let \[Y := \mathcal{E}^{(0)}_1 := \operatorname{diag}(p, 1) , \qquad \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_1 := Y + h_1(Z_j) \quad (j = 1, 2) ,\] so that \(A = 0\).

Tag 22Q8

Problem 25.5.1. These data satisfy (2), and \(\operatorname{length}\mathbb{Z}_p^2/Y\mathbb{Z}_p^2 = 1\).

Tag 22Q9

Problem 25.5.2. Let \(K = \operatorname{diag}(1, p)\) and \(a_1 = a_2 = p\). Then \(\gamma_j(0) = 0\), \(\gamma_j(1) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{Pr}_j = 0\) for \(j = 1, 2\), and \(\operatorname{gain}(\mathcal{K}_2\, Y^2) = 2\).

Tag 22QA

Problem 25.5.3. Let \(K = \operatorname{diag}(p, 1)\), \(a_1 = p - 1\) and \(a_2 = -2p\). Then \(\gamma_j(0) = 0\), \(\gamma_j(1) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{Pr}_j = 1\) for \(j = 1, 2\), and \(\operatorname{gain}(\mathcal{K}_2\, Y^2) = 0\).

In both chains each step, followed by the Hecke operator at its target, gains \(1\), and the sum of the stepwise maxima is \(2\). In the second chain the composite gains nothing on \(Y^2\mathrm{Int}_0\): the Hecke operator at the target of each step is not the image of the one at the source, and \(h_1(Z_j)\) is a unit. Theorem 25.4.1 gives the exact gain in both cases.

The theorem takes two inputs: the gains \(\gamma_j(i)\) of a single lift followed by a Hecke operator at its target, for which Chapters 16 and 17 give models through the divisibility of theta kernels, and the colength of \(Y\,\mathrm{Int}_0\), which Chapter 19 computes at one lattice. The price couples them, through the least valuation in the union \(Z_j\) of the blocks met so far (Problems 25.2.3 and 25.2.4).

Exercises

  1. The Newton polygon of \(\prod_{i=0}^t (1 + aQ^iX)\) over \(k\), with \(a \in k^\times\) and \(v(Q) > 0\), has the vertices \(\bigl(l, l\,v(a) + v(Q)\,l(l-1)/2\bigr)\) for \(0 \le l \le t+1\).

  2. Suppose that (2) holds with \(u_{j+1}^i K_j\) in place of \(K_j\) on the right, for units \(u_j \in \mathcal{O}^\times\). In terms of \(E_j(X) := \sum_{i \le r} \mathcal{E}^{(j)}_i X^i\) this reads \(E_{j+1}(X) K_j = K_j\, E_{x_{j+1}}(u_{j+1}X)\, E_j(u_{j+1}X)\) modulo \(X^{r+1}\); it is the relation of Section 24.5 with the family \(u_{j+1}x_{j+1}\), whose product \(\prod_a (1 + aX)\) is the \(P(X)\) written there. Then Theorem 25.3.1 holds with \(\mathcal{K}_j\mathcal{E}^{(0)}_r\) multiplied by \((u_1 \cdots u_j)^r\), and with \(Z_j\) replaced by the family \(Z'_j\) of the \(u_l u_{l+1} \cdots u_j\, a\) for \(l \le j\) and \(a\) in \(x_l\) [Adr, Prop. 10.8]. Theorem 25.4.1 holds with \(\delta_j\) computed from \(Z'_j\). These agree with the old \(\delta_j\) when \(Z_j\) has exactly one member of least valuation (Problem 25.2.4), but not in general: for \(p\) odd, \(x_1 = (1)\), \(x_2 = (-1)\), \(u_1 = -1\) and \(u_2 = 1\), one has \(h_1(Z_2) = 0\) while \(h_1(Z'_2)\) is a unit.
