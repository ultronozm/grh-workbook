Prerequisites.

Chapter 1, From zeros to a one-sided ramp sum (Sections 1.1–1.4): the functions \(G\) and \(G_\chi\) of its equations (1) and (3), the argument of Problem 1.2.4, and Theorem 1.2.3. Quadratic reciprocity with its supplements [SeA, Ch. I, §3], and the splitting of primes in an imaginary quadratic field [Ne, Ch. I, §8]. Orthogonality of Dirichlet characters and the prime number theorem in progressions to a fixed modulus [Dav, Ch. 4 and 20].

The arithmetic gives upper bounds for ramp sums over odd powers of primes inert in an imaginary quadratic field, not for the full ramp sum \(S\). How far is such a sum from \(G\), and what must be known about the quadratic characters involved to recover a bound for \(G\)?

§2.1. Inert primes and a quadratic character Tag 223A

Let \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) be a prime, let \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), and let \(\chi_d(n) := \bigl(\frac{n}{d}\bigr)\) be the Legendre symbol.

Tag 223B

Problem 2.1.1. (i) For every prime \(p\), \(\chi_d(p)\) is \(1\), \(-1\) or \(0\) according as \(p\) splits, is inert, or ramifies in \(K\). (ii) For a prime power \(r = p^h\), \(1 - \chi_d(r)\) is \(2\) if \(p\) is inert and \(h\) is odd, \(1\) if \(p = d\), and \(0\) otherwise.

The ramp sum over odd powers of inert primes, with weight \(2\), and over powers of \(d\), with weight \(1\), is therefore \[S_d(x) := \sum_r \Lambda(r) \bigl(1 - \chi_d(r)\bigr) \Bigl(\frac{x}{r} - 1\Bigr)_+ .\] For a primitive nonprincipal character \(\chi\) let \(\Psi_\chi(t) := \sum_{n \le t} \Lambda(n) \chi(n)\), and let \(c_\chi\) and \(G_\chi\) be as in (3).

Tag 223C

Problem 2.1.2. \(G_\chi(x) = -\sum_n \Lambda(n) \chi(n) \min(1, x/n) = -x \int_x^\infty \Psi_\chi(t) \, t^{-2} \, d t\).

Tag 223D

Problem 2.1.3. \(S_d(x) - x \log x + (1 + \gamma - c_{\chi_d}) x = G(x) - G_{\chi_d}(x)\).

The arithmetic thus bounds \(G - G_{\chi_d}\), not \(G\). How much does such a bound say about the zeros of \(\zeta\)?

Tag 223E

Problem 2.1.4. Let \(0 < \theta < 1\). Suppose that \(L(s, \chi_d)\) has no real zero in \((\theta, 1)\), and that \(G(x) - G_{\chi_d}(x) \le x^\theta\) for all large \(x\). Then every zero of \(\zeta(s)\) or of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) in \(\operatorname{Re}s > \theta\) is a zero of both, with the same multiplicity.

A one-sided bound for \(G - G_{\chi_d}\) thus cannot exclude a zero of \(\zeta\) that \(L(s, \chi_d)\) shares, and the argument needs a separate hypothesis on real zeros of \(L(s, \chi_d)\). The way out is to bound \(G_{\chi_d}(x)\) from both sides, for one well-chosen \(d\) and for \(x\) in a given range only (§2.2).

A second quadratic character enters through a table of residue classes. Let \(q \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) and \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be primes with \(q \mid d + 1\), and let \(\chi_q(n) := \bigl(\frac{n}{q}\bigr)\).

Tag 223F

Problem 2.1.5. \(\chi_d(2) = \chi_d(q) = 1\). Hence \(1 - \chi_d(r)\) vanishes at every power of \(2\) and of \(q\).

Let \(n\) be a power of \(2\), let \(N := 4n\), and let \(H := (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times/\{\pm 1\}\), a group of order \(n\); write \([r]\) for the class of an odd integer \(r\). Assign an odd prime power \(r\) prime to \(q\) to the cell \((v(r), [r])\), where \(v(r) := \frac12(1 + \chi_q(r))\) is the row. There are \(2n\) cells. To each cell \(i\) attach a weight \(w_i > 0\), and for \(a > 0\) consider the sum over these \(r\) \[P := \sum_r \Lambda(r) \bigl(1 - \chi_d(r)\bigr) \Bigl(\frac{a}{r} - w_{\mathrm{cell}(r)}\Bigr)_+ .\] As in Section 1.3, cell \(i\) contributes \(w_i\) times its own ramp sum at \(a/w_i\). Problem 4.1.4 of Chapter 4 fills in the table for \(q = 11\), \(d = 1319\) and \(N = 16\).

Let \(b > 0\) and \(T_2 \subset H\), and let \(\delta := \lvert T_2 \rvert/n\). For \(u \in H\), call the cells \((1, s)\) with \(s \in u T_2\) distinguished, give them weight \(b\), and give the others weights so small that \(a/w_i\) is enormous. Averaging over the translates \(u\) removes the column condition:

Tag 223G

Problem 2.1.6. For every odd \(r\) prime to \(q\), \(\frac1n \sum_{u \in H} \mathbf{1}\bigl[v(r) = 1,\ [r] \in u T_2\bigr] = \frac{\delta}{2}\bigl(1 + \chi_q(r)\bigr)\).

So the average over \(u\) of the distinguished part of \(P\) is \(\frac{\delta b}{2} S_{q,d}(a/b)\), where \[S_{q,d}(x) := \sum_r \Lambda(r) \bigl(1 + \chi_q(r)\bigr)\bigl(1 - \chi_d(r)\bigr)\Bigl(\frac{x}{r} - 1\Bigr)_+ .\]

Tag 223H

Problem 2.1.7. Let \(\eta := \chi_q \chi_d\). Then \[S_{q,d}(x) - x \log x + (1 + \gamma + c_{\chi_q} - c_{\chi_d} - c_\eta) x = G(x) + G_{\chi_q}(x) - G_{\chi_d}(x) - G_\eta(x) .\]

The function \(G\) appears with coefficient \(+1\), so an upper bound for the left side and two-sided bounds for \(G_{\chi_q}\), \(G_{\chi_d}\) and \(G_\eta\) give an upper bound for \(G\). The other cells test primes at the enormous scales \(a/w_i\). There the remainder of each cell must be small with a saving better than any power of \(x\), and for this one expands the cell in characters. By Problem 4.2.2 of Chapter 4, for \(r\) prime to \(2qd\) the function \(r \mapsto \bigl(1 - \chi_d(r)\bigr)\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{cell}(r) = (v, s)]\) is a linear combination \(\sum_\chi f_\chi \chi(r)\) of the \(4n\) characters \(\xi \chi_q^i \chi_d^j\), \(\xi\) a character of \(H\) and \(i, j \in \{0, 1\}\), with \(\sum_\chi \lvert f_\chi \rvert = 2\).

Hence, apart from explicit terms from the powers of \(2\), \(q\) and \(d\), the remainder of a cell is a combination, with total mass \(2\), of the functions \(G_{\chi^*}\), where \(\chi^*\) is the primitive character inducing \(\chi\) and \(G_{\chi^*} := G\) for \(\chi\) principal; and a uniform bound \(\lvert G_{\chi^*}(y) \rvert \le \vartheta y\), with \(\vartheta > 0\) small, for these characters at the large scales makes the other cells negligible. Such a bound comes from a zero-free region for the \(L(s, \chi)\) [Dav, Ch. 14], which is effective here, even if there is an exceptional real zero [Dav, Ch. 14, (12)], because the large scales may be taken with \(\log y > qdN\).

§2.2. Estimate B in a simple form Tag 223J

To use Problem 2.1.7 one needs two-sided bounds \(\lvert G_\chi(x) \rvert \le x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}\) for \(\chi = \chi_q\), \(\chi_d\) and \(\chi_q\chi_d\). For one fixed character and all large \(x\) this is as hard as the problem we started from:

Tag 223K

Problem 2.2.1. Let \(\chi\) be primitive and nonprincipal, and \(0 < \theta < 1\). If \(\lvert G_\chi(x) \rvert \le x^\theta\) for all large \(x\), then \(L(s, \chi) \ne 0\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s > \theta\).

What is needed is weaker: the bound for \(x\) in one interval \([Y, Y^{3/2}]\), for some \(q\) and \(d\) that may be chosen after \(Y\). One takes the moduli enormous, with \(\log q\) and \(\log d\) powers of \(Y\). For most of them a character sum of length far below the modulus behaves like a random sum, of size about \(\sqrt{x}\), and one good choice suffices.

For \(1 \le x \le Z\) let \(a_n(x) := \Lambda(n)\min(1, x/n)\), and for a Dirichlet character \(\chi\) let \[S_Z(x, \chi) := \sum_{n \le Z} a_n(x) \chi(n) , \qquad t_\chi(Z) := \sum_{n > Z} \frac{\Lambda(n) \chi(n)}{n} ,\] the second for \(\chi\) nonprincipal. By Problem 2.1.2, \(G_\chi(x) = -S_Z(x, \chi) - x\, t_\chi(Z)\) for primitive nonprincipal \(\chi\).

Tag 223L

Problem 2.2.2. \(\sum_{n \le Z} a_n(x)^2 \le C x \log x\) for \(2 \le x \le Z\), with \(C\) absolute.

Tag 223M

Problem 2.2.3. Let \(Q > Z\) be prime, and let \(b_n\) be complex numbers. Then \(\frac{1}{Q - 1}\sum_{\chi \bmod Q} \bigl\lvert \sum_{n \le Z} b_n \chi(n) \bigr\rvert^2 = \sum_{n \le Z} \lvert b_n \rvert^2\). Hence, for \(2 \le x \le Z\), at most \(C (Q - 1) x^{-2\varepsilon} \log x\) characters \(\chi\) modulo \(Q\) satisfy \(\lvert S_Z(x, \chi) \rvert > x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}\).

The characters of the argument are quadratic. For an odd prime \(q\) let \(\chi_q(n) := \bigl(\frac{n}{q}\bigr)\), and let \(\mathcal{P}(U)\) be the set of primes in \((U, 2U]\).

Tag 223N

Problem 2.2.4. Let \(m \ge 1\) be an integer that is not a square. There is a nonprincipal Dirichlet character \(\omega_m\) modulo \(4m\) with \(\bigl(\frac{m}{q}\bigr) = \omega_m(q)\) for every odd prime \(q \nmid m\).

Tag 223P

Problem 2.2.5. Fix \(2 \le x \le Z\) and real numbers \(b_n\) with \(\lvert b_n \rvert \le a_n(x)\). As \(U \to \infty\), \[\frac{1}{\lvert \mathcal{P}(U) \rvert} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{P}(U)} \Bigl( \sum_{n \le Z} b_n \chi_q(n) \Bigr)^2 \ \longrightarrow\ \sum_{\substack{n, n' \le Z \\ n n' = \square}} b_n b_{n'} \ \le\ C x \log x ,\] the sum being over the pairs with \(n n'\) a square.

Problem 2.2.5 and Chebyshev’s inequality give the following.

Tag 223Q

Theorem 2.2.1. Let \(2 \le x \le Z\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\). For all \(U\) large in terms of \(Z\), the proportion of \(q \in \mathcal{P}(U)\) with \(\lvert S_Z(x, \chi_q) \rvert > x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}\) is at most \(C x^{-2\varepsilon} \log x\), with \(C\) absolute.

A good modulus at one point \(x\) is not yet enough; one wants the bound at all \(x\) of an interval simultaneously.

Tag 223R

Problem 2.2.6. As a function of \(y \in [1, Z]\), \(S_Z(y, \chi)\) is continuous and piecewise linear, and off the integers its derivative \(D(y)\) satisfies \(y D(y) = \sum_{y < n \le Z} a_n(y) \chi(n)\).

Tag 223S

Problem 2.2.7. For \(1 \le W \le V \le \min(2W, Z)\) and \(y \in [W, V]\), \(\lvert S_Z(y, \chi) \rvert^2 \le 2 \lvert S_Z(W, \chi) \rvert^2 + 2W \int_W^{V} \lvert D(t) \rvert^2 \, d t\).

By Problem 2.2.6, Problem 2.2.5 applies to \(y D(y)\) as well. Applying Problems 2.2.5 and 2.2.7 on the blocks \([2^k Y, \min(2^{k+1} Y, Y^A)]\) and summing over \(k\) gives:

Tag 223T

Theorem 2.2.2. Let \(Y \ge 2\), \(A \ge 1\), \(Z \ge Y^A\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\). For all \(U\) large in terms of \(Z\), the proportion of \(q \in \mathcal{P}(U)\) for which \(\lvert S_Z(y, \chi_q) \rvert > y^{1/2 + \varepsilon}\) for some \(y \in [Y, Y^A]\) is \(O_\varepsilon(Y^{-\varepsilon})\).

It remains to make \(x\, t_{\chi_q}(Z)\) small. For a fixed character \(t_\chi(Z) \to 0\), but at a rate governed by the zeros of \(L(s, \chi)\) near \(\operatorname{Re}s = 1\), and here \(q\) is far larger than \(Z\). One needs a box \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1 - \delta_0\), \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le T\) free of zeros of \(L(s, \chi_q)\) for all but a small proportion of \(q \in \mathcal{P}(U)\); zero-density estimates near \(\operatorname{Re}s = 1\) give such boxes for most moduli [Mon, Theorem 12.2]. To have \(\log q\) a small power of \(Y\), instead of letting \(U \to \infty\), one needs primes in progressions to large moduli [Gal, Thm. 7], and Page’s theorem that at most one primitive real character of conductor at most \(R\) has a real zero within \(c/\log R\) of \(1\) [Pa], [Dav, Ch. 14].

The statement wanted, which we call Estimate B, is this: for every small \(\varepsilon > 0\) and every large \(Y\) there are primes \(q \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) and \(d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}\) with \(q \mid d + 1\) such that \(\lvert G_\chi(x) \rvert \le x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}\) for \(\chi = \chi_q\), \(\chi_d\), \(\chi_q\chi_d\) and all \(x \in [Y, Y^{3/2}]\). One chooses \(q\) first and then \(d\), and the mean squares over \(d\) involve the twists by \(\chi_q\). We do not carry out this combination here, and we do not know a published proof of Estimate B. Adriazola [Adr, Thms. 5.10 and 5.12] proves it, for most \(q\) and then most \(d\) in suitable ranges with \(\log q\) of size \(Y^{\varepsilon/5}\) and \(\log d\) of size \(Y^{\varepsilon/5}\log^2 Y\), from the inputs named above; there \(d\) is also taken \(\equiv -1\) modulo every odd prime up to \(Y^{\varepsilon/5}\). There is no circularity: the route sketched above uses only mean squares and zero-free boxes near \(\operatorname{Re}s = 1\) for most \(q\), and nothing about zeros near the critical line. For the function \(G^\psi\) of Section 1.4, Estimate B is needed for all the twists \(\xi \chi_q^i \chi_d^j\) with \(\xi\) a character modulo \(M\) and \((i, j) \ne (0, 0)\).

Hints

§2.1

2.1.1

For odd \(p \ne d\) compare \(\bigl(\frac{-d}{p}\bigr)\) with \(\bigl(\frac{p}{d}\bigr)\) by reciprocity. For \(p = 2\) use \(\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[(1 + \sqrt{-d})/2]\).

2.1.2

Use \(\sum_n \Lambda(n)\chi(n)/n = -c_\chi\), convergent by the prime number theorem for \(\chi\).

2.1.3

Write \(S_d\) as the ramp sum with coefficient \(1\) minus the one with coefficient \(\chi_d\), and use (1) and (3).

2.1.4

The Mellin transform of \(G - G_{\chi_d}\) is \(F(s) + \frac{L'}{L}(s, \chi_d)/(s(s-1)) - c_{\chi_d}/(s-1)\), with \(F\) as in (2). Follow Problem 1.2.4: a real zero of \(\zeta\) gives the residue sign of that problem, but a real zero of \(L(s, \chi_d)\) gives the opposite sign.

2.1.5

\(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) settles \(2\). For \(q\) use reciprocity with \(q, d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(d \equiv -1 \pmod q\).

2.1.7

Expand \((1 + \chi_q)(1 - \chi_d)\); the character \(\eta\) is primitive modulo \(qd\).

§2.2

2.2.1

The bound gives absolute convergence of the Mellin transform of \(G_\chi\) in \(\operatorname{Re}s > \theta\). Compute that transform as in Problem 1.1.3.

2.2.2

Split at \(n = x\) and use Chebyshev’s bound and partial summation on each part.

2.2.3

Expand the square; since \(Q > Z\), distinct \(n, n' \le Z\) are distinct modulo \(Q\).

2.2.4

Write \(m = 2^e m'\) with \(m'\) odd and use the Jacobi symbol. For nontriviality, separate the cases where \(m'\) is a square and where it is not.

2.2.5

Expand the square and apply Problem 2.2.4 with \(m = n n'\) and the prime number theorem modulo \(4nn'\). For the bound, \(n\) and \(n'\) are prime powers; the pairs with \(n = n'\) are covered by Problem 2.2.2. Group the pairs by prime, and treat the even powers separately.

2.2.7

Use Problem 2.2.6.
