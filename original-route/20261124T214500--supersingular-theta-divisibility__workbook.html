Prerequisites.

Formal groups of elliptic curves, their height, and supersingular reduction [Si1, Ch. IV and §§V.3–V.4]. The Weierstrass functions \(\wp\) and \(\sigma\) [Si2]. Legendre’s formula \(v_p(n!) = \sum_{i \ge 1} \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor\), Kummer’s theorem that \(v_p\binom{m+n}{m}\) is the number of carries in the base-\(p\) addition of \(m\) and \(n\), and the Lagrange inversion formula. From Chapter 12, Complex tori, theta functions and periods, we use the lattice \(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) of Problem 12.1.9. From Chapter 14, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime: formal groups, their height and the invariant derivation (Section 14.2), and Problem 14.2.3; Section 16.3 proves the congruence \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\) stated there. Chapter 17, The isotropic plane, continues this one.

§16.1. Gaussian moments Tag 22FH

Let \(\zeta = e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/8}\), and for integers \(a, w \ge 0\) consider the moments \[m(a,w) := \sum_{x \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]^2} (x_1 + \zeta x_2)^a\, (x_1 - \zeta x_2)^w\, e^{-\pi(\lvert x_1 \rvert^2 + \lvert x_2 \rvert^2)} .\] After a normalization they are rational numbers. How divisible are they by a prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), that is, by a prime that is inert in \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\)?

Let \(\operatorname{sl}\) be the lemniscate sine, the meromorphic function with \(\operatorname{sl}(0) = 0\), \(\operatorname{sl}'(0) = 1\) and \(\operatorname{sl}'^2 = 1 - \operatorname{sl}^4\), and let \(\varpi := 2\int_0^1 (1 - t^4)^{-1/2} \, d t\). Let \(\wp\) and \(\sigma\) be the Weierstrass functions with \(g_2 = 4\) and \(g_3 = 0\), whose period lattice is \(\Lambda = \varpi \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) (Problem 12.1.9). Let \[F := \sigma^2 \wp .\]

Tag 22FJ

Problem 16.1.1. \(\wp = \operatorname{sl}^{-2}\).

Tag 22FK

Problem 16.1.2. \(F\) is entire and even, \(F(\mathrm{i}u) = F(u)\), and \(F(u) = 1 + \tfrac16 u^4 + \tfrac{17}{2520} u^8 + O(u^{12})\).

Tag 22FL

Problem 16.1.3. Let \(\vartheta_0 := \sum_{\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} e^{-\pi \lvert \alpha \rvert^2}\). For \(z \in \mathbb{C}\), \[\sum_{\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} e^{-\pi\lvert\alpha\rvert^2 + z\alpha} = \vartheta_0\, F\Bigl(\frac{\varpi z}{2\pi}\Bigr).\]

Let \(\Theta(Z_1, Z_2) := F(Z_1) F(Z_2)\) on \(\mathbb{C}^2\), let \(e_\pm := (1, \pm\zeta)\), and let \[t(a,w) := \partial_s^a \partial_t^w \, \Theta(s e_+ + t e_-) \big|_{s = t = 0} , \qquad \Theta(s e_+ + t e_-) = F(s+t)\, F\bigl(\zeta(s-t)\bigr).\]

Tag 22FM

Problem 16.1.4. \(m(a,w) = \vartheta_0^2\, (\varpi/2\pi)^{a+w}\, t(a,w)\).

Tag 22FN

Problem 16.1.5. The numbers \(t(a,w)\) are rational, \(t(a,w) = t(w,a)\), and \(t(a,w) = 0\) unless \(a + 5w \equiv 0 \pmod 8\).

For instance \(t(3,1) = 8\), \(t(4,4) = 448\) and \(t(8,0) = -576\). The table lists \(v_{11}(t(a,w))\) for \(w \le 2\) and the first nine values of \(a \ge 8\) allowed by Problem 16.1.5.

\(w = 0\), \(a =\) 8 16 24 32 40 48 56 64 72
\(v_{11}\) 0 1 2 2 3 4 5 5 6
\(w = 1\), \(a =\) 11 19 27 35 43 51 59 67 75
\(v_{11}\) 0 0 1 2 2 3 4 5 5
\(w = 2\), \(a =\) 14 22 30 38 46 54 62 70 78
\(v_{11}\) 0 0 0 1 2 2 3 4 5

In this range \(v_{11}(t(a,w)) = \max(\lfloor a/11 \rfloor - w, 0)\). Each derivative in the second direction seems to cost one factor of \(11\). By contrast, \(t(8,0) = -576\) is prime to \(7\). This chapter treats one variable: what \(p\) divides the derivatives of a function on the lemniscate, and why \(F\) alone does not gain. The sequel, The isotropic plane, proves a lower bound of the shape above at every prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\).

§16.2. The lemniscate and its formal group Tag 22FP

Let \(E\) be the curve \(y^2 = 1 - X^4\) with origin \((0,1)\). It is a curve of genus one with good reduction at every odd prime. The map \(u \mapsto (\operatorname{sl}u, \operatorname{sl}' u)\) identifies \(\mathbb{C}/L\) with \(E(\mathbb{C})\), where \(L \subset \Lambda\) is the period lattice of \(\operatorname{sl}\). The function \(X\) is a parameter at the origin, \(du = dX/y\) is an invariant differential, and the addition law is \[\operatorname{sl}(u + v) = \frac{\operatorname{sl}u\, \operatorname{sl}' v + \operatorname{sl}v\, \operatorname{sl}' u}{1 + \operatorname{sl}^2 u\, \operatorname{sl}^2 v} .\] Let \(D := d/du = y \, d/dX\) be the invariant derivation, and let \(\ell(X) := \int_0^X (1 - v^4)^{-1/2} \, dv\) be the logarithm, so that \(\operatorname{sl}(\ell(X)) = X\).

Tag 22FQ

Problem 16.2.1. \(\operatorname{sl}u = u - \tfrac1{10}u^5 + \tfrac1{120}u^9 - \tfrac{11}{15600}u^{13} + \dotsb\). Only exponents \(\equiv 1 \pmod 4\) occur, \(\operatorname{sl}(\mathrm{i}u) = \mathrm{i}\, \operatorname{sl}u\), and the lattice \(L\) satisfies \(\mathrm{i}L = L\).

Tag 22FR

Problem 16.2.2. The formal group law \(X \oplus X'\), defined by \(\operatorname{sl}u \oplus \operatorname{sl}v = \operatorname{sl}(u + v)\), has coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12]\), and \(X \mapsto \mathrm{i}X\) is an endomorphism of it.

Tag 22FS

Problem 16.2.3. \(D\) maps \(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12][[X]]\) into itself, and \((D^n f)(0) = n!\, [u^n]\, f(\operatorname{sl}u)\) for \(f \in \mathbb{Q}[[X]]\).

Fix an odd prime \(p\), let \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}]\), and let \(v_p\) be the valuation with \(v_p(p) = 1\). For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(\mathcal{O}\) is the ring of integers of the unramified quadratic extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\). It contains \(\zeta\), since \(8\) divides \(p^2 - 1\). We write \(\hat E\) for the formal group of Problem 16.2.2 over \(\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22FT

Problem 16.2.4. For each \(\alpha \in \mathcal{O}\) the series \([\alpha](X) := \operatorname{sl}(\alpha \ell(X))\) lies in \(\mathcal{O}[[X]]\). It is the unique endomorphism of \(\hat E\) with \([\alpha](X) = \alpha X + O(X^2)\).

When \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), this action of \(\mathcal{O}\) makes \(\hat E\) a formal \(\mathcal{O}\)-module of the kind constructed by Lubin and Tate [LT]; we use only Problem 16.2.4. For these primes \(E\) is supersingular and \(\hat E\) has height two [Si1, Thm. V.3.1]. Indeed \(E\) is isogenous to \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) by \((X, y) \mapsto (X^{-2}, -yX^{-3})\), and that curve is supersingular exactly when \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) [Si1]. Section 16.3 shows what height two does to \(D\).

§16.3. The \(p\)th power of the invariant derivation Tag 22FU

Let \(f\) be a function on \(E\), regular at the origin, with expansion in \(\mathcal{O}[[X]]\). Its derivatives \((D^n f)(0)\) are integral by Problem 16.2.3. We show that they are divisible by \(p^{\lfloor n/p \rfloor}\) when \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). The smallest case is \(p = 3\): \(DX = y\), \(D^2X = -2X^3\) and \(D^3X = -6X^2y\).

For a series \(f\), let \(\tau_Y f(X) := f(X \oplus Y)\), a series in two variables.

Tag 22FV

Problem 16.3.1. Modulo \(p\), the operator \(D^p\) is a derivation of \(\mathcal{O}[[X]]\).

Tag 22FW

Problem 16.3.2. Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(p\), and let \(\delta\) be a continuous derivation of \(k[[X]]\) with \(\delta \tau_Y = \tau_Y \delta\). Then \(\delta = (\delta X)(0)\, D\). The operator \(D\) itself commutes with \(\tau_Y\).

Thus \(D^p \equiv \lambda_p D \pmod p\), with \(\lambda_p \equiv (D^p X)(0) = p!\, [u^p] \operatorname{sl}u\).

Tag 22FX

Problem 16.3.3. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), then \(\lambda_p \equiv 0\), so that \(D^p\, \mathcal{O}[[X]] \subseteq p\, \mathcal{O}[[X]]\).

Tag 22FY

Problem 16.3.4. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), let \(f \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\), and let \(D_1\), \(D_2\) be the invariant derivations in the two variables. Then \((D_1^a D_2^w f)(0) \in p^{\lfloor a/p \rfloor}\mathcal{O}\).

At \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) there is no such gain: \(\lambda_p\) is then a unit, since \(\lambda_p \equiv p\,[X^p]\,\ell = 4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m}\) for \(p = 4m+1\) by the argument of Problem 14.2.3, and for instance \((D^5X)(0) = -12\). Divisibilities of this kind on formal groups of any height are studied by Katz [Ka].

§16.4. The higher odd levels Tag 22FZ

At \(p = 3\), the integer \((D^{29}X)(0)\) is divisible exactly by \(3^{10}\), one more than \(\lfloor 29/3 \rfloor = 9\). Where does the extra factor come from? Throughout this section \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(q := p^2\), and we write \[E_p(n) := \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{n}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor .\] The answer is that \(D^n\) gains \(p^{E_p(n)}\). The extra factor at \(p^3\) comes from the coefficient of \(X^q\) in the logarithm.

Tag 22G2

Problem 16.4.1. \([X^{4m+1}]\, \ell = \binom{2m}{m} / \bigl(4^m (4m+1)\bigr)\), and \(\ell\) has \(p\)-integral coefficients in all degrees less than \(q\).

Tag 22G3

Problem 16.4.2. \(p\, [X^q]\, \ell \equiv -1 \pmod p\). (For \(p = 3\): \([X^9]\, \ell = 1/24\).)

By Problems 16.4.1 and 16.4.2, the truncation \(T\) of \(\ell(X) + X^q/p\) to degree at most \(q\) lies in \(X + X^2\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[X]\). It is therefore a parameter on \(\hat E\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\), and \(\mathcal{O}[[T]] = \mathcal{O}[[X]]\). In this parameter \[\ell = T - \frac{T^q}{p} + O(T^{q+1}), \qquad g(z) := \ell^{-1}(z) = z + \frac{z^q}{p} + O(z^{q+1}),\] where \(O(\cdot)\) allows arbitrary rational coefficients. Write the group law in \(T\) again as \(\oplus\), and define operators \(\Delta_j\) on \(\mathcal{O}[[T]]\) by \(f(T \oplus Y) = \sum_{j \ge 0} (\Delta_j f)(T)\, Y^j\). They commute with each other and with \(D\), and \(\Delta_1 = D\).

Tag 22G4

Problem 16.4.3. For \(f \in \mathcal{O}[[T]]\), \(f(T \oplus g(z)) = \sum_{n \ge 0} (D^n f)(T)\, z^n/n!\). Consequently \(D^p = p!\, \Delta_p\) and \(D^q = q!\, (\Delta_q + D/p)\).

Tag 22G5

Problem 16.4.4. \(\varepsilon := q!/p^{p+1}\) is a \(p\)-adic unit, and \(D^q = p^p \varepsilon\, H\) with \(H := D + p\Delta_q\).

Tag 22G6

Problem 16.4.5. Let \(A\), \(B\) be commuting \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear operators on a torsion-free \(\mathcal{O}\)-module \(V\) with \((A - B)V \subseteq pV\). Then \((A^p - B^p)V \subseteq p^2V\).

Let \(e_j := E_p(p^j) = p^{j-1} + p^{j-3} + \dotsb\), the sum over the exponents \(j - i\) with \(i \le j\) odd. Thus \(e_0 = 0\), \(e_1 = 1\), \(e_j = p^{j-1} + e_{j-2}\), and \(e_j \le p\, e_{j-1} + 1\).

Tag 22G7

Problem 16.4.6. For every \(j \ge 0\), as operators on \(\mathcal{O}[[T]]\), \[D^{p^j} \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{e_j}}, \qquad H^{p^j} \equiv D^{p^j} \pmod{p^{e_j + 1}} .\]

Tag 22G8

Problem 16.4.7. \(D^n\, \mathcal{O}[[X]] \subseteq p^{E_p(n)}\, \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) for every \(n \ge 0\).

Problems 16.4.1–16.4.7 prove the following.

Tag 22G9

Theorem 16.4.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(f \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\). Then \(v_p\bigl((D^n f)(0)\bigr) \ge E_p(n)\) for every \(n \ge 0\). The same holds for \(f \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\) and \(D = D_1\), the invariant derivation in the first variable.

The method gains nothing at the even levels. In Problem 16.4.4, \(D^q\) has the factor \(p^p\), not the \(p^{p+1}\) one would get from \(\lfloor q/p \rfloor + \lfloor q/q \rfloor\). The factor \(H\) gains nothing by itself, and the next gain comes from \(H^p \equiv D^p\).

§16.5. One theta function Tag 22GA

The function \(F\) of Section 16.1 is not a function on \(E\). Does it nevertheless gain? At \(p = 3\) the valuations \(v_3(F^{(k)}(0))\) for \(k = 4, 8, \dotsc, 60\) are \[0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 4,\ 4,\ 4,\ 4,\ 4,\ 5,\ 5,\ 7,\ 7,\] far below \(\lfloor k/3 \rfloor\).

Tag 22GB

Problem 16.5.1. \((\log F)'' = 2\operatorname{sl}^2\).

Let \(b_n := (D^n X^2)(0) = n!\, [u^n]\, \operatorname{sl}^2 u\), an element of \(\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12]\). By Problem 16.5.1, \[\log F(u) = 2 \sum_{n \ge 0} b_n \frac{u^{n+2}}{(n+2)!} .\]

Tag 22GC

Problem 16.5.2. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(m := (p-3)/4\). Then \(b_{p-1} \equiv -4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m} \pmod p\). In particular \(b_{p-1}\) is a \(p\)-adic unit. (For \(p = 3\): \(b_2 = 2\).)

Tag 22GD

Problem 16.5.3. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). Then \(F^{(p+1)}(0) \equiv 2 b_{p-1} \pmod p\). Hence \(F\), written as a series in \(X = \operatorname{sl}u\), does not have \(p\)-integral coefficients. (For \(p = 3\): \(F^{(4)}(0) = 4\).)

Thus the coefficient of \(u^{p+1}\) in \(\log F\) is \(c_p/p\) with \(c_p = 2b_{p-1}/\bigl((p+1)(p-1)!\bigr)\) a unit. For \(p = 3, 7, 11\) this coefficient is \(\tfrac16, -\tfrac1{140}, \tfrac1{2475}\), so \(c_p = \tfrac12, -\tfrac1{20}, \tfrac1{225}\). By the Exercise below, the exponential of such a term alone has derivatives of valuation about \(k/(p^2 - 1)\); the values above fit this, since \(v_3(F^{(60)}(0)) = 7\) and \(60/8 = 7.5\). We do not know whether \(v_p(F^{(k)}(0)) = k/(p^2-1) + O(\log k)\) holds in general for \(k\) divisible by \(4\); the other derivatives vanish at \(0\) by Problem 16.1.2.

The first non-integral term of \(\log F\) is \(c_p u^{p+1}/p\), a function of one variable. To remove it one needs a second variable. The plane should be chosen so that the restriction of the theta function to each axis is integral, and the remaining factor is \(1\) on both axes. Chapter 17, The isotropic plane, shows that the directions \(e_\pm\) of Section 16.1 achieve this.

Exercise

Let \(c\) be a \(p\)-adic unit, \(g(u) := \exp(c u^{p+1}/p)\), and \(k = (p+1)m\). Then \[v_p\bigl(k!\, [u^k]\, g\bigr) = \frac{k}{p^2 - 1} + \frac{s_p(m) - s_p(k)}{p-1},\] where \(s_p\) is the sum of base-\(p\) digits.
