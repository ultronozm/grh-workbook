§35.1. The field and its places

Let \(q\equiv3\pmod 8\) and \(d\equiv7\pmod{16}\) be primes with \(q\mid d+1\), let \(n\ge4\) be a power of \(2\), \(N:=4n\), and let \(E\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\), \(H\), \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\) and \(\Phi=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) be as in Chapter 4. Let \(F\) be the maximal real subfield of \(E\), and \(f:=\lvert\Phi\rvert=2n\). The elements \(\rho\in\Phi\) are the places; \(\rho=(s,e_1,1)\) is the cell \((v,s)\) with \(v=\frac12(1-e_1)\).

Three subsets of \(\Phi\) are singled out. A set \(\mathcal T\subset H\) of tops gives the top places \((0,t)\), \(t\in\mathcal T\). A set \(T_2\subset H\) and a translate \(\tau\in H\) give the thick places \((1,\tau s)\), \(s\in T_2\). The other places are thin. (Chapter 58 writes \(T\) for \(\mathcal T\).) The translate is fixed until Section 35.5.

Let \(p\nmid 2qd\) be inert in \(K\), and put \(\varphi_p:=c\operatorname{Frob}_p\), where \(c=([1],-1,-1)\) is complex conjugation.

Problem 35.1.1. \(\varphi_p\in\Phi\). Either \(\operatorname{Frob}_p=c\), or \(c\notin\langle\operatorname{Frob}_p\rangle\) and \(\varphi_p\) has the order of \(\operatorname{Frob}_p\), which is even.

Problem 35.1.2. Multiplication by \(\varphi_p\) sends the cell \((v,s)\) to \(\bigl(v+\frac12(1+\chi_q(p))\bmod 2,\ s[p]\bigr)\). So the orbits stay in one row when \(\chi_q(p)=-1\) and alternate between the rows when \(\chi_q(p)=1\).

The primes inert in \(K\) with \(\chi_q(p)=1\) are called signal primes, and those with \(\chi_q(p)=-1\) primes of inert class. When \(\varphi_p\) has order \(2\nu\), an orbit \(\{\rho\varphi_p^s:s\in\mathbb Z/2\nu\}\) of \(\varphi_p\) on \(\Phi\) is an orbit with its positions \(s\) in the sense of [Adr]; its even positions are the places \(\rho\operatorname{Frob}_p^s\), its odd positions the places \(\rho\operatorname{Frob}_p^sc\). Chapter 37 works along these orbits.

Problem 35.1.3. For \(q=11\), \(d=1319\), \(n=4\), the orbit of the place \((0,[1])\) is \((0,[1]),(0,[3]),(0,[7]),(0,[5])\) for \(p=13\), and \((0,[1]),(1,[7])\) for \(p=23\). For \(p=17\), \(\operatorname{Frob}_p=c\).

In the Lean formalization 1. The field is a record Host with required properties (Galois group, CM type cmType, Frobenius elements), not a field given by generators; for \(\zeta\) it is the field \(E\) above, by an inference from the definitions in [Rub, §9, Remark (1)]. Tops are Host.TT, top and thick places Host.topPlaces and Host.thickPlaces, the translate Host.u0, the classes of primes primeClass (splitK, signal, inertClass).

§35.2. Ranks and hermitian spaces

The construction has three integers \(k\), \(m\), \(t_0\), subject to \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:ranks}\tag{1} \begin{gathered} k,m\ \text{even},\qquad t_0\ \text{odd},\qquad 2\le m,\qquad m+3\le k,\\ k+m+4\le t_0,\qquad 3m<t_0,\qquad m\le f\le k,\qquad m^2\le N_m, \end{gathered} \end{equation}\] where \(N_j:=k+j(t_0+1)\) for \(0\le j\le m\). Put \(r:=k+m-1\). In the argument \(m\approx x^{1/2}\) is the number of lifts and \(k\approx x^{9/2}\) (Chapter 58); \(t_0\) is a power of \(x\) as well [Rub, §3]. Hermitian spaces are over \(E\); at each \(\rho\in\Phi\) a hermitian space has a signature, the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues of \(\rho\) applied to a Gram matrix. The construction uses the following spaces.

  • \(X_j\) for \(0\le j\le m\), positive definite of rank \(N_j\).

  • A vector \(z\in X_m\) with \(\langle z,z\rangle\ne0\), and \(X':=z^\perp\subset X_m\).

  • \(Y_j\) for \(0\le j\le m\), of rank \(r+jt_0\) and signature \(\bigl(r+jt_0-\lfloor\frac{m-j}2\rfloor,\lfloor\frac{m-j}2\rfloor\bigr)\) at every \(\rho\); isometric embeddings \(X_j\to Y_j\) for \(j<m\), and an isometry \(X'\to Y_m\).

  • A decomposition \(Y_0=X_0\perp Ez_0\perp P_1\perp\dots\perp P_{m/2-1}\) into the image of \(X_0\), a line with \(\langle z_0,z_0\rangle=-T\) for a prime \(T\) (Section 35.5), and hyperbolic planes \(P_i\).

  • \(W_j\) for \(1\le j\le m\), of rank \(r\) and signature \((k+j-1,m-j)\) at every \(\rho\), isometric to \(Y_0\) over \(F_v\) for every finite place \(v\) of \(F\).

The forms of Section 35.4 live on the definite groups \(U(X_j)\); the spaces \(Y_j\) and \(W_j\) carry the periods (Chapters 43 and 47).

Problem 35.2.1. For \(n=4\) the smallest ranks allowed by (1) are \((m,k,t_0)=(2,8,15)\). Then \(r=9\) and \(N_0,N_1,N_2=8,24,40\).

Problem 35.2.2. Under (1), \(r\) is odd, every \(N_j\) is even, \(N_m=(r+mt_0)+1\), and \(\operatorname{rk}Y_j-N_j=m-1-j\).

Problem 35.2.3. For \(j<m\) the orthogonal complement of \(X_j\) in \(Y_j\) has rank \(m-1-j\) and signature \(\bigl(\lceil\frac{m-j}2\rceil-1,\lfloor\frac{m-j}2\rfloor\bigr)\) at every \(\rho\). For \(0\le j\le m\), \(U(Y_j)\) is compact at the archimedean places exactly when \(j\ge m-1\).

The codimension \(m-1-j\) is odd for even \(j\) and even for odd \(j\); accordingly the periods between \(U(X_j)\) and \(U(Y_j)\) are Bessel periods for even \(j\) and Fourier–Jacobi periods for odd \(j\) [Zh]. At \(j=m\) the inclusion reverses: \(Y_m\cong X'\) has codimension one in \(X_m\).

Problem 35.2.4. The space \(Y_0\) of (S4) has rank \(r\) and signature \((k+\frac m2-1,\frac m2)\), as (S3) requires; this is the signature of \(W_{m/2}\). The space \(W_m\) is positive definite.

Problem 35.2.5. For \((m,k,t_0)=(2,8,15)\) the spaces \(Y_0,Y_1,Y_2\) have ranks \(9,24,39\) and signatures \((8,1),(24,0),(39,0)\); \(W_1,W_2\) have signatures \((8,1),(9,0)\); there are no hyperbolic planes in (S4).

The local condition in (S5) constrains the signatures. An isometry \(g\) multiplies the Gram determinant by the norm \(\det g\,\overline{\det g}\). So if \(W_j\) and \(Y_0\) are isometric at every finite place of \(F\), the quotient \(\delta\in F^\times\) of their Gram determinants is a norm from \(E_v\) at every finite \(v\). By Hilbert’s reciprocity law for the quadratic extension \(E/F\), \(\delta\) is then negative at an even number of real places of \(F\).

Problem 35.2.6. The sign of \(\delta\) at the real place of \(F\) below \(\rho\) is \((-1)^{m/2-j}\), the same at all \(f\) places. Since \(f\) is even, the parity condition holds for every \(j\).

The spaces are data of the records of the formalization, which requires (S1)–(S5) of them and constructs such records at every large scale (Section 35.5); [Rub, Hyp. O12] assumes them.

§35.3. Weights, nodes and words

At each place \(\rho\) the construction fixes numbers \(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_k\in\mathbb Z+\frac12\) and integers \(\xi'_1,\dots,\xi'_{k-1}\), \(\theta_1,\dots,\theta_m\), \(w_1,\dots,w_m\), all depending on \(\rho\), with \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:H}\tag{2} \xi_1>\xi'_1>\xi_2>\dots>\xi'_{k-1}>\xi_k>-\theta_1,\qquad w_j-\theta_j\ge\tfrac{t_0+3}2,\qquad \theta_{j+1}-w_j\ge\tfrac{t_0+3}2 . \end{equation}\] The weight of level \(j\) at \(\rho\) is \(\lambda^{(j)}=\lambda^{(j)}(\rho)\in\mathbb Z^{N_j}\): first \(\lambda^{(j)}_i=\xi_i+i-\frac{k+1}2\) for \(1\le i\le k\), then, for \(l=1,\dots,j\), a block of \(t_0+1\) entries equal to \(-w_l+\frac k2+(2l-1)\frac{t_0+1}2\). The numbers \(w_j(\rho)\) are the nodes. For a weight \(\lambda\) of rank \(N'\) the shifted numbers \(\lambda_i+\frac{N'+1}2-i\) are its Harish-Chandra parameters.

Problem 35.3.1. Under (2) every \(\lambda^{(j)}\) is integral and nonincreasing.

Problem 35.3.2. The Harish-Chandra parameters of \(\lambda^{(j)}\) are \(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_k\) together with the blocks \(B_l:=\{-w_l+\frac{t_0}2-c:0\le c\le t_0\}\), \(l\le j\), all translated by \(j\frac{t_0+1}2\). Thus the \(j\)-th lift keeps the old parameters, up to a common translation, and adds the block of \(t_0+1\) consecutive numbers centred at \(-w_j\).

At the top place \(\rho_t=(0,t)\) of a top \(t\) there are integers \(a_t\), the top weights, and \(b_t\), with \(\xi_1(\rho_t)=a_t+\frac{k-1}2\) and \(\xi_k(\rho_t)=-b_t-\frac{k-1}2\), where \(a\le a_t\le a(1+x^{-13}/k)\) and \(b'\le b_t\le(1+x^{-2})b'\) [Adr, Def. 18.5].

Problem 35.3.3. \(\lambda^{(0)}(\rho_t)\) begins with \(a_t\) and ends with \(-b_t\).

So the top weight \(a_t\approx xb\) is the length of the first row of the weight at the top place. The nodes lie in windows of a common width \(\Delta\): \(b\le w_j\le b+\Delta+1\) at thick places, \(b_t+b'/x\le w_j\le b_t+b'/x+\Delta\) at the top place of \(t\), and \(w''\le w_j\le w''+\Delta\) at thin places, where \(w'':=b'/(xn)\). This is the thick–thin condition (2) of Chapter 4, and the nodes are the weights \(w_j\colon\Phi\to(0,\infty)\) of the prime sums \(P_{t,w_j}(a_t)\) of Chapter 58. At places other than top places \(\xi_1-\xi'_1\) and all \(\xi'_i\) are bounded by a constant multiple of a size parameter \(S_c\) [Rub, Hyp. O24].

The words vary the middle of the parameter. There is an integer \(\Delta_\mathcal W\ge1\) with \(\xi'_l-\xi'_{l+1}=4\Delta_\mathcal W\) at every place. A word \(u\) is a string of letters \(\epsilon_1,\dots,\epsilon_{k-2}\in\{0,1\}\); its parameters are \(\xi_1\), \(\xi_k\), the \(\xi'\), \(\theta\), \(w\) above, and \[\xi_{l+1}(u)=\xi'_l-\tfrac12-\epsilon_l\Delta_\mathcal W\qquad(1\le l\le k-2),\] the same letters at every place. A finite set \(\mathcal W\) of words with \(\lvert\mathcal W\rvert\le2^{k-3}\) is chosen; Chapter 43 explains why there must be many (Problem 43.4.5).

Problem 35.3.4. Every word satisfies (2), and distinct words have distinct weights \(\lambda^{(0)}(\rho)\) at every place.

By the branching rule for \(U(k)\supset U(k-1)\), whose condition is interlacing of Harish-Chandra parameters, the representations of all words at \(\rho\) contain the representation of \(U(k-1)\) with parameter \(\xi'\): the words are weights of rank \(k\) above one fixed weight of rank \(k-1\) [Rub, §6].

How does a lift change the number of forms? Work in the model of Chapter 21, with one place and \(\mathbb Z[\mathrm i]\) in place of \(\mathcal O_E\). For a nonincreasing \(\lambda\in\mathbb Z^{N'}\) write \(z_i=\lambda_i+N'-i\), so that \(D_{N'}(z)\) is its Weyl dimension; this depends only on the differences of the \(\lambda_i\).

Problem 35.3.5. Let \(\lambda\in\mathbb Z^{N'}\) be nonincreasing, \(c\le\lambda_{N'}\) an integer, and \(\lambda^+\) the weight \(\lambda\) followed by \(t_0+1\) entries \(c\), with \(z^+\) defined in rank \(N'+t_0+1\). Then \[V_{N'+t_0+1}(z^+)=V_{N'}(z)\prod_{c'=1}^{t_0}c'!\ \prod_{i=1}^{N'}\prod_{l=1}^{t_0+1}\bigl(\lambda_i-c+N'-i+l\bigr).\]

Problem 35.3.6. In the situation of Problem 35.3.5, with the masses \(M_\nu\) of Chapter 21, \[\frac{M_{N'+t_0+1}D_{N'+t_0+1}(z^+)}{M_{N'}D_{N'}(z)} =\prod_{c'=1}^{t_0}c'!\ \prod_{i=1}^{N'}\prod_{l=1}^{t_0+1}\bigl(\lambda_i-c+N'-i+l\bigr)\prod_{\nu=N'+1}^{N'+t_0+1}\pi^{-\nu}\kappa_\nu .\] For \(\lambda=(3,1)\), \(t_0=1\), \(c=0\) this ratio is \(5/64\).

So the count of forms changes at each lift by a product of differences among the new parameters and between them and the old ones; the factor \(\prod_{c'}c'!\) is the Vandermonde product of the new block, and no factorial from the denominators of the Weyl formula is left over. Over \(E\) the Weyl factor is a product over \(\Phi\), and the mass ratio is an exact formula for unramified CM fields, used in Chapter 47.

§35.4. Forms and the lifts

For each level \(j\) the construction has a finite set \(\operatorname{Cl}_j\) of lattices in \(X_j\), representatives of the classes of a genus with level structure, and their finite stabilizers \(\Gamma_\Lambda\). A form of level \(j\) assigns to \(\Lambda\in\operatorname{Cl}_j\) a vector of the representation of weight \((\lambda^{(j)}(\rho))_{\rho\in\Phi}\) fixed by \(\Gamma_\Lambda\), with the norm \(\sum_\Lambda\lvert\Gamma_\Lambda\rvert^{-1}\lVert\varphi(\Lambda)\rVert^2\), as in Section 21.3 of Chapter 21. The \(j\)-th lift \(K_j\), from level \(j-1\) to level \(j\), is \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:lift}\tag{3} (K_j\varphi)(\Lambda')=\sum_{\Lambda\in\operatorname{Cl}_{j-1}}\frac{\epsilon_j(\Lambda,\Lambda')}{\lvert\Gamma_\Lambda\rvert}\,\mathbf K_j(\Lambda,\Lambda')\,\varphi(\Lambda),\qquad\lvert\epsilon_j(\Lambda,\Lambda')\rvert=1 . \end{equation}\] This is (1) of Chapter 23 with unit scalars. The block \(\mathbf K_j(\Lambda,\Lambda')\) is built from Taylor coefficients at \(0\) of a theta function of the lattice \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_E}(\Lambda,\Lambda')\), shifted by a fractional ideal and averaged over Heisenberg translates at the level primes, on \(V=\prod_{\rho\in\Phi}M_{N_j\times N_{j-1}}(\mathbb C)\) [Adr, Def. 11.10]; this is the kernel of the lift. The chain is the composite \(K_m\cdots K_1\). Let \(D_\Gamma\) be a common multiple of the orders \(\lvert\Gamma_\Lambda\rvert\), \(\Lambda\in\operatorname{Cl}_j\), \(j<m\).

Problem 35.4.1. \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_E}(\Lambda,\Lambda')\) is a free \(\mathbb Z\)-module of rank \(2fN_{j-1}N_j\), and its theta function is a function of \(fN_{j-1}N_j\) complex variables; for \((m,k,t_0)=(2,8,15)\) and \(j=1\) there are \(1536\) of them.

Problem 35.4.2. \((K_m\cdots K_1\varphi)(\Lambda_m)\) is the sum over \(\Lambda_j\in\operatorname{Cl}_j\), \(j<m\), of \(\prod_{j<m}\lvert\Gamma_{\Lambda_j}\rvert^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^m\epsilon_j(\Lambda_{j-1},\Lambda_j)\cdot\mathbf K_m(\Lambda_{m-1},\Lambda_m)\cdots\mathbf K_1(\Lambda_0,\Lambda_1)\varphi(\Lambda_0)\). Hence \(D_\Gamma^m\) clears the stabilizer denominators of the chain.

In [Adr] the lifts are [Adr, Def. 11.10], and \(D_\Gamma\) may be taken to be Minkowski’s bound \(M(2fN_m)\) for the orders of finite subgroups of \(\mathrm{GL}_{2fN_m}(\mathbb Z)\) [Adr, Prop. 11.14]; see Problem 37.3.1 of Chapter 37. The integrality of the kernels needed there rests in [Adr] on the hypotheses (N1)–(N3) of Chapter 37, assumed there; the formalization proves the final theorem without assuming them.

In the Lean formalization 4. The chain is ColumnChain: classes cls, stabilizers stab, weights wt\({}=1/\lvert\)stab\(\rvert\), the common multiple DGam with dvd_DGam, the block Kmap\({}=\)rhoDet\(\cdot\)rhoDet'\(\cdot\)(divDe\(\circ\)unf\(\circ\)sharp), the lift K and the composite col. Here rhoDet, rhoDet' are the unit scalars, and sharp, divDe rescale coordinates by powers with exponents \(w_j-N_j/2\) and \(w_j-N_{j-1}/2\). The block unf is abstract; its relation to theta functions is the separate statement ThetaX, which we have not followed. The forms of level \(0\) are Mu0, and the inner product is MemberForms.ipC.

§35.5. Terminal primes, lattices and period maps

Each word \(u\in\mathcal W\) has its own lattice classes and lifts, and a terminal prime \(T_u=\varpi_u\bar\varpi_u\) with \(\varpi_u\in\mathcal O_K\) and \(\varpi_u\equiv1\) modulo \(Nqd(g_0g_1)^2\), where \(g_0,g_1\) are the level primes (Chapter 58). The prime \(T\) of (S4) is \(T_{u_0}\) for a base word \(u_0\). The terminal primes are chosen in windows fixed by dimensions of spaces of forms, so that \(\lvert\log T_u-\log T_{u'}\rvert=O(\log k)\) [Adr, Def. 18.9 (K7)]; Chapter 47 computes their size. (Chapter 58 calls \(T_u\) the auxiliary prime.)

Problem 35.5.1. \(T_u\equiv1\pmod{Nqd}\), so \(T_u\) splits completely in \(E\). There are \(f\) primes of \(F\) above \(T_u\); each has residue field \(\mathbb F_{T_u}\) and splits in \(E\).

At each prime \(v\) of \(F\) above \(T_u\) the level structure of a lattice \(\Lambda\in\operatorname{Cl}_j\) contains an \(\mathbb F_{T_u}\)-vector space of dimension \(N_j\) with a nonzero linear form, and \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_{T_u}^\times\) acts on lattices with level structure, and on forms, by rescaling that form. A form of level \(0\) is ramified at \(T_u\) if \(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb F_{T_u}^\times}\alpha\cdot\varphi=0\) for each of the \(f\) primes \(v\).

The lattices are over the ring of integers of a coefficient field \(L\supset E\), a number field that is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\) and totally complex, with a fixed embedding in \(\mathbb C\) (Chapter 43 writes \(K\) for it). For a place \(w\) of \(L\) let \(\mathfrak p_w\), \(e_w\) be its prime and ramification index, and \(\ell_w:=\log N\mathfrak p_w/[L:\mathbb Q]\), as in Chapter 52.

  • The base lattice \(M_u\) is an \(\mathcal O_L\)-lattice spanning an \(L\)-structure of the space of forms of level \(0\) of the word \(u\), with the norm of Section 35.4 at the fixed embedding. Let \(M_u^{\rm ram}\) be its intersection with the ramified forms, of rank \(d_u\).

  • An \(\mathcal O_L\)-lattice \(N'_u\) of rank \(d_u\) in \(M_u^{\rm ram}\otimes L\), with the same norms, such that \(N'_{u,w}\subseteq M^{\rm ram}_{u,w}\) at every \(w\nmid T_u\), with equality above the primes that are not inert in \(K\) and above the inert primes at which no gain is taken; and the source lattice \(N_u:=I_uN'_u\), with \(I_u:=\prod_w\mathfrak p_w^{-\lfloor e_w\sigma_u(w)\rfloor}\) for numbers \(\sigma_u(w)\ge0\), the gains, nonzero at finitely many \(w\).

  • The top lattice \(R\), an \(\mathcal O_L\)-lattice spanning an \(L\)-structure of a finite-dimensional space \(\mathcal E\) of automorphic forms, the same for all words, and its dual \(R^\vee:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_L}(R,\mathcal O_L)\subset\operatorname{Hom}_L(R\otimes L,L)\), whose unit lattices are the dual unit lattices of Chapter 6.

  • The period map \(B_u\colon N_u\otimes L\to\operatorname{Hom}_L(R\otimes L,L)\). Over \(\mathbb C\), \(B_u(\varphi)(G)\) lifts \(\varphi\) along the chain \(K_m\cdots K_1\), restricts the result to the lattices of \(X'=z^\perp\), projects it to one weight of rank \(N_m-1\), and pairs it with the form on \(X'\cong Y_m\) attached to \(G\in\mathcal E\).

The record requires \(B_u(N_u)\subseteq R^\vee\) and \(h(B_u)\le h_u\) for numbers \(h_u\) (Chapter 43). Chapter 37 proves the inclusion, with the gains \(\sigma_u(w)\) at the inert primes; Chapter 43 identifies \(B_u\) with a Bessel period; Chapter 47 bounds \(h_u\). At the places above \(T_u\) the formalization imposes a separate condition on \(N'_u\), and it defines \(\mathcal E\) and \(R\) place by place from integral structures that depend on twisting data; we have not traced either, nor the projection in (B).

Problem 35.5.2. Let \(N'\subseteq M\) be \(\mathcal O_L\)-lattices of rank \(d\) in one space, with the same archimedean norms, and \(\operatorname{len}_w\) the length of \((M/N')\otimes\mathcal O_{L,w}\). Then \(\mu(N')=\mu(M)-\frac1d\sum_w2\ell_w\operatorname{len}_w\).

Problem 35.5.3. For integers \(n_w\), almost all zero, \(\mu\bigl(\prod_w\mathfrak p_w^{-n_w}N\bigr)=\mu(N)+\sum_w2\ell_wn_w\). If \(n_w=e_w\sigma_p\) for every \(w\mid p\), the contribution of \(p\) is \(2\sigma_p\log p\).

Combining Problems 35.5.2 and 35.5.3, \[\mu(N_u)=\mu(M_u^{\rm ram})-\frac1{d_u}\sum_{w\nmid T_u}2\ell_w\operatorname{len}_w(M_u^{\rm ram}/N'_u)+\sum_w2\ell_w\lfloor e_w\sigma_u(w)\rfloor\] when \(N'_{u,w}=M^{\rm ram}_{u,w}\) above \(T_u\). The last sum is the gain, about \(\sum_p2\sigma_p\log p\); the middle one is the loss at the inert primes. This is the shape of (A2) of Chapter 58, whose left side has \(\mu(M_u)\) in place of \(\mu(M_u^{\rm ram})\); we have not traced the comparison of these two slopes in the formalization.

Problem 35.5.4. \(B_u(N_u)\subseteq R^\vee\) if and only if \(B_u(\varphi)(y)\in\prod_w\mathfrak p_w^{\lfloor e_w\sigma_u(w)\rfloor}\) for all \(\varphi\in N'_u\) and \(y\in R\).

So the inclusion \(B_u(N_u)\subseteq R^\vee\) says that the periods of the lattice \(N'_u\) are divisible, at each place \(w\), by \(\mathfrak p_w^{\lfloor e_w\sigma_u(w)\rfloor}\); the gains are measured in units of \(v_p\).

The record.

For one scale and one translate, the objects \(\mathcal W\), \(T_u\), \(M_u\), \(N_u\), \(h_u\), \(R\), together with the local terms \(E_{u,j}\), the common local term \(\mathcal L\), the total gain \(\Gamma\) and the index loss, form a chain record [Adr, Def. 18.9] if they satisfy the eight conditions (K1)–(K8) listed there. These are the inequalities (A1)–(A7) of Chapter 58 with explicit errors. The formalization proves the final theorem; the corresponding step in [Adr], the existence of chain records for every admissible datum, is [Adr, Hyp. 18.10], assumed there. The formalization establishes records only for the data of one family class that it selects, which is weaker than the quantifiers of [Adr, Hyp. 18.10] and suffices for the final theorem.
