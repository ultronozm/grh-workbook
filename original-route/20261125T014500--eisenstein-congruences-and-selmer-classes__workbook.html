Prerequisites.

Modular forms of level \(1\), Eisenstein series, \(\Delta\) and the Hecke operators [SeA, Ch. VII]; Serre writes the weight as \(2k\) and numbers the Bernoulli numbers differently. Complete discrete valuation rings and Hensel’s lemma. The Chebotarev density theorem. From Chapter 29, Denominators and the Stickelberger element: the eigenspaces \(A^{(i)}\) of Section 29.2 and Theorem 29.2.2. From Chapter 30, Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists, with \(\ell = p\): the groups \(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\), \(G_p\), \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e)\), Problems 30.1.4 and 30.2.1, Theorem 30.1.1, and the Euler characteristic formula stated before Problem 30.1.8. From Chapter 31, Congruence modules: Problem 31.2.2. Galois representations attached to modular forms are stated in Section 32.3 with references.

Chapter 30 computed \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) for odd \(e\) from the theorem of Mazur and Wiles, which was used as a black box. How does one produce classes in \(\mathrm{Sha}\), and how does one bound them? Ribet’s answer to the first question [Ri] is that a cusp form congruent to an Eisenstein series gives a Galois representation that is reducible modulo \(p\), and a good choice of lattice turns it into an unramified class. Read in reverse, a class in \(\mathrm{Sha}\) is a tangent vector at the reducible “Eisenstein point”. This direction is the one that bounds \(\mathrm{Sha}\). We work with level \(1\), and the example to keep in mind is \(k = 12\), \(p = 691\). Throughout, \(p\) is an odd prime, \(\chi\) is the \(p\)-adic cyclotomic character and \(\omega\) is its reduction modulo \(p\).

§32.1. The congruence Tag 22UQ

For even \(k \ge 4\) let \(\sigma_{k-1}(n) := \sum_{d \mid n} d^{k-1}\), and normalize the Eisenstein series as \[E_k := 1 - \frac{2k}{B_k} \sum_{n \ge 1} \sigma_{k-1}(n)\, q^n ,\] so that \(E_k\) has rational coefficients and constant term \(1\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §4]. Let \(\Delta = q\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{24} = \sum \tau(n) q^n\). Since \(B_{12} = -691/2730\), the coefficients of \(E_{12}\) have \(691\) in the denominator. By Problem 31.2.2 of Chapter 31, \(691\,(E_4^3 - E_{12}) = 432000\,\Delta\).

Tag 22UR

Problem 32.1.1. \(\tau(n) \equiv \sigma_{11}(n) \pmod{691}\) for all \(n \ge 1\).

The same happens whenever \(p\) divides \(B_k/k\).

Tag 22US

Problem 32.1.2. Let \(k \ge 4\) be even, and let \(p\) divide the numerator of \(B_k/k\). There is a cusp form \(g\) of weight \(k\) and level \(1\) with coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\) such that \(a_n(g) \equiv \sigma_{k-1}(n) \pmod p\) for all \(n \ge 1\).

Tag 22UT

Problem 32.1.3. With \(g\) as in Problem 32.1.2, for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(n \ge 1\), \[a_n(T_\ell\, g) \equiv (1 + \ell^{k-1})\, a_n(g) \pmod p .\]

The cusp forms with coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\) form a lattice stable under the Hecke operators. By the lifting lemma of Deligne and Serre [DeS, Lemme 6.11], Problem 32.1.3 gives a normalized eigenform \(f\) of weight \(k\) and level \(1\), and a prime \(\lambda \mid p\) of the field generated by its coefficients, such that \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:congruence}\tag{1} a_\ell(f) \equiv 1 + \ell^{k-1} \pmod \lambda \qquad \text{for every prime } \ell . \end{equation}\] In particular \(a_p(f) \equiv 1\): \(f\) is ordinary at \(\lambda\). For \(k = 12\) we may take \(f = \Delta\), and \(\tau(691) \equiv 1 \pmod{691}\).

§32.2. Lattices Tag 22UU

A congruence between \(f\) and an Eisenstein series says that a Galois representation is reducible modulo \(\lambda\). Which reduction one sees depends on a lattice. Let \(K\) be a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) with ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}\), uniformizer \(\pi\), valuation \(v\) with \(v(\pi) = 1\), and residue field \(\mathbb{F}\). Let \(G\) be a profinite group and \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\) a continuous irreducible representation on a \(2\)-dimensional \(K\)-space. A lattice is a free \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule of rank \(2\) that spans \(V\). Suppose that for one, hence (by the Brauer–Nesbitt theorem [CR]) every, \(G\)-stable lattice \(L\), the semisimplification of \(L/\pi L\) is \(\chi_1 \oplus \chi_2\) with characters \(\chi_1 \ne \chi_2 \colon G \to \mathbb{F}^\times\).

Tag 22UV

Problem 32.2.1. \(G\)-stable lattices exist.

Tag 22UW

Problem 32.2.2. Choose \(\sigma_0 \in G\) with \(\chi_1(\sigma_0) \ne \chi_2(\sigma_0)\). Then \(\rho(\sigma_0)\) has eigenvalues \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\lambda_i \equiv \chi_i(\sigma_0) \pmod \pi\), and every \(G\)-stable lattice \(L\) has a basis \(e_1, e_2\) with \(\rho(\sigma_0) e_i = \lambda_i e_i\).

Fix such \(L\), \(e_1\), \(e_2\), write \(\rho(\sigma) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a(\sigma) & b(\sigma) \\ c(\sigma) & d(\sigma) \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) in this basis, and put \(m := \min_\sigma v(b(\sigma))\), \(n := \min_\sigma v(c(\sigma))\). By irreducibility \(b\) and \(c\) are not identically zero, so \(m, n\) are finite. For \(r \in \mathbb{Z}\) let \(L_r := \pi^r \mathcal{O}e_1 \oplus \mathcal{O}e_2\).

Tag 22UX

Problem 32.2.3. \(L_r\) is \(G\)-stable if and only if \(-n \le r \le m\), and every \(G\)-stable lattice is homothetic to exactly one \(L_r\): the stable lattices form a segment of length \(m+n\) in the tree of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(K)\).

Tag 22UY

Problem 32.2.4. \(m + n \ge 1\). On \(L_m/\pi L_m\) the group \(G\) acts by \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \chi_1 & * \\ 0 & \chi_2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), a nonsplit extension; on \(L_{-n}/\pi L_{-n}\) it acts by a nonsplit \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \chi_1 & 0 \\ * & \chi_2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), with sub \(\chi_2\) and quotient \(\chi_1\); and for \(-n < r < m\) it acts by \(\chi_1 \oplus \chi_2\).

Problems 32.2.1–32.2.4 prove the following. It is due to Ribet [Ri, Prop. 2.1].

Tag 22UZ

Theorem 32.2.1 (Ribet’s lemma). There is a \(G\)-stable lattice on whose reduction \(G\) acts by a nonsplit extension \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \chi_1 & * \\ 0 & \chi_2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\).

§32.3. The unramified class Tag 22V2

We state three inputs. Let \(f\) be a normalized eigenform of weight \(k\) and level \(1\), let \(\lambda \mid p\) be a prime of the field of its coefficients, and let \(K\) be the completion at \(\lambda\).

  • There is a continuous \(\rho_f \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(K)\) with \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_f(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell) = a_\ell(f)\) and \(\det \rho_f = \chi^{k-1}\), for every prime \(\ell \ne p\) [De].

  • \(\rho_f\) is irreducible [Ri2].

  • If \(a_p(f)\) is a \(\lambda\)-adic unit, then \(\rho_f|_{G_p}\) stabilizes a line on which the inertia group \(I_p\) acts by \(\chi^{k-1}\), and \(G_p\) acts on the quotient by an unramified character [Wi, Thm. 2.1.4].

Chapter 34, Black boxes: Galois representations and local–global compatibility, states Galois representations of this kind as black boxes, over CM fields. Its Section 34.4 shows in the simplest case, the eigenform \(f\) of weight \(2\) and level \(11\), where \(\rho_f\) comes from: it is the rational \(p\)-adic Tate module of \(J_0(11)\), and the Eichler–Shimura relation gives the analogue of (D).

From now on \(k\) is even with \(4 \le k \le p - 3\), \(p\) divides \(B_k\) (equivalently, since \(k < p\), the numerator of \(B_k/k\)), and \(f\), \(\lambda\) are as in (1).

Tag 22V3

Problem 32.3.1. The semisimplification of \(\rho_f\) modulo \(\lambda\) is \(1 \oplus \omega^{k-1}\), and \(1 \ne \omega^{k-1}\) on \(G_p\), and even on \(I_p\).

Apply Theorem 32.2.1 with \(\chi_1 = 1\) and \(\chi_2 = \omega^{k-1}\), and let \(\bar\rho = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \bar b \\ 0 & \omega^{k-1} \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) be the resulting reduction.

Tag 22V4

Problem 32.3.2. \(\kappa(\sigma) := \bar b(\sigma)\,\omega^{1-k}(\sigma)\) is a cocycle on \(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\) with values in \(\mathbb{F}(\omega^{1-k})\), and its class is not zero.

Tag 22V5

Problem 32.3.3. The class of \(\kappa\) vanishes on \(G_p\).

Since \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \mathbb{F}(\omega^{1-k})) = H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \mathbb{Z}/p(1-k)) \otimes_{\mathbb{F}_p} \mathbb{F}\), and similarly for \(G_p\), Problems 32.3.1–32.3.3, Problem 30.1.4 and Theorem 30.1.1 prove the following.

Tag 22V6

Theorem 32.3.1 (Ribet). Let \(k\) be even with \(4 \le k \le p-3\), and suppose that \(p \mid B_k\). Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \ne 0\), and \(A^{(p-k)} \ne 0\).

This is the converse to Herbrand’s theorem [Ri]; see also [Wa, Thm. 6.18]. For \(k = 12\) and \(p = 691\) the input is \(\Delta\), and the conclusion is that \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{691})\) has an unramified extension of degree \(691\) on which \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{691})/\mathbb{Q})\) acts through \(\omega^{-11} = \omega^{679}\). The proof gives \(\mathrm{Sha}\ne 0\) and no more. Congruences with Eisenstein series are measured by the Eisenstein ideal of the Hecke algebra, which Mazur introduced in weight \(2\) and prime level [Ma]. Mazur and Wiles refined Ribet’s method to show that the length of \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1)\) is at least \(v_p(B_k/k)\); the analytic class number formula gives the reverse inequality [MW].

§32.4. Tangent vectors at the Eisenstein point Tag 22V7

Ribet goes from a modular form to a class. Alpöge’s argument needs the reverse: a bound for \(\mathrm{Sha}\) from the modular side. What is a class of \(\mathrm{Sha}\) from the point of view of Galois representations? Let \(\rho_0 := 1 \oplus \omega^{k-1} \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\), the reduction of the representation \(1 \oplus \chi^{k-1}\) of the Eisenstein series \(E_k\); we call it the Eisenstein point. Let \(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon]\) be the ring with \(\varepsilon^2 = 0\). A lift of \(\rho_0\) is a continuous homomorphism \(\rho_\varepsilon \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon])\) that reduces to \(\rho_0\) modulo \(\varepsilon\). Let \(\operatorname{ad}\rho_0\) be the space \(M_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\) with \(\sigma\) acting by conjugation by \(\rho_0(\sigma)\). See [Mz] for the general theory.

Tag 22V8

Problem 32.4.1. Writing \(\rho_\varepsilon = (1 + \varepsilon M)\rho_0\) identifies the lifts of \(\rho_0\), up to conjugation by matrices \(1 + \varepsilon X\), with \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \operatorname{ad}\rho_0)\).

The torus \(T = \{\mathrm{diag}(1, t)\}\) commutes with \(\rho_0\), and acts on lifts by conjugation.

Tag 22V9

Problem 32.4.2. \(\operatorname{ad}\rho_0 = \mathbb{F}_p E_{11} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p E_{22} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{1-k}) E_{12} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{k-1}) E_{21}\), where \(E_{ij}\) are the matrix units, and \(T\) acts on these four summands, and on their \(H^1\), with weights \(0\), \(0\), \(-1\), \(+1\).

A cocycle \(\kappa\) with nonzero class in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{1-k}))\) gives the nonsplit \(\rho_\kappa = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \kappa\omega^{k-1} \\ 0 & \omega^{k-1} \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). For \(t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\), conjugating by \(\mathrm{diag}(t,1)\) replaces \(\kappa\) by \(t\kappa\), so all the \(\rho_{t\kappa}\) are isomorphic; at \(t = 0\) one reaches \(\rho_0\), and to first order at \(t = 0\) the family is the lift \((1 + \varepsilon\kappa E_{12})\rho_0\), of weight \(-1\). This is the sense in which a class is a tangent vector at the Eisenstein point.

Which lifts are allowed? By (O), \(\rho_f\) has a line on which \(I_p\) acts by \(\chi^{k-1}\) with unramified quotient. Call a lift \(\rho_\varepsilon\) ordinary if \(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon]^2\) has a \(G_p\)-stable direct summand \(L\) of rank \(1\), reducing to \(\mathbb{F}_p e_2\), on which \(I_p\) acts by \(\omega^{k-1}\), with \(I_p\) acting trivially on the quotient. Let \(t_{\mathrm{ord}} \subseteq H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \operatorname{ad}\rho_0)\) be the set of classes of ordinary lifts, and \(t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(j)}\) its part of weight \(j\).

Tag 22VA

Problem 32.4.3. The lift with \(M = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} \alpha_1 & \beta \\ \gamma & \alpha_2 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) is ordinary if and only if \(\alpha_1\) and \(\alpha_2\) vanish on \(I_p\) and the class of \(\beta\) vanishes in \(H^1(G_p, \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{1-k}))\). Hence \(t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(0)} = 0\), and \(t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(+1)} = H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{k-1}))\).

With Problem 30.1.4 and Theorem 30.1.1, Problems 32.4.1–32.4.3 prove the following.

Tag 22VB

Theorem 32.4.1. \(t_{\mathrm{ord}} = t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(-1)} \oplus t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(+1)}\), and \(t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(-1)} \cong \mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1)[p] \cong \operatorname{Hom}(A^{(p-k)}, \mathbb{F}_p)\).

Tag 22VC

Problem 32.4.4. \(\dim t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(+1)} \ge 1\).

The Hecke algebra sees a representation only through its traces.

Tag 22VD

Problem 32.4.5. Every ordinary lift of \(\rho_0\) to \(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon]\) has trace \(1 + \omega^{k-1}\).

The traces see \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) only through their products. Return to \(\rho_f\) with the basis \(e_1, e_2\) of Problem 32.2.2, with \(\chi_1 = 1\) and \(\chi_2 = \omega^{k-1}\), and let \(I \subseteq \mathcal{O}\) be the ideal generated by the products \(b(\sigma)c(\tau)\), \(\sigma, \tau \in G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\). Following Bellaïche and Chenevier [BC, Ch. 1], we call it the reducibility ideal.

Tag 22VE

Problem 32.4.6. \(I = \pi^{m+n}\mathcal{O}\), and \(a\) and \(d\) are characters of \(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\) modulo \(I\).

Tag 22VF

Problem 32.4.7. \(a \equiv 1\) and \(d \equiv \chi^{k-1}\) modulo \(I\). Hence \(v(a_\ell(f) - 1 - \ell^{k-1}) \ge m + n\) for every prime \(\ell \ne p\).

Tag 22VG

Problem 32.4.8. For \(f = \Delta\) and \(p = 691\), \(m + n = 1\): up to homothety \(\rho_\Delta\) has exactly two stable lattices. Moreover \(v_{691}(\tau(\ell) - 1 - \ell^{11}) = 1\) for every prime \(\ell \le 37\).

Thus the trace differences \(a_\ell(f) - 1 - \ell^{k-1}\) bound how far \(f\) lies from the Eisenstein point. To bound \(\mathrm{Sha}\) one bounds the weight \(-1\) tangent space of an ordinary deformation ring through \(\rho_0\) by the trace differences at its points. This requires knowing that all the points are modular, an automorphy theorem for residually reducible representations. For \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), such theorems are due to Skinner and Wiles [SW]. For \(k = 12\) and \(p = 691\) the two sides agree: \(\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q}, 11)\) has length \(1\) by Theorem 30.1.2, and \(v_{691}(\tau(2) - 1 - 2^{11}) = 1\). In the reconstruction [Adr] a class of \(\mathrm{Sha}\) gives, as here, a tangent vector of weight \(-1\) at an Eisenstein point of rank three, for a unitary group in three variables (the setting of [BC]), and the weight \(-1\) cotangent module is bounded by trace differences. The automorphy statement needed there is (G3) of [Adr]; its status is recorded in Section 33.4 of Chapter 33.

§32.5. From the CM field to characters Tag 22VH

The fields of the argument are abelian CM fields \(E\), not \(\mathbb{Q}\). A class over \(E\) becomes a class over \(\mathbb{Q}\) twisted by a character, as in Problem 30.2.1, now with \(E\) ramified anywhere.

Let \(E\) be an abelian number field with \(\Gamma := \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) of order prime to \(p\). Let \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mu_{\lvert\Gamma\rvert}]\), let \(W := W_e \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\) with \(W_e = \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p(-e)\) as in Chapter 30, and let \(G_E \subseteq G_\mathbb{Q}\) be the absolute Galois groups. Let \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}\) be the group of classes in \(H^1(G_E, W)\) that vanish on the inertia group \(I_w\) of every place \(w \nmid p\) and on the decomposition group \(D_w\) of every \(w \mid p\). Inflation–restriction shows that \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}= \mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\), with \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)\) as in Chapter 30. For a character \(\psi \colon \Gamma \to \mathcal{O}^\times\), let \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e; \psi)_\mathcal{O}\) be defined in the same way from \(H^1(G_\mathbb{Q}, W \otimes \psi^{-1})\). Let \(\Gamma\) act on \(\mathcal{O}[\Gamma]\) by left multiplication, so that \(G_\mathbb{Q}\) acts on it through \(\Gamma\), and let \(\gamma \in \Gamma\) act on \(H^1(G_E, W)\) by \((\gamma c)(h) := \tilde\gamma\, c(\tilde\gamma^{-1} h \tilde\gamma)\), for any lift \(\tilde\gamma \in G_\mathbb{Q}\) of \(\gamma\).

Tag 22VJ

Problem 32.5.1. \(H^1(G_E, W) \cong H^1(G_\mathbb{Q}, \mathcal{O}[\Gamma] \otimes_\mathcal{O}W)\), and \(\mathcal{O}[\Gamma] \otimes_\mathcal{O}W \cong \bigoplus_\psi W \otimes \psi\) as \(G_\mathbb{Q}\)-modules, the sum over all characters \(\psi\) of \(\Gamma\).

Tag 22VK

Problem 32.5.2. Let \(v\) be a prime of \(\mathbb{Q}\), and \(D = D_v\) or \(I_v\). A class of \(H^1(G_E, W)\) vanishes on \(D_w\), respectively on \(I_w\), for every place \(w \mid v\) of \(E\) if and only if its image under Problem 32.5.1 vanishes on \(D\).

Tag 22VL

Problem 32.5.3. \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}\cong \bigoplus_\psi \mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e; \psi)_\mathcal{O}\). The summand \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e; \psi)_\mathcal{O}\), which comes from the summand \(W \otimes \psi^{-1}\) of Problem 32.5.1, is the part on which \(\Gamma\) acts through \(\psi\).

So a class in the \(\psi\)-part of \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}\) that is killed by \(p\) is the image of a class in \(H^1(G_\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{F}(\omega^{-e}\bar\psi^{-1}))\), with \(\mathbb{F}\) the residue field of \(\mathcal{O}\), as in Problem 30.1.4. As in §32.4, this is a tangent vector of weight \(-1\) at the Eisenstein point \(1 \oplus \bar\psi\,\omega^{e}\). On the minus part each summand is bounded by a generalized Bernoulli number, by the main conjecture for abelian fields [MW]; over \(\mathbb{Q}\) this is Theorem 30.1.2, and for \(E \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\) the classes killed by \(p\) are given by Theorem 29.2.2 through Problem 30.2.2. On the plus part the bound must come from the trace differences at automorphic points, as in Problem 32.4.7.
