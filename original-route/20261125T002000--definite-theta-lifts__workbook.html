Prerequisites.

Hermitian forms over the Gaussian integers, at the level of definitions. The classification of positive definite unimodular \(\mathbb{Z}\)-lattices of rank at most \(8\) [CoS, Ch. 16]. Schur functors and the Weyl dimension formula for \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})\) [FH, Lecture 6, Theorem 6.3]. The values of Dirichlet \(L\)-functions at negative integers [Wa, Theorem 4.2], and the functional equations of \(\zeta(s)\) and \(L(s,\chi)\) [Dav, Ch. 8, 9]. Stirling’s formula. No earlier chapter is used.

In the argument, the lattices \(L_j\) of the chain of Chapter 8 (Section 8.4) lie in spaces of forms on unitary groups, and the maps \(\varphi_j\) are theta lifts between them. We take definite unitary groups over \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) as a model. A definite unitary group has finitely many lattice classes in a genus, and its automorphic forms are vectors attached to these classes. How many forms are there? The answer is the product of a mass and a Weyl dimension, and both factors carry \(\prod_{j<n} j!\), which cancels. The theta lifts between these spaces are the subject of Chapter 23, Definite theta lifts and their norms; Chapter 22 first evaluates the genus averages they need.

§21.1. Hermitian lattices over \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) Tag 22L2

Let \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\). A Hermitian lattice of rank \(n\) is a free \(\mathcal{O}\)-module \(L\) of rank \(n\) with a positive definite Hermitian form \(h \colon L \times L \to \mathcal{O}\), linear in the first variable. Its dual is \(L^\sharp := \{y \in L \otimes_\mathcal{O}K : h(x,y) \in \mathcal{O}\text{ for all } x \in L\}\), and \(L\) is unimodular if \(L^\sharp = L\). The basic example is \(I_n := \mathcal{O}^n\) with \(h(x,y) = \sum_k x_k \bar y_k\). Let \(\Gamma_L\) be the group of \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear automorphisms of \(L\) preserving \(h\); it is finite, since it permutes the finitely many vectors of bounded length that span \(L\).

Two Hermitian lattices are in the same genus if they become isometric over \(\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_p\) for every prime \(p\). Let \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) be the set of isometry classes in the genus of \(I_n\). By Problem 21.1.1 below, a class gives a unimodular \(\mathbb{Z}\)-lattice of rank \(2n\) together with an automorphism of it; there are finitely many such lattices up to isometry [Ca, Ch. 9], each with a finite automorphism group, so \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) is finite. It is the Shimura variety, of dimension zero, of the definite unitary group of \(I_n\); we call it the class set. Its mass is \[M_n := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \frac{1}{\lvert \Gamma_L \rvert}, \qquad M_0 := 1 .\] What do the class sets look like in small rank?

Tag 22L3

Problem 21.1.1. Let \(L\) be a Hermitian lattice and \(B(x,y) := \operatorname{Re} h(x,y)\). Then \(B\) is a positive definite symmetric \(\mathbb{Z}\)-bilinear form with \(B(x,x) = h(x,x)\), multiplication by \(\mathrm{i}\) preserves \(B\), and \(h(x,y) = B(x,y) + \mathrm{i}B(x, \mathrm{i}y)\). The dual of \(L\) for \(B\) is \(L^\sharp\); in particular \(L\) is unimodular exactly when \((L, B)\) is.

Tag 22L4

Problem 21.1.2. \(\Gamma_{I_n}\) is the group of monomial matrices whose nonzero entries lie in \(\mu_4 = \{\pm 1, \pm \mathrm{i}\}\). Its order is \(4^n n!\).

Tag 22L5

Problem 21.1.3. Every lattice in the genus of \(I_n\) contains a vector \(x\) with \(h(x,x)\) odd.

Tag 22L6

Problem 21.1.4. If \(n \le 4\), then \(\mathrm{Cl}_n = \{I_n\}\).

Combining Problems 21.1.2 and 21.1.4 gives \(M_n = 1/(4^n n!)\) for \(n \le 4\). In rank \(5\) a second class appears: \(E \perp I_1\), where \(E\) is \(E_8\) with a Hermitian structure over \(\mathcal{O}\), in which \(h(x,x)\) is always even, and \(\lvert \Gamma_E \rvert = 46080\) is the order of the reflection group \(G_{31}\) of Shephard and Todd [ShT]. There are no others (Problem 21.2.3).

§21.2. The mass of a genus Tag 22L7

How does \(M_n\) grow? Let \(\chi\) be the non-trivial character modulo \(4\), and let \(\chi^j\) be \(\chi\) for odd \(j\) and the trivial character for even \(j\), so that \(L(s, \chi^j) = \zeta(s)\) for even \(j\). For the genus of \(I_n\), the mass formula reads \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:mass}\tag{1} M_n = \delta_n \prod_{j=1}^{n} \frac{\lvert L(1-j, \chi^j) \rvert}{2}, \end{equation}\] where \(\delta_n\) is a positive rational number, a local factor at the prime \(2\), which is ramified in \(K\). This is the Hermitian analogue of Siegel’s mass formula for quadratic forms [Si], with the local factors computed by Gan and Yu [GY] and, at the ramified prime \(2\), by Cho [Cho]; made explicit in [JZ, Corollary 4.3], it gives \(\delta_n = 1\) for odd \(n\) and \(\delta_n = 2^n - 1\) for even \(n \ge 2\). We set \(\delta_0 := 1\), so that (1) also holds for \(n = 0\), where \(M_0 = 1\).

Tag 22L8

Problem 21.2.1. For \(j = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\), the numbers \(\lvert L(1-j, \chi^j) \rvert / 2\) are \(1/4\), \(1/24\), \(1/4\), \(1/240\), \(5/4\).

Tag 22L9

Problem 21.2.2. \(\delta_1 = \delta_3 = 1\), \(\delta_2 = 3\) and \(\delta_4 = 15\).

In rank \(5\), with \(\delta_5 = 1\), the formula gives \(M_5 = 1/73728\). Two unimodular lattices of the same rank over \(\mathcal{O}\), each containing a vector \(x\) with \(h(x,x)\) odd, lie in the same genus [JZ, Corollary 2.2]; thus \(E \perp I_1 \in \mathrm{Cl}_5\).

Tag 22LA

Problem 21.2.3. The only vectors \(x \in E \perp I_1\) with \(h(x,x) = 1\) are the four multiples \(u e\), \(u \in \mu_4\), of a generator \(e\) of \(I_1\); hence \(\Gamma_{E \perp I_1} = \Gamma_E \times \mu_4\). Moreover \(\mathrm{Cl}_5 = \{I_5, E \perp I_1\}\).

The functional equation turns the values at \(1 - j\) into values at \(j\), and in doing so brings out a factorial.

Tag 22LB

Problem 21.2.4. For \(j \ge 1\), \[\frac{\lvert L(1-j, \chi^j) \rvert}{2} = (j-1)!\, \pi^{-j} \beta_j, \qquad \beta_j := \begin{cases} 2^{j-1} L(j, \chi) & j \text{ odd},\\ 2^{-j} \zeta(j) & j \text{ even}. \end{cases}\]

For \(r \ge 1\) define \(\kappa_r > 0\) by \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:kappa}\tag{2} \frac{M_r}{M_{r-1}} = (r-1)!\, \pi^{-r} \kappa_r . \end{equation}\] By (1) and Problem 21.2.4, \(\kappa_r = (\delta_r/\delta_{r-1})\beta_r\). Multiplying (2) over \(r \le n\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:mass-product}\tag{3} M_n = \pi^{-n(n+1)/2} \prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j! \, \prod_{j=1}^{n} \kappa_j . \end{equation}\] So the mass is a product of factorials, up to a factor whose logarithm is linear in \(n\) at each step.

Tag 22LC

Problem 21.2.5. For \(r \le 4\), \(\kappa_r = \pi^r/(4 \cdot r!)\); thus \(\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_4\) are \(\pi/4\), \(\pi^2/8\), \(\pi^3/24\), \(\pi^4/96\). With \(\delta_5 = 1\), \(\kappa_5 = \pi^5/288\).

Tag 22LD

Problem 21.2.6. For every \(r \ge 1\), \(3/4 \le \kappa_r \le 2\); in particular \(\log \kappa_r = O(1)\).

By (3), \(\log M_n\) grows like \(\tfrac12 n^2 \log n\); since every \(\Gamma_L\) contains \(\mu_4\), the class set has at least \(4M_n\) elements, more than \(8 \cdot 10^5\) for \(n = 15\).

§21.3. Forms and the Weyl dimension formula Tag 22LE

On a definite group a form is a function on the class set, with values in a representation. How many are there?

For \(L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n\) let \(L_\mathbb{C}:= L \otimes_\mathcal{O}\mathbb{C}\), using the inclusion \(\mathcal{O}\subset \mathbb{C}\). The form \(h\) extends to a Hermitian inner product on \(L_\mathbb{C}\), and \(\Gamma_L\) is a finite subgroup of its unitary group. A weight is a sequence of integers \(\lambda = (\lambda_1 \ge \dots \ge \lambda_n \ge 0)\); write \(\lvert \lambda \rvert := \sum_k \lambda_k\). For a finite-dimensional Hermitian space \(A\), let \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(A) \subset A^{\otimes \lvert \lambda \rvert}\) be the image of the Young symmetrizer [FH], with the inner product induced from \(A^{\otimes \lvert\lambda\rvert}\). A linear map \(x \colon A \to B\) induces \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(x) \colon \mathbb{S}_\lambda(A) \to \mathbb{S}_\lambda(B)\), the restriction of \(x^{\otimes \lvert\lambda\rvert}\). Then \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(xy) = \mathbb{S}_\lambda(x)\mathbb{S}_\lambda(y)\) and \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(x)^* = \mathbb{S}_\lambda(x^*)\), the latter because \((x^*)^{\otimes k}\) commutes with the symmetric group. If \(\lambda\) has at most \(\dim A\) nonzero entries, \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda(A)\) is the irreducible representation of \(\mathrm{GL}(A)\) of highest weight \(\lambda\); otherwise it is zero [FH, Theorem 6.3]. For \(\lambda = (a)\) it is \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(A)\). There, for \(u \in A\) we write \(u^a := u \otimes \dots \otimes u\); for a basis \(e_1, \dots, e_n\) of \(A\) and \(\lvert\nu\rvert = a\), the vectors \(e^\nu\) are defined by \((\sum_k t_k e_k)^a = \sum_\nu (a!/\nu!)\, t^\nu e^\nu\), and \(e^\nu e^\mu := e^{\nu+\mu}\) is the product of the symmetric algebra.

Let \(V_\lambda(L) := \mathbb{S}_\lambda(L_\mathbb{C})\). Weyl’s dimension formula reads, with \(z_k := \lambda_k + n - k\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:weyl}\tag{4} \dim V_\lambda(L) = D_n(z) := \frac{V_n(z)}{\prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j!}, \qquad V_n(z) := \prod_{1 \le k < l \le n} (z_k - z_l) \end{equation}\] [FH, Theorem 6.3]. Fix one lattice in each class of \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\). A form of weight \(\lambda\) assigns to each \(L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n\) a vector \(\varphi(L) \in V_\lambda(L)\) fixed by \(\Gamma_L\). The forms make up a space \(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n)\), with the norm \[\lVert \varphi \rVert^2 := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \frac{1}{\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert}\, \lVert \varphi(L) \rVert^2 .\] These are the algebraic modular forms of Gross [Gr] on the unitary group of \(I_n\), of level one.

Tag 22LF

Problem 21.3.1. \(\dim \operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^n) = \binom{a+n-1}{n-1}\), and \(\dim \mathbb{S}_{(3,2,0)}(\mathbb{C}^3) = 15\). The denominator in (4) is \(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1} j! = V_n(n-1, \dots, 1, 0)\).

Tag 22LG

Problem 21.3.2. In \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(A)\), \(\langle u^a, v^a \rangle = \langle u, v \rangle^a\). If \(e_1, \dots, e_n\) is orthonormal, the \(e^\nu\) are orthogonal and \(\lVert e^\nu \rVert^2 = \nu!/a!\).

Tag 22LH

Problem 21.3.3. \(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n) = 0\) unless \(4 \mid \lvert\lambda\rvert\). The space \(\mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1)\) is one-dimensional when \(4 \mid a\).

Tag 22LJ

Problem 21.3.4. \(\dim \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n) = \sum_{L} \lvert\Gamma_L\rvert^{-1} \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_L} \operatorname{tr}(\gamma \mid V_\lambda(L))\). When \(4 \mid \lvert\lambda\rvert\), the scalars \(\gamma \in \mu_4\) contribute \(4 M_n \dim V_\lambda\) to this sum.

Tag 22LK

Problem 21.3.5. The \(\Gamma_{I_2}\)-invariants in the symmetric algebra of \(\mathbb{C}^2\) form the polynomial algebra on \(e_1^4 + e_2^4\) and \(e_1^4 e_2^4\). Hence, for \(4 \mid a\), \(\dim \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2) = \lfloor a/8 \rfloor + 1\), which is \((1 + o(1)) \cdot 4 M_2 \dim V_{(a)}\) as \(a \to \infty\).

Does \(\lvert\lambda\rvert \to \infty\) suffice? Compare two weights of the same size.

Tag 22LL

Problem 21.3.6. Let \(j \ge 1\), \(\lambda := (8j, 0)\) and \(\lambda' := (4j, 4j)\). Then \(\dim \mathcal{A}_\lambda(2) = j + 1\) and \(\dim \mathcal{A}_{\lambda'}(2) = 1\), while the scalar contributions \(4 M_2 \dim V\) of Problem 21.3.4 are \(j + \frac18\) and \(\frac18\). In both cases the non-scalar elements of \(\Gamma_{I_2}\) contribute exactly \(\frac78\).

The non-scalar elements contribute the same amount to both weights. Beside \(\dim V_\lambda = 8j + 1\) it is negligible; for \(\lambda'\), where \(V_{\lambda'} = \det^{4j}\) is a line on which all of \(\Gamma_{I_2}\) acts trivially, it is seven times the scalar term. What must grow is the gap \(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2\), not \(\lvert\lambda\rvert\).

The same holds in general. When \(4 \mid \lvert\lambda\rvert\) and all the gaps \(\lambda_k - \lambda_{k+1}\) tend to infinity, the Weyl character formula shows that each non-scalar \(\gamma\) contributes \(o(\dim V_\lambda)\), so \(\dim \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n) \sim 4 M_n \dim V_\lambda\). The count of forms is thus the product of the mass and the Weyl dimension. By (3) and (4) each factor contains \(\prod_{j<n} j!\), once in the numerator and once in the denominator; §21.4 returns to this.

§21.4. The count of forms Tag 22LM

When \(4 \mid \lvert\lambda\rvert\) and the gaps of \(\lambda\) are large, the space of forms of weight \(\lambda\) has dimension about \(4 M_n \dim V_\lambda\) (§21.3). The mass contains the product \(\prod_{j<n} j!\), whose logarithm is about \(\tfrac12 n^2 \log n\), and the Weyl dimension contains its inverse. The norm of a theta lift is controlled to within a term linear in the weight, so the count must be known to the same accuracy. Why is it?

Tag 22LN

Problem 21.4.1. \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \log j! = \tfrac12 n^2 \log n - \tfrac34 n^2 + O(n \log n)\).

Tag 22LP

Problem 21.4.2. Let \(r_0 < r_1\), and let \(u \in \mathbb{Z}^{r_0}\) and \(w \in \mathbb{Z}^{r_1}\) be strictly decreasing. Then \[\frac{M_{r_1} D_{r_1}(w)}{M_{r_0} D_{r_0}(u)} = \frac{V_{r_1}(w)}{V_{r_0}(u)}\; \pi^{-\sum_{r = r_0+1}^{r_1} r} \prod_{r = r_0+1}^{r_1} \kappa_r .\]

The extra \((r-1)!\) in the mass ratio (2) cancels the extra \((r-1)!\) in the denominator of (4). With \(r_0 = 0\), where \(M_0 = D_0 = V_0 = 1\) (empty products), Problem 21.4.2 reads as follows.

Tag 22LQ

Theorem 21.4.1 (The count identity). For every \(r \ge 1\) and every strictly decreasing \(z \in \mathbb{Z}^r\), \[M_r D_r(z) = V_r(z)\, \pi^{-r(r+1)/2} \prod_{j=1}^{r} \kappa_j .\]

No factorial survives: what remains is a product of differences of weights, a power of \(\pi\) and the factors \(\kappa_j\).

In the application one rank is added at a time, with one large new weight.

Tag 22LR

Problem 21.4.3. Let \(u \in \mathbb{Z}^{r_0}\) be strictly decreasing, and \(w = (w_1, u_1, \dots, u_{r_0})\) with \(w_1 > u_1\). Then \[\log \frac{M_{r_0+1} D_{r_0+1}(w)}{M_{r_0} D_{r_0}(u)} = \sum_{i=1}^{r_0} \bigl( \log(w_1 - u_i) - \log \pi \bigr) - \log\pi + \log \kappa_{r_0+1} .\]

Thus each new rank contributes, for every existing index \(i\), the quantity \(\log(w_1 - u_i) - \log\pi\), and since \(\log\kappa_r = O(1)\) (Problem 21.2.6) the remaining term is of lower order when \(w_1\) is large.
