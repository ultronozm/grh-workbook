Prerequisites.

This chapter continues Chapter 29, Denominators and the Stickelberger element, whose notation \(A\), \(A^{(i)}\), \(A^\pm\), \(\omega\) (Section 29.2, with \(p = \ell\)), Problem 29.2.2 and Theorem 29.2.2 we use. Continuous cohomology of profinite groups [NSW, Ch. I; Ch. II, §7], inflation–restriction, and the vanishing of \(H^q(G, M)\), \(q \ge 1\), for finite \(G\) of order invertible on \(M\) [SeL, Ch. VII–VIII]. The Artin isomorphism between the ideal class group of a number field and the Galois group of its Hilbert class field [Ne, Ch. VI]. The duality theorems for Galois cohomology of number fields are stated in the text with references. Section 30.3 uses the ramps of Problem 7.4.1 in The slope inequality in the mean. Throughout, \(\ell\) is an odd prime and \(\operatorname{num}(r)\) is the numerator of a rational number \(r\) in lowest terms.

§30.1. Tate twists over \(\mathbb{Q}\) Tag 22T3

In the previous chapter the denominator that no twist removes was measured by an eigenspace of a class group. Which Galois cohomology groups play this role for a Tate twist, and what are their orders? Let \(\mathbb{Q}_S\) be the largest extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\) unramified outside \(\ell\) and \(\infty\), \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell} := \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_S/\mathbb{Q})\), and \(\chi_\ell \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell} \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\) the cyclotomic character. For \(k \in \mathbb{Z}\), \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)\) is \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) with \(\sigma\) acting by \(\chi_\ell(\sigma)^k\), and similarly for \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell(k)\), \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)\) and \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell(k)\). Let \(W_e := \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(-e)\), and let \(G_\ell := \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)\), mapped to \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\) by restriction along a fixed embedding \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\).

Definition.

The Tate–Shafarevich group of the twist \(e\) is \[\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) := \ker\bigl( H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, W_e) \to H^1(G_\ell, W_e) \bigr) ,\] and \(h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q}) \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots, \infty\}\) is its length as a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-module. For a number field \(E\) one defines \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) and \(h_{\ell,e}(E)\) in the same way, with the local Galois groups at all primes of \(E\) above \(\ell\).

Tag 22T4

Problem 30.1.1. If \((\ell - 1) \nmid k\), then \(H^0(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)) = 0\). If \((\ell-1) \mid k\) and \(k \ne 0\), it is cyclic of order \(\ell^{1 + v_\ell(k)}\). The same holds for \(G_\ell\) in place of \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\).

By von Staudt and Clausen [Wa, Thm. 5.10], the denominator of \(B_k\), \(k \ge 2\) even, is the product of the primes \(p\) with \((p-1) \mid k\).

Tag 22T5

Problem 30.1.2. For even \(k \ge 2\), the power of \(\ell\) in the denominator of \(\zeta(1-k) = -B_k/k\) is \(\lvert H^0(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)) \rvert\).

So the denominators of the values \(\zeta(1-k)\) are orders of \(H^0\); their numerators will be orders of \(\mathrm{Sha}\).

Tag 22T6

Problem 30.1.3. Let \(p \ne \ell\), \(e \ne 0\), and \(G_p := \operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p/\mathbb{Q}_p)\). Every unramified class in \(H^1(G_p, W_e)\) is zero. Hence \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) is the group of classes in \(H^1(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}), W_e)\) that vanish at every prime.

To compute \(\mathrm{Sha}\), restrict to \(F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)\).

Tag 22T7

Problem 30.1.4. Suppose that \((\ell - 1) \nmid e\). Then \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}/\ell(-e)) \to H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, W_e)[\ell]\) is an isomorphism, and so is its analogue for \(G_\ell\). Hence \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)[\ell]\) is the group of classes in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}/\ell(-e))\) that vanish on \(G_\ell\).

Tag 22T8

Problem 30.1.5. Suppose that \((\ell - 1) \nmid e\). Restriction to \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_S/F)\), followed by the Artin map, identifies the group of Problem 30.1.4 with the group of homomorphisms \(\phi \colon A \to \mathbb{F}_\ell\) such that \(\phi(\sigma_a z) = \omega(a)^{-e} \phi(z)\), that is, with \(\operatorname{Hom}(A^{(-e)}, \mathbb{F}_\ell)\).

Problems 30.1.4 and 30.1.5 prove the following.

Tag 22T9

Theorem 30.1.1. Let \((\ell - 1) \nmid e\). Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)[\ell] \cong \operatorname{Hom}(A^{(-e)}, \mathbb{F}_\ell)\). In particular \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) = 0\) if and only if \(A^{(-e)} = 0\).

Tag 22TA

Problem 30.1.6. If \((\ell - 1) \mid e\) and \(e \ne 0\), then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) = 0\).

Tag 22TB

Problem 30.1.7. Let \(e \ge 1\) be odd. Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) \ne 0\) if and only if \(\ell \mid \operatorname{num}(B_{e+1}/(e+1))\). For example, \(\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q}, 11) \ne 0\) and \(\mathrm{Sha}_{37}(\mathbb{Q}, 31) \ne 0\).

We state four inputs as black boxes. By Soulé [So], \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) is finite for \(k \ge 2\). By Poitou–Tate duality [NSW, Ch. VIII, §6], \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) is dual to the kernel of \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1)) \to H^2(G_\ell, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\), and by local duality [NSW, Ch. VII, §2] \(H^2(G_\ell, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) is dual to \(H^0(G_\ell, \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1-k))\). By Tate’s global Euler characteristic formula [NSW, Ch. VIII, §7], for every finite \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\)-module \(M\) of \(\ell\)-power order, \(\lvert H^0(M) \rvert \lvert H^2(M) \rvert / \lvert H^1(M) \rvert = \lvert M^{c = 1} \rvert / \lvert M \rvert\), where \(c\) is a complex conjugation. Since \(\ell\) is odd, \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\) has \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension \(2\) [NSW, Ch. VIII, §3], so \(H^3(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, M) = 0\) for every \(\ell\)-primary \(M\).

Tag 22TC

Problem 30.1.8. Let \(e \ge 1\) be odd. Then \(H^2(G_\ell, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1)) = 0\), and \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) is dual to \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\).

Tag 22TD

Problem 30.1.9. Let \(k \ge 2\). The \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-module \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) has rank \(1\) if \(k\) is odd and rank \(0\) if \(k\) is even, and its torsion subgroup is isomorphic to \(H^0(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\).

The main conjecture of Iwasawa theory, proved by Mazur and Wiles [MW], gives the order; see [Wa, Ch. 15].

Tag 22TE

Theorem 30.1.2 (Mazur–Wiles). Let \(e \ge 1\) be odd. Then \[h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q}) = v_\ell\bigl(\operatorname{num}(B_{e+1}/(e+1))\bigr) .\]

Tag 22TF

Problem 30.1.10. Let \(k \ge 2\) be even. Then \[\frac{\lvert H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)) \rvert}{\lvert H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)) \rvert} = \lvert \zeta(1-k) \rvert_\ell^{-1} .\] For \(k = 12\) and \(\ell = 691\) both sides are \(691\).

This is the simplest case of the Bloch–Kato conjecture [BK]. For odd \(k \ge 3\), that is for even \(e = k - 1\), the value \(\zeta(1-k)\) vanishes, \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) has rank one (Problem 30.1.9), and \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, k-1)\) is dual to a subgroup of \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\). One expects the order of \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) to be the index in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) of the subgroup generated by cyclotomic elements, as for \(A^+\); this chapter does not use it.

§30.2. The minus part and the plus part Tag 22TG

Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the twists split by the parity of \(e\): odd \(e\) is controlled by a Bernoulli number, even \(e\) by the plus part of a class group. How does this look over an abelian field, and what does it say about sums over all \(\ell\)?

Let \(E \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)\) and \(\Delta_E := \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\). Then \(\Delta_E\) acts on \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\), which is the direct sum of its \(\eta\)-parts over the characters \(\eta\) of \(\Delta_E\), with values in \(\mu_{\ell-1} \subset \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\). Let \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e; \eta)\) be defined as \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\), with \(W_e\) replaced by \(W_e \otimes \eta^{-1}\). A character \(\eta\) is critical for \(e\) if \(\eta(-1) = (-1)^{e+1}\), and plus for \(e\) otherwise. The minus part and the plus part of \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) are the sums of the \(\eta\)-parts over the critical and over the plus characters.

Tag 22TH

Problem 30.2.1. The \(\eta\)-part of \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) is isomorphic to \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e; \eta)\). In particular \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) is the part fixed by \(\Delta_E\).

Tag 22TJ

Problem 30.2.2. Let \(\eta = \omega^j\) with \((\ell - 1) \nmid (e + j)\). Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e; \eta)[\ell] \cong \operatorname{Hom}(A^{(-e-j)}, \mathbb{F}_\ell)\), and \(\eta\) is critical for \(e\) if and only if \(-e-j\) is odd.

Tag 22TK

Problem 30.2.3. For every \(e \ne 0\), the minus part of \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell), e)\) has the same \(\ell\)-rank as \(A^-\), and the plus part has \(\ell\)-rank at least that of \(A^+\).

So the critical eigenspaces see the minus part of a class group, where Stickelberger’s theorem and Bernoulli numbers apply, and the plus eigenspaces see the plus part, where they do not. On the minus side one number controls every \(\ell\) at once.

Tag 22TL

Problem 30.2.4. For a nonzero integer \(N\), \(\sum_\ell v_\ell(N) \log \ell \le \log \lvert N \rvert\). Hence, for odd \(e \ge 1\), \[\sum_{\ell \text{ odd}} h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q}) \log \ell \le \log \Bigl\lvert \operatorname{num}\Bigl(\frac{B_{e+1}}{e+1}\Bigr) \Bigr\rvert .\]

Tag 22TM

Problem 30.2.5. For \(k \ge 1\), \(\log \lvert \operatorname{num}(B_{2k}/2k) \rvert \le 2k \log(2k) + 3\).

On the plus side no such number is available. One case is elementary.

Tag 22TN

Problem 30.2.6. If \(\ell \equiv 1 \pmod 4\), the class number of \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt\ell)\) is less than \(\ell\).

Tag 22TP

Problem 30.2.7. Let \(e \ge 2\) be even with \((\ell - 1) \mid 2e\). Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) = 0\). For example \(\mathrm{Sha}_5(\mathbb{Q}, 2) = 0\).

Tag 22TQ

Problem 30.2.8. If \(\ell\) does not divide the class number of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell + \zeta_\ell^{-1})\), then \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q}, e) = 0\) for every even \(e \ne 0\).

Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the pairs \((\ell, e)\) therefore fall into three cases: \(e\) odd, given by Theorem 30.1.2; \(e\) even with \((\ell - 1) \mid 2e\), zero by Problem 30.2.7; and \(e\) even with \((\ell - 1) \nmid 2e\), zero under Vandiver’s conjecture and unknown in general. The fields of the argument are abelian CM fields of large degree, and there the plus part is the difficulty; Alpöge’s note speaks of the classes “that we couldn’t kill by twisting” and of a “significant weakening of Vandiver” [Alp, item 17]. No proof of such a bound has been published. In [Adr] the bound the argument needs is [Adr, Hyp. 17.11]; see the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58.

§30.3. The size of the bound Tag 22TR

How large may these groups be before they spoil the slope inequality? For a number field \(E\) and an integer \(t \ge 2\) let \[V(E, t) := \sum_\ell \log \ell \sum_{1 \le e < t} h_{\ell,e}(E) ,\] the logarithm of the order of \(\bigoplus_{\ell, e} \mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) when this group is finite. By Problem 7.4.1, the divisibility at the inert and ramified primes, summed with weights \(\log p\), is \(w\) times a ramp sum at the scale \(x = a/w\). In Alpöge’s argument, as reconstructed in [Adr], the denominators left after gluing are paid for by \(V(E, t)\), for a CM field \(E\) whose degree and number of twists \(t\) are bounded by powers of \(x\), and the weight is \(w = b := \exp(x^\varepsilon)\) with a small fixed \(\varepsilon > 0\). The bound that the argument needs is \(V(E, t) \le \sqrt b\). In [Adr] it follows, on suitable windows of scales, from the Selmer bound on windows by [Adr, Prop. 18.4]; in [Adr] the Selmer bound is [Adr, Hyp. 17.11], assumed there. The Lean formalization proves it; Chapter 57 outlines the proof. See (W3) and the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58.

Tag 22TS

Problem 30.3.1. Suppose that \(b S \le b M + b R + V\) with real numbers \(S, M, R\) and \(0 \le V \le \sqrt b\). Then \(S \le M + R + \exp(-x^\varepsilon/2)\), and \(\exp(-x^\varepsilon/2) = o(x^{-C})\) for every \(C\).

In the applications \(S\) is a ramp sum over primes, \(M\) its main term of size \(x \log x\), and \(R\) must be \(O(x^{1/2 + \varepsilon})\).

Tag 22TT

Problem 30.3.2. If \(V(E, t) \le x^C\) for a constant \(C\), then \(V(E,t) \le \sqrt b\) for all large \(x\). The same holds if \(V(E,t) \le \exp(x^{\varepsilon'})\) with \(\varepsilon' < \varepsilon\). For \(c > 0\) and \(d \ge x\), on the other hand, \(\exp(\exp(c d)) > \sqrt b\) for all large \(x\).

Through Soulé’s Chern class maps [So], \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(E, e)\) is dual to a subquotient of \(K_{2e}(\mathcal{O}_E) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell\), so one expects \(V(E, t)\) to be polynomial in \(x\). Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) this follows from Vandiver’s conjecture.

Tag 22TU

Problem 30.3.3. Assume Vandiver’s conjecture for every odd \(\ell\). Then for \(t \ge 2\) \[\sum_{\ell \text{ odd}} \log \ell \sum_{1 \le e < t} h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q}) \le t^2 \log t + 3t .\]

The difficulty is therefore not the size of \(V(E,t)\) but its uniformity in \(\ell\), \(e\) and a field \(E\) that changes with \(x\). On the minus part one Bernoulli number bounds all \(\ell\) at once (Problem 30.2.4); on the plus part no such number is available, already over \(\mathbb{Q}\). Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the minus part is given exactly by Theorem 30.1.2. Over the fields \(E\) of the argument the formalization needs only an upper bound, and obtains it without the main conjecture, by a one-sided Euler-system argument with Gauss sums (Chapter 55); the plus part it bounds by congruences, case by case (Chapter 56).

Questions.

The following are not known to us. Is \(A^+ = 0\) for every odd prime \(p\) (Vandiver’s conjecture)? Is there a constant \(C\) such that \(\sum_{\ell \text{ odd}} \log \ell \sum_{1 \le e < t,\ e \text{ even}} h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q}) \le t^C\) for all \(t \ge 2\)? A positive answer to the second question would give, for the plus part over \(\mathbb{Q}\), far more than the argument requires: it needs only \(V(E, t) \le \sqrt b\), with \(t\) a power of \(x\) and \(b = \exp(x^\varepsilon)\), and only on windows of scales. In [Adr] this requirement is [Adr, Hyp. 17.11], assumed there; the formalization proves it (Chapter 57); see the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58. It implies a bound toward Vandiver’s conjecture stronger than the published ones [Adr, Prop. 17.22]. The route to it sketched in [Adr] passes through an automorphy statement for residually reducible Galois representations of unitary type in three variables, (G3) there; its status is recorded in Section 33.4 of Chapter 33. The route of the formalization is that of Chapters 55, 56 and 57.

§30.1

30.1.1

\(\chi_\ell\) maps \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\) and \(G_\ell\) onto \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\). Compute the least \(v_\ell(u^k - 1)\) over \(u \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\).

30.1.2

Combine Problem 30.1.1 with the theorem of von Staudt and Clausen. If \((\ell - 1) \nmid k\), then \(B_k/k\) is \(\ell\)-integral [Wa, Cor. 5.14].

30.1.3

An unramified class lies in \(H^1(\hat{\mathbb{Z}}, W_e) = W_e/(\varphi - 1)W_e\), where Frobenius \(\varphi\) acts by \(p^{-e}\). For the second statement, inflate from \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\); conversely, by inflation–restriction a class that is unramified outside \(\ell\) comes from \(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}\).

30.1.4

Use the sequence \(0 \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell(-e) \to W_e \xrightarrow{\ell} W_e \to 0\) and Problem 30.1.1.

30.1.5

Use inflation–restriction to \(G_F := \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_S/F)\), which acts trivially on \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell(-e)\). The prime above \(\ell\) in \(F\) is principal, and \([F_\lambda : \mathbb{Q}_\ell] = \ell - 1\) is prime to \(\ell\).

30.1.6

Now \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell(-e)\) is trivial and \(\mathbb{Q}\) has class number \(1\). Compare the images of \(H^0(W_e)/\ell\) globally and at \(\ell\) using Problem 30.1.1.

30.1.7

Let \(i \equiv -e \pmod{\ell - 1}\). Use Theorem 30.1.1, Theorem 29.2.2, and Kummer’s congruence \(B_m/m \equiv B_n/n \pmod \ell\) for even \(m, n \ge 2\) with \(m \equiv n \not\equiv 0 \pmod{\ell-1}\) [Wa, Ch. 5]; treat \(i \equiv 1\) separately, using \(A^{(1)} = 0\) [Wa, Prop. 6.16].

30.1.8

Problem 30.1.1 with \(k = -e\), and local duality.

30.1.9

Apply the Euler characteristic formula to \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)/\ell^n\) and let \(n \to \infty\); since the groups \(H^i(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)/\ell^n)\) are finite, their inverse limit is \(H^i(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))\) [NSW, Ch. II, §7]. For the upper bound on the rank, show that \(H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k)/\ell^n) = H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q},\ell}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k))/\ell^n\), which is bounded by Soulé’s theorem. For the torsion, use \(0 \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell(k) \to \mathbb{Q}_\ell(k) \to \mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(k) \to 0\).

30.1.10

Problems 30.1.8, 30.1.9 and 30.1.2, and Theorem 30.1.2 with \(e = k - 1\).
