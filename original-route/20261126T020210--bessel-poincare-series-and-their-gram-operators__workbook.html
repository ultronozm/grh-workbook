Prerequisites.

From Chapter 11: Poincaré series on \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), the unfolding of Problem 11.1.1, Problem 11.1.3, and the dimension as formal degree times covolume (Section 11.4). From Chapter 21: class sets, the groups \(\Gamma_L\), forms and their norm (Section 21.3), Problems 21.1.2, 21.1.4, 21.3.2 and 21.3.5, and the mass \(M_2=1/32\). From Chapter 23: the period of Section 23.4. From Chapter 35: the decomposition (S4) of \(Y_0\) and Problem 35.2.4. From Chapter 43: the Gram operators and Theorem 43.4.1. The Bessel periods of Gan, Gross and Prasad [GGP], at the level of the definition.

In Chapter 11 a Fourier coefficient of a cusp form is its inner product with a Poincaré series, and Petersson’s formula expands the inner products of two Poincaré series. Chapter 43 needs the same two facts for a unitary group, with Fourier coefficients replaced by Bessel periods. What is the Poincaré series, and why is its Gram operator a sum over double cosets? We give the definitions in the form the Lean formalization [Lean] uses, work them out on a definite group where every sum is finite, and then state what the formalization proves about them. As in Chapter 35, hermitian forms \(h\) are linear in the first variable.

§44.1. The Bessel subgroup

Let \(E/F\) be a CM extension, \(\psi_F\) a nontrivial character of \(F\backslash\mathbb A_F\), and let \(V\) be a hermitian space over \(E\) of rank \(r\) with a decomposition \[V=Ee_1\oplus\dots\oplus Ee_w\oplus X\oplus Ez\oplus Ef_1\oplus\dots\oplus Ef_w ,\] where \(h(e_a,e_b)=h(f_a,f_b)=0\), \(h(e_a,f_b)=\delta_{ab}\), the space \(X\) of rank \(k\) is orthogonal to the \(e_a\), \(f_a\) and \(z\), and \(h(z,z)=-T\) for a prime \(T\). So \(r=2w+k+1\). This is the decomposition (S4) of \(Y_0\) in Chapter 35, with \(w=m/2-1\), the hyperbolic planes \(Ee_a+Ef_a\), and \(X\) the image of \(X_0\).

Let \(N\) be the group of \(n\in U(V)\) such that \((n-1)e_a\) lies in the span of \(e_1,\dots,e_{a-1}\) for every \(a\), and \((n-1)y\) lies in the span of \(e_1,\dots,e_w\) for every \(y\) orthogonal to all \(e_a\). It is the unipotent radical of the stabilizer of the isotropic flag \(Ee_1\subset Ee_1+Ee_2\subset\cdots\). Embed \(U(X)\) in \(U(V)\) as the pointwise stabilizer of the \(e_a\), the \(f_a\) and \(z\). Put \(e_{w+1}:=z\) and, for \(n\in N\), \[\lambda(n):=\sum_{a=1}^wh(ne_{a+1},f_a),\qquad \psi_N(n):=\psi_F\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/F}\lambda(n)\bigr).\] The Bessel subgroup is \(R:=N\rtimes U(X)\). For automorphic forms \(f\) on \(U(V)\) and \(\phi\) on \(U(X)\), the Bessel period integrates \(f\) against \(\overline{\psi_N}\) over \(N(F)\backslash N(\mathbb A_F)\), and the result against \(\bar\phi\) over \(U(X)(F)\backslash U(X)(\mathbb A_F)\) [GGP]. For \(w=0\) the group \(N\) is trivial, and the Bessel period is the restriction period along \(U(X)\subset U(X\perp Ez)\).

Problem 44.1.1. For \(n\in N\), \(\lambda(n)\) is the sum over \(a\le w\) of the coefficient of \(e_a\) in \(ne_{a+1}\). The map \(\lambda\colon N\to E\) is a homomorphism, and \(\lambda(gng^{-1})=\lambda(n)\) for \(g\in U(X)\). Hence \(\psi_N\) is a character of \(N(\mathbb A_F)\), trivial on \(N(F)\) and fixed by \(U(X)\).

Problem 44.1.2. \(U(X)\) normalizes \(N\), and \(U(X)\cap N=\{1\}\).

Problem 44.1.3. As an algebraic group over \(F\), \(N\) has dimension \(w(r+k)\). For \((w,k)=(1,0)\) it is the three-dimensional unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of \(U(V)\), \(V\) of rank \(3\).

Different words of the construction use different lines. The record contains, for each word \(u\), a number \(c_u\in E^\times\) with \(c_u\bar c_uT_u=T_{u_0}\), where \(T_u\) is the terminal prime of \(u\) (Section 35.5), and the line of \(u\) is spanned by \(z_u:=c_u^{-1}z\), with \(z=z_{u_0}\).

Problem 44.1.4. \(h(z_u,z_u)=-T_u\). Replacing \(z\) by \(z_u\) changes neither \(N\) nor \(U(X)\); it multiplies the term \(a=w\) of \(\lambda\) by \(c_u^{-1}\) and leaves the others unchanged.

§44.2. Poincaré series

The Poincaré series of the argument is built like that of Chapter 11, with \(\Gamma_\infty\) replaced by \(R\) and \(e(mz)\) by a Bessel function. Let \(G:=U(V)\). The formalization fixes a discrete series representation \(\pi\) of \(G(F\otimes\mathbb R)\), an open compact subgroup \(K\subset G(\mathbb A_{F,f})\) with a character \(\chi\), and the space \(\mathcal H\) of automorphic forms on \(G\) of type \(\pi\) at infinity on which \(K\) acts by \(\chi\). For a word \(u\) it fixes a representation \(\pi_u\) of \(U(X)(F\otimes\mathbb R)\) (the weight of the word), and a function \(W_u\colon G(F\otimes\mathbb R)\to\pi_u\) with \[W_u(nhg)=\psi_N(n)\,\pi_u(h)W_u(g)\qquad(n\in N(F\otimes\mathbb R),\ h\in U(X)(F\otimes\mathbb R)),\] which is reproduced by convolution with a matrix coefficient of \(\pi\) and has norm one in \(L^2\) of a fundamental domain for \(R\). It is the archimedean Bessel function. Let \(C_u\) be the space of \(U(X)(F\otimes\mathbb R)\)-equivariant maps \(\mathsf T\) from \(\pi_u\) to functions on \(U(X)(F)\backslash U(X)(\mathbb A_F)\) with a fixed level and character: algebraic forms on the definite group \(U(X)\) of weight \(\pi_u\), the column forms of the word. For \(g\in G(\mathbb A_F)\) whose finite part is \(n\,h\,x_u\,\kappa\) with \(n\in N(\mathbb A_{F,f})\), \(h\in U(X)(\mathbb A_{F,f})\), \(\kappa\in K\) and a fixed translate \(x_u\), put \(\Phi_{\mathsf T}(g):=\psi_N(n)\chi(\kappa)\,\mathsf T(W_u(g_\infty))(h)\), and \(\Phi_{\mathsf T}(g):=0\) for the other \(g\). The Bessel–Poincaré series is \[P_u\mathsf T(g):=\sum_{\delta\in R(F)\backslash G(F)}\Phi_{\mathsf T}(\delta g).\] Status: these are the definitions of the formalization. It proves that the series converges in \(L^2\), lies in \(\mathcal H\) and is linear in \(\mathsf T\), under a hypothesis that \(r\ge1801\) and that consecutive weights \(b_j\) of the chain, which enter the Harish-Chandra parameter of \(\pi\), differ by at least \(16r^{19}\), from a decay statement for \(W_u\) that rests on uniqueness of archimedean Bessel models with a gap hypothesis; compare [JSZ]. We have not followed the proof of the decay statement. Formally, unfolding the sum against \(f\in\mathcal H\) gives the Bessel period of \(f\) against \(\mathsf T\), as in Problem 11.1.1.

On a definite group with one class every quotient is finite, and the series is a finite sum. Let \(\Gamma\) be a finite group acting unitarily on a space \(V_\pi\), and let the forms on \(G\) be the vectors \(V_\pi^\Gamma\), with \(\lVert\psi\rVert^2:=\lvert\Gamma\rvert^{-1}\lVert\psi\rVert_{V_\pi}^2\), as in Chapter 21 for one class. Let \(\Gamma_X\subset\Gamma\) be a subgroup, \(W\) a unitary representation of \(\Gamma_X\), and \(\iota\colon W\to V_\pi\) a \(\Gamma_X\)-equivariant linear map with \(\lVert\iota\xi\rVert^2=c_0\lVert\xi\rVert^2\) for a constant \(c_0>0\). The forms on \(X\) are \(W^{\Gamma_X}\), with \(\lVert\xi\rVert^2:=\lvert\Gamma_X\rvert^{-1}\lVert\xi\rVert_W^2\). Put \[B(\psi,\xi):=\lvert\Gamma_X\rvert^{-1}\langle\psi,\iota\xi\rangle,\qquad P\xi:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma/\Gamma_X}\gamma\,\iota\xi .\]

Problem 44.2.1. \(P\xi\) is well defined and lies in \(V_\pi^\Gamma\), and \(\langle\psi,P\xi\rangle=B(\psi,\xi)\) for every \(\psi\in V_\pi^\Gamma\).

Problem 44.2.2. The adjoint of \(P\) for the two norms is \(P^*\psi=\iota^*\psi\). In particular \(P\ne0\) if and only if \(B(\psi,\xi)\ne0\) for some \(\psi\) and \(\xi\).

Problem 44.2.3. Let \((W',\iota')\) be a second pair as above, with Poincaré map \(P'\). For \(\xi\in W^{\Gamma_X}\) and \(\xi'\in W'^{\Gamma_X}\), \[\langle P\xi,P'\xi'\rangle=\sum_{\delta\in\Gamma_X\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_X}\frac{\langle\delta\,\iota\xi,\iota'\xi'\rangle}{\lvert\Gamma_X\cap\delta\Gamma_X\delta^{-1}\rvert} ,\] and if \((W',\iota')=(W,\iota)\) the term of \(\delta=1\) is \(c_0\langle\xi,\xi'\rangle\).

So \(K:=c_0^{-1}P^*P\) is the identity plus a sum over the nontrivial double cosets. This is the finite form of Petersson’s formula, Theorem 11.2.1, where the double cosets \(\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_\infty\) give the Kloosterman terms.

§44.3. The smallest case

Take \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\mathrm i]\), \(\Gamma:=\Gamma_{I_2}\), of order \(32\) by Problem 21.1.2, and \(\mathrm{Cl}_2=\{I_2\}\) by Problem 21.1.4. Let \(z:=e_2\) and \(X:=\mathcal Oe_1\); here \(h(z,z)=1\), since everything is definite. The stabilizer of \(z\) is \(\Gamma_X=\{\operatorname{diag}(u,1):u\in\mu_4\}\). Let \(V_\pi:=\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb C^2)\), \(W:=\operatorname{Sym}^j(\mathbb Ce_1)\) and \(\iota(\xi):=\xi\,e_2^{a-j}\). Then \(B\) is the period \(P(\psi,\xi)\) of Section 23.4, and Problem 44.2.1 says that it is the inner product with \(P\xi\). For \(4\mid j\) let \(\xi_j\in W^{\Gamma_X}\) be the form with value \(e_1^j\).

Problem 44.3.1. \(\Gamma_X\backslash\Gamma/\Gamma_X\) has five elements: the classes of \(\operatorname{diag}(1,u)\), \(u\in\mu_4\), with four elements each, and the class of the sixteen antidiagonal matrices.

Problem 44.3.2. Let \(4\mid j\) and \(0\le j\le a\). Then \(P\xi_j=4\bigl(e_1^je_2^{a-j}+e_1^{a-j}e_2^j\bigr)\) if \(4\mid a\), and \(P\xi_j=0\) otherwise. For \(a=8\): \(P\xi_0=4(e_1^8+e_2^8)\) and \(P\xi_4=8e_1^4e_2^4\).

Problem 44.3.3. Here \(c_0=\binom aj^{-1}\). Let \(4\mid a\) and \(4\mid j,j'\), and put \(K_{jj'}:=\bigl(c_0(j)c_0(j')\bigr)^{-1/2}\langle P\xi_j,P\xi_{j'}\rangle/(\lVert\xi_j\rVert\lVert\xi_{j'}\rVert)\). Then \[K_{jj'}=4\bigl(\delta_{jj'}+\delta_{j',a-j}\bigr).\] In the expansion of Problem 44.2.3 the identity contributes \(1\) to \(K_{jj}\), the three classes \(\operatorname{diag}(1,u)\) with \(u\ne1\) contribute \(3\), and the antidiagonal class contributes \(4\delta_{j',a-j}\).

Problem 44.3.4. Let \(4\mid a\). The forms \(P\xi_j\) with \(4\mid j\) and \(2j\le a\) are an orthogonal basis of \(\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)\), their number is \(\lfloor a/8\rfloor+1\), and \(P\xi_{a-j}=P\xi_j\).

As in Problem 11.1.3, distinct Poincaré series can coincide.

Problem 44.3.5. Let \(4\mid a\) and \(D^*:=4M_2\dim\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb C^2)\). Then \(D^*=(a+1)/8\), and \(0<\dim\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)-D^*\le7/8\).

So in the toy the words \(j\) with \(2j<a\) give exactly orthogonal Poincaré series which span all but at most one dimension of the space, and \(\dim\mathcal A_{(a)}(2)\) is \(D^*\) up to a bounded error, as \(\dim S_k\) is \((k-1)/12\) up to a bounded error in Problem 11.4.2. But the diagonal is \(4\), not \(1\): the terms of the elements \(\operatorname{diag}(1,u)\), which preserve the line \(Ez\) without fixing \(z\), are as large as the identity term. The statement of the next section needs every term other than the identity term to be small, and the Bessel data are built so that the space of forms of each word is a small part of \(\mathcal H\). We have not followed how the formalization disposes of the terms of such elements.

§44.4. The Gram operators in the argument

Return to Section 44.2. Let \(d_u:=\dim C_u\), and let \(c_0(u)\) be the volume of the level subgroup, times the volume of a fundamental domain for \(N\) at infinity, times the volume of \(U(X)(F\otimes\mathbb R)\), divided by \(\dim\pi_u\). The Gram operators are \[K_{uu'}:=\bigl(c_0(u)c_0(u')\bigr)^{-1/2}P_u^*P_{u'}\colon C_{u'}\to C_u ,\] as in Chapter 43. Let \(D:=\dim\mathcal H\), and let \(D^*\) be the formal degree of \(\pi\) times the volume of \(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb A_F)\) times the value at \(1\) of the level idempotent. Fix the scale \(x\), \(y:=x^\varepsilon\) and \(b:=e^y\), and put \(\Gamma_0:=\exp(C_\Gamma m^5fy)\) for a constant \(C_\Gamma>0\) of the family.

Theorem 44.4.1 (near orthogonality). For every family of records of the formalization satisfying its size conditions there is \(x_0\) such that for every record at a scale \(x\ge x_0\) there are \(\eta_0,\eta_1,e_0\ge0\) with \(\eta_0,\eta_1,\sqrt{e_0}\le\exp(-x^{\varepsilon/8})\) with the following property. If \(1/3\le d_u\Gamma_0^2/D^*\le3\) for every word \(u\), then \[\lVert K_{uu'}\rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\le(1+\eta_0)\frac{d_ud_{u'}}{D^*}\quad(u\ne u'),\qquad \lVert K_{uu}-1\rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\le e_0d_u,\qquad D\le(1+\eta_1)D^* .\]

Status: proved in the formalization. The proof writes \(K_{uu'}\) as an absolutely convergent sum over a set of terms containing an identity term; by our reading these are the double cosets \(R(F)\backslash G(F)/R(F)\) of Problem 44.2.3, but we have not read their definition. It then proves four bounds: the terms of a class of degenerate terms sum to zero when \(u\ne u'\); their sum is small when \(u=u'\); the other terms of an off-diagonal block have squared norm at most \((1+b^{-6})d_ud_{u'}/D^*\); and the other terms of a diagonal block differ from the identity by little. The off-diagonal bound is derived from the Siegel–Weil formula in its convergent range and from a lower bound for Poincaré amplitudes; we have not followed the proofs of these four bounds. The statement is Theorem 43.4.1 of Chapter 43; in [Adr] it is derived from hypotheses assumed there.

The third inequality comes from a count of forms.

Theorem 44.4.2 (the count). There are \(C,c,c_b>0\) such that, if every gap of the Harish-Chandra parameter of \(\pi\) is at least \(Cr^{19}f^5\log(b\,\mathrm{rd}_E)\), \(\log f\le\sqrt r\) and \(\log\log b\le c_brf\log r\), then \(\lvert D-D^*\rvert\le\exp(-crf\log r)\,D^*\). Here \(\mathrm{rd}_E\) is the root discriminant of \(E\).

Status: proved in the formalization from the Siegel–Weil formula for unitary groups in its convergent range, through a statement on volumes; we have not followed the proof. It is a limit multiplicity statement with a rate, in the tradition of de George and Wallach [dGW]. Section 11.4 is its simplest case, where the identity term is the formal degree times the covolume, and Problem 44.3.5 is the definite case of rank two.

Problem 44.4.1. For \(x\ge1\), \(b^{-6}\le\exp(-x^{\varepsilon/8})\). Under the hypotheses of Theorem 44.4.2, if moreover \(crf\log r\ge x^{\varepsilon/8}\), Theorem 44.4.2 gives the third inequality of Theorem 44.4.1 with \(\eta_1=\exp(-crf\log r)\).

Problem 44.4.2. Suppose \(K_{uu'}=K^0_{uu'}+K''_{uu'}\) with \(K''_{uu'}=0\) for \(u\ne u'\) and \(\lVert K^0_{uu'}\rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\le(1+b^{-6})d_ud_{u'}/D^*\). Then the first inequality of Theorem 44.4.1 holds with \(\eta_0=b^{-6}\), for every \(x\ge1\).

Problem 44.4.3. In the toy of Section 44.3, with \(4\mid a\), the words \(j\) with \(4\mid j\) and \(2j<a\), and \(D^*=(a+1)/8\): the third inequality of Theorem 44.4.1 holds with \(\eta_1=7/(a+1)\), the left side of the first is \(0\), and the second fails for every \(e_0<9\).

In Chapter 43 the three inequalities are (e) of Theorem 43.3.1. The identification of the period maps with these Poincaré series, which is (d) there, is the subject of the following chapters.
