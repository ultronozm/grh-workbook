Prerequisites.

Modular forms for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\): \(q\)-expansions, the cusp form \(\Delta\) and the dimension formula [SeA, Ch. VII, §3]. The residue theorem and the mean value property of holomorphic functions. The power series \(J_\nu(x) = \sum_{j \ge 0} (-1)^j (x/2)^{\nu + 2j}/(j!\,\Gamma(\nu + j + 1))\). Gershgorin’s theorem on the eigenvalues of a matrix. From Chapter 10, The rank lemma and the zero estimate, Section 10.1 and the bound (10.1.1), and Theorem 7.3.1 of Chapter 7, for the closing paragraphs only.

Chapter 10 asked for a family of vectors in a space of automorphic forms whose Gram matrix is close to a scalar. Equidistribution of CM points gives such families only with a rate that amounts to a subconvex bound. There is a cheaper source. If the weight is large, Poincaré series of small index are almost orthogonal, and Petersson’s formula proves it with nothing more than a bound for a Bessel function. This chapter works it out on \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), where the vectors are holomorphic Poincaré series and their inner products are Fourier coefficients.

Throughout, \(k \ge 4\) is even, \(\Gamma := \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), \(\Gamma_\infty := \{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1 & n\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}\}\), \(j(\gamma,z) := cz + d\) for \(\gamma = \begin{pmatrix}a & b\\ c & d\end{pmatrix}\), \(e(z) := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\), and \(d\nu := y^{-2}\,dx\,dy\) on the upper half-plane \(\mathbb{H}\).

§11.1. Poincaré series Tag 22AZ

Let \(S_k\) be the space of cusp forms of weight \(k\) for \(\Gamma\), with the Petersson inner product \(\langle f, g\rangle := \int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}} f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,y^k\,d\nu(z)\). For \(f \in S_k\) write \(f(z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_f(n)\,e(nz)\). For \(m \ge 1\) the Poincaré series \[P_m(z) := \sum_{\gamma \in \Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma} j(\gamma,z)^{-k}\, e(m\gamma z)\] converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets, and \(P_m \in S_k\) [Iw].

Tag 22B2

Problem 11.1.1. For \(f \in S_k\) and \(m \ge 1\), \[\langle f, P_m\rangle = \frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi m)^{k-1}}\, a_f(m) .\]

Tag 22B3

Problem 11.1.2. The series \(P_m\), \(m \ge 1\), span \(S_k\).

So \(P_m = 0\) for \(k \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 14\}\), where \(S_k = 0\), although no term of the series vanishes. The first nonzero case is \(k = 12\), where \(S_{12} = \mathbb{C}\Delta\) with \(\Delta = \sum_{n \ge 1}\tau(n)\,e(nz)\).

Tag 22B4

Problem 11.1.3. For \(k = 12\), \[P_m = \frac{10!\;\tau(m)}{(4\pi m)^{11}\,\lVert\Delta\rVert^2}\,\Delta .\] In particular \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) are proportional.

So distinct Poincaré series need not be independent. Petersson’s formula, in the next section, measures how far they are from orthogonal.

§11.2. Petersson’s formula Tag 22B5

What is the Fourier expansion of \(P_m\)? For integers \(m, n\) and \(c \ge 1\) let \[S(m,n;c) := \sum_{\substack{d \bmod c\\ (d,c) = 1}} e\Bigl(\frac{m\bar d + nd}{c}\Bigr), \qquad d\bar d \equiv 1 \pmod c,\] be the Kloosterman sum. It is real, and \(S(m,n;c) = S(n,m;c)\).

Tag 22B6

Problem 11.2.1. The map \(\gamma \mapsto (c,d)\) induces a bijection from \(\Gamma_\infty\backslash\Gamma\) onto the set of pairs of coprime integers modulo \(\pm1\). If \(c \ne 0\), then \(\gamma z = \frac ac - \frac{1}{c(cz+d)}\).

Tag 22B7

Problem 11.2.2. Let \(\ell \ge 2\) be an integer, \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(y > 0\). Then \[\int_{\mathrm{i}y - \infty}^{\mathrm{i}y + \infty} z^{-\ell}\, e(-\beta z)\, dz = \begin{cases} (-2\pi\mathrm{i})^\ell\, \beta^{\ell-1}/(\ell-1)! & \beta > 0,\\ 0 & \beta \le 0. \end{cases}\]

Tag 22B8

Problem 11.2.3. Let \(\alpha > 0\), \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(y > 0\). Then \(\int_{\mathrm{i}y - \infty}^{\mathrm{i}y + \infty} z^{-k}\, e(-\alpha/z - \beta z)\, dz\) vanishes for \(\beta \le 0\), and for \(\beta > 0\) equals \[2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k} \Bigl(\frac\beta\alpha\Bigr)^{(k-1)/2} J_{k-1}\bigl(4\pi\sqrt{\alpha\beta}\bigr) .\]

Tag 22B9

Problem 11.2.4. \(P_m(z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} p_m(n)\, e(nz)\), where \[p_m(n) = \delta(m,n) + 2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k} \Bigl(\frac nm\Bigr)^{(k-1)/2} \sum_{c \ge 1} \frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}\, J_{k-1}\Bigl(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\Bigr) .\]

Problems 11.1.1 and 11.2.4 together give Petersson’s formula [Pe], [Iw], [ILS]. Let \[T_k(m,n) := 2\pi\mathrm{i}^{-k} \sum_{c \ge 1} \frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}\, J_{k-1}\Bigl(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\Bigr) ,\] a real symmetric function of \(m, n \ge 1\).

Tag 22BA

Theorem 11.2.1 (Petersson). For every orthonormal basis \((f_j)\) of \(S_k\) and all \(m, n \ge 1\), \[\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi\sqrt{mn})^{k-1}} \sum_j \overline{a_{f_j}(m)}\, a_{f_j}(n) = \delta(m,n) + T_k(m,n) .\]

Tag 22BB

Problem 11.2.5. For \(k \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 14\}\) and all \(m, n \ge 1\), \[\sum_{c \ge 1} \frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}\, J_{k-1}\Bigl(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\Bigr) = -\frac{\mathrm{i}^k}{2\pi}\,\delta(m,n) .\]

Numerically, for \(m = n = 1\) the partial sums over \(c \le 800\) reproduce these values to within \(3 \cdot 10^{-8}\) for \(k = 4\) and \(10^{-13}\) for \(k \ge 6\). For \(k = 12\) one finds \(1 + T_{12}(1,1) = 2.84028\ldots\), which by Theorem 11.2.1 is \(10!/\bigl((4\pi)^{11}\lVert\Delta\rVert^2\bigr)\), so that \(\lVert\Delta\rVert^2 = 1.03536\ldots \cdot 10^{-6}\).

§11.3. Near orthogonality Tag 22BC

Normalize the Poincaré series by \[\widetilde{P}_m := \frac{(4\pi m)^{(k-1)/2}}{\Gamma(k-1)^{1/2}}\, P_m ,\] and for \(M \ge 1\) let \(G_M\) be the \(M \times M\) Gram matrix \(\bigl(\langle \widetilde{P}_m, \widetilde{P}_n\rangle\bigr)_{1 \le m,n \le M}\).

Tag 22BD

Problem 11.3.1. \(\langle \widetilde{P}_m, \widetilde{P}_n\rangle = \delta(m,n) + T_k(m,n)\).

So \(G_M\) is the identity plus the matrix of Kloosterman terms, and the question is how small \(J_{k-1}\) is. We use Poisson’s integral \[J_\nu(x) = \frac{(x/2)^\nu}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\nu + \frac12)} \int_{-1}^1 (1 - t^2)^{\nu - 1/2} \cos(xt)\, dt \qquad (\nu > -\tfrac12,\ x > 0),\] see [DLMF].

Tag 22BE

Problem 11.3.2. For \(\nu > -\frac12\) and \(x > 0\), \(\lvert J_\nu(x)\rvert \le (x/2)^\nu/\Gamma(\nu + 1)\).

Tag 22BF

Problem 11.3.3. \(\lvert T_k(m,n)\rvert \le 2\pi\,\zeta(k-1)\,(2\pi\sqrt{mn})^{k-1}/(k-1)!\).

Tag 22BG

Problem 11.3.4. If \(1 \le M \le (k-1)/(4\pi e)\), then every eigenvalue of \(G_M\) lies within \(k\,2^{-k}\) of \(1\).

Problems 11.3.1–11.3.4 prove the following.

Tag 22BH

Theorem 11.3.1. If \(1 \le M \le (k-1)/(4\pi e)\), then \(\lVert G_M - 1\rVert \le k\,2^{-k}\). In particular \(P_1, \dots, P_M\) are linearly independent, and \(\dim S_k \ge \lfloor (k-1)/(4\pi e)\rfloor\).

Here \(e = 2.718\ldots\) is Euler’s number, and \(4\pi e = 34.15\ldots\). The threshold is not the true one. For \(x \le r\nu\) with \(r < 1\) fixed, the function \(J_\nu(x)\) is exponentially small in \(\nu\), and Siegel’s inequality makes this precise: for \(\nu \ge 0\) and \(0 < x \le 1\), \[\lvert J_\nu(\nu x)\rvert \le \Bigl(\frac{x\, e^{\sqrt{1 - x^2}}}{1 + \sqrt{1 - x^2}}\Bigr)^{\nu}\] [DLMF]. The base is increasing in \(x\) and equals \(1\) at \(x = 1\).

Tag 22BJ

Problem 11.3.5. Let \(0 < \theta < 1/(4\pi)\). There are \(\varrho < 1\) and \(C\), depending only on \(\theta\), such that \(\lVert G_M - 1\rVert \le C k\varrho^k\) whenever \(1 \le M \le \theta(k-1)\).

So for large \(k\) the \(\widetilde{P}_m\) with \(m \le \theta(k-1)\), for a fixed \(\theta < 1/(4\pi)\), are almost orthonormal. For \(m\) near \((k-1)/(4\pi)\) the argument \(4\pi m\) of the term \(c = 1\) of \(T_k(m,m)\) reaches the transition region \(x \approx \nu\) of \(J_\nu\), where \(J_\nu(x)\) is of size about \(\nu^{-1/3}\), and the diagonal entry is no longer exponentially close to \(1\). For \(k = 100\), where \((k-1)/(4\pi) = 7.87\ldots\) and \(\dim S_{100} = 8\), a computation of the Kloosterman terms gives the following. The eigenvalues of \(G_7\) lie in \([0.99999, 1.0094]\). Those of \(G_8\) lie in \([0.982, 1.808]\), so \(P_1, \dots, P_8\) are a basis of \(S_{100}\) but far from an orthogonal one. \(G_9\) is singular, as it must be.

§11.4. The dimension as formal degree times covolume Tag 22BK

How large a proportion of \(S_k\) does Theorem 11.3.1 fill? Let \(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}\) carry the measure \(\nu\).

Tag 22BL

Problem 11.4.1. \(\nu(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}) = \pi/3\).

Tag 22BM

Problem 11.4.2. \(\bigl\lvert \dim S_k - \frac{k-1}{12}\bigr\rvert \le \frac{13}{12}\) for every even \(k \ge 4\).

So the series of Problem 11.3.5 span a proportion close to \(12/(4\pi) = 3/\pi = 0.954\ldots\) of \(S_k\), and those of Theorem 11.3.1 a proportion \(3/(\pi e) = 0.351\ldots\). Where does \((k-1)/12\) come from? It is the product of \(\pi/3\) and a constant attached to the weight alone.

Let \(G := \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{R})\), acting on \(\mathbb{H}\), and fix the Haar measure \(dg\) on \(G\) with \(\int_G F(g\mathrm{i})\,dg = \int_\mathbb{H}F\,d\nu\); then \(\mathrm{PSO}(2)\) has volume \(1\), and \(\operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash G) = \pi/3\). Let \(\mathcal{A}_k\) be the space of holomorphic functions \(f\) on \(\mathbb{H}\) with \(\lVert f\rVert^2 := \int_\mathbb{H}\lvert f\rvert^2 y^k\,d\nu < \infty\), on which \(G\) acts unitarily by \((\pi(g)f)(z) := f(g^{-1}z)\,j(g^{-1},z)^{-k}\). This is the holomorphic discrete series of weight \(k\). For \(w \in \mathbb{H}\) put \[h_w(z) := \Bigl(\frac{z - \bar w}{2\mathrm{i}}\Bigr)^{-k} .\] For a unit vector \(u\) of an irreducible unitary representation \(\pi\) with square-integrable matrix coefficients, the formal degree \(d_\pi\) is defined by \(\int_G \lvert\langle\pi(g)u, u\rangle\rvert^2\,dg = 1/d_\pi\). It does not depend on \(u\), by the Schur orthogonality relations for such representations [HC]. We compute it with \(u\) proportional to \(h_\mathrm{i}\).

Tag 22BN

Problem 11.4.3. \(h_w \in \mathcal{A}_k\), and \(\langle f, h_\mathrm{i}\rangle = \frac{4\pi}{k-1}\, f(\mathrm{i})\) for every \(f \in \mathcal{A}_k\).

Tag 22BP

Problem 11.4.4. \(\pi(g)h_w = \overline{j(g,w)}^{\,-k}\, h_{gw}\) for \(g \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})\) and \(w \in \mathbb{H}\).

Tag 22BQ

Problem 11.4.5. Let \(u := h_\mathrm{i}/\lVert h_\mathrm{i}\rVert\), and \(w := g\mathrm{i}\). Then \(\lvert\langle\pi(g)u, u\rangle\rvert^2 = \bigl(4\operatorname{Im} w/\lvert w + \mathrm{i}\rvert^2\bigr)^k\), and the formal degree of \(\pi\) is \((k-1)/(4\pi)\).

With Problem 11.4.1 this gives \[d_\pi \cdot \operatorname{vol}(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash G) = \frac{k-1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac\pi3 = \frac{k-1}{12} ,\] which is \(\dim S_k\) up to the bounded error of Problem 11.4.2. This is not a coincidence. As in Problem 11.1.1, and using Problems 11.4.3 and 11.4.4, one checks that the reproducing kernel \(B(z,w) := \sum_j f_j(z)\overline{f_j(w)}\) of \(S_k\), for an orthonormal basis \((f_j)\), is \[B(z,w) = \frac{k-1}{4\pi} \sum_{\gamma \in \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})} j(\gamma,z)^{-k}\, h_w(\gamma z) .\] Since \(\dim S_k = \int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}} B(z,z)\,y^k\,d\nu(z)\), the term \(\gamma = 1\) contributes exactly the formal degree times the covolume, and by Problem 11.4.2 all other terms together contribute a bounded amount. On a definite unitary group the formal degree is the Weyl dimension of the representation at infinity, and the count of forms in Chapter 21 is the same product.

In the notation of Section 10.1, take \(\mathcal{H}= S_k\), \(D' = \dim S_k\), and the maps \(B_m \colon \mathbb{C}\to S_k\), \(1 \mapsto \widetilde{P}_m/\lVert\widetilde{P}_m\rVert\), for \(m\) in the range of Theorem 11.3.1, where \(\lVert\widetilde{P}_m\rVert\) is exponentially close to \(1\). The bound (10.1.1) asks for \(\lvert\langle B_m(1), B_n(1)\rangle\rvert^2 \le (1 + \eta_0)/D'\) for \(m \ne n\), about \(12/k\). Theorem 11.3.1 gives far more, an exponentially small bound, but only for \(m\) up to a fixed proportion of \(D'\). The images of these maps therefore do not fill \(S_k\), and the Poincaré series of larger index, which do fill it, are not nearly orthogonal. So Theorem 11.3.1 alone does not give the hypothesis of Theorem 7.3.1 for \(S_k\). The argument uses many families at once, each of small rank, whose mutual correlations are only as small as those of random subspaces.

The analogue in the argument.

In the reconstruction [Adr] of Alpöge’s argument [Alp], see also [Zh], \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) is replaced by a unitary group \(G = \mathrm{U}(V)\) of a hermitian space \(V\) over a CM extension \(E/F\), and \(\mathcal{A}_k\) by a holomorphic discrete series of large weight at each real place. The Fourier coefficient \(a_f(m)\), an integral of \(f\) against a character of the upper unipotent subgroup of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{R})\), becomes a Bessel period: an integral against a character of a subgroup \(S = \mathrm{U}(W) \ltimes N\), where \(W \subset V\) is a nondegenerate subspace and \(N\) is unipotent, as in the conjectures of Gan, Gross and Prasad [GGP]. To each word \(u\) of the construction corresponds a space of Bessel Poincaré series, obtained by summing over \(S(F)\backslash G(F)\) the analogue of \(j(\gamma,z)^{-k}e(m\gamma z)\), and unfolding their inner products is the analogue of Problem 11.1.1 [Adr, Prop. 14.5]. The near orthogonality of the Gram operators [Adr, Thm. 14.1], the bound (10.1.1) for families whose size grows with a parameter \(x\) and with \(\eta_0 \le \exp(-x^{c})\) for some \(c > 0\), is the analogue of Theorem 11.3.1, and as there no subconvex bound enters. The decay that replaces Problem 11.3.2 is that of the matrix coefficients of the discrete series, as in Problem 11.4.5, and the number \(D^*\) of [Adr] is the formal degree times the volume of \(G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})\) times the value at \(1\) of the level idempotent. The deduction of the near orthogonality, and of \(\dim\mathcal{H}\approx D^*\), from counting and pre-trace statements is carried out there, but these statements themselves are assumed there: in [Adr] they are [Adr, Hyps. 14.61 and 14.71–14.77]; see the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58. This completes the method. It needs maps whose values are divisible at the inert primes and of controlled size; the next part, from Chapter 12 on, finds the divisibility in theta functions with complex multiplication.
