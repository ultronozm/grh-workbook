Prerequisites.

Linear algebra over \(\mathbb{R}\) and \(\mathbb{C}\): the spectral theorem, the Hilbert–Schmidt norm \(\lVert A \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \operatorname{tr}(A^*A)\), exterior powers, and Hadamard’s inequality \(\lvert \det(v_1, \dots, v_n) \rvert \le \lVert v_1 \rVert \cdots \lVert v_n \rVert\). Lattices in a Euclidean space [Ne, Ch. I, §4]. Dedekind domains, the absolute norm of a fractional ideal, ramification indices and residue degrees, the Frobenius element of an unramified prime, and the product formula [Ne, Ch. I and Ch. III]. Legendre’s formula \(v_p(n!) = \sum_{e \ge 1} \lfloor n/p^e \rfloor\), Stirling’s formula, and the von Mangoldt function \(\Lambda\). No earlier chapter is used; one remark in Section 6.4 points to Chapter 16. The next chapter, The slope inequality in the mean, continues this one with many maps into one target.

§6.1. Divisibility against size Tag 226J

How can a divisibility statement bound a sum over primes? Chebyshev’s answer uses one integer [Ch]. Let \(\theta(x) := \sum_{p \le x} \log p\).

Tag 226K

Problem 6.1.1. Every prime \(p\) with \(n < p \le 2n\) divides \(\binom{2n}{n}\), and \(\binom{2n}{n} \le 4^n\). Hence \(\theta(2n) - \theta(n) \le n \log 4\).

The principle is the following. If a nonzero integer \(\Delta\) is divisible by \(p^{e_p}\) for each prime \(p\) in a set \(S\), then \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:det}\tag{1} \sum_{p \in S} e_p \log p \le \log \lvert \Delta \rvert . \end{equation}\]

For \(\Delta = n!\) both sides of (1) are known exactly.

Tag 226L

Problem 6.1.2. \(\log n! = \sum_{r \le n} \Lambda(r) \lfloor n/r \rfloor\).

By Stirling’s formula the left side is \(n\log n - n + O(\log n)\). Without the floor function the right side would be \(n \sum_{r \le n} \Lambda(r)/r\). The fractional parts contribute \(\sum_{r \le n} \Lambda(r) \{n/r\}\), which by Chebyshev’s bounds is of size \(n\), the size of the term \(-n\) on the left. So the identity cannot see \(\psi(n) - n\), where \(\psi(x) := \sum_{r \le x} \Lambda(r)\). To do better one needs an object whose exponent at \(p^e\) is a smooth function of \(p^e\), such as the ramp \((x/p^e - 1)_+\), where \(t_+ := \max(t, 0)\), and whose size is known with an error much smaller than its linear term.

The integer \(\Delta\) will be a minor of a matrix.

Tag 226M

Problem 6.1.3. Let \(A \in M_r(\mathbb{Z})\) with \(\det A \ne 0\), and suppose that the \(j\)th column \(A_j\) is divisible by the positive integer \(g_j\). Then \[g_1 \cdots g_r \le \lvert \det A \rvert \le \lVert A_1 \rVert \cdots \lVert A_r \rVert .\] For the columns \((6, 6)\) and \((10, 20)\) the three numbers are \(60\), \(60\) and \(60\sqrt{10}\).

The same principle holds over a number field \(K\), where the product formula accounts for the size of a number at every place. At a finite place \(v\) write \(\mathcal{O}_v\), \(\mathfrak{p}_v\) and \(q_v\) for the valuation ring, its maximal ideal and the size of its residue field, and let \(\lvert x \rvert_v := q_v^{-\operatorname{ord}_v x}\). At an archimedean place use the usual modulus. Let \(n_v := 2\) at complex places and \(n_v := 1\) at the other places. Then \(\sum_v n_v \log \lvert c \rvert_v = 0\) for \(c \in K^\times\). Hence, if \(\delta \in K^\times\) satisfies \(\lvert \delta \rvert_v \le \exp(b_v - g_v)\) at every place, where \(b_v\) and \(g_v\) are real and vanish for almost all \(v\), then \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:scalar}\tag{2} \sum_v n_v g_v \le \sum_v n_v b_v . \end{equation}\] Typically \(\delta\) is a minor of a matrix, \(b_v\) bounds it through the sizes of the entries, and \(g_v\) records an improvement from a congruence. The minor is chosen once over \(K\), but the basis in which one proves the congruence may vary with \(v\).

§6.2. A minor of a jet evaluation map Tag 226N

Where does a minor with many prime factors come from? One source is Frobenius. Let \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), so that a prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is inert and \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]/p\) is the field with \(p^2\) elements.

Tag 226P

Problem 6.2.1. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(u \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), then \(\bar u \equiv u^p \pmod p\).

Thus complex conjugation is the Frobenius at every inert prime. Let \(u_1, \dots, u_I \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), let \(\mathcal{T} \subset \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^2\) be a finite set of \(s\) exponents, and let \(J \ge 1\). On the span of the monomials \(X^a Y^b\), \((a,b) \in \mathcal{T}\), consider the jet evaluation map \[\mathrm{ev} \colon f \longmapsto \bigl( (\partial_X^j f)(u_i, \bar u_i) \bigr)_{1 \le i \le I,\ 0 \le j < J} .\] Its matrix has entries in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\). Let \(g := X^p - Y\).

Tag 226Q

Problem 6.2.2. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). For \(f \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}][X, Y]\), \(u \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and integers \(j, k \ge 0\), the number \(\bigl(\partial_X^j (g^k f)\bigr)(u, \bar u)\) is divisible by \(p^k\).

Tag 226R

Problem 6.2.3. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), and suppose that \((a - p, b + 1) \in \mathcal{T}\) whenever \((a, b) \in \mathcal{T}\) and \(a \ge p\). Then every \(s \times s\) minor of the matrix of \(\mathrm{ev}\) is divisible by \(p^{N_p}\), where \(N_p := \sum_{(a,b) \in \mathcal{T}} \lfloor a/p \rfloor\). The set \(\mathcal{T} = \{(a,b) : a + 2b \le N\}\) satisfies the hypothesis for every \(p\).

Let \(\beta := \max(1, \lvert u_1 \rvert, \dots, \lvert u_I \rvert)\), \(D := \sum_{\mathcal{T}} (a + b)\) and \(A_* := \max(\{1\} \cup \{a : (a,b) \in \mathcal{T}\})\). An entry in the column of \(X^aY^b\) has absolute value at most \(A_*^{J-1}\beta^{a+b}\), so every \(s \times s\) minor has absolute value at most \(s!\,A_*^{s(J-1)}\beta^D\). With Problem 6.2.3, the inequality (2) for one nonzero minor proves the following.

Tag 226S

Theorem 6.2.1. Suppose that \(\mathrm{ev}\) is injective, and let \(P\) be a set of primes \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) for which \(\mathcal{T}\) satisfies the hypothesis of Problem 6.2.3. Then \[\sum_{p \in P} \log p \sum_{(a,b) \in \mathcal{T}} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{a}{p} \Bigr\rfloor \le D \log\beta + \log s! + s(J-1)\log A_* .\]

Over a function field this is the mechanism of Stepanov’s proof of the Riemann hypothesis for curves [Bom].

§6.3. Normed spaces and slopes Tag 226T

The minor \(\delta\) of Section 6.2 is the top exterior power of a linear map, written in chosen bases. To let the bases vary with the place, one puts norms on the spaces.

Let \(K\) be a number field, with the conventions of Section 6.1. A normed space over \(K\) is a finite-dimensional \(K\)-vector space \(V\) with a norm on each \(V_v := V \otimes_K K_v\): a Euclidean or Hermitian norm at each archimedean place, and at each finite place the sup norm of a full \(\mathcal{O}_v\)-lattice \(L_v\), the unit lattice. Almost everywhere \(L_v\) is the \(\mathcal{O}_v\)-span of a fixed basis. Subspaces carry the restricted norms, quotients the quotient norms, the dual \(V^\vee\) the dual norms, and exterior powers the induced norms. For \(e \ne 0\) in \(V\) let \(h(e) := \sum_v n_v \log \lVert e \rVert_v\), and for a nonzero linear map \(\varphi \colon V \to F\) of normed spaces let \[h(\varphi) := \sum_v n_v \log \lVert \varphi \rVert_v ,\] with the operator norms, and \(h_\infty(\varphi)\) for the sum over the archimedean places alone. For \(r = \dim V > 0\) and a generator \(\omega\) of \(\det V := \wedge^r V\), let \[\deg V := -h(\omega) , \qquad \mu(V) := \deg V / r .\] By the product formula \(\deg V\) does not depend on \(\omega\). These are the degree and the slope of \(V\). We follow Bost [Bo1] and do not divide by \([K:\mathbb{Q}]\).

Tag 226U

Problem 6.3.1. Over \(\mathbb{Q}\), the lattice \(L := V \cap \bigcap_p L_p\) has \(L \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p = L_p\) for every \(p\), and \(\deg V = -\log \operatorname{covol}(L)\).

So over \(\mathbb{Q}\) a normed space is a Euclidean lattice.

Tag 226V

Problem 6.3.2. Let \(\tau \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0\), and give \(\mathbb{C}= \mathbb{R}^2\) its usual norm. Then the Euclidean lattice \(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\) has \(\deg(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau) = -\log \operatorname{Im}\tau\). If \(M \subseteq L\) is a sublattice of index \(N\), then \(\deg M = \deg L - \log N\). Multiplying the norm of \(L\) by \(c > 0\) subtracts \(r \log c\) from \(\deg L\).

Tag 226W

Problem 6.3.3. If \(U \subseteq V\) is a subspace, then \(\deg V = \deg U + \deg(V/U)\).

The domain of the slope method is a lattice of sections of a line bundle, or of automorphic forms. The simplest case is the projective line. For \(D \ge 0\) let \(E_D\) be the lattice of homogeneous polynomials \(f \in \mathbb{Z}[x, y]\) of degree \(D\), with \[\lVert f \rVert^2 := \int_\mathbb{C}\frac{\lvert f(z, 1) \rvert^2}{(1 + \lvert z \rvert^2)^D} \, \frac{d A(z)}{\pi (1 + \lvert z \rvert^2)^2} .\] This is the \(L^2\) norm of a section of \(\mathcal{O}(D)\) for the Fubini–Study metric and the Fubini–Study probability measure.

Tag 226X

Problem 6.3.4. The monomials are orthogonal, and \(\lVert x^a y^{D-a} \rVert^2 = a!\,(D-a)!/(D+1)!\).

Tag 226Y

Problem 6.3.5. \(\mu(E_D) = D/4 + O(\log D)\).

Problem 6.3.5 is the simplest case of the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem [GS2, Thm. 8].

Tag 226Z

Problem 6.3.6. Let \(\mathfrak{a}\) be a fractional ideal of \(K\), with unit lattices \(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_v\) and \(\lVert x \rVert_v = \lvert x \rvert_v\) at the archimedean places. Then \(\deg \mathfrak{a}= -\log N\mathfrak{a}\). Replacing every unit lattice \(L_v\) of \(V\) by \(\mathfrak{a}L_v\) lowers \(\mu(V)\) by \(\log N\mathfrak{a}\).

To compare slopes over fields of different degrees, one normalizes by the degree, and many authors also use squared norms. Let \[\mu^\sharp := \frac{2}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\, \mu , \qquad h^\sharp := \frac{2}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\, h .\] For a prime \(\mathfrak{p}\) of \(K\) above \(p\), with ramification index \(e_\mathfrak{p}\), let \(c_\mathfrak{p}:= 2 e_\mathfrak{p}\log N\mathfrak{p}/[K:\mathbb{Q}]\).

Tag 2272

Problem 6.3.7. \(\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\mid p} c_\mathfrak{p}= 2\log p\). If every unit lattice \(L_\mathfrak{p}\), \(\mathfrak{p}\mid p\), is replaced by \(p^{-e} L_\mathfrak{p}\), then \(\mu^\sharp\) increases by \(2e\log p\).

So a divisibility by \(p^e\) at every place above \(p\) is worth \(2e\log p\) in the normalized slopes.

§6.4. The height inequality for an image Tag 2273

How does a linear map compare the slopes of its source and target? Let \(\varphi \colon V \to F\) be an injective map of normed spaces with image \(U\), and let \(r = \dim V > 0\). On determinant lines it induces \(\wedge^r\varphi \colon \det V \to \det U\).

Tag 2274

Problem 6.4.1. \(\sum_v n_v \log \lVert \wedge^r \varphi \rVert_v = r\bigl(\mu(V) - \mu(U)\bigr)\), and \(\lVert \wedge^r\varphi \rVert_v \le \lVert \varphi \rVert_v^r\) at every place.

Summing local bounds gives the following.

Tag 2275

Theorem 6.4.1. Suppose that \(\log \lVert \wedge^r\varphi \rVert_v \le b_v - g_v\) at every place, with \(b_v = g_v = 0\) almost everywhere. Then \[\sum_v n_v g_v \le \sum_v n_v b_v + r\bigl(\mu(U) - \mu(V)\bigr) .\] In particular \(\mu(V) \le \mu(U) + h(\varphi) \le \mu_{\max}(F) + h(\varphi)\), where \(\mu_{\max}(F)\) is the supremum of the slopes of the nonzero subspaces of \(F\).

The last inequality is the slope inequality of Bost [Bo1], [Bo2, Prop. 4.5]. For \(V = F = K\) with the standard norms and \(\varphi\) the multiplication by \(\delta\), Theorem 6.4.1 is (2).

Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the local norms at the primes are divisibilities.

Tag 2276

Problem 6.4.2. Let \(L\) and \(M\) be Euclidean lattices and \(\varphi \colon L_\mathbb{Q}\to M_\mathbb{Q}\) nonzero. Then \(\lVert \varphi \rVert_p = p^{-e}\) for the largest integer \(e\) with \(\varphi(L) \subseteq p^e (M \otimes \mathbb{Z}_{(p)})\). In particular \(\sum_p \log\lVert\varphi\rVert_p \le 0\) when \(\varphi(L) \subseteq M\), with every divisibility of the values making it smaller.

When the ranks agree, an identity replaces the inequality.

Tag 2277

Problem 6.4.3. Suppose that \(\dim L_\mathbb{Q}= \dim M_\mathbb{Q}\), that \(\varphi\) is injective, and that \(\varphi(L) \subseteq M\). Then \[\deg M - \deg L = \log [M : \varphi(L)] - \log \lvert \det \varphi_\mathbb{R}\rvert ,\] where \(\det \varphi_\mathbb{R}\) is computed in orthonormal bases.

Where do divisible values and controlled norms come from together? The simplest example is the \(a\)th derivative at \(0\) of a polynomial with integer coefficients, which is divisible by \(a!\), on a space of polynomials with a Gaussian norm. For \(f \in \mathbb{C}[z]\) let \[\lVert f \rVert^2 := \frac{1}{\pi} \int_\mathbb{C}\lvert f(z) \rvert^2 e^{-\lvert z \rvert^2} \, d A(z) .\]

Tag 2278

Problem 6.4.4. The monomials are orthogonal, and \(\lVert z^a \rVert^2 = a!\).

Tag 2279

Problem 6.4.5. Let \(a < D\), let \(L\) be the lattice of polynomials in \(\mathbb{Z}[z]\) of degree less than \(D\) with the norm above, and let \(\varphi(f) := f^{(a)}(0) \in \mathbb{Z}\), with \(\mathbb{Z}\) the standard lattice. Then \(\sum_p \log\lVert\varphi\rVert_p = -\log a!\) and \(h_\infty(\varphi) = \tfrac12 \log a!\).

By Problem 6.1.2 the gain \(\log a!\) at the finite places is a sum over primes, and the cost at infinity is half of it. In the applications the function is a theta function with complex multiplication, and at a supersingular prime the divisibility that one proves is only about \(a \sum_{e\ \mathrm{odd}} p^{-e}\) of the \(v_p(a!) \approx a \sum_{e \ge 1} p^{-e}\) (Chapter 16, Theorem 16.4.1).

§6.5. Divisibility of a pairing as a change of lattice Tag 227A

In the applications the values of the map are periods, that is, values of a bilinear pairing. Let \(P \colon V \times W \to K\) be a bilinear pairing of normed spaces, with unit lattices \(L_v\) and \(R_v\), and let \(\varphi_P \colon V \to W^\vee\) be the induced map.

Tag 227B

Problem 6.5.1. At a finite place, if \(P \ne 0\), then \(\lVert \varphi_P \rVert_v = q_v^{-k}\) for the largest \(k\) with \(P(L_v, R_v) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_v^k\). Multiplying the norms of \(W\) at the archimedean places by constants \(\lambda_v > 0\) adds \(\sum_v n_v \log\lambda_v\) to \(\mu(W^\vee)\) and subtracts it from \(h(\varphi_P)\).

So only \(h(\varphi_P) + \mu(W^\vee)\) is independent of the normalization of \(W\). To estimate \(P\) at different places one may use different factorizations.

Tag 227C

Problem 6.5.2. Suppose that \(P(x, w) = c_v\, P'_v(A_{m,v} \cdots A_{1,v}\, x,\ C_{m,v} \cdots C_{1,v}\, w)\) at a place \(v\), where \(c_v \in K_v^\times\), the intermediate spaces carry norms and \(P'_v\) has the bilinear operator norm. Then \[\log \lVert \varphi_P \rVert_v \le \log \lvert c_v \rvert_v + \log \lVert P'_v \rVert_v + \sum_{j=1}^m \bigl( \log \lVert A_{j,v} \rVert_v + \log \lVert C_{j,v} \rVert_v \bigr) .\] At a finite place, an inclusion \(A_{j,v} L_{j-1} \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_v^k L_j\), where \(L_{j-1}\) and \(L_j\) are the unit lattices of the source and the target of \(A_{j,v}\), contributes \(-k\log q_v\), for every integer \(k\).

Thus local gains add along a chain of maps, while the slopes of \(V\) and \(W^\vee\) enter only once. Now record a gain as a change of the unit lattice of \(V\).

Tag 227D

Problem 6.5.3. Let \(v\) be finite, let \(L'_v \subseteq L_v\) be a full sublattice, and let \(k\) be an integer with \(P(L'_v, R_v) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_v^k\). Replace \(L_v\) by \(N_v := \mathfrak{p}_v^{-k} L'_v\). Then the new norm of \(\varphi_P\) at \(v\) is at most \(1\), and \(\deg V\) increases by \(\log q_v \bigl( k\dim V - \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal{O}_v}(L_v/L'_v) \bigr)\).

Problem 6.5.3 and Theorem 6.4.1 prove the following.

Tag 227E

Theorem 6.5.1. Let \(P \colon V \times W \to K\) be a pairing with \(\varphi_P\) injective and \(r = \dim V > 0\). For each finite place \(v\) let \(L'_v \subseteq L_v\) be a full sublattice and \(k_v\) an integer with \(P(L'_v, R_v) \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_v^{k_v}\), where \(L'_v = L_v\) and \(k_v = 0\) almost everywhere. Then \[\sum_{v \nmid \infty} k_v \log q_v \le h_\infty(\varphi_P) + \mu_{\max}(W^\vee) - \mu(V) + \frac1r \sum_{v \nmid \infty} \log\, [L_v : L'_v] .\]

For \(V = W = K\) with the standard norms, \(P(x, y) = cxy\) with \(0 \ne c \in \mathcal{O}_K\), \(L'_v = L_v\) and \(k_v = \operatorname{ord}_v(c)\), this is the product formula for \(c\), with equality. The left side is the unknown sum over primes. The right side involves \(\mu_{\max}\) of the target, so a single map sees only the part of the target that contains its image; the sequel removes this by using many maps.
