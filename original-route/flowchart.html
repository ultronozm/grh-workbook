How the chapters fit together

Back to the index.

Each box is a chapter and links to it. Parts I–V are the lower columns, read from the bottom up: a chapter sits above the chapters it builds on. Above them are the later parts, which state the ingredients in general, each over the Part that treats it in its simplest setting. In each later part, the chapters that supply inputs to the assembly are on top and the chapters that develop those inputs from the Lean code are below them. On top of everything is the final chapter, Assembly at one scale. Hover over a chapter to pick out its arrows.

Legend.

  • Dark arrow: the later chapter builds on the earlier one (from its Prerequisites).
  • Light dashed arrow: a chapter of Parts I–V is a simplest incarnation of a chapter of a later part (from its Prerequisites, its Simplest incarnation remarks, and the map of the inputs in Assembly at one scale).
  • Pale boxes: chapters of a later part without a Deliverable paragraph, which set up the objects or develop inputs in the form the Lean formalization proves; arrows to and from them come from Prerequisites.
  • Red arrow: a chapter of a later part supplies the inputs named on it to the assembly (where its Deliverable paragraph and the map of the inputs agree). The conditions (K1)–(K8) of a chain record are counted as the inputs (A1)–(A7) they give.
  • An arrow is left out when a path of other arrows already leads from the one chapter to the other.
  • Not supplied by any one chapter: (V1), (W4); see the map of the inputs in Assembly at one scale.

Sample seminar plans

These are suggestions drafted by AI agents and have not been tested in a seminar. The sessions are 90 minutes.

One semester, 13 sessions (main line)

Parts IV–V enter only through the inputs listed in §58.2 of Chapter 58, Assembly at one scale, stated as black boxes.

  1. Chapter 1, From zeros to a one-sided ramp sum, §1.1–1.3 (state Thms 1.4.1 and 1.5.1)
  2. Chapter 2, Inert primes and Estimate B (state Thm 3.3.1)
  3. Chapter 4, Fourier analysis on the Galois group, §4.1–4.4 (state Behrend, Thms 5.2.1–5.2.2)
  4. Chapter 6, The slope method
  5. Chapter 7, The slope inequality in the mean
  6. Chapter 8, The chain and the square root (Thm 8.3.1 as motivation, then §8.4–8.5)
  7. Chapter 10, The rank lemma and the zero estimate (state Thm 11.3.1)
  8. Chapter 14, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime, with a recap of Chapter 12, Complex tori, theta functions and periods
  9. Chapter 16, The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime, §16.2–16.4
  10. Chapter 17, The isotropic plane, the gain at the odd levels (state Thm 18.3.1, the credit at every inert prime)
  11. Chapter 19, Hecke nondegeneracy on the tree (the loss)
  12. Chapter 23, Definite theta lifts and their norms, with Chapter 21, Masses and the count of forms as reading
  13. Chapter 58, Assembly at one scale, §58.1 and §58.3–58.5

Optional on a first pass: Chapters 3, 5, 9, 11–13, 15, 18, 20, 22, 24–34, S1 and S2.

The later parts state the ingredients in general; their main chapters, Chapters 36, 37, 43, 47 and 52, are reading for the presenters of the last two sessions rather than sessions of their own.

A second semester could take an arithmetic track: Chapters 31, 32 (two sessions), 33 (two sessions) and 34, then the side series: S1, The sign-only proof of the Riemann hypothesis for curves (two sessions) with S2, Deligne's bound by the sign-only method.

Practical notes
