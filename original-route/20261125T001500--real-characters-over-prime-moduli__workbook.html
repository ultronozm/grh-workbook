Prerequisites.

Chapter 2, Inert primes and Estimate B, Section 2.2: the coefficients \(a_n(x) = \Lambda(n)\min(1, x/n)\), the sums \(S_Z(x, \chi) = \sum_{n \le Z} a_n(x)\chi(n)\) and \(t_\chi(Z) = \sum_{n > Z} \Lambda(n)\chi(n)/n\) and the identity \(G_\chi(x) = -S_Z(x, \chi) - x\, t_\chi(Z)\), the bound in Problem 2.2.5, Problems 2.2.6 and 2.2.7, and the proof of Theorem 2.2.2; also \(\Psi_\chi(t) = \sum_{n \le t} \Lambda(n)\chi(n)\) from Section 2.1. Chebyshev’s bounds and the prime number theorem [Dav, Ch. 18]. From Davenport [Dav]: primitive real characters and Kronecker symbols (Ch. 5), the functional equation (Ch. 9), zero-free regions and the exceptional zero (Ch. 14), the number of zeros (Ch. 16), the explicit formula for \(\Psi_\chi(x)\), there \(\psi(x, \chi)\) (Ch. 19) and the prime number theorem for characters (Ch. 20).

Chapter 2 obtained a good modulus \(q\) by averaging \(\chi_q(n) = \bigl(\frac nq\bigr)\) over primes \(q \in (U, 2U]\) and letting \(U \to \infty\) with everything else fixed. How large must the primes be, as a function of the window \([Y, Y^A]\)? The average of \(\bigl(\frac sq\bigr)\) over \(q\) is a sum over primes of a character of conductor about \(s\), and it is small unless that character has a real zero near \(1\). By Page’s theorem at most one \(s\) is affected. The tail \(t_{\chi_q}(Z)\) needs a zero-free box for \(L(s, \chi_q)\), and a density estimate gives one for all but few \(q\). The answer is that \(\log q\) of size \((\log Y)^3\) suffices.

Throughout, \(Q\) is large, \(\mathcal{P}\) is the set of primes in \((Q, 2Q]\) (so \(Q\) and \(\mathcal{P}\) are the \(U\) and \(\mathcal P(U)\) of Chapter 2), and for squarefree \(s \ge 1\) \[\Sigma(s) := \sum_{q \in \mathcal{P}} \Bigl(\frac{s}{q}\Bigr) .\] Constants \(c, C\) are positive and absolute unless their dependence is shown, and may change from line to line.

§3.1. The symbol \((s/q)\) as a function of \(q\) Tag 223V

For squarefree \(s > 1\) let \(D_s := s\) if \(s \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) and \(D_s := 4s\) otherwise; this is the discriminant of \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt s)\). Let \(\omega_s(n) := \bigl(\frac{D_s}{n}\bigr)\) be the Kronecker symbol. It is a primitive real character of conductor \(D_s\), and every primitive real character is a Kronecker symbol of a fundamental discriminant [Dav, Ch. 5].

Tag 223W

Problem 3.1.1. For squarefree \(s > 1\) and every odd prime \(q \nmid s\), \(\bigl(\frac sq\bigr) = \omega_s(q)\). The character \(\omega_s\) is even, and \(s \mapsto \omega_s\) is injective.

Tag 223X

Problem 3.1.2. \(\omega_2\) has conductor \(8\) and \(\omega_2(q) = 1\) exactly when \(q \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 8\); \(\omega_3\) has conductor \(12\) and \(\omega_3(q) = 1\) exactly when \(q \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{12}\); and \(\omega_5(q) = \bigl(\frac q5\bigr)\).

Two inputs are used. The first is the prime number theorem for characters [Dav, Ch. 20], [Dav, Ch. 14]: there are \(c_1 \in (0, \frac12]\) and \(C_1\) such that for every primitive character \(\chi\) modulo \(N \ge 3\) and every \(u \ge 2\) with \(\log N \le \sqrt{\log u}\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:pnt}\tag{1} \Bigl\lvert \Psi_\chi(u) + \frac{u^\beta}{\beta} \Bigr\rvert \le C_1\, u\, e^{-c_1\sqrt{\log u}} , \end{equation}\] where the term with \(\beta\) is present only when \(\chi\) is real and \(L(s, \chi)\) has a real zero \(\beta > 1 - c_1/\log N\); there is at most one such zero. The second is Page’s theorem [Pa], [Dav, Ch. 14]: there is \(c_2 > 0\) such that for every \(R \ge 3\) at most one primitive real character of conductor at most \(R\) has a real zero in \((1 - c_2/\log R, 1)\). Call it the exceptional character at level \(R\).

Fix \(R \ge 3\) with \(\log(4R) \le \sqrt{\log Q}\). If the exceptional character at level \(4R\) exists and equals \(\omega_s\) for some squarefree \(s \le R\), let \(s^*\) be this \(s\); by Problem 3.1.1 there is at most one. If there is none, a condition \(s \ne s^*\) is empty.

Tag 223Y

Problem 3.1.3. Let \(1 < s \le R\) be squarefree with \(s \ne s^*\). Then \(\lvert \Psi_{\omega_s}(u) \rvert \le C u\, e^{-c\sqrt{\log Q}}\) for \(Q \le u \le 2Q\).

Tag 223Z

Problem 3.1.4. For \(s\) as in Problem 3.1.3, \(\lvert \Sigma(s) \rvert \le C \lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert\, e^{-c\sqrt{\log Q}}\).

Tag 2242

Theorem 3.1.1. There are \(c_0, C_0 > 0\) such that, with \(\eta_Q := C_0 e^{-c_0\sqrt{\log Q}}\), \(\lvert \Sigma(s) \rvert \le \eta_Q \lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert\) for every squarefree \(s\) with \(1 < s \le R\) and \(s \ne s^*\).

The exception is real. An exceptional zero very close to \(1\) makes almost every prime of \(\mathcal{P}\) inert in \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s^*})\):

Tag 2243

Problem 3.1.5. Let \(s\) vary with \(Q\), with \(\log(4s) \le \sqrt{\log Q}\), and suppose \(L(\sigma, \omega_s)\) has a real zero \(\beta\) with \((1 - \beta)\log Q \to 0\). Then \(\Sigma(s) = -\lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert\,(1 + o(1))\) as \(Q \to \infty\).

By Theorem 9.3.1 of Chapter 9 no such \(s\) exists, and \(s^*\) is not defined once \(c_2\) is at most half the constant \(c\) there, since \(D_s \le 4R\). We keep \(s^*\), as an exceptional set of at most one element, so that this chapter does not depend on Chapter 9.

§3.2. The mean square Tag 2244

Let \(Z < Q\) and let \(c_n\), for \(1 \le n \le Z\), be complex numbers. Write \(n n' = \square\) if \(n n'\) is a square, and \(n n' \in s^* \square\) if \(n n'/s^*\) is a square; terms involving \(s^*\) are absent if \(s^*\) is not defined.

Tag 2245

Problem 3.2.1. Suppose \(Z^2 \le R\). Then \[\frac{1}{\lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{P}} \Bigl\lvert \sum_{n \le Z} c_n \Bigl(\frac{n}{q}\Bigr) \Bigr\rvert^2 \le \sum_{n n' = \square} \lvert c_n c_{n'} \rvert + \sum_{n n' \in s^* \square} \lvert c_n c_{n'} \rvert + \eta_Q \Bigl( \sum_{n \le Z} \lvert c_n \rvert \Bigr)^2 .\]

We apply this to \(c_n = \Lambda(n) \kappa(n) \min(1, y/n)\) with \(\lvert \kappa(n) \rvert \le 1\) and \(3 \le y \le Z\), so that \(\lvert c_n \rvert \le a_n(y)\). The first sum is at most \(C y \log y\) by the bound in Problem 2.2.5.

Tag 2246

Problem 3.2.2. For every squarefree \(s^* > 1\), \[\sum_{n n' \in s^* \square} a_n(y)\, a_{n'}(y) \le C \sqrt y \log y .\]

Tag 2247

Problem 3.2.3. \(\sum_{n \le Z} a_n(y) \le C y \log Z\).

Problems 3.2.1–3.2.3 give the following.

Tag 2248

Theorem 3.2.1. Let \(3 \le y \le Z\), take \(R := Z^2\), and suppose \(\log(4 Z^2) \le \sqrt{\log Q}\) and \(\eta_Q\, y \log^2 Z \le 1\). For all \(\kappa(n)\) with \(\lvert \kappa(n) \rvert \le 1\), \[\frac{1}{\lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{P}} \Bigl\lvert \sum_{n \le Z} \Lambda(n) \kappa(n) \min\Bigl(1, \frac yn\Bigr) \Bigl(\frac{n}{q}\Bigr) \Bigr\rvert^2 \le C y \log y .\]

For \(Q = 10^6\), \(\kappa = 1\), \(y = 100\) and \(Z = 1000\) the left side is \(988.3\), and the square pairs \(n n' = \square\) contribute \(1034.1\).

By Problem 2.2.6, \(y D(y)\) is a sum of the same form with \(\kappa(n) = 1\) for \(n > y\) and \(\kappa(n) = 0\) otherwise. The proof of Theorem 2.2.2, with Theorem 3.2.1 in place of Problem 2.2.5, therefore gives:

Tag 2249

Theorem 3.2.2. Let \(0 < \tau \le \frac12\), \(A \ge 1\), \(Y \ge 3\) and \(Z \ge 2Y^A\), take \(R := Z^2\), and suppose \(\log(4Z^2) \le \sqrt{\log Q}\) and \(2\eta_Q\, Y^A \log^2 Z \le 1\). The number of \(q \in \mathcal{P}\) with \(\lvert S_Z(y, \chi_q) \rvert > \frac12 y^{1/2 + \tau}\) for some \(y \in [Y, Y^A]\) is at most \(C_\tau \lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert\, Y^{-2\tau} \log Y\).

Heath-Brown’s large sieve for real characters [HB, Thm. 1] gives, with no information about zeros, \[\sideset{}{^*}\sum_{m \le V} \Bigl\lvert \sideset{}{^*}\sum_{n \le W} a_n \Bigl(\frac{n}{m}\Bigr) \Bigr\rvert^2 \le C_\varepsilon (VW)^\varepsilon (V + W) \sum_n \lvert a_n \rvert^2 ,\] the sums running over odd squarefree \(m\) and \(n\). Here the loss \(Q^\varepsilon = e^{\varepsilon (\log Y)^3}\) would be fatal, and Theorem 3.1.1 avoids it at the price of the exceptional \(s^*\).

§3.3. Zero-free boxes for most \(q\) Tag 224A

It remains to bound \(x\, t_{\chi_q}(Z)\). For fixed \(\chi\) the tail \(t_\chi(Z)\) tends to \(0\) at a rate set by the zeros of \(L(s, \chi)\) near \(\operatorname{Re}s = 1\) with \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert\) up to a power of the conductor. For one \(q\) nothing better than (1) is known; for most \(q\) a density estimate does better. Gallagher’s density estimate [Gal], in the form of [TZ2]: there are \(c_3 \ge 1\) and \(C_3\) such that for \(T \ge 3\) and \(0 \le \sigma \le 1\) \[\sum_{N \le T} \ \sideset{}{^*}\sum_{\chi \bmod N} \#\{\rho = \beta + i\gamma : L(\rho, \chi) = 0,\ \beta > \sigma,\ \lvert \gamma \rvert \le T\} \le C_3\, T^{c_3(1 - \sigma)} ,\] where \(\sum^*\) runs over primitive characters and zeros are counted with multiplicity. For other density estimates of large sieve type see [Mon, Ch. 12]. Put \(\delta_0 := 1/(2c_3)\), and call \(q \in \mathcal{P}\) good if \(L(s, \chi_q)\) has no zero with \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1 - \delta_0\) and \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le 2Q\).

Tag 224B

Problem 3.3.1. At most \(C_3 (2Q)^{1/2}\) primes \(q \in \mathcal{P}\) are not good.

Tag 224C

Problem 3.3.2. If \(q\) is good, every zero \(\rho = \beta + i\gamma\) of \(L(s, \chi_q)\) in \(0 < \beta < 1\) with \(\lvert \gamma \rvert \le 2Q\) has \(\delta_0 \le \beta \le 1 - \delta_0\). In particular \(\lvert \rho \rvert \ge \delta_0\), and for \(Q \ge e^{c_1/\delta_0}\) there is no real zero in \((1 - c_1/\log q, 1)\).

The explicit formula [Dav, Chs. 16 and 19]: there is \(C_4\) such that for every primitive \(\chi\) modulo \(N \ge 3\) the number of zeros with \(\lvert \gamma - t \rvert \le 1\) is at most \(C_4 \log(N(\lvert t \rvert + 2))\), and, if \(L(s, \chi)\) has no real zero in \((1 - c_1/\log N, 1)\), then for \(2 \le T \le u\) \[\Bigl\lvert \Psi_\chi(u) + \sum_{\lvert \gamma \rvert < T} \frac{u^\rho}{\rho} \Bigr\rvert \le C_4 \Bigl( \frac{u \log^2(Nu)}{T} + u^{1/4} \log u \Bigr) .\]

In Problems 3.3.3–3.3.6, \(q \in \mathcal{P}\) is good, \(Q \ge e^{c_1/\delta_0}\), and \(\lambda := \log q\).

Tag 224D

Problem 3.3.3. For \(2^{1/\delta_0} \le u \le e^{\lambda^2}\), \(\lvert \Psi_{\chi_q}(u) \rvert \le C \lambda^4 (u^{1-\delta_0} + u/Q)\).

Tag 224E

Problem 3.3.4. For \(u \ge e^{\lambda^2}\), \(\lvert \Psi_{\chi_q}(u) \rvert \le 2u^{1-\delta_0} + C_1 u\, e^{-c_1\sqrt{\log u}}\).

Tag 224F

Problem 3.3.5. For every primitive nonprincipal \(\chi\) and \(Z \ge 1\), \(t_\chi(Z) = -\Psi_\chi(Z)/Z + \int_Z^\infty \Psi_\chi(u)\, u^{-2}\, d u\).

Tag 224G

Problem 3.3.6. For \(2^{1/\delta_0} \le Z \le e^{\lambda^2}\), \(\lvert t_{\chi_q}(Z) \rvert \le C\bigl( \lambda^6 (Z^{-\delta_0} + Q^{-1}) + \lambda e^{-c_1\lambda} \bigr)\).

Now choose the parameters. Let \(A \ge 1\) and \(0 < \tau \le \frac12\), and put \[X := Y^A, \qquad Z := X^{2/\delta_0}, \qquad \log Q := (\log Y)^3 .\]

Tag 224H

Problem 3.3.7. For \(Y \ge Y_0(A)\): (i) the hypotheses of Theorem 3.2.2 hold, and \(Q \ge e^{c_1/\delta_0}\); (ii) \(y\, \lvert t_{\chi_q}(Z) \rvert \le 1\) for every good \(q\) and every \(y \le X\).

Combining Theorem 3.2.2, Problems 3.3.1 and 3.3.7 and the identity \(G_\chi = -S_Z - x\, t_\chi(Z)\) proves the following simple form of Estimate B.

Tag 224J

Theorem 3.3.1. Let \(A \ge 1\) and \(0 < \tau \le \frac12\). For \(Y \ge Y_0(A, \tau)\) and \(\log Q = (\log Y)^3\), all \(q \in \mathcal{P}\) outside a set of at most \(Y^{-\tau} \lvert \mathcal{P}\rvert\) elements satisfy: \(L(s, \chi_q) \ne 0\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s > 1 - \delta_0\), \(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert \le 2Q\); and \(\lvert G_{\chi_q}(y) \rvert \le y^{1/2 + \tau}\) for every \(y \in [Y, Y^A]\).

The exponent \(3\) has room. Problem 3.1.3 needs \(\log Q \ge (\log 4R)^2\), of order \((\log Y)^2\), and Problem 3.3.7 needs \(\lambda\) larger than a multiple of \(\log Y\); any \(\log Q = (\log Y)^{2 + \varepsilon}\) with \(\varepsilon > 0\) works. The range \(\log N \le \sqrt{\log u}\) of (1) is what costs the square. At the other end, \(\log Q\) may be as large as a small power of \(Y\): besides the lower bounds just named, Problem 3.3.7 needs only \(\lambda^6 \le cX\) with \(c\) small. Adriazola takes this upper end, \(e^{z/2} < q \le e^z\) with \(z = Y^{\varepsilon/5}\), where now \(0 < \varepsilon \le \frac1{52}\) is the exponent of Chapter 58 [Adr, Thms. 5.10 and 5.12]; so does Chapter 58 (Section 58.5).

§3.4. The second prime and Linnik’s range Tag 224K

Estimate B also needs a second prime \(d\), chosen after \(q\), in a residue class modulo a multiple of \(q\).

Tag 224L

Problem 3.4.1. Let \(q \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) be prime. The primes \(d \equiv 7 \pmod{16}\) with \(q \mid d + 1\) are the primes in one class \(a\) modulo \(M_q := 16q\), with \((a, M_q) = 1\), and each such \(d\), with \(q\), satisfies the congruences assumed in Problem 2.1.5.

Which \(s\) have a biased average over these \(d\)? For a modulus \(M\), a class \(a\) prime to \(M\) and a primitive character \(\chi\) of conductor \(D\), put \(L := \operatorname{lcm}(M, D)\); the fiber of \(a\) is the set of classes \(r \bmod L\) with \((r, L) = 1\) and \(r \equiv a \pmod M\).

Tag 224M

Problem 3.4.2. The average of \(\chi\) over the fiber of \(a\) is \(\chi(a)\) if \(D \mid M\), and \(0\) otherwise.

Tag 224N

Problem 3.4.3. Take \(q = 11\), so that \(M_q = 176\) and \(a = 87\). The averages of \(\omega_2\), \(\omega_{11}\) and \(\omega_3\) over the fiber of \(a\) are \(1\), \(1\) and \(0\). In general, for \(q \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) and \(a\) as in Problem 3.4.1, the average of \(\omega_s\) over the fiber of \(a\), for squarefree \(s > 1\), is \(1\) if \(s \in \{2, q, 2q\}\) and \(0\) otherwise.

So conditioning on the class \(a\) moves exactly one term of the mean square. In the analogue of Problem 3.2.1 for the primes \(d \equiv a \pmod{M_q}\), with \(n, n' \le Z < q\), the values \(s = q, 2q\) cannot occur, but the pairs with \(n n' \in 2\square\) no longer average to zero: they join the square pairs as a main term \(\sum_{n n' \in 2\square} \lvert c_n c_{n'} \rvert\), at most \(C \sqrt y \log y\) for \(\lvert c_n \rvert \le a_n(y)\), by Problem 3.2.2 with \(2\) in place of \(s^*\). The class plays the part of an exceptional \(s^*\) that is always present and costs no more.

The average of \((\frac sd)\) over these \(d\) is a sum over primes of the characters \(\xi \omega_s\), \(\xi\) modulo \(M_q\), whose conductors divide \(\operatorname{lcm}(M_q, D_s)\). With (1) this needs \(\log d \ge (\log M_q)^2\). One can do with less: apart from the effect of an exceptional zero, the prime number theorem in progressions modulo \(k\) holds with a relative error \(e^{-c\log x/\log k}\) as soon as \(\log x\) is a large multiple of \(\log k\). This is the range of Linnik’s theorem on the least prime [Lin]; the statement we use is Gallagher’s.

Let \(k \ge 3\). By a theorem of McCurley, as stated in [TZ1], \(\prod_{\chi \bmod k} L(s, \chi)\) has at most one zero in \(\operatorname{Re}s \ge 1 - 1/(13 \log(k(\lvert \operatorname{Im}s \rvert + 3)))\); if it exists it is real and simple, call it \(\beta_1\), and \(L(\beta_1, \chi_1) = 0\) for a unique real nonprincipal \(\chi_1\) modulo \(k\). For \((a, k) = 1\) and \(h \le x\) put \(\lambda_a := h^{-1} \int_{x-h}^x (1 - \chi_1(a) t^{\beta_1 - 1})\, d t\) if \(\beta_1\) exists and \(\lambda_a := 1\) otherwise. Gallagher’s Theorem 7 [Gal, Thm. 7] gives, in the form of [TZ1]: there are \(c_4, c_5 > 0\) and \(C_5\) such that if \(e^{\sqrt{\log x}} \le k \le x^{c_4}\) and \(x/k \le h \le x\), then \[\Bigl\lvert 1 - \frac{\varphi(k)}{\lambda_a h} \sum_{\substack{x - h < p \le x \\ p \equiv a \ (k)}} \log p \Bigr\rvert \le C_5\, \mu\, e^{-c_5 \log x/\log k} ,\] with \(\mu := (1 - \beta_1)\log k\) if \(\beta_1\) exists and \(\mu := 1\) otherwise.

In Problems 3.4.4 and 3.4.5, \(x := k^B\) and \(h := x/2\), with \(B\) and then \(k\) large.

Tag 224P

Problem 3.4.4. If \(\beta_1\) does not exist, then \(\sum_{x/2 < p \le x,\, p \equiv a\ (k)} \log p \ge x/(4\varphi(k))\).

Tag 224Q

Problem 3.4.5. If \(\beta_1\) exists, then \(\lambda_a \ge \frac12 \min\bigl(1, (1 - \beta_1)\log(x/2)\bigr)\), and the sum of Problem 3.4.4 is at least \(\lambda_a x/(4\varphi(k)) > 0\).

Problems 3.4.4 and 3.4.5 prove Linnik’s theorem [Lin] for large \(k\); for the other \(k\) it is Dirichlet’s theorem, with a suitable \(C\).

Tag 224R

Theorem 3.4.1. There is an absolute \(B_0\) such that for every \(k \ge 2\) and every \(a\) prime to \(k\) there is a prime \(p \equiv a \pmod k\) with \(p \le C k^{B_0}\).

The factor \(\mu = (1 - \beta_1)\log k\) is the Deuring–Heilbronn phenomenon, which Gallagher’s proof uses through [Gal]: an exceptional zero pushes the other zeros away from \(1\). Without it, Problem 3.4.5 would need \(e^{-c_5 B} < \lambda_a\); the classical effective lower bound \(1 - \beta_1 \ge c k^{-1/2} (\log k)^{-2}\) [Dav, Ch. 14] would force \(B\) of order \(\log k\), that is, \(\log x\) of order \((\log k)^2\), the range of (1) again. Theorem 9.3.1 gives instead \(1 - \beta_1 \ge c/\log(2k)\), so that \(\mu\) is bounded below; as with \(s^*\), we do not use it here. For Estimate B the class \(a\) is the one of Problem 3.4.1, and the analogues of Sections 3.1–3.3 for \(d\) run with the main term \(\lambda_a\) in place of \(1\); to make \(\eta_Q\) smaller than \(Y^{-A}\) one takes \(\log d\) of order \((\log M_q)(\log Y)^2\). We do not carry this out here. Adriazola [Adr] does, from the inputs of this chapter; there \(d\) is also taken \(\equiv -1\) modulo \(q\) and modulo every odd prime up to \(z\), with \(q\) and \(z\) as at the end of Section 3.3, and the exceptional set of Section 3.1 is replaced by a larger one.
