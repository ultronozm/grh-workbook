Prerequisites.

Finite fields and their additive characters. The formalism of \(\ell\)-adic cohomology with compact supports for schemes of finite type over a finite field, as summarized in [SGA4\(\frac12\), Rapport]: the Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula [SGA4\(\frac12\), Rapport, Théorème 3.2], the Künneth formula, Poincaré duality, Tate twists, Artin’s vanishing theorem, and the long exact sequence of an open subscheme and its closed complement; see also [Mil]. Noether normalization. The Artin–Schreier sheaf, whose trace function is the additive character \(e\) of §S1.1 [SGA4\(\frac12\), Sommes trig.]. One theorem of weak Lefschetz type, stated in §S1.3. No earlier chapter is used. The sequel, Deligne’s bound by the sign-only method, extends the argument to all dimensions.

§S1.1. The identity \(qN = M^k + R\) Tag 22XE

How large is a weighted count of the zeros of a quadratic form, and which part of it has square-root size? Alpöge’s note sketches a “\(qN = M^k + R\) sign-only proof of Weil and Deligne” using the form \(x_1x_2 + \dots + x_{k-1}x_k\) [Alp and footnote 3], without defining \(t\), \(M\), \(N\) or \(R\). We follow the reading of [Dup] and the proof written out in [Ald]. That text reports that its proof was produced by an AI model from Alpöge’s sketch and checked by two others, and that no human has checked it. D. Zhang has since written out the same proof for lisse sheaves on the affine line [Zh]. His note is the primary source for §S1.3 wherever it applies, and the text says where [Ald] goes further.

Let \(q = p^f\), and let \(e(y) := \exp\bigl(2\pi i \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_q/\mathbb{F}_p}(y)/p\bigr)\) for \(y \in \mathbb{F}_q\). For \(h \in \mathbb{F}_q\) let \(K_h\) be the \(q \times q\) matrix with entries \(K_h(x,y) := e(hxy)\).

Tag 22XF

Problem S1.1.1. Every additive character of \(\mathbb{F}_q\) is \(y \mapsto e(ay)\) for a unique \(a \in \mathbb{F}_q\), and \(\sum_{h \in \mathbb{F}_q} e(hy)\) equals \(q\) if \(y = 0\) and \(0\) otherwise.

Tag 22XG

Problem S1.1.2. If \(h \ne 0\), then \(K_h K_h^* = q\). So \(K_h\) has operator norm \(\sqrt q\).

Tag 22XH

Problem S1.1.3. Let \(t(x) := e(x)\) for \(x \ne 0\) and \(t(0) := 0\). Then \(\sum_x t(x) = -1\) and \(\sum_x t(x)\, e(x\xi) = q\,\delta_{\xi,-1} - 1\).

Let \(k \ge 2\), let \(Q(x) := x_1x_2 + x_2x_3 + \dots + x_{k-1}x_k\) be the quadratic form of the path with vertices \(1, \dots, k\), and let \(Z \subset \mathbb{A}^k\) be the quadric \(Q = 0\). For \(t \colon \mathbb{F}_q \to \mathbb{C}\) let \[N := \sum_{x \in Z(\mathbb{F}_q)} t(x_1) \cdots t(x_k), \qquad M := \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_q} t(x),\] \[R := \sum_{h \ne 0} \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_q^k} t(x_1) \cdots t(x_k)\, e\bigl(hQ(x)\bigr).\]

Tag 22XJ

Problem S1.1.4. \(qN = M^k + R\).

The identity holds for every \(t\). Its content is that \(R\) is controlled by Fourier analysis, and \(N\) and \(M^k\) by cohomology. Let \(D_t\) be the diagonal matrix with entries \(t(x)\), and regard \(t\) as a column vector.

Tag 22XK

Problem S1.1.5. For every \(h\), \[\sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_q^k} t(x_1) \cdots t(x_k)\, e(hQ(x)) = t^{\mathsf T} K_h D_t K_h D_t \cdots D_t K_h\, t,\] with \(k-1\) factors \(K_h\) and \(k-2\) factors \(D_t\).

Tag 22XL

Problem S1.1.6. If \(\lvert t \rvert \le T\) and \(h \ne 0\), the sum in Problem S1.1.5 has absolute value at most \(T^k q^{(k+1)/2}\). Hence \(\lvert R \rvert \le T^k q^{(k+3)/2}\).

The trivial bound is \(\lvert R \rvert \le T^kq^{k+1}\). If \(M\) has the hoped-for size \(\sqrt q\), then \(M^k\) has size \(q^{k/2}\), and Problem S1.1.6 exceeds it by \(q^{3/2}\) for every \(k\). After a \(k\)-th root the loss \(q^{3/(2k)}\) tends to \(1\). In §S1.3 the weights vary with the variable and the field.

Tag 22XM

Problem S1.1.7. Let \(E\) be a finite field with \(r\) elements, \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(E\), and \(t_1, \dots, t_k \colon E \to \mathbb{C}\) functions bounded by \(T\). For \(h \in E^\times\) and \(c \in E\), \(\bigl\lvert \sum_{x \in E^k} t_1(x_1) \cdots t_k(x_k)\, \psi\bigl(hQ(x) + hc\bigr) \bigr\rvert \le T^k r^{(k+1)/2}\).

This is [Zh, Lemma 2.6] and [Ald, Lemma 3]. For \(k = 2\) it is a finite-field form of Vinogradov’s bilinear bound [Zh, Remark 2.7]. The path is what matters. For odd \(q\) and \(t(x) := e(-x^2/2)\), completing the square gives \(\lvert \sum_{x,y} t(x)t(y)\,e(xy) \rvert = q^{3/2}\). So with \(k\) even and the disjoint pairs \(x_1x_2 + x_3x_4 + \dots + x_{k-1}x_k\) in place of \(Q\), the sum can reach \(q^{3k/4}\), and the loss after a \(k\)-th root does not tend to \(1\) [Zh, Remark 2.7].

Tag 22XN

Problem S1.1.8. Let \(t\) be as in Problem S1.1.3. Then \(N = 1 - q\) for \(k = 3\), and \(N = q - 2\) for \(k = 4\).

§S1.2. The sign under a vanishing hypothesis Tag 22XP

Problem S1.1.6 bounds \(R\), but \(qN\) and \(M^k\) may each be much larger than \(R\). Why can they not cancel? The note’s answer is that their Frobenius eigenvalues sit in adjacent cohomological degrees, “one off” [Alp, footnote 3].

Let \(U \subset \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{F}_q}\) be a nonempty open subset with \(0 \notin U\), and \(\mathcal{F}\) a lisse \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\)-sheaf on \(U\) of rank \(\rho\), pointwise pure of weight \(0\) for a fixed embedding \(\iota \colon \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\to \mathbb{C}\). Let \(t(x)\) be the trace of Frobenius on the stalk at \(x \in U(\mathbb{F}_q)\), and \(t(x) := 0\) for \(x \notin U\); then \(\lvert t \rvert \le \rho\). Over \(\mathbb{F}_{q^n}\) write \(M_n\), \(N_n\), \(R_n\) for the sums of §S1.1. Cohomology is taken over \(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q\), and \(\mathrm{Fr}\) is the geometric Frobenius. Let \(\mathcal{F}_k := \mathcal{F}^{\boxtimes k}\) on \(U^k\) and \(Z_k := Z \cap U^k\). By the trace formula \(M_n = \sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Fr}^n \mid H^i_c(U, \mathcal{F}))\) and \(N_n = \sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Fr}^n \mid H^i_c(Z_k, \mathcal{F}_k))\). Following [Dup], assume:

  • \(H^i_c(U, \mathcal{F}) = 0\) for \(i \ne 1\);

  • for infinitely many \(k\), \(H^i_c(Z_k, \mathcal{F}_k) = 0\) for \(i \ne k - 1\).

Let \(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_d\) be the eigenvalues of \(\mathrm{Fr}\) on \(H^1_c(U, \mathcal{F})\), and \(\alpha_I := \alpha_{i_1} \cdots \alpha_{i_k}\) for \(I = (i_1, \dots, i_k)\). Under (H1), \(M_n = -\sum_j \alpha_j^n\).

Let \(\beta_1, \dots, \beta_e\) be the eigenvalues of \(\mathrm{Fr}\) on \(H^{k-1}_c(Z_k, \mathcal{F}_k)\).

Tag 22XT

Problem S1.2.4. Under (H1), and (H2) for the given \(k\), \(R_n = (-1)^{k-1}\bigl(\sum_j (q\beta_j)^n + \sum_I \alpha_I^n\bigr)\) for all \(n \ge 1\).

Every eigenvalue enters \(R_n\) with the same sign: \(M^k\) comes from degree \(k\), and \(qN\) from degree \(k - 1\).

Tag 22XV

Theorem S1.2.1 ([Dup, Theorem 2.10.2]). Under (H1) and (H2), every eigenvalue \(\alpha\) of \(\mathrm{Fr}\) on \(H^1_c(U, \mathcal{F})\) satisfies \(\lvert \alpha \rvert \le \sqrt q\), and \(\lvert \sum_{x \in \mathbb{F}_q} t(x) \rvert \le d \sqrt q\).

Tag 22XW

Problem S1.2.6. Let \(U = \mathbb{A}^1 \smallsetminus \{0\}\) and \(\mathcal{F}\) the Artin–Schreier sheaf, with trace function \(t\) as in Problem S1.1.3. Then \(\mathcal{F}\) is geometrically nonconstant, (H1) holds with \(d = 1\) and \(\alpha_1 = 1\), and the vanishing in (H2) fails for \(k = 3\) and for \(k = 4\).

Replacing \(Q\) by \(\sum_i a_ix_ix_{i+1}\) with \(a_i \ne 0\) dilates the weight in each variable, by different factors. For a given sheaf this can matter: in Problem S1.2.6 with \(k = 3\), the cohomology of \(a_1x_1x_2 + a_2x_2x_3 = 0\) in \(U^3\) is concentrated in degree \(2\) when \(a_1 \ne a_2\). But (H2) asks for infinitely many \(k\), and the proof below does not rest on a choice of coefficients. What is wanted is a hypersurface in \(U^k\) whose cohomology above the middle degree is forced to match that of \(U^k\). This is a weak Lefschetz property, and it holds for a generic hyperplane section, which has linear terms as well as quadratic ones.

§S1.3. Weak Lefschetz in place of (H2) Tag 22XX

The argument of [Ald] and [Zh] keeps the identity and the estimate, and replaces (H2) by a weak Lefschetz theorem. The higher cohomology of the hypersurface cancels against that of \(U^k\), and what survives is the trace on an actual vector space.

Duality and the Gysin map.

Let \(X\) be smooth of pure dimension \(D\) over a finite field \(\mathbb{F}_r\), and \(\mathcal{G}\) lisse on \(X\). Poincaré duality pairs \(H^i_c(X, \mathcal{G})\) perfectly with \(H^{2D-i}(X, \mathcal{G}^\vee)(D)\), compatibly with Frobenius, and the twist \((-1)\) multiplies Frobenius eigenvalues by \(r\). If \(X\) is affine, Artin’s theorem and duality give \(H^i_c(X, \mathcal{G}) = 0\) for \(i < D\). Let \(Y \subset X\) be smooth and closed of pure dimension \(D - 1\), or empty. The transpose of restriction \(H^a(X, \mathcal{G}^\vee) \to H^a(Y, \mathcal{G}^\vee)\) is the Gysin map \(H^j_c(Y, \mathcal{G})(-1) \to H^{j+2}_c(X, \mathcal{G})\), \(j = 2D - 2 - a\). We say that \(Y\) has property (WL) for \(\mathcal{G}\) if this restriction is bijective for \(a < D - 1\) and injective for \(a = D - 1\).

Tag 22XY

Problem S1.3.1. If \(Y\) has property (WL) for \(\mathcal{G}\), the Gysin map \(H^j_c(Y, \mathcal{G})(-1) \to H^{j+2}_c(X, \mathcal{G})\) is bijective for \(j > D - 1\) and surjective for \(j = D - 1\).

Now let \(U\) be a smooth affine curve over \(\mathbb{F}_r\), \(\mathcal{F}\) lisse on \(U\), \(X := U^k\), \(D = k\), and \(\mathcal{F}_k := \mathcal{F}^{\boxtimes k}\). Let \(Y \subset X\) be as above, with property (WL) for \(\mathcal{F}_k\). Let \(M_n\) and \(N_n\) be the sums of the traces of Frobenius on the stalks of \(\mathcal{F}\) over \(U(\mathbb{F}_{r^n})\) and of \(\mathcal{F}_k\) over \(Y(\mathbb{F}_{r^n})\), let \(R_n := r^nN_n - M_n^k\), and let \(K\) be the kernel of the Gysin map \(H^{k-1}_c(Y, \mathcal{F}_k)(-1) \to H^{k+1}_c(X, \mathcal{F}_k)\).

Tag 22XZ

Problem S1.3.2. For all \(n \ge 1\), \((-1)^{k-1} R_n = \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\mathrm{Fr}^n \mid H^1_c(U, \mathcal{F})^{\otimes k}\bigr) + \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\mathrm{Fr}^n \mid K\bigr).\)

This is [Ald, Proposition 4]. In [Zh, Lemma 2.2 and Definition 2.3] the right side is the trace on the single group \(H^k_c(X, Rj_*j^*\mathcal{F}_k)\), where \(j\) is the inclusion of \(X \smallsetminus Y\). That group is an extension of \(K\) by \(H^k_c(X, \mathcal{F}_k)\), and property (WL) says that it is the only nonzero one. Up to one global sign, \(R_n\) is the trace of Frobenius on an actual vector space, so Problem S1.2.5 applies with positive integer coefficients. Nothing is assumed about the higher cohomology of \(U\) or of \(Y\).

Tag 22Y3

Theorem S1.3.1 (Deligne; [Kat93, Appendix, Corollary A.5]). Let \(V\) be smooth of pure dimension \(D\) over \(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q\), let \(f \colon V \to \mathbb{P}^m\) be quasi-finite, and let \(\mathcal{G}\) be lisse on \(V\). For \(H\) in a dense open subset of the space of hyperplanes of \(\mathbb{P}^m\), restriction \(H^a(V, \mathcal{G}) \to H^a(f^{-1}H, \mathcal{G})\) is bijective for \(a < D - 1\) and injective for \(a = D - 1\).

Katz states this for a perverse sheaf \(\mathcal{K}\), in degrees \(a < -1\) and \(a = -1\). The case above is \(\mathcal{K} = \mathcal{G}[D]\), which is perverse because \(V\) is smooth of pure dimension \(D\) [Kat93, proof of Corollary 3.4.1]. The proof uses generic base change, duality and Artin’s theorem, and no weights.

Tag 22YC
