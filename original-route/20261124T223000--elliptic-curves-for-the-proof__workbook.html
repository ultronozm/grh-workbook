Prerequisites.

Complex analysis: Liouville’s theorem, the argument principle, Weierstrass products, and Euler’s Gamma function with the Beta integral and the reflection formula. The Gaussian integers. Elliptic curves over \(\mathbb{C}\) at the level of the statements of [Si1, Ch. VI]. No earlier chapter is used. Chapter 14, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime, continues this one.

§12.1. Complex tori Tag 22BS

How does one compute on a curve of genus one over \(\mathbb{C}\)? One uniformizes it. Let \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}\) be a lattice, and for \(k \ge 3\) let \(G_k(\Lambda) := \sum'_{\omega \in \Lambda} \omega^{-k}\), where the prime means that \(\omega = 0\) is omitted. The Weierstrass function \[\wp(u) := \frac{1}{u^2} + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda}{}' \Bigl( \frac{1}{(u - \omega)^2} - \frac{1}{\omega^2} \Bigr)\] is elliptic, that is meromorphic and \(\Lambda\)-periodic. Let \(g_2 := 60 G_4(\Lambda)\) and \(g_3 := 140 G_6(\Lambda)\). The map \(u \mapsto (\wp(u), \wp'(u))\) is an analytic isomorphism of groups from \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) onto the curve \(y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3\), and every elliptic curve over \(\mathbb{C}\) arises in this way [Si1].

Tag 22BT

Problem 12.1.1. \(\wp(u) = u^{-2} + \sum_{k \ge 1} (2k+1) G_{2k+2}(\Lambda)\, u^{2k}\), and \(\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - g_2\wp - g_3\).

Let \[\zeta(u) := \frac1u + \sum_{\omega \in \Lambda}{}' \Bigl( \frac{1}{u - \omega} + \frac{1}{\omega} + \frac{u}{\omega^2} \Bigr), \qquad \sigma(u) := u \prod_{\omega \in \Lambda}{}' \Bigl(1 - \frac{u}{\omega}\Bigr) e^{u/\omega + u^2/2\omega^2} .\] Then \(\zeta' = -\wp\) and \(\sigma'/\sigma = \zeta\). The function \(\sigma\) is entire and odd, with simple zeros exactly at the points of \(\Lambda\).

Tag 22BU

Problem 12.1.2. There is a \(\mathbb{Z}\)-linear map \(\eta \colon \Lambda \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(\zeta(u + \omega) = \zeta(u) + \eta(\omega)\).

Tag 22BV

Problem 12.1.3. If \(\omega_1, \omega_2\) is a basis of \(\Lambda\) with \(\operatorname{Im}(\omega_2/\omega_1) > 0\), then \(\eta(\omega_1)\omega_2 - \eta(\omega_2)\omega_1 = 2\pi\mathrm{i}\).

This is Legendre’s relation.

Tag 22BW

Problem 12.1.4. \(\sigma(u + \omega) = \psi(\omega)\, e^{\eta(\omega)(u + \omega/2)} \sigma(u)\) for \(\omega \in \Lambda\), where \(\psi(\omega) = 1\) if \(\omega \in 2\Lambda\) and \(\psi(\omega) = -1\) otherwise.

Tag 22BX

Problem 12.1.5. \(\dfrac{\sigma(u+v)\,\sigma(u-v)}{\sigma(u)^2\,\sigma(v)^2} = \wp(v) - \wp(u)\).

Tag 22BY

Problem 12.1.6. \(\zeta(u+v) - \zeta(u) - \zeta(v) = \dfrac12 \, \dfrac{\wp'(u) - \wp'(v)}{\wp(u) - \wp(v)}\). Consequently \[\wp(u+v) = \frac14 \Bigl( \frac{\wp'(u) - \wp'(v)}{\wp(u) - \wp(v)} \Bigr)^2 - \wp(u) - \wp(v) .\]

Now let \(\Lambda\) be square, that is \(\mathrm{i}\Lambda = \Lambda\).

Tag 22BZ

Problem 12.1.7. If \(\mathrm{i}\Lambda = \Lambda\), then \(\wp(\mathrm{i}u) = -\wp(u)\), \(g_3 = 0\), and \(\eta(\mathrm{i}\omega) = -\mathrm{i}\,\eta(\omega)\).

Tag 22C2

Problem 12.1.8. There is exactly one real \(\lambda > 0\) with \(g_2(\lambda\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) = 4\).

Let \(\varpi := 2 \int_0^1 (1 - t^4)^{-1/2}\, d t = 2.62205\ldots\) be the lemniscate constant.

Tag 22C3

Problem 12.1.9. The lattice \(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) has \(g_2 = 4\) and \(g_3 = 0\). Moreover \(\varpi = \Gamma(\tfrac14)^2 / (2\sqrt{2\pi})\).

From now on, the lemniscatic lattice is \(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), and \(\wp\), \(\zeta\), \(\sigma\) without further mention are its Weierstrass functions. With \(x = \wp\) and \(y = \wp'/2\) it is the lattice of the curve \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\), and \(dx/2y\) pulls back to \(d u\).

§12.2. Theta functions Tag 22C4

The function \(\sigma\) is not periodic, but by Problem 12.1.4 it changes by an exponential factor under the lattice. Entire functions with such a law are theta functions. How many are there, and how are they made?

Let \(\tau\) lie in the upper half-plane, and let \(\vartheta(z) := \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{\pi\mathrm{i}n^2\tau + 2\pi\mathrm{i}n z}\).

Tag 22C5

Problem 12.2.1. \(\vartheta(z+1) = \vartheta(z)\), \(\vartheta(z + \tau) = e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}\tau - 2\pi\mathrm{i}z}\vartheta(z)\), and \(\vartheta(-z) = \vartheta(z)\).

Tag 22C6

Problem 12.2.2. In each period parallelogram of \(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\), \(\vartheta\) has exactly one zero. It is simple, and it lies at \((1+\tau)/2\) modulo \(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\).

Tag 22C7

Problem 12.2.3. Let \(\sigma\) and \(\eta\) belong to \(\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\). Then there is a constant \(c\) with \[\sigma(u) = c\, e^{\eta(1) u^2/2 + \pi\mathrm{i}u}\, \vartheta\bigl(u + \tfrac{1+\tau}{2}\bigr) .\]

The theta functions of Chapter 16 are built from the Gaussian integers: \[\Theta(z) := \sum_{\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} e^{-\pi\lvert\lambda\rvert^2 + \lambda z}, \qquad \theta(z) := \sum_{a \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-\pi a^2 + a z} .\]

Tag 22C8

Problem 12.2.4. \(\Theta(z) = \theta(z)\,\theta(\mathrm{i}z)\), \(\Theta(\mathrm{i}z) = \Theta(z)\), and for \(\beta \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) \[\Theta(z + 2\pi\beta) = e_\beta(z)\, \Theta(z), \qquad e_\beta(z) := e^{\pi\lvert\beta\rvert^2 + \bar\beta z} .\]

Let \(T\) be the space of entire functions \(f\) with \(f(z + 2\pi\beta) = e_\beta(z) f(z)\) for all \(\beta \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

Tag 22C9

Problem 12.2.5. \(\dim T = 2\), and every \(f \in T\) is even.

Tag 22CA

Problem 12.2.6. A nonzero \(f \in T\) has exactly two zeros, counted with multiplicity, in each period parallelogram of \(2\pi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

Tag 22CB

Problem 12.2.7. If \(f \in T\), then \(f(\mathrm{i}z) \in T\). The \(f \in T\) with \(f(\mathrm{i}z) = f(z)\) form the line \(\mathbb{C}\,\Theta\).

The last fact about \(\sigma\) that we need is the theorem of the cube [Mu, §10], which for \(\sigma\) takes the following elementary form.

Tag 22CC

Problem 12.2.8. The function \[\Xi(x, y, w) := \frac{\sigma(x+y+w)\,\sigma(x)\,\sigma(y)\,\sigma(w)}{\sigma(x+y)\,\sigma(y+w)\,\sigma(w+x)}\] is elliptic in each variable separately, and \(1/\Xi = \zeta(x) + \zeta(y) + \zeta(w) - \zeta(x+y+w)\).

The Taylor coefficients of \(\Theta\) at \(0\) are not rational. Chapter 16 expresses \(\Theta\) through \(\sigma\) and \(\wp\) (Problem 16.1.3); then the \(n\)th derivative at \(0\) is \(\Theta(0)\) times a rational multiple of \((\varpi/2\pi)^n\). So one needs to know \(\varpi\), and the rational numbers that \(\wp\) produces.

§12.3. Periods Tag 22CD

By Problem 12.1.1, the Laurent coefficients of \(\wp\) are the lattice sums \(G_k\). For the lemniscatic lattice they are rational. Write \(\mu\) for an element of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

Tag 22CE

Problem 12.3.1. \(G_k(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) \in \mathbb{Q}\) for every \(k \ge 3\), and \(G_k = 0\) unless \(4 \mid k\). Define the Hurwitz numbers \(H_{4k}\) by \[\sum_{\mu}{}' \mu^{-4k} = \frac{(2\varpi)^{4k}}{(4k)!}\, H_{4k} .\] Then \(H_4 = \tfrac{1}{10}\) and \(H_8 = \tfrac{3}{10}\).

When \(\bar\mu\) also appears, \(\pi\) enters. Summed first over \(m\) and then over \(n\), Eisenstein’s series \(G_2(\tau) := \sum_n \sum_m{}' (m + n\tau)^{-2}\) satisfies \(G_2(-1/\tau) = \tau^2 G_2(\tau) - 2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau\) [We].

Tag 22CF

Problem 12.3.2. \(G_2(\mathrm{i}) = \pi\).

For integers \(j \ge 0\) and \(k > j + 2\), let \(S(j, k) := \sum'_\mu \bar\mu^j \mu^{-k}\).

Tag 22CG

Problem 12.3.3. \(S(j, k) = 0\) unless \(j + k \equiv 0 \pmod 4\), and \(S(0, k) = \varpi^k G_k(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}])\).

Damerell proved that \(S(j, k)\) is an algebraic multiple of \(\varpi^{j+k}\pi^{-j}\) [Dam]; for instance \(S(1, 7) = \tfrac{2}{315}\varpi^8/\pi\).

How big is \(\varpi\) in terms of classical constants? Let \(\chi\) be the non-trivial character modulo \(4\). In the rest of this section, \(\eta(\tau) := e^{\pi\mathrm{i}\tau/12}\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}n\tau})\) is Dedekind’s function, and \(\zeta(s)\), with argument \(s\), is Riemann’s.

Tag 22CH

Problem 12.3.4. For \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), \(\sum'_\mu \lvert\mu\rvert^{-2s} = 4\,\zeta(s)\,L(s, \chi)\).

Kronecker’s first limit formula states that, for \(\tau = x + \mathrm{i}y\) with \(y > 0\), \[\sum_{(m,n) \ne (0,0)} \frac{y^s}{\lvert m + n\tau\rvert^{2s}} = \frac{\pi}{s-1} + 2\pi\bigl(\gamma - \log 2 - \log(\sqrt{y}\,\lvert\eta(\tau)\rvert^2)\bigr) + O(s-1)\] [La, Ch. 20]; Theorem 20.1.1 of Chapter 20 proves it.

Tag 22CJ

Problem 12.3.5. \(L'(1, \chi)/L(1, \chi) = \gamma - 2\log 2 - 4\log\eta(\mathrm{i})\).

The discriminant \(\Delta(\Lambda) := g_2^3 - 27g_3^2\) satisfies \(\Delta(\lambda\Lambda) = \lambda^{-12}\Delta(\Lambda)\) and \(\Delta(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau) = (2\pi)^{12}\eta(\tau)^{24}\) [SeA].

Tag 22CK

Problem 12.3.6. \(\eta(\mathrm{i})^2 = \varpi/(\sqrt2\,\pi)\), and so \(\eta(\mathrm{i}) = \Gamma(\tfrac14)/(2\pi^{3/4})\).

Problems 12.1.9, 12.3.5 and 12.3.6 prove the following.

Tag 22CL

Theorem 12.3.1. \(\dfrac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)} = \gamma + 2\log 2 + 3\log\pi - 4\log\Gamma(\tfrac14) = 0.24560\ldots\)

Chowla and Selberg found the general form [CS]. Chapter 20 proves it, together with Kronecker’s limit formula, and states the period relation of Gross [Gr]: the periods of an elliptic curve over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal{O}_K\) are algebraic multiples of an explicit product \(\Omega_K\) of values of \(\Gamma\). For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) this product is \(\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(\tfrac14)/\Gamma(\tfrac34)\).

Tag 22CM

Problem 12.3.7. \(\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(\tfrac14)/\Gamma(\tfrac34) = 2\varpi\). Moreover \[\Omega := \frac{2\varpi^2}{\pi} = \frac{\Gamma(\tfrac14)^4}{4\pi^2} = 4\pi\,\eta(\mathrm{i})^4 .\]

Hints

§12.1

12.1.1

Expand \((u - \omega)^{-2} - \omega^{-2}\) in powers of \(u\). The function \(\wp'^2 - 4\wp^3 + g_2\wp + g_3\) is elliptic, has no poles, and vanishes at \(0\).

12.1.2

The derivative of \(\zeta(u+\omega) - \zeta(u)\) is \(0\).

12.1.3

Integrate \(\zeta\) around a period parallelogram whose boundary avoids \(\Lambda\).

12.1.4

The logarithmic derivative of \(\sigma(u+\omega)/\sigma(u)\) is \(\eta(\omega)\). To find the constant for \(\omega \notin 2\Lambda\), use that \(\sigma\) is odd; for \(\omega \in 2\Lambda\), apply the law twice with \(\omega/2\).

12.1.5

Both sides are elliptic in \(u\) with the same zeros and poles. Compare the leading terms at \(u = 0\).

12.1.6

Take the logarithmic derivative of Problem 12.1.5 in \(u\) and in \(v\), and add. For the second formula, differentiate the first in \(u\).

12.1.7

\(G_k(\mathrm{i}\Lambda) = \mathrm{i}^{-k} G_k(\Lambda)\).

12.1.8

\(g_2(\lambda\Lambda) = \lambda^{-4} g_2(\Lambda)\), and the values of \(\wp\) at the three half-periods of a rectangular lattice are real, distinct, and are the roots of \(4x^3 - g_2 x - g_3\).

12.1.9

Let \(\lambda\) be as in Problem 12.1.8. Then \(\wp(\mathrm{i}\lambda/2) = -\wp(\lambda/2)\) and \(\wp((1+\mathrm{i})\lambda/2) = 0\). On \((0, \lambda/2]\), \(\wp\) is real and decreasing, and \(\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - 4\wp\). Substitute \(x = t^{-2}\). For the Gamma value use the Beta integral and \(\Gamma(\tfrac14)\Gamma(\tfrac34) = \pi\sqrt2\).

§12.2

12.2.1

Replace \(n\) by \(n + 1\) and by \(-n\).

12.2.2

Argument principle, as in Problem 12.1.3. At \(z = (1+\tau)/2\), pair the terms \(n\) and \(-1-n\).

12.2.3

Both sides have simple zeros exactly at \(\Lambda\), and the same law under \(u \mapsto u+1\) and \(u \mapsto u + \tau\), by Problem 12.1.4 and Legendre’s relation.

12.2.4

Write \(\lambda = a + b\mathrm{i}\). For the law, substitute \(\lambda = \mu + \bar\beta\) and note \(\operatorname{Im}(\mu\beta) \in \mathbb{Z}\).

12.2.5

\(e^{-z^2/4\pi} f(z)\) has period \(2\pi\). Expand it in a Fourier series \(\sum_n a_n e^{\mathrm{i}n z}\), and use \(\beta = \mathrm{i}\).

12.2.6

Integrate \(f'/f\) around a parallelogram with sides \(2\pi\) and \(2\pi\mathrm{i}\).

12.2.7

If \(f(\mathrm{i}z) = f(z)\), only powers \(z^{4k}\) occur in the Taylor series of \(f\). Use Problem 12.2.6 and \(\Theta(0) > 0\).

12.2.8

Use Problem 12.1.4 in \(x\). Both \(\Xi\) and \(1/(\zeta(x) + \zeta(y) + \zeta(w) - \zeta(x+y+w))\) are elliptic in \(x\) with zeros at \(0\), \(-y-w\) and poles at \(-y\), \(-w\).

§12.3

12.3.1

\(g_2 = 4\) and \(g_3 = 0\). Differentiate \(\wp'^2 = 4\wp^3 - 4\wp\) to get \(\wp'' = 6\wp^2 - 2\), and compare coefficients in Problem 12.1.1.

12.3.3

Replace \(\mu\) by \(\mathrm{i}\mu\).

12.3.4

Count the \(\mu\) of norm \(n\) by unique factorization in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

12.3.5

Take \(\tau = \mathrm{i}\), and compare with the Laurent expansion of \(4\zeta(s)L(s, \chi)\) at \(s = 1\). \(L(1, \chi) = \pi/4\).

12.3.6

\(\Delta(\varpi\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) = 64\), and \(\eta(\mathrm{i}) > 0\).

12.3.7

\(\Gamma(\tfrac14)\Gamma(\tfrac34) = \pi\sqrt2\), and Problems 12.1.9 and 12.3.6.
