§14.1. Complex multiplication Tag 22DL

Which tori have endomorphisms other than \(u \mapsto nu\)? Every holomorphic map \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda \to \mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) that fixes \(0\) is \(u \mapsto \alpha u\) with \(\alpha\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\) [Si1, Thm. VI.4.1]. So if \(E\) corresponds to \(\Lambda\), then \(\operatorname{End}(E) = \{\alpha \in \mathbb{C}: \alpha\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\}\), and we write \([\alpha]\) for \(u \mapsto \alpha u\).

Tag 22DM

Problem 14.1.1. If \(\operatorname{End}(E) \ne \mathbb{Z}\), then \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\alpha)\) is imaginary quadratic for every \(\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E) \smallsetminus \mathbb{Z}\), \(\operatorname{End}(E)\) is an order in \(K\), and \(\Lambda\) is homothetic to a lattice contained in \(K\).

We then say that \(E\) has complex multiplication by that order.

Tag 22DN

Problem 14.1.2. \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]) = \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[2\mathrm{i}]) = \mathbb{Z}[2\mathrm{i}]\), and \(\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}[\rho]) = \mathbb{Z}[\rho]\) with \(\rho = e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\).

Tag 22DP

Problem 14.1.3. For \(\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E)\), \(\alpha \ne 0\), the kernel of \([\alpha]\) on \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) has \(\lvert\alpha\rvert^2\) elements.

The function \(\wp(\alpha u)\) is even and elliptic for \(\Lambda\), so it is a rational function of \(\wp(u)\), and \([\alpha]\) is a rational map of the curve.

Tag 22DQ

Problem 14.1.4. For the lemniscatic lattice, \(\wp((1+\mathrm{i})u) = \dfrac{\wp(u)^2 - 1}{2\mathrm{i}\, \wp(u)}\).

Tag 22DR

Problem 14.1.5. On \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\), \([\mathrm{i}](x, y) = (-x, \mathrm{i}y)\). For every \(\alpha \in \operatorname{End}(E)\), \([\alpha]^*(dx/2y) = \alpha\, dx/2y\).

Tag 22DS

Problem 14.1.6. For \(a, b \in \mathbb{C}^\times\), the curve \(y^2 = x^3 + ax\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), and \(y^2 = x^3 + b\) has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\rho]\).

The endomorphisms of \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\) have coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}, \tfrac12]\), so they can be reduced modulo an odd prime \(p\). What do they look like near the origin? There an endomorphism is a power series in a local parameter, and this is the formal group.

§14.2. The formal group Tag 22DT

Let \(E\) be given by a Weierstrass equation over a ring \(R\), and let \(z := -x/y\). The group law, written in \(z\), is a formal group law \(F(z_1, z_2) \in R[[z_1, z_2]]\), and every endomorphism defined over \(R\) becomes a series \([\alpha](z) \in R[[z]]\) [Si1]. The invariant differential becomes \(\omega(z) = \sum_{n \ge 1} c_n z^{n-1}\, d z\) with \(c_1 = 1\) and \(c_n\) integral over the coefficients, and the logarithm \(\ell(z) := \sum_{n \ge 1} c_n z^n/n\) satisfies \(F(z_1, z_2) = \ell^{-1}(\ell(z_1) + \ell(z_2))\) [Si1]. Over \(\mathbb{C}\), \(\ell(z(u)) = u\). The invariant derivation is \(D := d/du = \ell'(z)^{-1}\, d/dz\).

For a one-parameter formal group over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), either \([p](z) \equiv 0 \pmod p\) or \([p](z) \equiv g(z^{p^h}) \pmod p\) with \(g'(0) \not\equiv 0\); the integer \(h\) is the height [Si1]. For an elliptic curve with good reduction at \(p\), \(h \in \{1, 2\}\), and \(h = 2\) exactly when the reduction is supersingular [Si1, Thm. V.3.1]. The two extreme models are \(\hat G_a(X, Y) = X + Y\) and \(\hat G_m(X, Y) = X + Y + XY\).

Tag 22DU

Problem 14.2.1. For \(\hat G_m\), \(\ell(X) = \log(1+X)\), \([p](X) \equiv X^p \pmod p\), and the height is \(1\).

Tag 22DV

Problem 14.2.2. As operators on \(\mathbb{Z}_p[[X]]\) modulo \(p\): for \(\hat G_a\), \(D^p \equiv 0\); for \(\hat G_m\), \(D = (1+X)\,d/dX\) and \(D^p \equiv D\).

For any formal group over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) one has \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\) for a constant \(\lambda\); Problems 16.3.1 and 16.3.2 of Chapter 16 prove this for the lemniscate, and their proofs apply to any formal group. Which constant is \(\lambda\)?

Tag 22DW

Problem 14.2.3. Let \(\hat G\) be the formal group of a Weierstrass equation over \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\), with \(D^p \equiv \lambda D \pmod p\). Then \((D^n z)(0) = n!\, [u^n]\, \ell^{-1}(u)\) for every \(n\), and \(\lambda \equiv c_p \pmod p\).

Tag 22DX

Problem 14.2.4. For \(\hat G\) as in Problem 14.2.3, the coefficient of \(z^p\) in \([p](z)\) is congruent to \(c_p\) modulo \(p\). Hence the height is \(1\) exactly when \(p \nmid c_p\).

Now we compute \(c_n\) for \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\). Let \(w := -1/y\).

Tag 22DY

Problem 14.2.5. For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), \(w + z w^2 = z^3\), and \(\omega(z) = d z / \sqrt{1 + 4z^4}\).

Tag 22DZ

Problem 14.2.6. For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), \(\ell(z) = \sum_{m \ge 0} (-1)^m \binom{2m}{m} z^{4m+1}/(4m+1)\). For odd \(n\), \(c_n\) is the coefficient of \(x^{n-1}\) in \((x^3 - x)^{(n-1)/2}\).

For a curve \(y^2 = f(x)\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) with \(f\) cubic, the coefficient \(A_p\) of \(x^{p-1}\) in \(f^{(p-1)/2}\) is the Hasse invariant, and the curve is supersingular exactly when \(A_p = 0\) [Si1, Thm. V.4.1(a)]. By Problem 14.2.6, \(c_p = A_p\) for \(y^2 = x^3 - x\), and Problems 14.2.3 and 14.2.4 recover the criterion for this curve. When \(c_p = 0\), the first term of \([p](z)\) modulo \(p\) lies beyond \(z^p\). To find it one needs the Frobenius endomorphism.

§14.3. Reduction at an inert prime Tag 22E2

Which primes are supersingular for a curve with complex multiplication? Deuring’s answer [Deu]: if \(E\) has complex multiplication by an order in \(K\) and good reduction at a prime above \(p\), the reduction is supersingular if and only if \(p\) does not split in \(K\). For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) these are the \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). We check this by hand.

Tag 22E3

Problem 14.3.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). Then \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1\), that is, \(a_p := p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = 0\).

Tag 22E4

Problem 14.3.2. For \(p = 4m+1\), \(A_p = (-1)^m \binom{2m}{m} \not\equiv 0 \pmod p\). For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(A_p = 0\).

For \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) one has \(a_p = \gamma + \bar\gamma\), where \(\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) has \(\gamma\bar\gamma = p\) and \(\gamma \equiv 1 \pmod{2 + 2\mathrm{i}}\) [IR, Ch. 18].

Tag 22E5

Problem 14.3.3. \(a_5 = -2\) and \(a_{13} = 6\), in agreement with \(\gamma = -1 + 2\mathrm{i}\) and \(\gamma = 3 + 2\mathrm{i}\).

Now let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), let \(\phi(x, y) := (x^p, y^p)\) be the Frobenius endomorphism of \(E\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\), and reduce \([\mathrm{i}]\) modulo \(p\) into \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}\).

Tag 22E6

Problem 14.3.4. \(\phi^2 = [-p]\) and \(\phi \circ [\mathrm{i}] = [-\mathrm{i}] \circ \phi\). Hence \(\operatorname{End}(E_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p})\) is not commutative.

Tag 22E7

Problem 14.3.5. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). In the parameter \(z = -x/y\), \([p](z) \equiv -z^{p^2} \pmod p\), and \([\mathrm{i}](z) = \mathrm{i}z\).

For instance \([3](z) \equiv -z^9 \pmod 3\). So modulo \(p\) the multiplication by \(-p\) is exactly the \(p^2\)-power map. This is the situation of Lubin and Tate [LT]. Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be the ring of integers of a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), with uniformizer \(\pi\) and residue field of order \(q\). Lubin and Tate prove: (i) each \(f \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) with \(f(X) \equiv \pi X \bmod X^2\) and \(f(X) \equiv X^q \bmod \pi\) is an endomorphism of a unique formal group \(F_f\) over \(\mathcal{O}\); (ii) each \(a \in \mathcal{O}\) gives a unique endomorphism \([a]_f\) of \(F_f\) with linear term \(aX\), and \(a \mapsto [a]_f\) is a ring homomorphism; (iii) for two such \(f\), \(g\) there is a unique isomorphism \(\theta \colon F_f \to F_g\) over \(\mathcal{O}\) with linear term \(X\), and it commutes with the \([a]\). By Hazewinkel’s functional-equation lemma [Ha], the model logarithm \(\ell_\pi(X) := X + X^q/\pi + X^{q^2}/\pi^2 + \dotsb\) is the logarithm of such an \(F_f\).

Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}]\), so that \(\pi := -p\) is a uniformizer and \(q = p^2\).

Tag 22E8

Problem 14.3.6. The series \(f := [-p]\) satisfies the hypotheses of (i), the formal group \(\hat E\) of \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) is \(F_f\), and \([a]_f\) extends \(\alpha \mapsto [\alpha]\) from \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) to \(\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22E9

Problem 14.3.7. There is \(\theta \in z + z^2\mathcal{O}[[z]]\) with \(\ell = \ell_{-p} \circ \theta\). Consequently the coefficients of \(\ell\) in degrees less than \(p^2\) are \(p\)-integral, and \(p \cdot [z^{p^2}]\, \ell \equiv -1 \pmod p\).

For \(p = 3\): \([z^9]\,\ell = \tfrac23\), and \(3 \cdot \tfrac23 \equiv -1 \pmod 3\). Chapter 16 proves the same congruence for the lemniscate with binomial coefficients (Problem 16.4.2).

In the model logarithm the denominators occur only in the degrees \(q, q^2, \dotsc\), and there they are \(p, p^2, \dotsc\). Divisibility of derivatives at inert primes comes from this sparseness. The functions whose derivatives one needs are not functions on \(E\), however; they are theta functions. Chapter 15 uses \(D^p\) in a single determinant; theta functions are the subject of Chapters 16 and 17.
