Prerequisites.

Euler’s Gamma function: the reflection and duplication formulas, the Beta integral, and \(\psi := \Gamma'/\Gamma\) with \(\psi(1) = -\gamma\). Poisson summation. Dirichlet \(L\)-functions: the functional equation [Dav, Ch. 9], the class number formula and binary quadratic forms [Dav, Ch. 6]. The Hurwitz zeta function [Ap, Ch. 12]. Compact Riemann surfaces and their holomorphic differentials, for Section 20.4 only. From Chapter 12, Complex tori, theta functions and periods: Section 12.3, in particular Problems 12.1.9, 12.3.4 and 12.3.7 and Theorem 12.3.1, with Problems 12.3.5 and 12.3.6.

Chapter 12 took Kronecker’s limit formula from the literature and computed \(L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\) for the character \(\chi\) modulo \(4\) through the discriminant of the lemniscatic lattice. Here we prove the limit formula and reach the same value through \(s = 0\), where no elliptic functions appear. Comparing the two routes is the method of Chowla and Selberg [CS]. It shows that, for the character \(\chi\) of every imaginary quadratic field, \(e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)}\) is, up to an algebraic factor, the square of a period of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication divided by \(\pi^2\).

§20.1. Eisenstein series and Kronecker’s limit formula Tag 22JX

What is the constant term at \(s = 1\) of \(\sum'_{\mu \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} \lvert\mu\rvert^{-2s}\)? The answer comes from letting the lattice vary. For \(\tau = x + \mathrm{i}y\) with \(y > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), let \[E(\tau, s) := \sum_{(m,n) \ne (0,0)} \frac{y^s}{\lvert m + n\tau\rvert^{2s}} .\] Let \(q := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}\), let \(\eta(\tau) := q^{1/24}\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)\) be Dedekind’s function, and let \(\sigma_t(n) := \sum_{e \mid n} e^t\). For \(z > 0\), let \(K_\nu(z) := \tfrac12 \int_0^\infty t^{\nu - 1} e^{-z(t + t^{-1})/2} \, d t\).

Tag 22JY

Problem 20.1.1. \(E(g\tau, s) = E(\tau, s)\) for every \(g \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\).

Tag 22JZ

Problem 20.1.2. For real \(x\), \(y > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12\), \[\sum_{m \in \mathbb{Z}} \frac{1}{((m + x)^2 + y^2)^{s}} = \frac{\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(s - \frac12)}{\Gamma(s)\, y^{2s-1}} + \frac{4\pi^s}{\Gamma(s)} \sum_{k \ge 1} \Bigl(\frac{k}{y}\Bigr)^{s - \frac12} \! K_{s - \frac12}(2\pi k y) \cos(2\pi k x) .\]

Tag 22K2

Problem 20.1.3. \(E(\tau, s)\) equals \[\begin{multline*} 2\zeta(2s) y^s + \frac{2\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(s - \frac12)}{\Gamma(s)} \zeta(2s - 1) y^{1-s} \\ + \frac{8\pi^s \sqrt y}{\Gamma(s)} \sum_{n \ge 1} n^{s - \frac12} \sigma_{1 - 2s}(n) K_{s - \frac12}(2\pi n y) \cos(2\pi n x) . \end{multline*}\] In particular \(E(\tau, s)\) continues meromorphically to \(\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12\), with only a simple pole at \(s = 1\).

Tag 22K5

Problem 20.1.6. As \(s \to 1\), \[\frac{2\sqrt\pi\, \Gamma(s - \frac12)}{\Gamma(s)} \zeta(2s - 1) y^{1-s} = \frac{\pi}{s - 1} + 2\pi\bigl(\gamma - \log 2 - \tfrac12 \log y\bigr) + O(s - 1) .\]

Since \(2\zeta(2) y = \pi^2 y/3 = -4\pi \log\lvert q^{1/24}\rvert\), Problems 20.1.3–20.1.6 prove the following [We], [La, Ch. 20, 21].

Tag 22K6

Theorem 20.1.1 (Kronecker’s first limit formula). \(E(\tau, s) = \dfrac{\pi}{s - 1} + 2\pi\bigl(\gamma - \log 2 - \log(\sqrt y\, \lvert\eta(\tau)\rvert^2)\bigr) + O(s - 1)\).

Tag 22K7

Problem 20.1.7. \(\lvert\eta(-1/\tau)\rvert = \lvert\tau\rvert^{1/2} \lvert\eta(\tau)\rvert\).

Tag 22K8

Problem 20.1.8. Let \(Z(s) := \sum'_{\mu \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]} \lvert\mu\rvert^{-2s}\), and let \(\chi\) be the non-trivial character modulo \(4\). Then \(Z(s) = \pi/(s-1) + 2\pi(\gamma - \log 2 - 2\log\eta(\mathrm{i})) + O(s-1)\), and \[\frac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)} = \gamma - 2\log 2 - 4\log\eta(\mathrm{i}) .\]

This is Problem 12.3.5, now with a proof. It expresses \(L'/L(1,\chi)\) through a value of a modular form. To see Gamma values instead, we move to \(s = 0\), where \(L(s,\chi)\) is a finite combination of Hurwitz zeta functions.

§20.2. Lerch’s formula and the functional equation Tag 22K9

For \(x > 0\) and \(\operatorname{Re} s > 1\), let \(\zeta(s, x) := \sum_{n \ge 0} (n + x)^{-s}\). Euler–Maclaurin summation gives \[\zeta(s, x) = \frac{x^{1-s}}{s - 1} + \frac{x^{-s}}{2} - s \int_0^\infty \frac{\{t\} - \frac12}{(t + x)^{s+1}} \, d t ,\] which continues \(\zeta(s, x)\) to \(\operatorname{Re} s > -1\) with a single pole at \(s = 1\). Write \(\zeta'(s, x)\) for \(\partial_s \zeta(s, x)\).

Tag 22KA

Problem 20.2.1. \(\zeta(0, x) = \frac12 - x\), and \(\zeta(s, \frac12) = (2^s - 1)\zeta(s)\).

Tag 22KB

Problem 20.2.2. \(\partial_x^2\, \zeta'(0, x) = \zeta(2, x) = (\log\Gamma)''(x)\), and \(\zeta'(0, x+1) - \zeta'(0, x) = \log x\).

Problems 20.2.1 and 20.2.2, with \(\zeta(0) = -\frac12\) and \(\Gamma(\frac12) = \sqrt\pi\), prove the following.

Tag 22KC

Tag 22KD

Problem 20.2.3. Let \(\chi\) be an odd character modulo \(N\). Then \(L(0, \chi) = -\frac1N \sum_{a=1}^{N} a\,\chi(a)\), and \[L'(0, \chi) = \sum_{a=1}^{N-1} \chi(a) \log\Gamma(a/N) - L(0, \chi) \log N .\]

Let \(\chi\) be a primitive odd character modulo \(N\), and let \[\Lambda(s, \chi) := (N/\pi)^{(s+1)/2}\, \Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{s+1}{2}\bigr) L(s, \chi) .\] Then \(\Lambda(s, \chi) = W(\chi)\Lambda(1 - s, \bar\chi)\) with \(\lvert W(\chi)\rvert = 1\) [Dav, Ch. 9]; moreover \(W(\chi) = \tau(\chi)/(\mathrm{i}\sqrt N)\), which is \(1\) if \(\chi\) is real, because then the Gauss sum is \(\tau(\chi) = \mathrm{i}\sqrt N\) [Dav, Ch. 6].

Here it comes from computing one constant, \(L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\), in two ways: through \(s = 1\) by Theorem 20.1.1, and through \(s = 0\) by Theorem 20.2.1. The argument used only that \(Z(s)\) is both an Eisenstein series and \(4\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)\); the next section runs it for every imaginary quadratic field.

§20.3. The general odd character Tag 22KH

Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant \(-d\), with class number \(h\) and \(w\) roots of unity, and let \(\chi\) be its character, a primitive real odd character modulo \(d\). The class number formula is \(L(1, \chi) = 2\pi h/(w\sqrt d)\). For each class of positive definite forms of discriminant \(-d\), choose the reduced representative \(Q = [a, b, c]\), so that \(Q(m,n) = am^2 + bmn + cn^2\), \(b^2 - 4ac = -d\), \(\lvert b\rvert \le a \le c\), and \(b \ge 0\) if \(\lvert b\rvert = a\) or \(a = c\). Let \(\tau_Q := (-b + \mathrm{i}\sqrt d)/2a\) and \(y_Q := \operatorname{Im}\tau_Q\). The forms \(Q\) together represent each \(n \ge 1\) exactly \(w \sum_{e \mid n} \chi(e)\) times [Dav, Ch. 6], so \[\sum_Q \sum_{(m,n) \ne (0,0)} Q(m,n)^{-s} = w\, \zeta(s) L(s, \chi) \qquad (\operatorname{Re} s > 1) .\] The sum over \(Q\) has \(h\) terms. When \(h > 1\), can one class be taken alone?

Tag 22KJ

Problem 20.3.1. For \(d = 20\) the reduced forms are \(Q_1 = [1, 0, 5]\) and \(Q_2 = [2, 2, 3]\). The form \(Q_1\) represents \(1\) twice and \(3\) not at all, \(Q_2\) represents \(1\) not at all and \(3\) four times, and \(w\sum_{e \mid n}\chi(e)\) is \(2\) for \(n = 1\) and \(4\) for \(n = 3\). Hence neither \(\sum'_{(m,n)} Q_i(m,n)^{-s}\) is a constant multiple of \(\zeta(s)L(s, \chi)\).

So a formula through \(L(s, \chi)\) can see only the sum over classes. At \(s = 1\) this sum is a sum of logarithms, and what one can hope to evaluate is the product over \(Q\) of the quantities \(\sqrt{y_Q}\,\lvert\eta(\tau_Q)\rvert^2\), not each one.

Tag 22KK

Problem 20.3.2. \(L(0, \chi) = 2h/w\). For \(d = 3, 4, 7, 23\) this is \(\frac13, \frac12, 1, 3\).

Tag 22KL

Problem 20.3.3. \(\displaystyle \frac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)} = \gamma + \log 2\pi - \frac{w}{2h} \sum_{a=1}^{d-1} \chi(a) \log\Gamma\Bigl(\frac ad\Bigr)\). For \(d = 3, 7, 23\) it is \(0.36828\ldots\), \(0.01563\ldots\) and \(-0.42212\ldots\).

Tag 22KN

Problem 20.3.5. \(\displaystyle \frac{L'(1, \chi)}{L(1, \chi)} = \gamma - \log 2 - \frac12\log d - \frac2h \sum_Q \log\bigl(\sqrt{y_Q}\, \lvert\eta(\tau_Q)\rvert^2\bigr)\).

§20.4. Periods Tag 22KS

Why should \(\prod_a \Gamma(a/d)^{\chi(a)}\) be a period? Because values of \(\Gamma\) at rational numbers come, up to algebraic numbers and powers of \(\pi\), from integrals of algebraic differentials on Fermat curves.

Let \(N \ge 3\), and let \(F_N\) be the smooth projective curve with affine equation \(u^N + v^N = 1\). For integers \(a, b \ge 1\) with \(a + b \le N - 1\), the differential \(\omega_{a,b} := u^{a-1}v^{b-N}\, d u\) is holomorphic on \(F_N\), as one checks at the points with \(v = 0\) and at infinity. Let \(\kappa\) be the path \(u \mapsto (u, (1 - u^N)^{1/N})\), \(0 \le u \le 1\), from \((0,1)\) to \((1,0)\). Let \(\zeta_N := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/N}\), and let \(A(u,v) := (\zeta_N u, v)\) and \(B(u,v) := (u, \zeta_N v)\).

Tag 22KT

Problem 20.4.1. \(\int_\kappa \omega_{a,b} = \frac1N \mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac bN)\).

Tag 22KU

Problem 20.4.2. The chain \((1 - A)(1 - B)\kappa\) is a closed cycle on \(F_N\), and the integral of \(\omega_{a,b}\) over it is \(\frac1N (1 - \zeta_N^a)(1 - \zeta_N^b)\, \mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac bN)\).

So each \(\mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac bN)\) with \(a, b \ge 1\) and \(a + b \le N - 1\) is, up to an algebraic factor, a period of a holomorphic differential on \(F_N\) defined over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

Tag 22KV

Problem 20.4.3. For integers \(a \ge 1\), \(\Gamma(\frac aN)^N = \Gamma(a) \prod_{j=1}^{N-1} \mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac{ja}N)\).

Since \(\mathrm{B}(x, y+1) = \mathrm{B}(x, y)\, y/(x+y)\), every \(\mathrm{B}(\frac aN, \frac bN)\) with \(a, b \ge 1\) is a rational multiple of one with \(1 \le a, b \le N\), which is \(N\) times an integral of the algebraic differential \(\omega_{a,b}\) along \(\kappa\) (Problem 20.4.1). So by Problem 20.4.3, \(\Gamma(\frac aN)^N\) is, up to a rational factor, a product of such integrals.

Tag 22KW

Problem 20.4.4. The map \((u, v) \mapsto (u, v^2)\) from \(F_4\) to the curve \(y^2 = 1 - t^4\) pulls \(d t / y\) back to \(\omega_{1,2}\), and \(\mathrm{B}(\frac14, \frac12) = 2\varpi\).

For \(K\) as in Section 20.3, let \[\Omega_K := \sqrt\pi \prod_{a=1}^{d-1} \Gamma\Bigl(\frac ad\Bigr)^{w\chi(a)/4h} .\] For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), \(\Omega_K = 2\varpi\) (Problem 12.3.7, where \(\Omega\) denotes \(\Omega_K^2/2\pi = 2\varpi^2/\pi\)), which by Problems 20.4.2 and 20.4.4 is a period of \(d t / y\) on \(y^2 = 1 - t^4\) up to a factor in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\).

Tag 22KX

Problem 20.4.5. For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})\), \[\Omega_K = \sqrt\pi\, \bigl(\Gamma(\tfrac13)/\Gamma(\tfrac23)\bigr)^{3/2} = 3^{1/4}2^{-1/2}\, \mathrm{B}(\tfrac13, \tfrac13) .\]

Here \(F_3\) is itself a curve of genus one, \(\omega_{1,1}\) is its holomorphic differential, and \(A\) multiplies \(\omega_{1,1}\) by \(\zeta_3\): the curve has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\rho]\). This is the general pattern. Chowla and Selberg deduced the following from Theorem 20.3.1 [CS]; we give it in the formulation of Gross [Gr]. Every nonzero period of a nonzero holomorphic differential defined over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) on an elliptic curve over \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal{O}_K\) lies in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\, \Omega_K\). Gross proved this period relation geometrically, by relating such curves to Jacobians of Fermat curves, whose periods are Beta values by Problem 20.4.2 [Gr].

Tag 22KY

Problem 20.4.6. \(e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)} = \Omega_K^2/2\pi^2\). For \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) this is \(2\varpi^2/\pi^2 = \Gamma(\frac14)^4/4\pi^3 = 1.39320\ldots\)

Thus \(e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)} = -2\,(\Omega_K/2\pi\mathrm{i})^2\): up to a factor in \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^\times\), it is the square of the ratio of a period of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal{O}_K\) to \(2\pi\mathrm{i}\), the period of \(d z/z\) on the multiplicative group. In Chapter 28, Covolumes and periods, the two constants enter in this form for \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), through the Faltings height of such a curve (Problem 28.1.10).
