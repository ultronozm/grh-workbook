Prerequisites.

Local and global class field theory, including ray class groups and the Chebotarev density theorem [Ne, Ch. V–VI, §VII.13]. Elliptic curves at the level of [Si1, Ch. III, V]; the curve \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) as in Chapter 14, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime. Modular forms of weight \(2\) and Hecke operators [DS, Ch. 1–6]. Parabolic induction and the Steinberg representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) over a \(p\)-adic field [BH, Ch. 3]. The words crystalline, potentially semistable and Hodge–Tate weight are used only in statements. Everything below that is not posed as a problem is a published theorem, stated with a reference, and is a black box for this chapter.

§34.1. Weil–Deligne representations and local Langlands maps Tag 22WL

What does a Galois representation remember at a prime \(p\ne\ell\), and what does an automorphic representation give to compare it with?

Let \(K/\mathbb{Q}_p\) be finite, with uniformizer \(\varpi_K\) and residue field of order \(q\). Let \(W_K\) be its Weil group, \(I_K\) the inertia subgroup, and \(\mathrm{Frob}\in W_K\) a geometric Frobenius. Let \(\operatorname{Art}_K \colon K^\times \to W_K^{ab}\) be the reciprocity map, normalized so that \(\varpi_K\) goes to a geometric Frobenius, and let \(\lvert w \rvert := \lvert \operatorname{Art}_K^{-1}(w) \rvert\), so that \(\lvert \mathrm{Frob}\rvert = q^{-1}\). A Weil–Deligne representation is a pair \((r, N)\) of a representation \(r \colon W_K \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\) on a finite-dimensional space over \(\mathbb{C}\) or \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), trivial on an open subgroup of \(I_K\), and an endomorphism \(N\) with \(r(w) N r(w)^{-1} = \lvert w \rvert N\). It is Frobenius-semisimple if \(r(\mathrm{Frob})\) is semisimple. Let \(\operatorname{Sp}(2)\) be the pair with \(V = \mathbb{C}^2\) (or \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^2\)), \(r(w) = \mathrm{diag}(1, \lvert w \rvert)\) and \(N e_1 = e_2\), \(N e_2 = 0\).

Tag 22WM

Problem 34.1.1. In a Weil–Deligne representation, \(N\) is nilpotent.

Tag 22WN

Problem 34.1.2. A two-dimensional Weil–Deligne representation with \(N \ne 0\) is isomorphic to \(\operatorname{Sp}(2) \otimes \chi\) for exactly one character \(\chi\) of \(W_K\).

Let \(\ell \ne p\). By Grothendieck’s monodromy theorem [Ta], every continuous \(\rho \colon W_K \to \mathrm{GL}_n(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) determines a Weil–Deligne representation \(\operatorname{WD}(\rho) = (r, N)\), characterized by \(\rho(\mathrm{Frob}^m \sigma) = r(\mathrm{Frob}^m \sigma) \exp(t_\ell(\sigma) N)\) for \(m \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(\sigma \in I_K\), where \(t_\ell \colon I_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell\) is a surjection onto the \(\ell\)-part of tame inertia. We write \(\operatorname{WD}(\rho)^{F\text{-ss}}\) for its Frobenius-semisimplification.

Tag 22WP

Problem 34.1.3. If \(\rho(I_K)\) is finite, then \(N = 0\). If \(\rho(I_K)\) is a nontrivial group of unipotent matrices, then \(N \ne 0\).

For characters \(\chi_1, \dots, \chi_n\) of \(K^\times\), let \(i(\chi_1, \dots, \chi_n)\) be the normalized parabolic induction from the Borel subgroup of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(K)\). Let \(\mathrm{St}\) be the Steinberg representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(K)\), the irreducible subquotient of \(i(\lvert\cdot\rvert^{1/2}, \lvert\cdot\rvert^{-1/2})\) that is not a character.

Tag 22WQ

Theorem 34.1.1 (Harris–Taylor, Henniart). For each \(n \ge 1\) there is a bijection \(\operatorname{rec}_K\) from irreducible admissible representations of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(K)\) to Frobenius-semisimple \(n\)-dimensional Weil–Deligne representations of \(W_K\) such that \(\operatorname{rec}_K(\chi) = \chi \circ \operatorname{Art}_K^{-1}\) for \(n = 1\), \(\operatorname{rec}_K(\pi \otimes \chi \circ \det) = \operatorname{rec}_K(\pi) \otimes \operatorname{rec}_K(\chi)\), \(\det \operatorname{rec}_K(\pi) = \operatorname{rec}_K(\omega_\pi)\) for the central character \(\omega_\pi\), \(\operatorname{rec}_K(\pi^\vee) = \operatorname{rec}_K(\pi)^\vee\), and \(L\)- and \(\epsilon\)-factors of pairs are preserved. These properties determine \(\operatorname{rec}_K\).

See [HT], [He] and, for another proof, [Sch]. If \(i(\chi_1, \dots, \chi_n)\) is irreducible, then \(\operatorname{rec}_K\) sends it to \(\bigoplus_i \chi_i \circ \operatorname{Art}_K^{-1}\) with \(N = 0\); for every irreducible subquotient \(\pi\) of \(i(\chi_1, \dots, \chi_n)\), the semisimplification of the \(W_K\)-representation underlying \(\operatorname{rec}_K(\pi)\) is \(\bigoplus_i \chi_i \circ \operatorname{Art}_K^{-1}\); and \(\operatorname{rec}_K(\mathrm{St}\otimes \chi) = \operatorname{Sp}(2) \otimes \chi\lvert\cdot\rvert^{-1/2} \circ \operatorname{Art}_K^{-1}\) [Ku]. Only these properties are used below.

§34.2. Galois representations over CM fields Tag 22WR

Let \(F\) be a CM field with totally real subfield \(F^+\) and complex conjugation \(c\), let \(\ell\) be a prime and \(\iota \colon \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\cong \mathbb{C}\). Let \(\epsilon_\ell\) be the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character. A cuspidal automorphic representation \(\pi\) of \(\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_F)\) is polarized if \(\pi^c \cong \pi^\vee \otimes (\chi \circ N_{F/F^+} \circ \det)\) for a character \(\chi\) of \(\mathbb{A}_{F^+}^\times/(F^+)^\times\) satisfying a sign condition at the real places, and regular algebraic if \(\pi_\infty\) has the infinitesimal character of an irreducible algebraic representation of \(\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{GL}_a\); see [BLGGT].

Tag 22WS

Theorem 34.2.1. Let \(\pi\) be a regular algebraic polarized cuspidal automorphic representation of \(\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_F)\). There is a continuous semisimple \(r = r_{\ell,\iota}(\pi) \colon G_F \to \mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) such that: (i) \(r^c \cong r^\vee \otimes \epsilon_\ell^{1-a} r_{\ell,\iota}(\chi)|_{G_F}\); (ii) for every finite place \(w \nmid \ell\), \(\iota\operatorname{WD}(r|_{G_{F_w}})^{F\text{-ss}} \cong \operatorname{rec}_{F_w}(\pi_w \otimes \lvert\det\rvert^{(1-a)/2})\); (iii) for every \(w \mid \ell\), \(r|_{G_{F_w}}\) is potentially semistable, crystalline if \(\pi_w\) is unramified, with Hodge–Tate weights determined by \(\pi_\infty\), and (ii) holds for the Weil–Deligne representation attached to it by Fontaine.

The representations are constructed in the cohomology of Shimura varieties attached to unitary groups, to which \(\pi\) is first descended, by Kottwitz, Clozel, Harris–Taylor, Shin and Chenevier–Harris [Cl; HT; Shi; CH]. Part (ii) is due to Taylor–Yoshida and Caraiani [TY; Car1], and part (iii) to Barnet-Lamb–Gee–Geraghty–Taylor and Caraiani [BLGGT, Thm. 2.1.1], [Car2]. For Alpöge’s argument [Alp], the reconstruction [Adr] lists Theorem 34.2.1, with local–global compatibility at all places, among the published inputs of its Galois-theoretic steps.

The smallest case is \(a = 1\) over an imaginary quadratic field \(K\), where the unitary group is \(U(1) := \ker(N \colon \mathrm{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{G}_m)\).

Tag 22WT

Problem 34.2.1. \(U(1)(\mathbb{R})\) is the unit circle. \(U(1)(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^\times\) if \(p\) splits in \(K\), and \(U(1)(\mathbb{Q}_p)\) is compact otherwise.

Tag 22WU

Problem 34.2.2. Let \(\phi\) be a character of \(\mathbb{A}_K^\times/K^\times\) that is trivial on \(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times\). Then \(\phi^c = \phi^{-1}\), and \(\phi = \mu(x/x^c)\) for exactly one character \(\mu\) of \(U(1)(\mathbb{A})/U(1)(\mathbb{Q})\). Conversely each such \(\mu\) gives such a \(\phi\).

Tag 22WV

Problem 34.2.3. If \(\phi^c = \phi^{-1}\), then \(\phi|_{\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times}\) is either trivial or the quadratic character \(\omega_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\).

So the characters with \(\phi^c = \phi^{-1}\) fall into two classes, and by Problem 34.2.2 only the first comes from \(U(1)\).

§34.3. The Hecke character of \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) Tag 22WW

What is the representation of Theorem 34.2.1 when \(a = 1\), and how does it compare with the curve \(E \colon y^2 = x^3 - x\)? Let \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(\mathfrak{f} = (2 + 2\mathrm{i})\).

Tag 22WX

Problem 34.3.1. Every ideal \(\mathfrak{a}\) of \(\mathcal{O}\) prime to \(1 + \mathrm{i}\) has exactly one generator \(\alpha \equiv 1 \pmod{\mathfrak{f}}\). Let \(\psi(\mathfrak{a}) := \alpha\). Then \(\psi\) is multiplicative, \(\psi(\mathfrak{a}^c) = \overline{\psi(\mathfrak{a})}\), and \(\psi(\mathfrak{a})\psi(\mathfrak{a}^c) = N\mathfrak{a}\).

For \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) and \(\mathfrak{p} \mid p\), one has \(p + 1 - \#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = \psi(\mathfrak{p}) + \psi(\mathfrak{p}^c)\) [IR, Ch. 18, §4].

Tag 22WY

Problem 34.3.2. \(\psi((1 + 2\mathrm{i})) = -1 - 2\mathrm{i}\) and \(\psi((3 + 2\mathrm{i})) = 3 + 2\mathrm{i}\), and \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_5) = \#E(\mathbb{F}_{13}) = 8\).

Tag 22WZ

Problem 34.3.3. Let \(n \ge 1\). If ideals \(\mathfrak{a}, \mathfrak{b}\) prime to \(2\ell\) have the same class in the ray class group modulo \(\mathfrak{f}\ell^n\), then \(\psi(\mathfrak{a}) \equiv \psi(\mathfrak{b}) \pmod{\ell^n\mathcal{O}}\).

Combining Problem 34.3.3 with Artin reciprocity for the ray class fields modulo \(\mathfrak{f}\ell^n\) gives the following.

Tag 22X2

Theorem 34.3.1. There is a continuous character \(\psi_\ell \colon G_K \to (\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell)^\times\), unramified outside \(2\ell\), with \(\psi_\ell(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{p}) = \psi(\mathfrak{p})\) for every prime \(\mathfrak{p} \nmid 2\ell\), where \(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{p}\) is an arithmetic Frobenius.

By Problem 34.3.1, \(\psi\psi^c\) is the norm, so \(\psi\) is polarized, and \(\psi_\ell\), followed by an embedding \(\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), is the representation of Theorem 34.2.1 for \(a = 1\), up to the normalizations of the reciprocity maps. At the places not above \(\ell\) the theorem is then class field theory; the next problem looks at the places above \(\ell\).

Tag 22X3

Problem 34.3.4. Let \(\ell \equiv 1 \pmod 4\), \(\ell = \lambda\bar\lambda\), and let \(\psi_\lambda \colon G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\) be \(\psi_\ell\) followed by \(\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to \mathcal{O}_\lambda = \mathbb{Z}_\ell\). Then \(\psi_\lambda = \epsilon_\ell\) on an open subgroup of the inertia group at \(\lambda\), and \(\psi_\lambda\) has finite image on the inertia group at \(\bar\lambda\).

So \(\psi_\lambda\) has Hodge–Tate weights \(1\) and \(0\) at the two places above \(\ell\) (in the convention where \(\epsilon_\ell\) has weight \(1\)), and the polarization appears as \(\mathrm{HT}_{\tau c} = 1 - \mathrm{HT}_\tau\).

The automorphism \([\mathrm{i}](x, y) = (-x, \mathrm{i}y)\) of \(E\) is defined over \(K\), so \(V_\ell(E) := T_\ell(E) \otimes \mathbb{Q}\) is free of rank one over \(K \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell\), and \(G_K\) acts on it through a character \(G_K \to (K \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell)^\times\).

Tag 22X4

Problem 34.3.5. Let \(\mathfrak{p} \nmid 2\ell\). If \(N\mathfrak{p} = p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\), then \(\mathrm{Frob}_\mathfrak{p}\) acts on \(V_\ell(E)\) as \(\psi(\mathfrak{p})\). If \(\mathfrak{p} = (p)\) with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), it acts as \(-p = \psi((p))\).

By the Chebotarev density theorem, Problem 34.3.5 proves the following; it is due to Deuring, see [Si2, Ch. II, §10].

Tag 22X5

Theorem 34.3.2. The character of \(G_K\) on \(V_\ell(E)\) is \(\psi_\ell\).

Tag 22X6

Problem 34.3.6. Let \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G_K \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^\times\) be \(\psi_\ell\) followed by the two embeddings \(\mathcal{O}\otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\). Then \(\psi_2 = \psi_1^c\), \(V_\ell(E) \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\cong \operatorname{Ind}_{G_K}^{G_\mathbb{Q}} \psi_1\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p \mid V_\ell(E)) = 0\) for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(p \ne \ell\).

Restricted to \(G_K\), \(V_\ell(E)\) is \(\psi_1 \oplus \psi_1^c\), which is reducible. So the weight-two form of \(E\), base changed to \(K\), is not cuspidal, and it enters Theorem 34.2.1 only through \(\psi\), with \(a = 1\). For a cuspidal example with \(a = 2\) one needs a curve without complex multiplication by \(K\). We take \(X_0(11)\) and stay over \(\mathbb{Q}\); its base change to \(K\) is cuspidal, and the local questions are the same.

§34.4. Eichler–Shimura for \(X_0(11)\) Tag 22X7

How does one get Frobenius eigenvalues out of the cohomology of a Shimura variety, and what does each method give? Let \[f = q \prod_{n \ge 1} (1 - q^n)^2 (1 - q^{11n})^2 = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n ,\] the normalized eigenform spanning \(S_2(\Gamma_0(11))\) [DS]. The curve \(X_0(11)\) has genus one and the model \(E \colon y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10x - 20\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\), with good reduction away from \(11\). For \(p \ne 11\), the Hecke operator \(T_p\) acts on \(E = J_0(11)\) as \([a_p]\), and by the Eichler–Shimura relation [DS, Thm. 8.7.2], [Sh, Thm. 7.9], the reduction of \(T_p\) modulo \(p\) is \(F + V\), where \(F\) is the Frobenius endomorphism and \(FV = VF = p\).

Tag 22X8

Problem 34.4.1. \(a_2 = -2\), \(a_3 = -1\), \(a_5 = 1\), \(a_7 = -2\), \(a_{11} = 1\), \(a_{13} = 4\).

Tag 22X9

Problem 34.4.2. For \(p \notin \{11, \ell\}\), the arithmetic Frobenius at \(p\) on \(V_\ell(E)\) satisfies \(\mathrm{Frob}_p^2 - a_p\mathrm{Frob}_p + p = 0\).

For \(p \notin \{11, \ell\}\), the arithmetic Frobenius at \(p\) has determinant \(p\) on \(V_\ell(E)\), by the Weil pairing [Si1], and \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p^k}) = p^k + 1 - \operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p^k \mid V_\ell(E))\) for \(k \ge 1\) [Si1].

Tag 22XA

Problem 34.4.3. Let \(\Phi \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell)\) satisfy \(\Phi^2 - a\Phi + p = 0\) with \(a \in \mathbb{Z}\), and let \(\det\Phi = p\). Then the characteristic polynomial of \(\Phi\) is \(X^2 - aX + p\). Hence, taking \(\ell \ne p\), \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1 - a_p\) for \(p \ne 11\).

The relation of Problem 34.4.2 kills Frobenius; it does not give the multiplicities of its roots. In dimension two the determinant supplies them. In dimension three, \(\mathrm{diag}(\alpha, \alpha, \beta)\) and \(\mathrm{diag}(\alpha, \beta, \beta)\) are killed by the same quadratic polynomial. The published constructions behind Theorem 34.2.1 obtain the whole characteristic polynomial by counting points, following Langlands and Kottwitz [Ko]. The simplest instance is the trace formula for \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p^k})\) stated before Problem 34.4.3.

Tag 22XB

Problem 34.4.4. \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1 - a_p\) for \(p = 2, 3, 5, 7, 13\) by direct count. The numbers \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_p)\) and \(\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p^2})\) determine the characteristic polynomial of \(\mathrm{Frob}_p\); for \(p = 2\) both equal \(5\).

At \(11\) the curve has split multiplicative reduction. By Tate’s theory [Si2, Ch. V], \(E \cong \mathbb{G}_m/q^\mathbb{Z}\) over \(\mathbb{Q}_{11}\) for some \(q \in 11\mathbb{Z}_{11}\), and \(T_\ell(E)\) is an extension of \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) by \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)\) on which the inertia group at \(11\) acts through the Kummer classes of \(q^{1/\ell^n}\).

Tag 22XC

Problem 34.4.5. For \(\ell \ne 11\), \(\operatorname{WD}(V_\ell(E)|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{11}}}) \cong \operatorname{Sp}(2) \otimes \chi\) for an unramified character \(\chi\); in particular \(N \ne 0\).

Correspondingly, \(\pi_{11}\) is an unramified twist of \(\mathrm{St}\), and the compatibility of Theorem 34.2.1(ii), in its version over \(\mathbb{Q}\) due to Carayol [Ca], matches the monodromy at \(11\). The later parts, from Chapter 35 on, state each ingredient in the generality the proof uses and assemble them.

§34.1

34.1.1

Take \(w = \mathrm{Frob}\): \(N\) is conjugate to \(q^{-1}N\).

34.1.2

\(\ker N\) is a line stable under \(W_K\); compare the characters on \(\ker N\) and on \(V/\ker N\). The extension is trivial on inertia, and splits because \(\lvert\mathrm{Frob}\rvert \ne 1\).

34.1.3

\(\exp(t_\ell(\sigma)N) = 1\) on an open subgroup of \(I_K\) exactly when \(N = 0\).

§34.2

34.2.1

\(K \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p\) is \(\mathbb{Q}_p \times \mathbb{Q}_p\) or a field.

34.2.2

Hilbert’s Theorem 90 for \(K\) and for each \(K \otimes \mathbb{Q}_v\), and for the units at the unramified places.

34.2.3

\(\phi\) is trivial on \(\mathbb{Q}^\times\) and on the norms \(x x^c\); use \(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q}^\times/\mathbb{Q}^\times N(\mathbb{A}_K^\times) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\).

§34.3

34.3.1

The units \(\pm 1, \pm\mathrm{i}\) map bijectively onto \((\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{f})^\times\), which has order \(4\); and \(\mathfrak{f}^c = \mathfrak{f}\).

34.3.3

Write \(\mathfrak{a} = \mathfrak{b}(\beta)\) with \(\beta \equiv 1 \pmod{^\times \mathfrak{f}\ell^n}\); then \(\beta\) is the generator of \((\beta)\) that \(\psi\) picks.

34.3.4

Approximate an idèle \(u \in 1 + \ell^m\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) placed at \(\lambda\) by a global element of \(\mathcal{O}\). With uniformizers sent to arithmetic Frobenius, as in Theorem 34.3.1, the local reciprocity map sends \(u\) to an element acting on \(\ell\)-power roots of unity by \(u^{-1}\).

34.3.5

The \(p\)-power Frobenius of the reduction commutes with \([\mathrm{i}]\), so it is \([\alpha]\) with \(\alpha \in \mathcal{O}\) of norm \(p\). It is inseparable, so \(\alpha \in \mathfrak{p}\); fix the unit with \(a_p = \psi(\mathfrak{p}) + \psi(\mathfrak{p}^c)\). For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), use \(a_p = 0\).

34.3.6

Complex conjugation acts on \(V_\ell(E)\) and anticommutes with \([\mathrm{i}]\).

34.4.1

Expand the product to order \(q^{13}\).

34.4.2

34.4.4

34.4.5

Problems 34.1.2 and 34.1.3; \(v_{11}(q) \ne 0\).
