Prerequisites.

Section 7.3 of Chapter 7, The slope inequality in the mean: Problem 7.3.1, Theorem 7.3.1, and their notation. Finite-dimensional spectral theory and Schur’s lemma. Binary quadratic forms and their reduction [Cox]. For Section 10.4 only, the spectral expansion of an automorphic kernel on \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}\), where \(\mathbb{H}\) is the upper half-plane [Iw2, Ch. 7].

Theorem 7.3.1 bounds the codimension of a sum of many images by a second moment. Where can such a bound come from? In this chapter it comes from Gram matrices, from evaluations at points, and from a relative trace formula, each in its simplest case.

§10.1. Gram matrices Tag 22A6

As in Section 7.3, let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a complex inner product space, now of dimension \(D' > 0\). Let \(B_i \colon X_i \to \mathcal{H}\), \(i \in I\), be linear maps from inner product spaces of dimensions \(r_i\), with \(R := \sum_i r_i > 0\), let \(\gamma_i > 0\), and let \(K_i := \gamma_i B_i^* B_i\) and \(Q := \sum_i \gamma_i B_i B_i^*\). Let \(X := \bigoplus_i X_i\) and \(B \colon X \to \mathcal{H}\), \(B(x_i)_i := \sum_i \sqrt{\gamma_i} B_i x_i\), so that \(Q = BB^*\). The Gram matrix of the family is \(G := B^*B\). Its blocks are \(G_{ij} = \sqrt{\gamma_i\gamma_j}\, B_i^* B_j \colon X_j \to X_i\), and \(G_{ii} = K_i\). When every \(X_i\) is a line spanned by a unit vector \(\xi_i\), \(G\) is the Gram matrix of the vectors \(\sqrt{\gamma_i} B_i \xi_i\).

Tag 22A7

Problem 10.1.1. \(Q(\mathcal{H}) = \sum_i B_i(X_i)\).

Tag 22A8

Problem 10.1.2. \(Q\) and \(G\) have the same nonzero eigenvalues, with multiplicities. Hence \(\operatorname{tr}Q = \sum_i \operatorname{tr}K_i\) and \(\operatorname{tr}Q^2 = \sum_{i,j} \lVert G_{ij} \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\).

With Problem 7.3.1 this proves the following.

Tag 22A9

Theorem 10.1.1. If some \(B_i\) is nonzero, then \[\dim \sum_i B_i(X_i) \ge \frac{\bigl( \sum_i \operatorname{tr}K_i \bigr)^2}{\sum_{i,j} \lVert G_{ij} \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2} .\]

Unlike the left side, the right side changes little when the \(B_i\) are perturbed.

Tag 22AA

Problem 10.1.3. Let \(v, w \in \mathbb{C}^2\) be unit vectors, \(X_1 = X_2 = \mathbb{C}\), \(B_1(1) = v\), \(B_2(1) = w\) and \(\gamma_1 = \gamma_2 = 1\). The right side of Theorem 10.1.1 is \(2/(1 + \lvert \langle v, w \rangle \rvert^2)\).

So two nearly parallel lines count as about one direction. Suppose now that each weighted map is an isometry, \(K_i = 1\), and that for some \(\eta_0 \ge 0\) \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:pair}\tag{10.1.1} \lVert G_{ij} \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le (1 + \eta_0) \frac{r_i r_j}{D'} \qquad (i \ne j) . \end{equation}\] This is hypothesis (G1) of [Adr, Prop. 13.11], with \(D'\) replaced there by a number close to \(\dim\mathcal{H}\).

Tag 22AB

Problem 10.1.4. Under (10.1.1) and \(K_i = 1\) for all \(i\), \(\operatorname{tr}Q^2 \le (1 + \eta_0 + D'/R)(\operatorname{tr}Q)^2/D'\).

Combining Problem 10.1.4 with Theorem 7.3.1, for \(e = 0\), gives a zero estimate from pairwise bounds.

Tag 22AC

Theorem 10.1.2. Assume (10.1.1) and \(K_i = 1\) for all \(i\). Let \(Y_i \subseteq X_i\) be subspaces with \(\sum_i \operatorname{codim}Y_i \le \alpha R\), where \(0 \le \alpha \le 1\). Then \[\operatorname{codim}_\mathcal{H}\sum_i B_i(Y_i) \le \Bigl( \eta_0 + \frac{D'}{R} + 4\alpha \Bigr) D' .\]

The constant \(r_i r_j/D'\) in (10.1.1) is an average over relative positions.

Tag 22AD

Problem 10.1.5. Let \(B \colon \mathbb{C}^r \to \mathcal{H}\) and \(B' \colon \mathbb{C}^{r'} \to \mathcal{H}\) be isometries, and let \(u\) be distributed according to Haar measure on the unitary group of \(\mathcal{H}\). Then \(\mathbb{E}\lVert B^* u B' \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = r r'/D'\).

So (10.1.1) asks that no two images overlap, in the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, by more than \(1 + \eta_0\) times this average. The number of maps by itself proves nothing.

Tag 22AE

Problem 10.1.6. Let \(\mathcal{H}= \mathbb{C}^{D'}\) with \(D' \ge 2\) and standard basis \(e_1, \dots, e_{D'}\). Let \(X_0 = \mathbb{C}^{D'}\) with \(B_0 = 1\), and for \(1 \le i \le N\) let \(X_i = \mathbb{C}\) with \(B_i(1) = e_1\); let all \(\gamma_i = 1\). Then \(B_0\) is onto, but omitting \(X_0\), a proportion \(D'/(N + D')\) of the source directions, leaves a sum of images of codimension \(D' - 1\). The ratio \(D' \operatorname{tr}Q^2/(\operatorname{tr}Q)^2\) equals \(D'\bigl((N+1)^2 + D' - 1\bigr)/(N + D')^2\).

For large \(N\) this ratio is close to \(D'\), and Theorem 7.3.1 gives nothing, as it must. Here (10.1.1) fails by a factor \(D'\) for every pair \(i, j \ge 1\).

§10.2. Evaluation at points Tag 22AF

The simplest maps are evaluations at points. Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on a space \(\mathcal{X}\), and let \(\mathcal{H}\subset L^2(\mathcal{X}, \nu)\) be a space of bounded continuous functions of dimension \(D'\), with an orthonormal basis \(\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{D'}\). Its reproducing kernel is \(K(x, y) := \sum_n \varphi_n(x) \overline{\varphi_n(y)}\). For points \(x_1, \dots, x_N \in \mathcal{X}\) and weights \(\gamma_i > 0\), let \(X_i = \mathbb{C}\) and \(B_i(1) := K(\cdot, x_i)\). Problem 7.3.2 is an example.

Tag 22AG

Problem 10.2.1. \(B_i^* f = f(x_i)\), so that \(\langle Qf, f \rangle = \sum_i \gamma_i \lvert f(x_i) \rvert^2\), and \(G_{ij} = \sqrt{\gamma_i\gamma_j}\, K(x_i, x_j)\). Moreover \(\int_{\mathcal{X}} K(x, x) \, d\nu(x) = D'\).

Tag 22AH

Problem 10.2.2. \(\langle Q\varphi_n, \varphi_m \rangle = \sum_i \gamma_i (\varphi_n \overline{\varphi_m})(x_i)\). Hence \(Q = 1\) exactly when the measure \(\sum_i \gamma_i \delta_{x_i}\) integrates every \(\varphi_n \overline{\varphi_m}\) as \(\nu\) does.

Tag 22AJ

Problem 10.2.3. The functions \(f \in \mathcal{H}\) that vanish at every \(x_i\) form a space of dimension at most \(\lVert Q - 1 \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2\).

In particular, if the weighted points integrate every \(\varphi_n \overline{\varphi_m}\) exactly, no nonzero \(f \in \mathcal{H}\) vanishes at all of them. The simplest families with \(Q\) exactly scalar are orbits of a group.

Tag 22AK

Problem 10.2.4. Let a finite group \(\Gamma\) act unitarily and irreducibly on \(\mathcal{H}\), and let \(Y \subseteq \mathcal{H}\) be a subspace of dimension \(r\) with inclusion \(\iota\). For \(g \in \Gamma\) let \(B_g := g\iota \colon Y \to \mathcal{H}\) with weight \(1/\lvert \Gamma \rvert\). Then \(Q = (r/D') \cdot 1\).

For instance, let \(\nu\) be the normalized rotation-invariant measure on the sphere \(S^2 \subset \mathbb{R}^3\), and let \(\mathcal{H}\) be the space of restrictions of linear forms. Since \(\int x_k^2 \, d\nu = 1/3\), the functions \(\sqrt3\, x_k\) are an orthonormal basis, and \(K(x, y) = 3\langle x, y \rangle\). The rotation group of a regular tetrahedron acts irreducibly on \(\mathcal{H}\), and the orbit of the line spanned by \(K(\cdot, x_0)\), for a vertex \(x_0\), gives, up to the factor \(K(x_0, x_0) = 3\), the evaluations at the four vertices.

Tag 22AL

Problem 10.2.5. Let \(x_1, \dots, x_4\) be the vertices of a regular tetrahedron inscribed in \(S^2\), and \(\gamma_i = 1/4\). Then \(G = 1 - J/4\), where \(J\) is the \(4 \times 4\) matrix of ones, and \(Q = 1\). For the points \(\pm e_1, \pm e_2\) instead, with \(\gamma_i = 1/4\), \(\operatorname{rank}Q = (\operatorname{tr}Q)^2/\operatorname{tr}Q^2 = 2\).

§10.3. Heegner points Tag 22AM

The simplest point sets with arithmetic meaning are CM points. On the modular curve their equidistribution is a theorem of Duke. Let \(\mathcal{X} := \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}\) with the probability measure \(d\nu = (3/\pi) \, y^{-2} \, dx \, dy\). A primitive positive definite form \([a, b, c] = ax^2 + bxy + cy^2\) of discriminant \(b^2 - 4ac = -d\) has the root \(z_{[a,b,c]} := (-b + \mathrm{i}\sqrt d)/(2a)\) in \(\mathbb{H}\), and the map from forms to roots is \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\)-equivariant. For \(d > 4\) let \(\mathcal{H}_d \subset \mathcal{X}\) be the set of these roots. Every form is equivalent to exactly one reduced form [Cox, Thm. 2.8], so \(\mathcal{H}_d\) has \(h(-d)\) points, one for each reduced form, where \(h(-d)\) is the class number, the number of classes of primitive positive definite forms of discriminant \(-d\).

Tag 22AN

Problem 10.3.1. \(h(-23) = 3\), and \(\mathcal{H}_{23}\) consists of \((-1 + \mathrm{i}\sqrt{23})/2\) and \((\pm 1 + \mathrm{i}\sqrt{23})/4\).

Tag 22AP

Theorem 10.3.1 (Duke). Let \(d \to \infty\) through integers for which \(-d\) is a fundamental discriminant. Then for every continuous compactly supported function \(F\) on \(\mathcal{X}\), \[\frac{1}{h(-d)} \sum_{z \in \mathcal{H}_d} F(z) \to \int_{\mathcal{X}} F \, d\nu .\]

See [Du, Thm. 1], where it is proved, with a rate, for convex sets; continuous \(F\) follow by approximation. The proof rests on Iwaniec’s bound for the Fourier coefficients of forms of half-integral weight [Iw1].

Tag 22AQ

Problem 10.3.2. Theorem 10.3.1 holds for every bounded continuous \(F\).

Tag 22AR

Problem 10.3.3. Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be as in Section 10.2 on \(\mathcal{X}\), for instance the span of \(1\) and finitely many Maass cusp forms. Let \(Q_d\) be the operator of the evaluations at the points of \(\mathcal{H}_d\) with weights \(1/h(-d)\). Then \(Q_d \to 1\) as \(d \to \infty\) as in Theorem 10.3.1. Hence, for all large such \(d\), no nonzero \(f \in \mathcal{H}\) vanishes on \(\mathcal{H}_d\).

§10.4. What a relative trace formula supplies Tag 22AS

How can one verify (10.1.1)? A block \(G_{ij}\) is a sum over an orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal{H}\) of products of values, by Problem 10.2.1. A trace formula turns such a sum into a sum over the group. Begin with the circle. Let \(\mathcal{X} = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\) with Lebesgue measure, \(e(t) := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}t}\), and let \(\mathcal{H}\) be spanned by the \(e(kx)\) with \(\lvert k \rvert < n\). Then \(D' = 2n - 1\), and \(K(x, y) = D_n(x - y)\) with \(D_n(t) := \sum_{\lvert k \rvert < n} e(kt)\). For points \(x_1, \dots, x_N\) with \(\gamma_i = 1/N\), let \(W(k) := N^{-1} \sum_i e(kx_i)\); then \(\operatorname{tr}Q = D'\).

Tag 22AT

Problem 10.4.1. \(\displaystyle \operatorname{tr}Q^2 = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum_{i,j} \lvert D_n(x_i - x_j) \rvert^2 = \sum_{\lvert k \rvert < D'} (D' - \lvert k \rvert) \lvert W(k) \rvert^2 .\)

The middle term is a sum over pairs of points, and is large when many pairs lie at distance less than \(1/D'\). The right side is a sum over frequencies. So \(Q\) is almost scalar when the Weyl sums are small up to frequency \(D'\), or equivalently when there are not too many close pairs.

Tag 22AU

Problem 10.4.2. If the \(x_i\) are independent and uniformly distributed, then \(\mathbb{E}\operatorname{tr}Q^2 = D' + D'(D'-1)/N\).

Here \(K_i = D'/N\). After rescaling to \(\gamma_i = 1/D'\), so that \(K_i = 1\), the ratio \(D' \operatorname{tr}Q^2/(\operatorname{tr}Q)^2\), which does not depend on the scaling, is \(1 + (D'-1)/N\) in the mean, within the value \(1 + D'/N\) that Problem 10.1.4 allows for \(\eta_0 = 0\) and \(R = N\).

In the argument the maps are periods, not evaluations. On \(\mathcal{X} = \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}) \backslash \mathbb{H}\), with \(\mathcal{H}\) as in Problem 10.3.3, let \(P_d(f) := \sum_{z \in \mathcal{H}_d} f(z)\). For \(d > 4\) let \(X_d = \mathbb{C}\), let \(\gamma_d > 0\), and let \(B_d \colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathcal{H}\) be the map with \(B_d^* = P_d\). The family \((B_d)\), over a set of \(d\), satisfies a zero estimate if few \(f \in \mathcal{H}\) have all their periods zero.

Tag 22AV

Problem 10.4.3. \(\displaystyle G_{dd'} = \sqrt{\gamma_d \gamma_{d'}} \sum_{z \in \mathcal{H}_d} \sum_{w \in \mathcal{H}_{d'}} K(z, w) = \sqrt{\gamma_d \gamma_{d'}} \sum_n P_d(\varphi_n) \overline{P_{d'}(\varphi_n)} .\)

Now replace \(K\) by an automorphic kernel. Let \(\rho\) be the hyperbolic distance, \(\cosh \rho(z, w) = 1 + \lvert z - w \rvert^2/(2 \operatorname{Im} z \operatorname{Im} w)\), let \(k\) be a smooth compactly supported function on \([1, \infty)\), and let \(K_k(z, w) := \sum_{g \in \mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})} k(\cosh \rho(z, gw))\).

Tag 22AW

Problem 10.4.4. For forms \([a, b, c]\) and \([a', b', c']\) of discriminants \(-d\) and \(-d'\), \(\cosh \rho(z_{[a,b,c]}, z_{[a',b',c']}) = (2ac' + 2a'c - bb')/\sqrt{dd'}\).

Tag 22AX

Problem 10.4.5. For \(d, d' > 4\), \[\sum_{z \in \mathcal{H}_d} \sum_{w \in \mathcal{H}_{d'}} K_k(z, w) = \sum_{[a,b,c]} \sum_{[a',b',c']} k\Bigl( \frac{2ac' + 2a'c - bb'}{\sqrt{dd'}} \Bigr) ,\] where \([a, b, c]\) runs over the reduced forms of discriminant \(-d\), and \([a', b', c']\) over all primitive positive definite forms of discriminant \(-d'\).

The right side counts integral points. The left side has a spectral expansion. By Selberg’s pre-trace formula [Iw2, Thm. 7.4], \(K_k(z, w) = \sum_j h(t_j) u_j(z) \overline{u_j(w)}\) plus an integral over the Eisenstein series, where \(u_j\) runs over a basis of Maass forms orthonormal for the measure \(y^{-2}\,dx\,dy = (\pi/3)\,d\nu\), and \(h\) is the Selberg–Harish-Chandra transform of \(k\) for that measure. So, apart from the continuous spectrum, the left side of Problem 10.4.5 is \(\sum_j h(t_j) P_d(u_j) \overline{P_{d'}(u_j)}\). If \(h(t_j) > 0\) for the \(u_j\) spanning \(\mathcal{H}\), and \(\mathcal{H}\) is given the inner product for which the \(\sqrt{h(t_j)}\, u_j\) are orthonormal, then by Problem 10.4.3, whose proof uses only that \(K\) is the reproducing kernel of \(\mathcal{H}\), this is the Gram block \(G_{dd'}\), up to the factor \(\sqrt{\gamma_d \gamma_{d'}}\) and the contribution of the other \(u_j\). A bound of the form (10.1.1) thus becomes a count of pairs of forms with \(2ac' + 2a'c - bb'\) in a given range.

This is the simplest case of a relative trace formula. For a reductive group \(G\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\) with subgroups \(H_1\) and \(H_2\), one integrates the automorphic kernel \(\sum_{\gamma \in G(\mathbb{Q})} f(x^{-1}\gamma y)\) over \(x \in H_1(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash H_1(\mathbb{A})\) and \(y \in H_2(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash H_2(\mathbb{A})\). Spectrally one obtains a sum over automorphic forms \(\varphi\) of products of an \(H_1\)-period of \(\varphi\) and the conjugate of an \(H_2\)-period. Geometrically one obtains a sum over the double cosets \(H_1(\mathbb{Q}) \backslash G(\mathbb{Q}) / H_2(\mathbb{Q})\) of orbital integrals. For \(G = \mathrm{PGL}_2\) and \(H_1\), \(H_2\) tori, which contains Problem 10.4.5, Jacquet used this identity to prove Waldspurger’s formula [Ja]. In Alpöge’s argument [Alp], see also [Zh], the maps are Bessel periods on unitary groups [GGP], and the pairwise bounds do not come from equidistribution of CM points but from near orthogonality of Poincaré series of large weight; Chapter 11 treats it, and its last section describes the form it takes in the argument.
