Prerequisites.

Chapter 6, The slope method, whose notation we keep: normed spaces over a number field \(K\), their degrees and slopes, the heights \(h\) and \(h_\infty\) of a linear map, and Theorems 6.4.1 and 6.5.1. The spectral theorem and the Hilbert–Schmidt norm \(\lVert A \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \operatorname{tr}(A^*A)\). Northcott’s theorem: a projective space over \(K\) has only finitely many points of bounded height. The prime number theorem, also in arithmetic progressions, with its classical error term. Section 7.4 refers to Chapters 12, 14, 16 and 17, but proves nothing from them.

Theorem 6.5.1 bounds a sum over primes by \(\mu_{\max}\) of the target. Which part of the target can the image of one map reach, and how do many maps reach its mean slope?

§7.1. The Harder–Narasimhan filtration Tag 227G

Theorem 6.4.1 has \(\mu_{\max}(F)\) on the right, not \(\mu(F)\). This cannot be avoided.

Tag 227H

Problem 7.1.1. Let \(T > 0\). Let \(L = \mathbb{Z}\) with \(\lVert 1 \rVert = e^{-T}\), and let \(M = \mathbb{Z}e_1 \oplus \mathbb{Z}e_2\) with \(e_1 \perp e_2\), \(\lVert e_1 \rVert = e^{-T}\) and \(\lVert e_2 \rVert = e^T\). The map \(\varphi(1) = e_1\) is injective with \(h(\varphi) = 0\), while \(\mu(L) = T\) and \(\mu(M) = 0\).

The image of one map is a subspace, and only \(\mu_{\max}\) bounds the slope of a subspace. To see which part of the target the image can reach, one filters the target by slopes. A nonzero normed space \(V\) is semistable if \(\mu(U) \le \mu(V)\) for every nonzero subspace \(U\). Set \(\deg 0 := 0\).

Tag 227J

Problem 7.1.2. For subspaces \(U, U'\) of \(V\), \(\deg(U \cap U') + \deg(U + U') \ge \deg U + \deg U'\).

Tag 227K

Problem 7.1.3. The slopes of the nonzero subspaces of \(V\) have a largest value, and among the subspaces of that slope there is one that contains all the others.

Applying Problem 7.1.3 to the quotient by that subspace, and so on, one obtains a filtration.

Tag 227L

Theorem 7.1.1 (Stuhler, Grayson). A nonzero normed space \(V\) has a unique chain of subspaces \(0 = V_0 \subsetneq V_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq V_l = V\) whose successive quotients are semistable with \(\mu(V_1/V_0) > \dots > \mu(V_l/V_{l-1})\). Every nonzero subspace of \(V\) has slope at most \(\mu(V_1)\), and every nonzero quotient of \(V\) has slope at least \(\mu(V/V_{l-1})\).

See [St], [Gr], and [Ga] for global fields; for function fields this is the theorem of Harder and Narasimhan [HN]. The slopes of \(V\) are the numbers \(d_1 \ge \dots \ge d_r\) in which \(\mu(V_i/V_{i-1})\) occurs \(\dim(V_i/V_{i-1})\) times. Their sum is \(\deg V\). Write \(\mu_{\max}(V) := d_1\) and \(\mu_{\min}(V) := d_r\). For real \(\lambda\) let \(V^{\ge\lambda}\) be the largest \(V_i\) all of whose slopes are at least \(\lambda\). By uniqueness, the filtrations of \(V_i\) and of \(V/V_i\) are induced by that of \(V\). Hence, when the spaces in question are nonzero, \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:trunc}\tag{1} \dim V/V^{\ge\lambda} = \#\{j : d_j < \lambda\} , \qquad \mu_{\min}(V^{\ge\lambda}) \ge \lambda , \qquad \mu_{\max}(V/V^{\ge\lambda}) < \lambda . \end{equation}\]

Tag 227M

Problem 7.1.4. Let \(\tau\) lie in the usual fundamental domain, \(\lvert \tau \rvert \ge 1\) and \(\lvert \operatorname{Re}\tau \rvert \le \tfrac12\). Then \(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau \subset \mathbb{C}\) is semistable if and only if \(\operatorname{Im}\tau \le 1\). Otherwise \(V_1\) is the line spanned by \(1\).

Tag 227N

Problem 7.1.5. Let \(\dim V = D\), and let \(s\) and \(H \ge 0\) be real with \(\mu_{\min}(V) \ge s - H\). Then \[s - \mu(V) \le \frac1D \sum_j (s - d_j)_+ = \int_0^H \frac{\#\{j : d_j < s - t\}}{D} \, d t .\]

Tag 227P

Problem 7.1.6. Let \(\varphi \colon V \to F\) be a nonzero map of normed spaces. Then \(\mu_{\min}(V) \le \mu_{\max}(F) + h(\varphi)\). If \(U \subseteq V\) is a nonzero subspace with \(\mu_{\min}(U) \ge \lambda + h(\varphi)\), then \(\varphi(U) \subseteq F^{\ge\lambda}\).

So a map sends the parts of large slope of its source into the parts of large slope of its target.

§7.2. Several maps to the same target Tag 227Q

Theorem 6.5.1 involves \(\mu_{\max}(W^\vee)\): a map of small rank sees only the part of its target that contains its image. To reach the mean slope of a target \(F\), one uses a family of maps into \(F\). A line of large slope in \(F\) could contain the image of every member of the family, so the number of maps alone gives nothing. What is needed is a bound for the codimension of the span of the images that survives when the sources are cut down.

Let \(F\) be a normed space of dimension \(D > 0\). Let \(\varphi_i \colon V_i \to F\), for \(i\) in a finite nonempty set \(I\), be linear maps from nonzero normed spaces, and let \(h_i\) be real numbers with \(h(\varphi_i) \le h_i\) whenever \(\varphi_i \ne 0\). Let \(r_i := \dim V_i\) and \(R := \sum_i r_i\). The adjusted slopes of the family are the \(R\) numbers \(d - h_i\), where \(d\) runs over the slopes of \(V_i\). Let \(s\) be real, \(u > 0\) and \(H \ge 0\), with \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:suH}\tag{2} \sum_i r_i \bigl( \mu(V_i) - h_i \bigr) \ge Rs , \qquad \max_i \bigl( \mu_{\max}(V_i) - h_i \bigr) \le s + u , \qquad \mu_{\min}(F) \ge s - H . \end{equation}\] Thus the adjusted slopes have mean at least \(s\) and are at most \(s + u\), and no slope of \(F\) lies more than \(H\) below \(s\). The family satisfies the rank hypothesis with constants \(\theta, c \ge 0\) if, for all subspaces \(U_i \subseteq V_i\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:rank}\tag{3} \operatorname{codim}_F \sum_i \varphi_i(U_i) \le \Bigl( \theta + \frac{c}{R} \sum_i \operatorname{codim}_{V_i} U_i \Bigr) D . \end{equation}\] For \(t > 0\) let \(U_i(t) := V_i^{\ge s - t + h_i}\).

Tag 227R

Problem 7.2.1. The number \(k(t) := \sum_i \operatorname{codim}U_i(t)\) of adjusted slopes below \(s - t\) satisfies \(k(t) \le Ru/(u + t)\).

Tag 227S

Problem 7.2.2. \(\varphi_i(U_i(t)) \subseteq F^{\ge s - t}\) for every \(i\). Hence, under (3), the number of slopes of \(F\) below \(s - t\) is at most \(\bigl(\theta + cu/(u+t)\bigr) D\).

Inserting Problem 7.2.2 in Problem 7.1.5, and using \(\int_0^H u/(u+t) \, d t = u\log(1 + H/u)\), proves the following.

Tag 227T

Theorem 7.2.1. Assume (2) and (3). Then \[\mu(F) \ge s - \theta H - c\, u \log\Bigl( 1 + \frac{H}{u} \Bigr) .\]

Statements of this kind are part of Alpöge’s argument [Alp]. Theorem 7.2.1, with this proof, is [Zh, Prop. 4.3], which is stated with the constants \(\theta\) and \(c\) found at the end of Section 7.3.

Tag 227U

Problem 7.2.3. Let \(\varphi \colon V \to F\) be bijective with \(V\) semistable, and take \(h_1 = h(\varphi)\). Then (3) holds with \(\theta = 0\) and \(c = 1\), and Theorem 7.2.1 with \(u \to 0\) gives \(\mu(F) \ge \mu(V) - h(\varphi)\).

This is Theorem 6.4.1 for \(\varphi\), with \(U = F\). If \(V\) is not semistable, then \(u \ge \mu_{\max}(V) - \mu(V)\), and the theorem is weaker by \(u\log(1 + H/u)\): this is the price of using only the dimensions of the spans. The share \(u/(u+t)\) of discarded directions in Problem 7.2.1 varies with the level, and the integration uses this.

Tag 227V

Problem 7.2.4. Let \(\theta = 0\). Using in Problem 7.1.5 the trivial bound \(D\) for \(t < t_0\) and the bound of Problem 7.2.2 at \(t_0\) for \(t \ge t_0\), where \(0 < t_0 \le H\), gives the loss \(t_0 + cu(H - t_0)/(u + t_0)\), which is at least \(2\sqrt{cu(H + u)} - (1+c)u\).

So when the a priori bound \(H\) is much larger than \(u\), a single threshold costs about \(\sqrt{uH}\), whereas Theorem 7.2.1 costs \(u\log(H/u)\).

Tag 227W

Problem 7.2.5. Let \(D\) be even, let \(F = \bigoplus_{k=1}^D \mathbb{Z}v_k\) with orthogonal \(v_k\) of lengths \(e^{-s_k}\), where \(s_k = 0\) for half of the \(k\) and \(s_k = T > 0\) for the others. Let \(V_k = \mathbb{Z}\) with \(\lVert 1 \rVert = e^{-s_k}\), and \(\varphi_k(1) := v_k\). Then (3) holds with \(\theta = 0\) and \(c = 1\), and Theorem 7.2.1 with \(s = u = H = T/2\) gives \(\mu(F) \ge (1 - \log 2)\,T/2\).

Here \(\mu(F) = T/2\). The surjection \(\bigoplus_k V_k \to F\), through Theorem 7.1.1, gives only \(\mu(F) \ge \min_k s_k = 0\).

§7.3. The rank hypothesis from Gram matrices Tag 227X

How can one verify (3) when \(R\) is much larger than \(D\)? By a second moment at one complex place. Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be a complex inner product space of dimension \(D' > 0\). Let \(B_i \colon X_i \to \mathcal{H}\), \(i \in I\), be linear maps from inner product spaces of dimensions \(r_i\), with \(R := \sum_i r_i > 0\), and let \(\gamma_i > 0\). Let \[K_i := \gamma_i B_i^* B_i , \qquad Q := \sum_i \gamma_i B_i B_i^* ,\] and suppose that \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:gram}\tag{4} \sum_i \lVert K_i - 1 \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 \le eR , \qquad \operatorname{tr}(Q^2) \le (1 + \eta) \frac{(\operatorname{tr}Q)^2}{D'} , \end{equation}\] with \(0 \le e \le \tfrac14\) and \(\eta \ge 0\). The first condition says that each weighted map nearly preserves lengths.

Tag 227Y

Problem 7.3.1. If \(Q \ge 0\) and \(Q \ne 0\), then \(\operatorname{rank} Q \ge (\operatorname{tr}Q)^2/\operatorname{tr}Q^2\), with equality exactly when \(Q\) is a multiple of a projection. Moreover \(\lVert Q - \operatorname{tr}Q/D' \rVert_\mathrm{HS}^2 = \operatorname{tr}Q^2 - (\operatorname{tr}Q)^2/D'\).

So the second condition in (4) says that \(Q\) is nearly scalar. Let \(v_1, \dots, v_R\) be the images under the maps \(\sqrt{\gamma_i} B_i\) of orthonormal bases of the \(X_i\). Then \(Q = \sum_k v_k v_k^*\) and \(\operatorname{tr}Q^2 = \sum_{k, l} \lvert\langle v_k, v_l \rangle\rvert^2\), so \(Q\) is scalar exactly when the \(v_k\) form a tight frame of \(\mathcal{H}\). Benedetto and Fickus characterize the tight frames of \(R \ge D'\) unit vectors as the minimizers of \(\sum_{k,l} \lvert\langle v_k, v_l \rangle\rvert^2\) among \(R\)-tuples of unit vectors [BF], and Zhang compares (4) with tight frames [Zh]. When the \(B_i^*\) are evaluations at points \(x_i\), \(\langle Q f, f \rangle = \sum_i \gamma_i \lvert f(x_i) \rvert^2\), and \(Q\) is scalar exactly when the weighted points form a cubature formula that is exact on the functions \(\lvert f \rvert^2\), \(f \in \mathcal{H}\), as a spherical design is exact on polynomials of bounded degree [DGS].

Tag 227Z

Problem 7.3.2. Let \(\mathcal{H}\) be the space of polynomials in \(\mathbb{C}[z]\) of degree less than \(D'\), with \(\lVert f \rVert^2 := \int_0^1 \lvert f(e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\theta}) \rvert^2 \, d\theta\). Let \(N \ge D'\) and \(\zeta := e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/N}\). For \(0 \le j < N\) let \(X_j = \mathbb{C}\), let \(B_j(1)\) be the polynomial with \(\langle f, B_j(1) \rangle = f(\zeta^j)\) for all \(f\), and \(\gamma_j := 1/D'\). Then (4) holds with \(e = \eta = 0\), and \(Q = N/D'\).

Now let \(Y_i \subseteq X_i\) be subspaces with \(\sum_i \operatorname{codim}Y_i \le \alpha R\), where \(0 \le \alpha \le 1\). Let \(Z\) be the orthogonal complement of \(\sum_i B_i(Y_i)\), let \(w := \dim Z\), let \(\Pi\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(Z\), and let \(\tau := \operatorname{tr}Q\).

Tag 2282

Problem 7.3.3. \(\lvert \tau - R \rvert \le R\sqrt e\), so that \(\tau \ge R/2\).

Tag 2283

Problem 7.3.4. \(\operatorname{tr}(\Pi Q) \le R\bigl(\alpha + \sqrt{\alpha e}\bigr)\).

Tag 2284

Problem 7.3.5. \(\operatorname{tr}(\Pi Q) \ge \tau w/D' - \tau\sqrt{\eta w/D'}\).

Combining Problems 7.3.3–7.3.5, and using \(\sqrt{\eta D' w} \le (w + \eta D')/2\), gives the following.

Tag 2285

Theorem 7.3.1. Under (4), \(\operatorname{codim}_\mathcal{H}\sum_i B_i(Y_i) \le \bigl(\eta + 4\alpha + 4\sqrt e\bigr) D'\).

This is [Zh, Lemma 4.2]. To apply it to maps \(\varphi_i \colon V_i \to F\) of normed spaces, fix an embedding \(\sigma \colon K \to \mathbb{C}\), and suppose that there are a surjection \(\pi \colon \mathcal{H}\to F \otimes_\sigma \mathbb{C}\) and isomorphisms \(\iota_i \colon X_i \to V_i \otimes_\sigma \mathbb{C}\) with \(\pi B_i = (\varphi_i \otimes 1)\iota_i\). If \(D \ge (1 - \delta) D'\) with \(0 \le \delta < 1\), then the rank hypothesis (3) holds with \(\theta = (\eta + 4\sqrt e)/(1 - \delta)\) and \(c = 4/(1-\delta)\), since a surjection does not increase codimension. The inner products on \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(X_i\) need not be related to the norms that define the slopes: only dimensions are transferred. But the positivity of \(Q\) is essential, and so is the complex place.

§7.4. The inputs Tag 2286

The slope method is due to Bost [Bo1], [Bo2]; see [CL] for an account that compares it with the classical ingredients of a transcendence proof, and [CDT] for a recent use with lattices of polynomials. Alpöge’s argument for the Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions [Alp], see also [Zh] and the reconstruction [Adr], can be organized around Theorems 6.5.1, 7.2.1 and 7.3.1; in the reconstruction the inputs are the seven inequalities of [Adr, Prop. 3.1, (3.1)–(3.7)]. The sources are spaces \(V_\alpha\) of algebraic automorphic forms on unitary groups of hermitian spaces over a CM field \(E\) containing an imaginary quadratic field \(K_0\), defined over the totally real subfield \(E^+\) of \(E\) [Zh], the target \(F\) is the dual of a space of test forms, and the maps come from periods computed through a chain of theta lifts. The status in [Adr] of each input below is recorded in Sections 58.5 and 58.7 of Chapter 58.

The finite places. At a prime \(p\) inert in \(K_0\), a jet of a theta function of order \(a\), and of order \(w\) at the conjugate place, is divisible by about \(p\) to the power \(\sum_{e\ \mathrm{odd}} (a/p^e - w)_+\); the simplest cases are Theorems 16.4.1 and 17.3.1 of Chapters 16 and 17, and Section 14.3 of Chapter 14. The gains are recorded as in Problems 6.5.2, 6.5.3 and 6.3.7. Let \(\chi\) be the quadratic character of \(K_0\).

Tag 2287

Problem 7.4.1. Let \(a \ge w > 0\) and \(x := a/w\). Then \[\begin{multline*} 2 \sum_{\chi(p) = -1} \log p \sum_{e\ \mathrm{odd}} \Bigl( \frac{a}{p^e} - w \Bigr)_+ + \sum_{\chi(p) = 0} \log p \sum_{e \ge 1} \Bigl( \frac{a}{p^e} - w \Bigr)_+ \\ = w \sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n) \bigl(1 - \chi(n)\bigr) \Bigl( \frac{x}{n} - 1 \Bigr) . \end{multline*}\]

So the exponents are ramps in \(p^e\), as asked for in Section 6.1, and only the primes inert or ramified in \(K_0\) occur.

Tag 2288

Problem 7.4.2. \(\sum_{n \le x} \Lambda(n) \bigl(1 - \chi(n)\bigr) (x/n - 1) = x \bigl( \log x - 1 - \gamma + \tfrac{L'}{L}(1, \chi) \bigr) + o(x)\), where \(\gamma\) is Euler’s constant.

The error term in Problem 7.4.2 carries the information on the zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) and \(L(s, \chi)\), which enter with opposite signs.

The infinite places. The bound for \(h_\infty(\varphi_\alpha)\) is a Gaussian norm as in Problem 6.4.4, together with the \(a\)th power of a period, which brings in \(\gamma\) and \(\tfrac{L'}{L}(1, \chi)\) by the Chowla–Selberg formula (Chapter 12, Theorem 12.3.1, for \(K_0 = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\); Chapter 20, Problem 20.4.6, in general).

The slopes of the sources. By (2), Theorem 7.2.1 needs two bounds on the sources: a lower bound \(s\) for the mean of the adjusted slopes, and an upper bound \(s + u\) for their maximum, which enters the loss through \(u\). The slope of a source is that of a base lattice raised by the gains at the finite places, so the gains enter the lower bound; for the base lattice see [Adr and §15.7].

The target. Theorem 7.2.1 bounds \(\mu(F)\) from below, so the argument also needs an upper bound for \(\mu(F)\), that is, for the covolume of the lattice of test forms whose dual is \(F\). The analogue for sections of a line bundle is the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem [GS2, Thm. 8], whose simplest case is Problem 6.3.5; here the bound is needed with explicit errors while the groups and the field grow [Adr]. Theorem 7.2.1 also uses the a priori bound \(\mu_{\min}(F) \ge s - H\), and by Problem 7.2.4 the logarithm in Theorem 7.2.1 makes a large \(H\) cheap. In the reconstruction these inputs enter through [Adr], which comes from its frame theorem, and the height inequality [Adr], which bounds the slope of the target together with the heights of the period maps.

The rank hypothesis. This is Theorem 7.3.1 for the family of periods, whose simplest case is Problem 7.3.2. In the argument it comes from near orthogonality of Poincaré series of large weight, not from equidistribution of CM points; see Chapter 11, Section 11.4.

The main term in Problem 7.4.2 has size \(x\log x\), and to reach the exponent \(\tfrac12\) every error term must be \(O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) for each \(\varepsilon > 0\) [Zh and Remark 6.2].
