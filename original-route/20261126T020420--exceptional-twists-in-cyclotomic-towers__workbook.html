Prerequisites.

Chapter 30, Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists: the modules \(W_e=\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell(-e)\), the groups \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(F,e)\) and their lengths \(h_{\ell,e}(F)\), Problems 30.1.1 and 30.2.1, and Theorem 30.1.2. Iwasawa’s theory of \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-extensions and \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_\ell[[T]]\)-modules [Wa, Ch. 13]; continuous cohomology and the duality theorems of Poitou and Tate [NSW, Ch. VII–VIII]. In this chapter \(\ell\) is any prime, \(2\) included. For a number field \(F\), \(G_{F,\ell}\) is the Galois group of the largest extension of \(F\) unramified outside \(\ell\) and \(\infty\), and \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(F,e)\) is the group of classes in \(H^1(G_{F,\ell},W_e)\) that vanish on the decomposition groups at all places above \(\ell\). We write \(\mu'_\ell=\mu_\ell\) if \(\ell\) is odd and \(\mu'_2=\mu_4\).

§54.1. Layers

In Alpöge’s argument the twist \(e\) runs up to a power of the scale \(x\), and the field changes with \(x\). Along which fields do the lengths \(h_{\ell,e}\) stay small uniformly in \(e\), and how does this fail? Iwasawa’s answer: along a cyclotomic tower they grow linearly with the layer, except at twists \(e\) that are \(\ell\)-adically close to finitely many points.

Let \(L_0\) be a number field inside a fixed algebraic closure of \(\mathbb{Q}\). A layer of degree \(\ell^m\) over \(L_0\) is a field \(L_0(\zeta_{\ell^k})\), \(k\ge0\), with \([L_0(\zeta_{\ell^k}):L_0]=\ell^m\).

Problem 54.1.1. Suppose \(\mu'_\ell\subset L_0\). Then \(L_\infty:=L_0(\mu_{\ell^\infty})\) is a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-extension of \(L_0\), and for every \(m\ge0\) there is exactly one layer \(L_m\) of degree \(\ell^m\) over \(L_0\): the fixed field of \(\operatorname{Gal}(L_\infty/L_0)^{\ell^m}\).

Problem 54.1.2. Over \(L_0=\mathbb{Q}\) and \(\ell=2\) the layers are the fields \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^k})\). The layer of degree \(2\) is \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\), which does not lie in the cyclotomic \(\mathbb{Z}_2\)-extension of \(\mathbb{Q}\), the union of the real fields \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{2^k}+\zeta_{2^k}^{-1})\).

This is why \(\mu_4\) is required when \(\ell=2\). The discriminants of the layers are controlled by the conductor.

Problem 54.1.3. If a number field \(L\) contains a primitive \(\ell^k\)-th root of unity, then \(\ell^k\le2[L:\mathbb{Q}]\).

Problem 54.1.4. If \(L\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_N)\), then \(\lvert\Delta_L\rvert\le N^{[L:\mathbb{Q}]}\).

Problem 54.1.5. Let \(L_0\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N_0P})\) with \([L_0:\mathbb{Q}]\le n_1\), and let \(L\) be a layer of degree \(\ell^j\) over \(L_0\). Then \([L:\mathbb{Q}]\le n_1\ell^j\) and \(\log(2\lvert\Delta_L\rvert)\le C(1+\log P)\), with \(C\) depending only on \(\ell\), \(j\), \(n_1\) and \(N_0\).

In the application \(P=qd\) is the product of the two auxiliary primes, and \(\log P\) is at most a small power of the scale.

§54.2. Where the lengths grow

Let \(\ell\) be odd, let \(\kappa\in1+\ell\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) with \(v_\ell(\kappa-1)=1\), and for \(y\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) put \(\kappa^y=\exp(y\log\kappa)\). In a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-extension a generator \(\gamma\) of the Galois group acts on \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell(-e)\) by such a power of \(\kappa\), and Iwasawa’s theory compares the groups at the \(m\)-th layer with the coinvariants of \(\gamma^{\ell^m}\) on a module attached to the whole tower [Wa, Ch. 13]. Here is a model.

Problem 54.2.1. For \(y\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell\), \(v_\ell(\kappa^y-1)=1+v_\ell(y)\).

Problem 54.2.2. Let \(s\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell\), \(e\in\mathbb{Z}\), \(m\ge0\), and let \(\gamma\) act on \(X=\mathbb{Z}_\ell\) by \(\kappa^{s-e}\). Then \(\operatorname{len}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}X/(\gamma^{\ell^m}-1)X=m+1+v_\ell(s-e)\), infinite if \(s=e\).

Problem 54.2.3. Let \(\gamma\) act on \(X=\mathbb{Z}_\ell^r\) by an upper triangular matrix with diagonal entries \(\kappa^{s_1-e},\dots,\kappa^{s_r-e}\). If \(v_\ell(s_i-e)\le B\) for all \(i\), then \(\operatorname{len}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}X/(\gamma^{\ell^m}-1)X\le r(m+1+B)\).

So the length is linear in the layer \(m\) and in \(B\), and blows up only for twists \(e\) near one of the centres \(s_i\). Near how many twists?

Problem 54.2.4. Let \(s\) lie in a field containing \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\) with an absolute value \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_\ell\) extending that of \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\), let \(Z\ge1\), and \(B=\lfloor\log_\ell Z\rfloor\). At most one integer \(1\le e\le Z\) satisfies \(\lvert s-e\rvert_\ell<\ell^{-B}\).

Problem 54.2.5. For \(\ell=5\), \(s=-1/3\in\mathbb{Z}_5\) and \(Z=10^4\) one has \(B=5\); the only \(e\le Z\) with \(v_5(s-e)>5\) is \(e=5208\), with \(v_5(s-e)=6\), while \(v_5(s-2083)=5\).

Exceptional twists are not an artefact of the model. By Theorem 30.1.2, for odd \(e\) with \((\ell-1)\nmid(e+1)\), \[h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q})=v_\ell\bigl(\operatorname{num}(B_{e+1}/(e+1))\bigr).\]

Problem 54.2.6. Let \(1\le e<49284\) be odd with \(e\equiv31\pmod{36}\). Then \(h_{37,e}(\mathbb{Q})=1\) unless \(e\equiv283\pmod{1332}\), and then \(h_{37,e}(\mathbb{Q})\ge2\). The only such \(e\) with \(h_{37,e}(\mathbb{Q})\ge3\) is \(e=37579\), where \(h_{37,e}(\mathbb{Q})=3\).

Problem 54.2.7. For every \(e\ge1\), \(h_{37,e}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{37}))\ge h_{37,e}(\mathbb{Q})\). Hence over \(L_0=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{37})\), which contains \(\mu_{37}\), the lengths at the bottom layer are at least \(3\) at \(e=37579\).

§54.3. Ferrero–Washington and the tower bound

What makes the lengths along a tower linear rather than exponential in \(m\) is the vanishing of Iwasawa’s \(\mu\)-invariant. Let \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_\ell[[T]]\) and \(\omega_m=(1+T)^{\ell^m}-1\).

Problem 54.3.1. Let \(X\) be a finitely generated \(\Lambda\)-module with \(\dim_{\mathbb{F}_\ell}X/(\ell,\omega_m)X\le r\) for all \(m\). Then \(X\) is generated by \(r\) elements as a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-module.

For the class groups of the layers, Iwasawa’s theory turns a bound for their \(\ell\)-ranks into such a bound for the inverse limit of their \(\ell\)-parts [Wa, Ch. 13]. The bound for the ranks is the theorem of Ferrero and Washington, in the following form.

Theorem 54.3.1 (Ferrero–Washington). Let \(K\) be an abelian number field with \(\mu'_\ell\subset K\). There is \(r\) such that \(\lvert\operatorname{Cl}(K')/\operatorname{Cl}(K')^\ell\rvert\le\ell^r\) for every layer \(K'\) over \(K\).

Status: Ferrero and Washington proved \(\mu=0\) for abelian fields [FW]; Sinnott gave another proof [Si]; the bounded-rank form follows by Iwasawa’s theory [Wa, Ch. 13]. The Lean formalization [Lean] proves Theorem 54.3.1 itself. The names of its steps (Stickelberger annihilation, an \(\ell\)-adic independence lemma of Sinnott, Kummer reflection) indicate Sinnott’s route; we have not read that proof.

The formalization deduces from this a bound along the tower. Put \(j_0=\lfloor\log_\ell R\rfloor+3\).

Lemma 54.3.2 (tower bound). Let \(L_0\) be totally imaginary with \(\mu'_\ell\subset L_0\), with layers \(L_m\), and suppose that a certain Iwasawa module of \(L_\infty\) is generated by \(R\ge1\) elements over \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\).

  • There are \(c_0\ge0\) with \(\mu_{\ell^{c_0}}\subset L_0\), \(r\le R\), and \(s_1,\dots,s_r\) in a field containing \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\), such that for all \(e\ge1\), \(m\ge0\) and integers \(B\ge0\) with \(\lvert s_i-e\rvert_\ell\ge\ell^{-B}\) for every \(i\), \(h_{\ell,e}(L_m)\le r(\max(m,j_0)+c_0+B)\).

  • For all \(e\ge1\) and \(m\ge0\), \(h_{\ell,e}(L_m)\le\operatorname{len}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}H^2(G_{L_{j_0},\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))+R\max(m-j_0,0)\).

Status: proved in the Lean formalization, from the generator bound, Poitou–Tate duality and local Tate duality. For abelian \(L_0\) the generator bound is deduced there from Theorem 54.3.1. We have not identified the Iwasawa module, and we have not read how the centres \(s_i\) arise; the formalization does not say that they are the zeros of a characteristic power series, as Problem 54.2.3 suggests. Part (a) is stated in this form in [Zh].

Part (b) compares \(\mathrm{Sha}\) with \(H^2\). Let \(L\) be totally imaginary, or \(\ell\) odd, and \(e\ge1\). By Poitou–Tate duality and local duality [NSW, Ch. VII, §2; Ch. VIII, §6], when all lengths are finite, \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:pt}\tag{1} \operatorname{len}H^2(G_{L,\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))+\operatorname{len}H^0(G_{L,\ell},W_e)=h_{\ell,e}(L)+\sum_{v\mid\ell}\operatorname{len}H^0(G_v,W_e). \end{equation}\]

For \(e\ge1\) the group \(H^2(G_{L,\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\) is finite [So79], and \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(L,e)\) is dual to a subgroup of it, so all lengths in (1) are finite.

Problem 54.3.2. Let \(\ell\) be odd, \(\mu_\ell\subset L\), and \(\ell^c=\lvert\mu_{\ell^\infty}(L)\rvert\). For \(e\ne0\), \(\operatorname{len}H^0(G_{L,\ell},W_e)=c+v_\ell(e)\).

Problem 54.3.3. In Problem 54.3.2, for \(e\ge1\), \(h_{\ell,e}(L)\le\operatorname{len}H^2(G_{L,\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))+c+v_\ell(e)\).

§54.4. Second cohomology and the exceptional twists

At the exceptional twists only a bound for the whole group \(H^2\) is available.

Theorem 54.4.1 (bound for \(H^2\)). There is an absolute constant \(C_H\) such that for every prime \(\ell\), every totally imaginary number field \(L\) with \(\mu'_\ell\subset L\), and every \(e\ge1\), the group \(H^2(G_{L,\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\) is finite and \[\log\bigl\lvert H^2(G_{L,\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\bigr\rvert\le\exp\bigl((2e[L:\mathbb{Q}])^{C_H}\log(2\lvert\Delta_L\rvert)\bigr).\]

Status: finiteness is due to Soulé [So79]. Soulé also bounds these groups through the torsion in the homology of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_L)\) [So03], but only for \(\ell\) large compared with \(e\) and \([L:\mathbb{Q}]\); we know no published estimate covering every \(\ell\), as noted in [Rub]. The Lean formalization proves Theorem 54.4.1, by way of a rank bound for the homology of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_L)\) of Borel’s type; we have not read that proof. The bound is stated in [Zh].

The bound is doubly exponential in \(e\). It is useful only for small twists.

Problem 54.4.1. Let \(\ell\) and \(L\) be as in Theorem 54.4.1, \(x\ge1\), \(\varepsilon>0\), \([L:\mathbb{Q}]\le n_0\), \(\log(2\lvert\Delta_L\rvert)\le c_1x^{\varepsilon/4}\), and \(1\le e\le x^{\varepsilon/(2C_H)}\). Then \(\operatorname{len}H^2(G_{L,\ell},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(e+1))\le2\exp(Cx^{3\varepsilon/4})\) with \(C=(2n_0)^{C_H}c_1\).

Problem 54.4.2. Let \(C_H\ge1\), \(b=\exp(x^\varepsilon)\) with \(0<\varepsilon<1\), and \(e\ge x\). Then the right side of Theorem 54.4.1 exceeds \(\sqrt b\) for every \(x\ge1\), whatever \(L\).

Compare Problem 30.3.2: the target in the argument is a bound by \(\sqrt b\) for a sum of \(\log\ell\) times lengths. Problem 54.2.4, Lemma 54.3.2 and Theorem 54.4.1 give the following.

Theorem 54.4.2 (twists along a tower). Let \(L_0\) and \(R\) be as in Lemma 54.3.2, and \(n=[L_{j_0}:\mathbb{Q}]\). For every \(Z\ge1\) there is a set \(\mathcal E\) of at most \(R\) integers in \([1,Z]\) such that for every \(m\ge0\) and \(1\le e\le Z\), \[h_{\ell,e}(L_m)\le \begin{cases} R(\max(m,j_0)+c_0+\log_\ell Z), & e\notin\mathcal E,\\ Rm+\exp\bigl((2en)^{C_H}\log(2\lvert\Delta_{L_{j_0}}\rvert)\bigr)/\log\ell, & e\in\mathcal E. \end{cases}\]

The set \(\mathcal E\) depends on \(Z\) but not on \(m\). The first bound is logarithmic in \(Z\); the second is affordable only when \(e\) is a small power of the scale, by Problems 54.4.1 and 54.4.2. The exceptional twists cannot be moved, so the scale must be moved: this is the origin of the windows.
