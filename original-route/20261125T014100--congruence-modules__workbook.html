Prerequisites.

Modules over a Dedekind domain and the elementary divisor theorem. Ideals in quadratic fields. Modular forms of level \(1\): the structure of \(M_k\) and \(S_k\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §3.2, Thm. 4], Eisenstein series and \(\Delta\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §§4.2–4.3], Hecke operators and their action on \(q\)-expansions, and normalized eigenforms [SeA, Ch. VII, §§5.3–5.5]; Serre writes the weight as \(2k\) and numbers the Bernoulli numbers differently. The Petersson product, for which the Hecke operators are self-adjoint [DS]. From Chapter 29, Denominators and the Stickelberger element: the cost of a denominator (Problem 29.1.5) and Theorem 29.2.2.

In Chapter 29 a linear map that does not respect integral structures cost \(\log [M + T(L) : M]\). Which map does the argument have to divide by? The period pairing sees a lifted form only through its components along Hecke eigenspaces, so the map is the projection of a lattice of integral forms onto a sum of eigenspaces [Adr]. What does such a projection cost, and what controls the cost? The answer is a congruence module. We compute it for level \(1\), in weight \(12\), where it is \(\mathbb{Z}/691\mathbb{Z}\), and in weight \(24\), where it detects a congruence modulo the prime \(144169\) between the two newforms.

§31.1. Congruence modules Tag 22TW

Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be a Dedekind domain with fraction field \(K\). A lattice in a finite-dimensional \(K\)-vector space \(V\) is a finitely generated \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule that spans \(V\). Let \(V = V_1 \oplus V_2\), let \(\pi_1, \pi_2\) be the two projections, and let \(\Lambda \subset V\) be a lattice. The congruence module of \(V_1\) in \(\Lambda\) is \[\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) := \pi_1(\Lambda) / (\Lambda \cap V_1) .\]

Tag 22TX

Problem 31.1.1. \(\pi_1(\Lambda) = V_1 \cap (\Lambda + V_2)\), and \[\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) \cong \Lambda / \bigl((\Lambda \cap V_1) \oplus (\Lambda \cap V_2)\bigr) \cong \mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_2) .\] In particular \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1)\) is a finitely generated torsion \(\mathcal{O}\)-module, and it vanishes exactly when \(\Lambda = (\Lambda \cap V_1) \oplus (\Lambda \cap V_2)\).

Tag 22TY

Problem 31.1.2. \((\Lambda + \pi_1(\Lambda))/\Lambda \cong \mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1)\). For \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}\), the least positive integer \(c\) with \(c\,\pi_1(\Lambda) \subseteq \Lambda\) is the exponent of \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1)\).

So for \(L = M = \Lambda\) and \(T = \pi_1\) the index \([M + T(L) : M]\) of Problem 29.1.5 is \(\lvert \mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) \rvert\): the projection costs \(\log \lvert \mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) \rvert\).

Tag 22TZ

Problem 31.1.3. Let \(N \ge 1\), \(V = \mathbb{Q}^2 = \mathbb{Q}e_1 \oplus \mathbb{Q}e_2\), and \(\Lambda = \{(a,b) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : a \equiv b \pmod N\}\). Then \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; \mathbb{Q}e_1) \cong \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\), whereas \(\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{Z}^2; \mathbb{Q}e_1) = 0\).

The lattices of interest carry an action of a Hecke algebra. Let \(X\) be a finite set, and let \(\mathbf{T}\subseteq \prod_{x \in X} \mathcal{O}\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-subalgebra, finitely generated as an \(\mathcal{O}\)-module, which separates the points of \(X\). Write \(t(x)\) for the coordinates of \(t \in \mathbf{T}\). Let \(\mathbf{T}\) act on \(V = \bigoplus_{x \in X} V_x\) by \(t \cdot v = (t(x) v_x)_x\), and let \(\Lambda\) be a \(\mathbf{T}\)-stable lattice. The congruence ideal of \(x\) is \[\mathfrak{c}_x := \{ t(x) : t \in \mathbf{T},\ t(y) = 0 \text{ for all } y \ne x \} \subseteq \mathcal{O},\] and we write \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_x)\) for the congruence module with respect to \(V = V_x \oplus \bigoplus_{y \ne x} V_y\).

Tag 22U2

Problem 31.1.4. \(\mathfrak{c}_x\) annihilates \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_x)\). If \(\mathcal{O}\) is a discrete valuation ring with valuation \(v\), then \(\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_x) \le \dim V_x \cdot \min v(\mathfrak{c}_x)\).

Tag 22U3

Problem 31.1.5. If \(\Lambda \cong \mathbf{T}\) as a \(\mathbf{T}\)-module, then \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_x) \cong \mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{c}_x\). For \(\mathbf{T}= \{(a,b) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : a \equiv b \pmod N\}\) acting on itself, \(\mathfrak{c}_x = N\mathbb{Z}\) at both points.

Thus the denominator of the projection onto an eigenspace is the congruence module; it is killed by the congruence ideal, and equals \(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{c}_x\) when the lattice is free of rank one over \(\mathbf{T}\).

A congruence module can also be read off from a pairing. Suppose that \(\Lambda\) carries a perfect pairing \(\langle\,,\rangle \colon \Lambda \times \Lambda \to \mathcal{O}\), that is, an \(\mathcal{O}\)-bilinear form for which \(\lambda \mapsto \langle \lambda, \cdot \rangle\) is an isomorphism \(\Lambda \to \operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(\Lambda, \mathcal{O})\), and that \(\langle V_1, V_2 \rangle = \langle V_2, V_1 \rangle = 0\). For a lattice \(L \subset V_1\) let \(L^\vee := \{ v \in V_1 : \langle v, L \rangle \subseteq \mathcal{O}\}\).

Tag 22U4

Problem 31.1.6. \(\pi_1(\Lambda) = (\Lambda \cap V_1)^\vee\). Hence \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) \cong (\Lambda \cap V_1)^\vee / (\Lambda \cap V_1)\).

Tag 22U5

Problem 31.1.7. In the setting of Problem 31.1.6: (i) if \(\Lambda \cap V_1 = \mathcal{O}f\), then \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) \cong \mathcal{O}/\langle f, f \rangle\mathcal{O}\); (ii) if \(\Lambda \cap V_1 = \mathcal{O}x \oplus \mathcal{O}y\) with \(\langle x, x \rangle = \langle y, y \rangle = 0\), then \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; V_1) \cong (\mathcal{O}/\langle x, y \rangle\mathcal{O}) \oplus (\mathcal{O}/\langle y, x \rangle\mathcal{O})\). For \(\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}^2\) with the dot product and \(V_1 = \mathbb{Q}(1,1)\), \(V_2 = \mathbb{Q}(1,-1)\), the congruence module is \(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\).

This is the mechanism behind Hida’s identity in Section 31.4: a congruence module of an eigenform is the discriminant of the eigenform’s piece of a self-dual lattice. Compare [DDT, Lemma 4.17], where a power of the congruence ideal of the Hecke algebra is shown to lie in the ideal of this discriminant, with equality when the lattice is free over the Hecke algebra.

§31.2. The Eisenstein line in weight \(12\) Tag 22U6

For even \(k \ge 4\) let \(M_k\) and \(S_k\) be the spaces of modular forms and cusp forms of weight \(k\) for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), identified with their \(q\)-expansions, and let \(M_k(\mathbb{Z}) := M_k \cap \mathbb{Z}[[q]]\) and \(S_k(\mathbb{Z}) := S_k \cap \mathbb{Z}[[q]]\). Let \(\sigma_{k-1}(n) := \sum_{d \mid n} d^{k-1}\) and \[E_k := 1 - \frac{2k}{B_k} \sum_{n \ge 1} \sigma_{k-1}(n) q^n , \qquad \Delta := q \prod_{n \ge 1} (1 - q^n)^{24} = \sum_{n \ge 1} \tau(n) q^n ,\] so that \(E_4 = 1 + 240 \sum \sigma_3(n) q^n\), \(E_6 = 1 - 504 \sum \sigma_5(n) q^n\), and \(B_{12} = -691/2730\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §4]. We use the decomposition \(M_k \otimes \mathbb{Q}= \mathbb{Q}E_k \oplus S_k(\mathbb{Q})\), in which both summands are stable under the Hecke operators.

Tag 22U7

Problem 31.2.1. \(M_{12}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}E_4^3 \oplus \mathbb{Z}\Delta\).

Tag 22U8

Problem 31.2.2. \(E_4^3 = E_{12} + \frac{432000}{691}\Delta\). Hence \(\pi_\Delta(M_{12}(\mathbb{Z})) = \frac{1}{691}\mathbb{Z}\Delta\), and \(\mathrm{C}(M_{12}(\mathbb{Z}); \mathbb{Q}\Delta) \cong \mathbb{Z}/691\mathbb{Z}\).

The same computation for the Eisenstein line works in every weight.

Tag 22U9

Problem 31.2.3. Let \(k \ge 4\) be even, and let \(N_k\) be the numerator of \(B_k/2k\) in lowest terms. Then \(\mathrm{C}(M_k(\mathbb{Z}); \mathbb{Q}E_k) \cong \mathbb{Z}/N_k\mathbb{Z}\). For \(k = 4, 6, 8, 10, 14\) one has \(N_k = \pm1\), and \(\lvert N_{16} \rvert = 3617\), \(\lvert N_{22} \rvert = 77683 = 131 \cdot 593\), \(\lvert N_{24} \rvert = 236364091 = 103 \cdot 2294797\).

Let \(\mathbf{T}_{12}\) be the \(\mathbb{Z}\)-algebra of endomorphisms of \(M_{12} \otimes \mathbb{Q}\) generated by the Hecke operators \(T_n\), \(n \ge 1\), normalized so that \(T_n \Delta = \tau(n)\Delta\) and \(T_n E_{12} = \sigma_{11}(n) E_{12}\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §5]. Map \(\mathbf{T}_{12}\) to \(\mathbb{Z}\times \mathbb{Z}\) by its eigenvalues on \((\Delta, E_{12})\).

Tag 22UA

Problem 31.2.4. Each \(T_n\) preserves \(M_{12}(\mathbb{Z})\), and \(\mathbf{T}_{12} = \{(a,b) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : a \equiv b \pmod{691}\}\). Thus the congruence ideal of \(\Delta\) is \(691\mathbb{Z}\). (Here \(\tau(2) = -24\) and \(\tau(3) = 252\).)

So the bound of Problem 31.1.4 is attained, although the lattice is not free.

Tag 22UB

Problem 31.2.5. \(M_{12}(\mathbb{Z})\) is not free of rank one over \(\mathbf{T}_{12}\).

The prime \(691\) is also the one in Theorem 29.2.2: since \(691 \mid B_{12}\), the eigenspace \(A^{(679)}\) of the \(691\)-part of the class group of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{691})\) is nonzero. That the same prime measures both is not an accident; Chapter 32 builds the class from the congruence.

§31.3. Two newforms of weight \(24\) Tag 22UC

The space \(S_{24}\) has dimension \(2\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §3.2], and its two normalized eigenforms are not rational. Let \(D := 144169\), a prime with \(D \equiv 1 \pmod{24}\), let \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt D)\), \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathcal{O}_K\), and \(\mathfrak{p}:= \sqrt D\,\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22UD

Problem 31.3.1. \(S_{24}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}g_1 \oplus \mathbb{Z}g_2\), where \(g_1 = \Delta E_4^3 - 696\Delta^2 = q + 195660 q^3 + \cdots\) and \(g_2 = \Delta^2 = q^2 - 48q^3 + \cdots\).

On \(S_{24}\) one has \(a_n(T_2 f) = a_{2n}(f) + 2^{23} a_{n/2}(f)\), with \(a_{n/2} := 0\) for odd \(n\); also \(a_4(g_1) = 12080128\) and \(a_4(g_2) = 1080\).

Tag 22UE

Problem 31.3.2. \(T_2 g_1 = 20468736\, g_2\) and \(T_2 g_2 = g_1 + 1080\, g_2\). The characteristic polynomial of \(T_2\) on \(S_{24}\) is \(x^2 - 1080x - 20468736\), with discriminant \(24^2 D\) and roots \(\lambda_\pm = 540 \pm 12\sqrt D\). The normalized eigenforms are \(f_\pm = g_1 + \lambda_\pm g_2\).

Let \(\mathbf{T}_{24}\) be the \(\mathbb{Z}\)-algebra generated by the \(T_n\) on \(S_{24}\).

Tag 22UF

Problem 31.3.3. \(\mathbf{T}_{24} = \mathbb{Z}[T_2] = \mathbb{Z}\oplus \mathbb{Z}T_2\), and \(S_{24}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbf{T}_{24}\, g_2\) is free of rank one over \(\mathbf{T}_{24}\).

Let \(\Lambda := S_{24}(\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathcal{O}\) in \(V := S_{24} \otimes K = K f_+ \oplus K f_-\), and let \(\mathbf{T}_{24} \otimes \mathcal{O}\) act on it, through its eigenvalues, as a subalgebra of \(\mathcal{O}\times \mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22UG

Problem 31.3.4. The congruence ideal of \(f_+\) is \((\lambda_+ - \lambda_-)\mathcal{O}= 24\sqrt D\,\mathcal{O}\). Hence \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; K f_+) \cong \mathcal{O}/24\sqrt D\,\mathcal{O}\), and this can also be seen directly from Problem 31.3.2.

Tag 22UH

Problem 31.3.5. \(a_n(f_+) \equiv a_n(f_-) \pmod{24\sqrt D\,\mathcal{O}}\) for all \(n\). The primes \(2\) and \(3\) split in \(K\), and \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; K f_+)\) has length \(1\) at \(\mathfrak{p}\), length \(3\) at each prime above \(2\), and length \(1\) at each prime above \(3\).

The congruence modulo \(\mathfrak{p}\), the ramified prime of the Hecke field, is the interesting one. The primes \(2\) and \(3\) are smaller than the weight, outside the range \(p > k\) of Hida’s theorem below (Theorem 31.4.1).

§31.4. Hida’s identity Tag 22UJ

What, in terms of \(f\) alone, decides whether \(\mathrm{C}(\Lambda; K f)\) is nonzero at \(\mathfrak{p}\)? Let \(k \ge 12\) be even, let \(f = \sum a(n) q^n \in S_k\) be a normalized eigenform, let \(K \subset \mathbb{C}\) be a number field containing the coefficients of all normalized eigenforms in \(S_k\), \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathcal{O}_K\), and \(\Lambda := S_k(\mathbb{Z}) \otimes \mathcal{O}\). Let \(Y \subset S_k \otimes K\) be the span of the other normalized eigenforms, and write \(\mathrm{C}(f) := \mathrm{C}(\Lambda; K f)\) for the decomposition \(S_k \otimes K = K f \oplus Y\). Let \[\langle g, h \rangle := \int_{\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash \mathcal H} g(z)\,\overline{h(z)}\, y^{k} \,\frac{dx\,dy}{y^2}\] be the Petersson product.

Tag 22UK

Problem 31.4.1. \(Y\) is the orthogonal complement of \(f\), and the projection to \(K f\) is \(g \mapsto \frac{\langle g, f \rangle}{\langle f, f \rangle} f\). Hence \(\mathrm{C}(f) \cong J_f/\mathcal{O}\), where \(J_f\) is the \(\mathcal{O}\)-module generated by the numbers \(\langle g, f \rangle / \langle f, f \rangle\) with \(g \in S_k(\mathbb{Z})\). For \(f = f_+\) of Section 31.3, \(J_f = (24\sqrt D)^{-1}\mathcal{O}\).

So the denominator is a division by \(\langle f, f \rangle\), and Hida’s identity says how far that division fails to be integral. For \(p\) prime write \(a(p) = \alpha_p + \beta_p\) with \(\alpha_p\beta_p = p^{k-1}\), and let \[L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f, s) := \prod_p \bigl(1 - \alpha_p^2 p^{-s}\bigr)^{-1} \bigl(1 - p^{k-1-s}\bigr)^{-1} \bigl(1 - \beta_p^2 p^{-s}\bigr)^{-1} \qquad (\operatorname{Re} s > k) .\] In the unitary normalization, the adjoint \(L\)-function is \(L(s, \operatorname{Ad}f) := L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f, s + k - 1)\); its value at \(s = 1\) is \(L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f, k)\).

Tag 22UL

Problem 31.4.2. If \(a_m := (\alpha^{m+1} - \beta^{m+1})/(\alpha - \beta)\) and \(w := \alpha\beta\), then \[\sum_{m \ge 0} a_m^2 X^m = \frac{1 + wX}{(1 - \alpha^2 X)(1 - wX)(1 - \beta^2 X)} .\] Hence \(\sum_{n \ge 1} a(n)^2 n^{-s} = \zeta(s-k+1)\, L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f, s) / \zeta(2s-2k+2)\).

Rankin’s method [Ra] computes the residue of this Dirichlet series at \(s = k\); in terms of \(L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f, s)\), which is holomorphic there [Sh], \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:rankin}\tag{1} \langle f, f \rangle = \frac{(k-1)!}{2^{2k-1} \pi^{k+1}} \, L(\operatorname{Sym}^2 f, k) \end{equation}\] [Za]. For \(f = \Delta\) both sides equal \(1.0353620568\ldots \times 10^{-6}\), the value of \(\lVert\Delta\rVert^2\) found from Petersson’s formula in Section 11.2 of Chapter 11.

The transcendental part of \(\langle f, f \rangle\) is measured by periods. Let \[r_f(X) := \int_0^{\mathrm{i}\infty} f(z) (X - z)^{k-2} \, dz ,\] and let \(r_f^+\) and \(r_f^-\) be its even and odd parts in \(X\). By Manin’s theorem there are \(\omega_f^\pm \in \mathbb{C}^\times\) with \(r_f^\pm \in \omega_f^\pm K[X]\) [Mn]. For \(f = \Delta\) one has [KZ] \[\begin{gather*} r_\Delta^+ = \omega^+ \Bigl( \tfrac{36}{691}(X^{10} - 1) + X^2 - 3X^4 + 3X^6 - X^8 \Bigr), \\ r_\Delta^- = \omega^- \bigl( 4X^9 - 25X^7 + 42X^5 - 25X^3 + 4X \bigr) . \end{gather*}\]

Tag 22UM

Problem 31.4.3. The two polynomials in parentheses, and \(X^{10} - 1\), satisfy \(P(X) + X^{10} P(-1/X) = 0\).

The polynomial \(X^{10} - 1\) is a coboundary in the Eichler–Shimura cohomology, and the prime in front of it is the \(691\) of Problem 31.2.2. For a cusp form one discards this direction. Fix a prime \(\mathfrak{p}\) of \(\mathcal{O}\), and let \(\Omega_f^\pm \in \mathbb{C}^\times\) be such that the coefficients of \(X^n\) in \(r_f^\pm\) with \(0 < n < k-2\) lie in \(\Omega_f^\pm \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}\) and generate it, where \(\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}\) is the localization of \(\mathcal{O}\) at \(\mathfrak{p}\). For \(\mathfrak{p}\) above a prime \(p > k\) these are Hida’s periods, defined by generators of the \(f\)-eigenlines in the parabolic cohomology of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) with coefficients in polynomials of degree at most \(k - 2\) over \(\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}\): the period polynomial represents the class of \(f\) there, modulo the coboundary \(X^{k-2} - 1\). The following theorem is due to Hida [Hi1; Hi2], with a contribution of Ribet [Ri]; see [Gh, Thm. 1]. We use it as a black box.

Tag 22UN

Theorem 31.4.1 (Hida). The number \(\eta_f := \langle f, f \rangle / (\mathrm{i}\,\Omega_f^+ \Omega_f^-)\) lies in \(K\). If \(\mathfrak{p}\) lies above a prime \(p > k\), then \(\mathrm{C}(f) \otimes \mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}\ne 0\) if and only if \(\eta_f \in \mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_\mathfrak{p}\).

By (1), \(\eta_f\) is, up to a \(\mathfrak{p}\)-adic unit, the algebraic part \(L(1, \operatorname{Ad}f)/(\pi^{k+1}\mathrm{i}\,\Omega_f^+\Omega_f^-)\) of the adjoint \(L\)-value. In outline, the proof applies Problem 31.1.7(ii) to the parabolic cohomology lattice, self-dual for \(p > k\), whose \(f\)-part is spanned by isotropic \(\pm\) lines; the main difficulty is to compare this cohomological congruence module with \(\mathrm{C}(f)\) [Gh]; in weight \(2\) see [DDT]. Two checks with PARI/GP [PARI]: with the coefficient of \(X^2\) in \(r_\Delta^+\) as \(\Omega^+\) and \(\omega^-\) as \(\Omega^-\), one has \(\eta_\Delta = \pm 2^{-10}\), with no prime \(p > 12\), as it must be since \(S_{12} = \mathbb{C}\Delta\); had one kept the coefficient \(36/691\) of \(X^{10} - 1\), one would have found \(691\). For \(f = f_+\) of Section 31.3, take for \(\Omega^+\) and \(\Omega^-\) the coefficients of \(X^2\) in \(r_f^+\) and of \(X\) in \(r_f^-\), which are admissible at every prime above \(p > 24\). Then the norm of \(\eta_f\) is \(D\) times a rational number whose numerator and denominator have no prime factor above \(24\), so \(v_\mathfrak{p}(\eta_f) = 1\), matching Problem 31.3.5.

The denominator in the argument.

In the reconstruction [Adr], at a twisted place the lattice \(\Lambda\) of integral lifted forms is split into the eigencomponents \(V_{\mathrm{good}}\) seen by the period pairing and the others \(V_{\mathrm{bad}}\), and the defect is the length of \(V_{\mathrm{good}} \cap (\Lambda + V_{\mathrm{bad}}) / (\Lambda \cap V_{\mathrm{good}})\). By Problems 31.1.1 and 31.1.2, this is the congruence module of \(V_{\mathrm{good}}\): the denominator of the projection that the period pairing forces. By Problem 31.1.4 it is bounded by congruence ideals [Adr]. In the setting of Theorem 31.4.1 such congruences are detected by adjoint \(L\)-values, which the argument does not control. It uses instead that a congruence with a reducible eigensystem produces a Galois cohomology class, as the congruence ideal \(691\mathbb{Z}\) of \(\Delta\) in Section 31.2 produces the nonzero group \(A^{(679)}\). Chapter 30 computed the groups of this kind for Tate twists over \(\mathbb{Q}\), and Chapter 32 bounds congruence ideals by them in this way.

Exercise.

For \(k = 16, 18, 20, 22, 26\) the space \(S_k\) is spanned by one normalized eigenform \(f_k\), and \(a_\ell(f_k) \equiv 1 + \ell^{k-1} \pmod{N_k}\) for every prime \(\ell\).
