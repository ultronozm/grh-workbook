From Chapter 12: Problems 12.1.9 and 12.3.7, and \(\Delta(\mathbb Z+ \mathbb Z\tau) = (2\pi)^{12}\eta(\tau)^{24}\). From Chapter 28, Covolumes and periods: the Faltings height \(h_{\mathrm F}\) in Deligne’s normalization, Problems 28.1.6 and 28.1.9, and Problem 28.3.1. From Chapter 47: Theorem 47.1.1 and its notation, and Problem 47.1.2. From Chapter 49: \(\mu^\sharp\) and the main term \(T_j\) of Section 49.4. The theory of complex multiplication for elliptic curves is used through three classical facts, recalled in Section 50.1.

The covolume estimate of Theorem 47.2.2 has the main term \(T_j = \sum_\rho(p_j - q_j)w_j(\rho)\log\Omega\). Which period is \(\Omega\), and how does it reach a lattice of automorphic forms on a unitary Shimura variety? In the Lean formalization [Lean] the answer involves only elliptic curves: \(\Omega\) is the Chowla–Selberg period of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal O_K\), \(K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\), and it enters through the degree of one metrized line, the flat line, at points of the Shimura variety built from two such curves.

§50.1. The constant

Let \(d > 4\) be prime with \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(K = \mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\) of class number \(h\), \(\chi\) its character, and \(c_\chi = L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\). For a lattice \(L \subset \mathbb C\) put \(\mathcal A(L) := A(L)\lvert\Delta(L)\rvert^{1/6}\), which is invariant under homothety (Problem 28.1.6). Let \[\log\Omega_K := \gamma + \log\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt d} - c_\chi .\] For a CM field \(E \supseteq K\) as in Theorem 47.1.1, the constant there is \(\log\Omega = \log\Omega_K - \log(\operatorname{rd}_E/\sqrt d)\); for \(E = K\) the two agree.

Problem 50.1.1. \(\mathcal A(\mathbb Z+ \mathbb Z\tau) = 4\pi^2\operatorname{Im}(\tau)\,\lvert\eta(\tau)\rvert^4\).

Problem 50.1.2. \(\displaystyle \log\Omega_K = \frac1h\sum_Q\log\frac{\mathcal A(\mathbb Z+ \mathbb Z\tau_Q)}{\pi} = \frac1h\sum_Q\log\bigl(4\pi y_Q\lvert\eta(\tau_Q)\rvert^4\bigr)\).

Problem 50.1.3. \(\log\Omega_K = 1.426502\ldots\), \(1.300363\ldots\), \(1.027786\ldots\) for \(d = 7, 11, 19\). For \(d = 23\) the three classes give \(\log(4\pi y_Q\lvert\eta(\tau_Q)\rvert^4) = 0.894533\ldots\) for \(Q = [1,1,6]\) and \(1.456932\ldots\) for \(Q = [2,\pm1,3]\), with mean \(\log\Omega_K = 1.269466\ldots\)

The formula of Problem 50.1.2 also holds for \(d = 4\), where it gives \(1.476335\ldots\) (Problem 47.1.2).

Problem 50.1.4. If \(E \supseteq K\) is a number field, \(\operatorname{rd}_E := \lvert\operatorname{disc}E\rvert^{1/[E:\mathbb Q]}\) (for CM fields this is the root discriminant of Chapter 47) and \(\mathfrak d_{E/K}\) the relative discriminant, then \(\operatorname{rd}_E = \sqrt d\,N(\mathfrak d_{E/K})^{1/[E:\mathbb Q]}\). Hence \(\log\Omega \le \log\Omega_K\), with equality exactly when \(E/K\) is unramified at every finite prime.

The formalization does not average over ideal classes but over Galois conjugates of one lattice. We use three classical facts. First, the reduced form \(Q = [a, b, c]\) corresponds to the ideal \(a\mathbb Z+ \frac{-b + \sqrt{-d}}{2}\mathbb Z\) of \(\mathcal O_K\), which is homothetic to \(\mathbb Z+ \mathbb Z\tau_Q\), and this gives a bijection from reduced forms to ideal classes [Co, Thm. 7.7]. Second, two lattices with the same \(j\) are homothetic [Si1, Ch. VI]. Third, the values \(j(\mathfrak a)\), for \(\mathfrak a\) running over representatives of the \(h\) ideal classes of \(K\), are distinct algebraic integers, and they are the roots of a monic irreducible polynomial over \(\mathbb Z\) of degree \(h\) [Si2, Ch. II].

Problem 50.1.5. For \(d = 23\), the three numbers \(j(\tau_Q)\) are \(-3493225.6999\ldots\) and \(737.8499\ldots \pm 1764.0189\ldots\mathrm i\), and they are the roots of \[X^3 + 3491750X^2 - 5151296875X + 12771880859375 .\]

Problem 50.1.6. Let \(L\) be a lattice homothetic to an ideal of \(\mathcal O_K\) with \(\Delta(L) = 1\), and let \(M \subset \mathbb C\) be a number field containing \(g_2(L)\) and \(g_3(L)\). For each embedding \(\sigma \colon M \to \mathbb C\) let \(L^\sigma\) be the lattice with invariants \(\sigma g_2(L)\), \(\sigma g_3(L)\); these are the period lattices of the curve \(E_0 \colon y^2 = 4x^3 - g_2(L)x - g_3(L)\) over \(M\) with the differential \(dx/y\). Then \[\frac{1}{[M:\mathbb Q]}\sum_\sigma\log\frac{A(L^\sigma)}{\pi} = \log\Omega_K .\]

Problem 50.1.7. For every lattice \(L\), the complex conjugate lattice \(\bar L\) has \(A(\bar L) = A(L)\) and \(\lvert\Delta(\bar L)\rvert = \lvert\Delta(L)\rvert\). If \(L\) is homothetic to an ideal \(\mathfrak a\), then \(\bar L\) is homothetic to \(\bar\mathfrak a\), has \(\Delta(\bar L) = 1\) and invariants \(\overline{g_2(L)}\), \(\overline{g_3(L)}\). So Problem 50.1.6 applies to \(\bar L\) and the field \(\bar M\), and the conjugate curve \(\bar E_0\) has the same mean \(\log\Omega_K\).

Status: Problem 50.1.6 is proved in the formalization from Chowla–Selberg in the form of Problem 50.1.2, itself obtained there from Kronecker’s limit formula.

Problem 50.1.8. If \(E_0\) is an elliptic curve over a number field with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal O_K\), then \(h_{\mathrm F}(E_0) = -\frac12(\log\Omega_K + \log\pi)\). For \(d = 7\) this is \(-1.285615\ldots\). For \(K = \mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\), where \(h = 1\) and the formula of Problem 50.1.2 also holds, the same argument gives \(-1.310532\ldots\), in agreement with Problem 28.1.9.

§50.2. The main term

The \(j\)th lift lives on a unitary group of signature \((p_j, q_j) = (k+j-1, m-j)\), \(1 \le j \le m\), so \(p_j + q_j = k + m - 1\) does not depend on \(j\). In the formalization \(k \ge m + 3\).

Problem 50.2.1. \(p_j - q_j = k + 2j - 1 - m\), and \(\sum_{j=1}^m(p_j - q_j) = mk\). If \(k \ge m + 3\), then \(p_j - q_j \ge 4\) for every \(j\), and \(q_m = 0\).

So only the last lift lives on a definite group, and every main term \(T_j\) has a positive coefficient in front of each weight. The slope moves linearly in the weight when a lattice of sections is tensored with a line.

Problem 50.2.2. Let \(M\) be a nonzero lattice and \(\ell\) a lattice of rank one, in normed spaces over a number field, and give \(M \otimes \ell\) the tensor product norms and unit lattices. Then \(\mu^\sharp(M \otimes \ell) = \mu^\sharp(M) + \mu^\sharp(\ell)\). Hence \(\mu^\sharp(M \otimes \ell^{\otimes w}) = \mu^\sharp(M) + w\,\mu^\sharp(\ell)\) for \(w \ge 0\).

Problem 50.2.3. For the lemniscatic lattice \(\varpi\mathbb Z[\mathrm i]\), \(\mathcal A(\varpi\mathbb Z[\mathrm i])/\pi = 2\varpi^2/\pi\). So for \(K = \mathbb Q(\mathrm i)\) the constant \(\Omega\) of Theorem 47.1.1 is the number \(\Omega\) of Problem 12.3.7, and the shift \(\lvert\lambda\rvert\log A(c\,\mathcal O)\) of Problem 28.3.1 equals \(\lvert\lambda\rvert(\log\Omega + \log\frac\pi2)\).

Thus on a definite group (\(q = 0\)) the period enters the slope of the lattice of forms linearly in the weight, with coefficient \(\log\Omega\) up to a constant per unit of weight. In the formalization the same happens in signature \((p, q)\), with coefficient \((p-q)\log\Omega\) per unit of weight, through the flat line of the next section.

§50.3. The flat line

Let \(X\) be the unitary Shimura variety of signature \((p, q)\) attached to \(E \supseteq K\), and let \(E_0\) and \(E_0'\) be elliptic curves with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal O_K\) through the two embeddings of \(K\), with \(\Delta = 1\). In the formalization certain points of \(X\) are built from \(E_0\) and \(E_0'\), with \(p\) and \(q\) copies respectively, and carry a line of invariant differentials of a fixed type; a section of this line that is constant along such points is a flat generator, and its metrized line the flat line. We do not reproduce the definitions of the formalization, which fix the type and the metric through a Hermitian form on the \(\mathcal O_E\)-lattice of the point.

Theorem 50.3.1 (flat degree). The flat line at a point of type \((p, q)\), over a field of definition \(F\), has degree divided by \([F:\mathbb Q]\) (half the normalization of Theorem 47.1.1) equal to \[\frac{p - q}{2}\log\Omega + q\,\bigl(\log d - \log\operatorname{rd}_E\bigr) .\]

Status: proved in the formalization. Its degree is a sum of local terms. At the archimedean places it is the logarithm of a Gram determinant of CM vectors, expressed through the norms of the invariant differentials of \(E_0\) and \(E_0'\), whose mean over conjugates is \(\log\Omega_K\) (Problems 50.1.6 and 50.1.7). At the finite places it is a transition determinant of absolute value \(1\) together with the different of \(\mathcal O_E\). We have read this proof but not the definitions of the flat generator and of its metric, so we state only the result.

Problem 50.3.1. For \(E = K\), the flat degree is \(\frac{p-q}2\log\Omega_K + \frac q2\log d\).

Problem 50.3.2. Let \(F_j\) be the flat degree in signature \((p_j, q_j)\). Then \(F_{j+1} - F_j = \log\Omega_K - \frac12\log d\) for \(1 \le j < m\), whatever \(E\) is. For \(d = 7\) this difference is \(0.453546\ldots\)

Problem 50.3.3. Write \(F^\circ_j := \frac{p_j - q_j}{2}\log\Omega\) for the period part of \(F_j\). Then \(T_j = 2\sum_\rho w_j(\rho)F^\circ_j\), and \(\sum_jT_j = fmkw\log\Omega\) when \(w_j(\rho) = w\) for all \(j\) and \(\rho\), where \(f\) is the number of places \(\rho \in \Phi\).

By Problem 50.2.2, tensoring with \(w\) copies of a line whose \(\mu^\sharp\) is twice its normalized degree \(F^\circ_j\) moves \(\mu^\sharp\) by \(2wF^\circ_j\); Problem 50.3.3 says that \(T_j\) is exactly this shift for the weights \(w_j(\rho)\). The formalization uses this as follows. An integer \(\alpha_\rho\), the anchor, is attached to each place, with \[\Bigl\lvert T_j - \sum_\rho(p_j - q_j)\,\alpha_\rho\log\Omega\Bigr\rvert \le Cu ,\] in the notation of Section 49.4. Multiplication by a flat section of the corresponding tensor power of the flat line then compares two weights on a cover.

Problem 50.3.4. If \(\lvert\alpha_\rho - w_j(\rho)\rvert \le \eta\) for every \(\rho\), then \(\bigl\lvert T_j - \sum_\rho(p_j - q_j)\alpha_\rho\log\Omega\bigr\rvert \le f(k + m - 1)\,\eta\,\lvert\log\Omega\rvert\).

Theorem 50.3.2 (flat comparison). On a good cover of the Shimura variety of the \(j\)th lift, \(j < m\), the lattices of integral cusp forms of the weights \(\lambda_-\) and \(\lambda_M\) of Chapter 51 satisfy \[\mu^\sharp(\lambda_-) \ge \mu^\sharp(\lambda_M) - T_j - C_Fu ,\] with \(C_F\) independent of the point and the lift.

Status: proved in the formalization from Theorem 50.3.1, the anchor bound and the statements on the cover listed at the end of Chapter 51. The anchor bound is proved there from the definitions of the anchors, which we have not traced; we do not know how close \(\alpha_\rho\) is to \(w_j(\rho)\).

Where else the constant occurs.

The same closed formula \(\gamma + \log(2\pi/\operatorname{rd}_E) - c_\chi\) is used in the formalization for the main term \(T_j\), for the flat degree, and in the bounds for the theta kernels of the chain, where Problem 50.1.6 is applied. So the cancellation of the period between the covolumes and the heights of the period maps is built into the definitions. Only elliptic curves with complex multiplication by \(\mathcal O_K\) enter this step; no period relation for abelian varieties with complex multiplication by a larger field is used.
