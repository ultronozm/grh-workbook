Prerequisites.

Frobenius elements and inertia groups in abelian extensions of \(\mathbb{Q}\) [Ne, Ch. I, §9]; Dirichlet characters as characters of Galois groups, with their conductors [Wa, Ch. 3]; orthogonality of characters of finite abelian groups [SeA, Ch. VI, §1]. From Chapters 1 and 2, From zeros to a one-sided ramp sum and Inert primes and Estimate B: the functions \(G\), \(G_\chi\) and constants \(c_\chi = \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi)\) of equations (1) and (3) there, Section 1.4, and Sections 2.1–2.2 with Estimate B.

In Chapter 2 an odd prime power was put in a cell of a table with two rows and \(n\) columns, and averaging over translates turned some of the cells into four ramp sums (Problem 2.1.6). Here the cells are the archimedean places of an abelian CM field, a weighted prime sum is an exact combination of the functions \(G_\chi\) over the characters of its Galois group, and averaging over translates is the projection onto the characters trivial on the translations. We follow [Dup, Ch. 5–6], an account of Alpöge’s argument [Alp].

Throughout, \(q \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) and \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) are primes with \(q \mid d + 1\); \(n \ge 2\) is a power of \(2\), \(N := 4n\), \(H := (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times/\{\pm 1\}\), and \([r] \in H\) is the class of an odd \(r\). Let \(\chi_q(r) := \bigl(\frac rq\bigr)\) and \(\chi_d(r) := \bigl(\frac rd\bigr)\) be the Legendre symbols, \(\eta := \chi_q \chi_d\) and \(v(r) := \frac12\bigl(1 + \chi_q(r)\bigr)\). The example is \(q = 11\), \(d = 1319\), \(N = 16\), with \(H = \{[1], [3], [5], [7]\}\).

§4.1. The host field and its places Tag 224T

Why are the class of \(r\) modulo \(N\) up to sign and \(\chi_q(r)\) the right labels for a prime power? Let \[E := \mathbb{Q}\bigl(\zeta_N + \zeta_N^{-1}, \sqrt{-q}, \sqrt{-d}\bigr) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{Nqd}), \qquad K := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}),\] \(\Gamma := H \times \{\pm 1\} \times \{\pm 1\}\), and for \(r\) prime to \(2qd\) let \(\operatorname{Frob}_r \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) be the restriction of \(\zeta \mapsto \zeta^r\).

Tag 224U

Problem 4.1.1. There is an isomorphism \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}) \to \Gamma\) with \(\operatorname{Frob}_r \mapsto \bigl([r], \chi_q(r), \chi_d(r)\bigr)\) for every \(r\) prime to \(2qd\). In particular \([E : \mathbb{Q}] = 4n\).

We identify \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) with \(\Gamma\) and \(H\) with \(H \times 1 \times 1\). Then \(\Phi := \operatorname{Gal}(E/K) = H \times \{\pm 1\} \times 1\), and \(E\) is a CM field whose complex conjugation is \(c := ([1], -1, -1)\) under every embedding. Fix an embedding \(\sigma_0 \colon E \to \mathbb{C}\).

Tag 224V

Problem 4.1.2. The conjugate of \(\sigma_0 \circ g\) is \(\sigma_0 \circ g c\). Hence each of the \(2n\) complex places of \(E\) contains exactly one embedding \(\sigma_0 \circ \rho\) with \(\rho \in \Phi\).

So the CM type \(\Phi\) indexes the places. We call \((s, e_1, 1) \in \Phi\) the cell \((v, s)\), in row \(v := \frac12(1 - e_1)\) and column \(s\); the group \(H\) translates the columns. In [Dup, Def. 6.1.3] a prime power \(r\) counts exactly when \(\operatorname{Frob}_r \notin \Phi\), and it counts at the place of \(\sigma_0 \circ \operatorname{Frob}_r\), represented in \(\Phi\) by \(\operatorname{Frob}_r c\).

Tag 224W

Problem 4.1.3. Let \(p \nmid 2qd\) be prime. Then \(\operatorname{Frob}_{p^h} \notin \Phi\) if and only if \(p\) is inert in \(K\) and \(h\) is odd, and then \(\operatorname{Frob}_{p^h} c\) is the cell \(\bigl(v(p^h), [p^h]\bigr)\).

Tag 224X

Problem 4.1.4. For \(q = 11\), \(d = 1319\), \(N = 16\), the odd primes \(p \le 61\), \(p \ne 11\), with \(\operatorname{Frob}_p \notin \Phi\) lie in the cells below; the others are squares modulo \(1319\). \[\begin{array}{c|cccc} \text{row} \backslash \text{column} & [1] & [3] & [5] & [7] \\ \hline 0 & 17 & 13,\, 29,\, 61 & - & 41 \\ 1 & 47 & - & 37,\, 59 & 23 \end{array}\]

At \(p \mid 2qd\) let \(I_p \subset \Gamma\) be the inertia group and \(F_p\) the coset of \(I_p\) inducing the Frobenius on the fixed field of \(I_p\) [Ne, Ch. I, §9]; for \(p \nmid 2qd\) let \(I_p := 1\) and \(F_p := \{\operatorname{Frob}_p\}\). For \(r = p^h\) let \(F(r) := F_p^h\), the \(h\)-th power of \(F_p\) in \(\Gamma/I_p\) (a coset of \(I_p\), not the set of \(h\)-th powers of its elements), and \(\Lambda_d(r) := \Lambda(r)\bigl(1 - \chi_d(r)\bigr)\).

Tag 224Y

Problem 4.1.5. (i) \(F(2^h), F(q^h) \subset \Phi\). (ii) \(F(d^h)\) has exactly one element \(g\) outside \(\Phi\), and \(g c\) is the cell \(\bigl(v(d^h), [d^h]\bigr)\); for the example, \(d\) and \(d^2\) lie in \((0, [7])\) and \((1, [1])\).

So \(F(r) \smallsetminus \Phi\) is empty or a single element \(g_r\); in the second case let \(\operatorname{cell}_t(r) := t g_r c\) for \(t \in H\), the cell of \(r\) with shift \(t\).

Tag 224Z

Problem 4.1.6. For every prime power \(r = p^h\), \(\ 2 \lvert I_p \rvert^{-1} \#\bigl(F(r) \smallsetminus \Phi\bigr) \log p = \Lambda_d(r)\).

So \(\Lambda_d(r) = 0\) when \(r\) has no cell, and a term with the factor \(\Lambda_d(r)\) that involves \(\operatorname{cell}_t(r)\) is then read as \(0\).

§4.2. Characters and the balance identity Tag 2252

The characters of \(\Gamma\) are \(\theta_{\xi, j, l}(s, e_1, e_2) := \xi(s) e_1^j e_2^l\), with \(\xi\) a character of \(H\) and \(j, l \in \{0, 1\}\). Let \(\chi_\theta\) be the primitive character inducing \(r \mapsto \theta(\operatorname{Frob}_r)\), let \(\theta_K := \theta_{1, 0, 1}\), and let \(G_1 := G\) and \(G_\theta := G_{\chi_\theta}\), \(c_\theta := c_{\chi_\theta}\) for \(\theta \ne 1\). For a character \(\xi\) of \(H\) we also write \(G_\xi\), \(G_{\xi\chi_q}\), \(G_{\xi\chi_d}\), \(G_{\xi\eta}\) for \(G_\theta\) with \(\theta = \theta_{\xi, 0, 0}\), \(\theta_{\xi, 1, 0}\), \(\theta_{\xi, 0, 1}\), \(\theta_{\xi, 1, 1}\).

Tag 2253

Problem 4.2.1. \(\chi_{\theta_{\xi, j, l}}\) induces \(\xi \chi_q^j \chi_d^l\), with conductor \(f(\xi) q^j d^l\), \(f(\xi) \mid N\). For \(N = 16\) and \(\xi([3]) = i\), the conductors of \(\xi^k\), \(k = 0, 1, 2, 3\), are \(1, 16, 8, 16\), and \(\xi^2\) induces \(\chi_8(r) = (-1)^{(r^2 - 1)/8}\).

Tag 2254

Problem 4.2.2. For every prime power \(r\), \(t \in H\) and \(\rho \in \Phi\), \[\Lambda_d(r) \, \mathbf{1}\bigl[\operatorname{cell}_t(r) = \rho\bigr] = \frac{\Lambda(r)}{2n} \sum_\theta \overline{\theta(t^{-1} \rho c)} \, \chi_\theta(r) .\]

Tag 2255

Problem 4.2.3. \(\sum_{\rho \in \Phi} \overline{\theta(t^{-1} \rho c)}\) is \(2n\) if \(\theta = 1\), \(-2n\) if \(\theta = \theta_K\), and \(0\) otherwise; and \(\chi_{\theta_K} = \chi_d\).

The arithmetic of the argument gives a smoothed Legendre count over the prime powers with a cell, each weighted by its cell [Dup, Rem. 6.1.5]. For \(a > 0\), \(t \in H\) and \(w \colon \Phi \to (0, \infty)\) let \[P_{t, w}(a) := \sum_r \Lambda_d(r) \Bigl(\frac{a}{r} - w\bigl(\operatorname{cell}_t(r)\bigr)\Bigr)_+ , \qquad M_w(a) := a \bigl( \log a - 1 - \gamma + c_{\chi_d} - \langle \log w \rangle \bigr) ,\] where \(\langle \log w \rangle\) is the mean of \(\log w\) over \(\Phi\). The term \(a \langle \log w \rangle\) is what an even spread of the primes over the cells would give, since the cell \(\rho\) tests primes up to \(a/w(\rho)\).

Tag 2256

Problem 4.2.4. The finite parts \(\sum_{r \le y} \Lambda(r) \chi_\theta(r)(y/r - 1)\) of the \(G_\theta(y)\) satisfy \[\frac{1}{2n} \sum_{\rho} w(\rho) \sum_\theta \overline{\theta(t^{-1} \rho c)} \!\! \sum_{r \le a/w(\rho)} \!\! \Lambda(r) \chi_\theta(r) \Bigl(\frac{a}{w(\rho) r} - 1\Bigr) = P_{t, w}(a) .\]

Tag 2257

Problem 4.2.5. The linear parts, \(-y \log y + (1 + \gamma) y\) for \(\theta = 1\) and \(c_\theta y\) otherwise, contribute \(-M_w(a)\) in the same way.

Adding Problems 4.2.4 and 4.2.5 gives the case of [Dup, Prop. 6.1.4] needed here.

Tag 2258

Theorem 4.2.1 (balance identity). For every \(a > 0\), \(t \in H\) and \(w \colon \Phi \to (0, \infty)\), \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:balance}\tag{1} P_{t, w}(a) - M_w(a) = \sum_{\rho \in \Phi} w(\rho) R_{t, \rho}\bigl(a/w(\rho)\bigr) , \qquad R_{t, \rho} := \frac{1}{2n} \sum_\theta \overline{\theta(t^{-1} \rho c)} \, G_\theta . \end{equation}\]

Both sides are finite sums; no prime number theorem enters. So an upper bound \(P \le M + \mathcal{E}\), of the kind divisibility gives, bounds a combination of the \(G_\theta\). For \(w\) constant the right side is \(b(G - G_{\chi_d})(a/b)\), as in Problem 2.1.3. In the example, with \(t = [1]\), \(a = 10^4\), and \(w\) equal to \(10\) at \((1, [1])\) and \(0.05\) elsewhere, \(P_{t, w}(a) = 84701.0317\) and \(M_w(a) = 84463.3942\).

§4.3. Averaging over translates Tag 2259

Why not use the cells one at a time?

Tag 225A

Problem 4.3.1. \(\sum_{\rho \in \Phi} \theta(t^{-1} \rho c) R_{t, \rho} = G_\theta - G_{\theta \theta_K}\). Hence \(\lvert G_\xi - G_{\xi \chi_d} \rvert \le 2n \max_\rho \lvert R_{t, \rho} \rvert\) for every character \(\xi \ne 1\) of \(H\).

Bounds for single cells at the scale \(x\) would thus bound \(G_\xi(x) - G_{\xi\chi_d}(x)\) for the fixed characters \(\xi \ne 1\) of conductor dividing \(N\). These involve the fixed functions \(L(s, \xi)\), and a two-sided bound \(\lvert G_\xi(x) \rvert \le x^{1/2 + \varepsilon}\) at all large \(x\), for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), is as strong as the Riemann hypothesis for \(L(s, \xi)\) (Problem 2.2.1). The characters \(\xi \ne 1\) must be removed. For \(u \in H\) let \(w_u(\rho) := w(u^{-1} \rho)\).

Tag 225B

Problem 4.3.2. Independently of \(t\), \[\frac1n \sum_{u \in H} \bigl( P_{t, w_u}(a) - M_{w_u}(a) \bigr) = \frac{1}{2n} \sum_{\rho} w(\rho) \sum_{j, l} \overline{\theta_{1, j, l}(\rho c)} \, G_{\theta_{1, j, l}}\bigl(a/w(\rho)\bigr) .\]

Tag 225C

Problem 4.3.3. For \(\rho\) in row \(1\) the inner sum is \(R_{q, d} := G + G_{\chi_q} - G_{\chi_d} - G_\eta\), the right side of Problem 2.1.7; in row \(0\) it is \(R'_{q, d} := G - G_{\chi_q} - G_{\chi_d} + G_\eta\).

Tag 225D

Theorem 4.3.1. For every \(a > 0\), \(t \in H\) and \(w \colon \Phi \to (0, \infty)\), \[\begin{multline*} \frac1n \sum_{u \in H} \bigl( P_{t, w_u}(a) - M_{w_u}(a) \bigr) \\ = \frac{1}{2n} \sum_{\rho \text{ in row } 1} w(\rho) R_{q, d}\Bigl(\frac{a}{w(\rho)}\Bigr) + \frac{1}{2n} \sum_{\rho \text{ in row } 0} w(\rho) R'_{q, d}\Bigl(\frac{a}{w(\rho)}\Bigr) . \end{multline*}\]

Averaging is the projection onto the characters of \(\operatorname{Gal}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}, \sqrt{-d})/\mathbb{Q}\bigr)\); the column characters vanish exactly, for every weight. The survivors other than \(1\) have conductors \(q\), \(d\), \(qd\), within reach of Estimate B, and \(G\) has coefficient \(+1\). In the example the four excesses \(P_{t, w_u}(a) - M_{w_u}(a)\) are \(237.64\), \(-300.26\), \(-176.73\), \(229.13\), with mean \(-2.5545\); Theorem 4.3.1 writes it as \(\frac54 R_{11, 1319}(1000) = -2.7034\) plus \(0.1488\) from the cells at the scale \(200000\).

§4.4. Thick and thin weights, and two scales Tag 225E

In [Dup, Def. 6.2.1] the weights are not translates of one function, and they are of two kinds; from here on \(w_u\), \(u \in H\), is any family of weights \(\Phi \to (0, \infty)\). Fix a nonempty \(T_2 \subset H\), \(\delta := \lvert T_2 \rvert/n\), and let \(\Theta_u\) be the cells in row \(1\) with column in \(u T_2\). Let \(x \ge 2\), \(\lvert a/b - x \rvert \le 1\), \(\Delta \ge 0\), \(X > x + 1\), \(\vartheta \ge 0\), and suppose that for every \(u\) \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:thick-thin}\tag{2} b \le w_u(\rho) \le b + \Delta \quad (\rho \in \Theta_u,\ \text{thick}), \qquad a/w_u(\rho) \ge X \quad (\rho \notin \Theta_u,\ \text{thin}), \end{equation}\] and that \(\lvert G_\theta(y) \rvert \le \vartheta y\) for all \(\theta\) and \(y \ge X\) (the thin-scale bound). Thick cells test primes near \(x\); thin ones test primes beyond \(X\). There, for a small fixed \(\varepsilon > 0\), \(b = \exp(x^\varepsilon)\) and \(X = \frac13 \exp(x^{\varepsilon/3})\).

Tag 225F

Problem 4.4.1. The thin cells contribute at most \(4 n a \vartheta\) to the right side of (1) for \(w_u\).

Tag 225G

Problem 4.4.2. Replacing \(w_u\) by \(b\) on \(\Theta_u\) changes \(P_{t, w_u}(a) - M_{w_u}(a)\) by at most \[\Delta \Bigl( 2 \sum_{r \le x + 1} \Lambda(r) + x + 1 \Bigr) .\]

Tag 225H

Problem 4.4.3. With weight \(b\) on \(\Theta_u\), the average over \(u \in H\) of the thick part of (1) is \(\frac{\delta b}{2} R_{q, d}(a/b)\).

Tag 225J

Problem 4.4.4. For \(\lvert y - x \rvert \le 1\), \(\lvert R_{q, d}(y) - R_{q, d}(x) \rvert \le \mathcal{K}(x)\), where \[\mathcal{K}(x) := 4 \sum_{r \le x + 1} \frac{\Lambda(r)}{r} + \log(x + 1) + 1 + \lvert c_{\chi_q} \rvert + \lvert c_{\chi_d} \rvert + \lvert c_\eta \rvert .\]

Tag 225K

Theorem 4.4.1. Under (2) and the thin-scale bound, for every \(t \in H\), \[\begin{multline*} R_{q, d}(x) \le \frac{2}{\delta b} \cdot \frac1n \sum_{u \in H} \bigl( P_{t, w_u}(a) - M_{w_u}(a) \bigr) \\ + \mathcal{K}(x) + \frac{8 n (x + 1) \vartheta}{\delta} + \frac{2\Delta}{\delta b} \Bigl( 2 \sum_{r \le x + 1} \Lambda(r) + x + 1 \Bigr) . \end{multline*}\]

At the scale \(x\) only the quadratic characters appear; at the scale \(X\) all \(4n\) do, but only a bound of prime number theorem type is needed there. The cost conditions of [Dup, Def. 6.2.3(2)] make the last two terms \(O(1/(\delta x))\). The thin-scale bound follows from the classical zero-free region [Dav, Ch. 14], since \(\log X\) is large compared with the logarithms of the conductors, and from the exclusion of exceptional zeros: by Theorem 9.3.1 of Chapter 9, or by the choice of \(q, d\) [Dup, Def. 5.4.2], [Adr]. We do not carry out the computation here.

§4.5. Averaging over a subgroup, and top places Tag 225L

For \(L(s, \psi)\), averaging over all of \(H\) would remove \(\psi\). In the simplest case \(\psi\) is a character of \(H\) (in general \(E\) is enlarged, [Dup, Def. 5.1.3]). Let \(o\) be its order and \(A_0 := \ker \psi\); in the example \(\psi := \xi^2\), \(\chi_\psi = \chi_8\), \(o = 2\), \(A_0 = \{[1], [7]\}\). Now \(u\) runs over \(A_0\), \(T_2 \subset A_0\), thick weights equal \(b\), and the shift \(t\) runs over a nonempty set \(T \subset H\) of top places [Dup].

Tag 225M

Problem 4.5.1. The characters of \(\Gamma\) trivial on \(A_0\) are the \(4o\) characters \(\theta_{\xi, j, l}\) with \(\xi \in \langle \psi \rangle\); in the example the conductors of their \(\chi_\theta\) are \(1\), \(11\), \(1319\), \(14509\), \(8\), \(88\), \(10552\), \(116072\).

Tag 225N

Problem 4.5.2. The average over \(u \in A_0\) of the thick part of (1) is \[\frac{\delta b}{2} \sum_{\xi \in \langle \psi \rangle} \xi(t) R_\xi(a/b) , \qquad R_\xi := G_\xi + G_{\xi \chi_q} - G_{\xi \chi_d} - G_{\xi \eta}\] (primitive characters), with \(R_1 = R_{q, d}\).

Tag 225P

Problem 4.5.3. If a proportion \(\lambda\) of \(T\) lies in \(A_0\) and \(\lambda'\) in each other coset, then the mean of \(\xi(t)\) over \(t \in T\) is \(1\) for \(\xi = 1\) and \(\kappa := \lambda - \lambda'\) for \(\xi \in \langle \psi \rangle \smallsetminus 1\).

Combining Problems 4.5.2 and 4.5.3: the average over \(u \in A_0\) and \(t \in T\) of the thick part is \(\frac{\delta b}{2}\bigl(R_{q, d} + \kappa \sum_{\xi \ne 1} R_\xi\bigr)(a/b)\). Its untwisted part \(G + \kappa \sum_{\xi \ne 1} G_\xi\) is the \(G^\psi\) of Section 1.4 for the shares \(\lambda = 4/(3o + 1)\), \(\lambda' = 3/(3o + 1)\) of [Dup, Def. 5.3.3], where \(T\) has these shares up to \(x^{-1 + \varepsilon}\). The characters \(\xi \notin \langle \psi \rangle\), of small conductor, are gone; the rest are twists of large conductor.

Tag 225Q

Problem 4.5.4. For odd \(r\), the coefficient of \(\Lambda(r)(y/r - 1)_+\) in \(G + \kappa \sum_{\xi \ne 1} G_\xi\) is \(o\) times the proportion of \(T\) in the coset of \([r]\); for \(o = 2\), \(\lambda = 4/7\), it is \(8/7\) or \(6/7\), as in Problem 1.4.1.

The upper bound for the mean of \(P - M\) that this averaging consumes is the main inequality of the argument, not treated here [Dup, Thm. 6.3.4].
