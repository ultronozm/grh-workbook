Prerequisites.

Complete local noetherian rings and Hensel’s lemma; lengths of modules; elementary divisors of matrices over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\); graded rings. From Chapter 30, Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists: the groups \(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\), \(G_p\), \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e)\), Problem 30.1.1 and Theorem 30.1.2. From Chapter 32, Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes: Problem 32.1.1, the congruence (1), the lattices of Section 32.2 (Problems 32.2.1–32.2.4), the inputs (D), (I), (O) of Section 32.3 with Problems 32.3.1 and 32.3.3, and Section 32.4, in particular Problems 32.4.6–32.4.8.

Chapter 32 ended with the observation that the Hecke algebra sees a Galois representation only through its traces. A Hecke algebra is not a domain: it has an Eisenstein component and cuspidal components, and its traces do not in general come from a representation over it. What structure do traces alone carry, and how do they measure \(\mathrm{Sha}\)? The structure is a pseudocharacter; the ideal of products \(b \cdot c\) of Chapter 32 can be read off from the traces; and the quotient by it carries extension classes. In a graded model of the deformation ring we then bound the length of the cotangent module that stands for the Selmer group by the length of a quotient of the model’s Hecke algebra, and so by valuations of trace differences. Throughout, \(p\) is an odd prime.

§33.1. Pseudocharacters of dimension two Tag 22VN

Let \(G\) be a group and \(R\) a commutative ring in which \(2\) is invertible. A pseudocharacter of dimension \(2\) of \(G\) with values in \(R\) is a pair \((T, D)\) of a function \(T \colon G \to R\) and a homomorphism \(D \colon G \to R^\times\) such that \(T(1) = 2\) and, for all \(g, h \in G\), \[T(gh) = T(hg), \qquad T(gh) + D(g)\, T(g^{-1}h) = T(g)\, T(h) .\] By [Ch, Lemma 1.9] such pairs are the same as Chenevier’s determinants of dimension \(2\). The function \(T\) is then a pseudocharacter of dimension \(2\) in the sense of Taylor [Ta] and Rouquier [Ro], and conversely every such \(T\) arises in this way [Ch, Prop. 1.29]; see also [BC]. The notion is due to Wiles in dimension two and to Taylor in general [BC].

Tag 22VP

Problem 33.1.1. For every homomorphism \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(R)\), \((\operatorname{tr}\rho, \det\rho)\) is a pseudocharacter. In particular, so is \((\psi_1 + \psi_2, \psi_1\psi_2)\) for any two characters \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G \to R^\times\).

Tag 22VQ

Problem 33.1.2. If \((T, D)\) is a pseudocharacter, then \(D(g) = \tfrac12\bigl(T(g)^2 - T(g^2)\bigr)\) and \(T(g^{-1}) = D(g)^{-1} T(g)\) for all \(g \in G\).

So \(D\) is determined by \(T\), and we often write \(T\) for the pair.

Tag 22VR

Problem 33.1.3. Let \(R \subseteq R_1 \times R_2\) be a subring in which \(2\) is invertible, and let \((T_i, D_i)\) be pseudocharacters with values in \(R_i\). If \((T_1(g), T_2(g)) \in R\) for every \(g\), then \(((T_1, T_2), (D_1, D_2))\) is a pseudocharacter with values in \(R\).

The first example of a pseudocharacter that is not visibly a trace is the one carried by a Hecke algebra with one Eisenstein and one cuspidal point. Let \(G\) be profinite, let \(\chi \colon G \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) be a continuous character whose reduction \(\bar\chi\) is not trivial, and let \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)\) be a continuous representation with \(\operatorname{tr}\rho(G) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_p\), \(\operatorname{tr}\rho \equiv 1 + \chi \pmod p\) and \(\operatorname{tr}\rho \ne 1 + \chi\). Put \[e := \min_{g \in G} v_p\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\rho(g) - 1 - \chi(g)\bigr), \qquad \mathcal{T}(g) := \bigl(1 + \chi(g), \operatorname{tr}\rho(g)\bigr) \in \mathbb{Z}_p\times \mathbb{Z}_p.\]

Tag 22VS

Problem 33.1.4. The \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-subalgebra of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\times \mathbb{Z}_p\) generated by the \(\mathcal{T}(g)\) is \(\mathbf{T}_e := \{(u, v) : u \equiv v \pmod{p^e}\}\), and \(x \mapsto (0, p^e)\) induces \(\mathbb{Z}_p[x]/(x(x - p^e)) \cong \mathbf{T}_e\). The function \(\mathcal{T}\) is a pseudocharacter with values in \(\mathbf{T}_e\).

The ring \(\mathbf{T}_e\) is the toy Hecke algebra: \(x = 0\) is the Eisenstein point, \(x = p^e\) the cuspidal point, and \(e\) measures how close they are. It occurs in nature.

Tag 22VT

Problem 33.1.5. The \(\mathbb{Z}_{691}\)-subalgebra of \(\mathrm{End}(M_{12} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_{691})\) generated by the Hecke operators \(T_n\), \(n \ge 1\), on modular forms of weight \(12\) and level \(1\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb{Z}_{691}[x]/(x(x - 691))\).

§33.2. The reducibility ideal Tag 22VU

Let \(R\) be a complete local noetherian ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) and residue field \(\mathbb{F}\) of characteristic \(\ne 2\), and let \((T, D)\) be a pseudocharacter of \(G\) with values in \(R\) such that \(T \equiv \chi_1 + \chi_2 \pmod{\mathfrak m}\) for two distinct characters \(\chi_1, \chi_2 \colon G \to \mathbb{F}^\times\). Fix \(\sigma_0 \in G\) with \(\chi_1(\sigma_0) \ne \chi_2(\sigma_0)\).

Tag 22VV

Problem 33.2.1. The polynomial \(X^2 - T(\sigma_0)X + D(\sigma_0)\) has roots \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \in R\) with \(\lambda_i \equiv \chi_i(\sigma_0) \pmod{\mathfrak m}\), and \(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 \in R^\times\).

Define \(a, d \colon G \to R\) by \[a(g) := \frac{T(\sigma_0 g) - \lambda_2 T(g)}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_2} , \qquad d(g) := T(g) - a(g) .\]

Tag 22VW

Problem 33.2.2. Suppose that \(T = \operatorname{tr}\rho\) for \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(R)\) with \(\rho(\sigma_0) = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)\), and write \(\rho = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a' & b \\ c & d' \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Then \(a = a'\), \(d = d'\), and \(a(gh) - a(g)a(h) = b(g)c(h)\).

So \(a\) and \(d\) are the diagonal entries of a representation that need not exist, and the defect of \(a\) from being multiplicative stands in for the products \(b(g)c(h)\).

Tag 22VX

Problem 33.2.3. \(a \equiv \chi_1\) and \(d \equiv \chi_2 \pmod{\mathfrak m}\). In particular \(a(g) \in R^\times\).

Tag 22VY

Problem 33.2.4. \(D(g) = a(g)T(g) - a(g^2)\) for every \(g \in G\).

The reducibility ideal \(I \subseteq R\) is the ideal generated by the elements \(a(gh) - a(g)a(h)\), \(g, h \in G\).

Tag 22VZ

Problem 33.2.5. Modulo \(I\), \(a\) and \(d\) are characters \(G \to (R/I)^\times\), \(T \equiv a + d\) and \(D \equiv ad\).

Tag 22W2

Problem 33.2.6. Let \(J \subsetneq R\) be an ideal, and let \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G \to (R/J)^\times\) be characters with \(\psi_i \equiv \chi_i \pmod{\mathfrak m}\) and \(T \equiv \psi_1 + \psi_2 \pmod J\). Then \(\psi_1 \equiv a\) and \(\psi_2 \equiv d \pmod J\), and \(I \subseteq J\).

Problems 33.2.5 and 33.2.6 prove the following; in particular \(I\) does not depend on \(\sigma_0\). For pseudocharacters of any dimension whose residual constituents are absolutely irreducible and pairwise distinct, this is due to Bellaïche and Chenevier [BC, Prop. 1.5.1].

Tag 22W3

Theorem 33.2.1. \(I\) is the smallest ideal \(J\) of \(R\) such that \(T\) is congruent modulo \(J\) to a sum of two characters lifting \(\chi_1\) and \(\chi_2\).

For a representation \(\rho\) over a discrete valuation ring as in Section 32.2, Problems 33.2.2 and 32.4.6 give \(I = \pi^{m+n}\mathcal{O}\), where \(m + n\) is the length of the segment of stable lattices. Return to the toy Hecke algebra.

Tag 22W4

Problem 33.2.7. In Problem 33.1.4, suppose that \(\rho\) is irreducible, and let \(m + n\) be the length of its segment of stable lattices (Problem 32.2.3, with \(\chi_1 = 1\), \(\chi_2 = \bar\chi\)). Then \(m + n \ge e\), and the reducibility ideal of \(\mathcal{T}\) is \(\{0\} \times p^{m+n}\mathbb{Z}_p= p^{m+n-e}\, x\mathbf{T}_e\).

The Eisenstein ideal of a pseudocharacter \(T\) with respect to two given characters \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G \to R^\times\) is the ideal \(I_\mathrm{Eis}\) generated by the \(T(g) - \psi_1(g) - \psi_2(g)\). By Theorem 33.2.1, \(I \subseteq I_\mathrm{Eis}\) whenever the \(\psi_i\) lift the \(\chi_i\). In the setting of Chapter 32 the two ideals coincide.

Tag 22W5

Problem 33.2.8. Let \(k\), \(p\), \(f\), \(\lambda\) be as in Section 32.3, and let \(m + n\) be the length of the segment of stable lattices of \(\rho_f\). Then \[m + n = \min_{\ell \ne p} v\bigl(a_\ell(f) - 1 - \ell^{k-1}\bigr) ,\] the minimum over primes. So the reducibility ideal of \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_f\) equals its Eisenstein ideal for \((1, \chi^{k-1})\).

For \(\Delta\) and \(p = 691\) both sides are \(1\) (Problem 32.4.8). When \(K = \mathbb{Q}_p\), in the toy Hecke algebra built from \(\rho = \rho_f\) and \(\chi^{k-1}\) we get \(e = m + n\), and the reducibility ideal is \(x\mathbf{T}_e\): the Eisenstein component \(\mathbf{T}_e/x\mathbf{T}_e = \mathbb{Z}_p\) is the largest quotient on which \(\mathcal{T}\) is reducible.

§33.3. The quotient by the reducibility ideal and \(\mathrm{Ext}^1\) Tag 22W6

Ribet’s lemma produced a class modulo \(\pi\). The reducibility ideal produces a class modulo \(I\), which generates a copy of \(\mathcal{O}/I\). Keep the notation of Section 32.2, with \(\chi_1 \ne \chi_2\), and write \(\rho\) in the basis \(\pi^m e_1, e_2\) of \(L_m\) as \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Then the values of \(b\) generate \(\mathcal{O}\), the values of \(c\) generate \(I = \pi^{m+n}\mathcal{O}\), and by Problem 33.2.5 the reductions \(\psi_1, \psi_2\) of \(a, d\) modulo \(I\) are characters. Put \(\psi := \psi_1\psi_2^{-1}\).

Tag 22W7

Problem 33.3.1. \(\kappa(g) := b(g)\,d(g)^{-1} \bmod I\) is a cocycle on \(G\) with values in \((\mathcal{O}/I)(\psi)\).

Tag 22W8

Problem 33.3.2. The \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule of \(H^1(G, (\mathcal{O}/I)(\psi))\) generated by the class of \(\kappa\) is isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}/I\).

Thus the quotient \(\mathcal{O}/I\) embeds in a group of extensions of \(\psi_2\) by \(\psi_1\). For such pseudocharacters over a henselian local ring, Bellaïche and Chenevier [BC, Thm. 1.5.5] construct an injection of the dual, modulo \(I\), of a module of off-diagonal entries into the corresponding \(\mathrm{Ext}^1\); this is the general form of the embedding. Now take \(G = G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\), and \(f\), \(\lambda\), \(\mathcal{O}\) as in Problem 33.2.8, so that \(\psi \equiv \chi^{1-k} \pmod I\). As in Section 32.5, \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\) is the group of classes in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, (K/\mathcal{O})(1-k))\) that vanish on \(G_p\).

Tag 22W9

Problem 33.3.3. The class of \(\kappa\) vanishes on \(G_p\), and its image in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, (K/\mathcal{O})(1-k))\) generates a submodule isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}/I\). Hence \(\operatorname{length}_\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \otimes \mathcal{O}\bigr) \ge m + n\).

This is the direction of Ribet and of Mazur and Wiles [MW], for one eigenform: a congruence of exponent \(m + n\) produces a cyclic piece of \(\mathrm{Sha}\) of that length. For \(\Delta\) it gives \(\operatorname{length}\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q}, 11) \ge 1\), and Theorem 30.1.2 gives equality. Alpöge’s argument needs the reverse inequality, an upper bound for the length of \(\mathrm{Sha}\) by trace differences. Traces cannot see a single class (Problem 32.4.5); they see only products of a class of weight \(-1\) with a class of weight \(+1\).

§33.4. A graded model and the length bound Tag 22WA

The torus \(\{\mathrm{diag}(1, t)\}\) of Section 32.4 acts on a deformation ring of \(\rho_0 = 1 \oplus \omega^{k-1}\), and so grades it: upper right entries have weight \(-1\), lower left entries weight \(+1\), and traces weight \(0\). The reconstruction [Adr] reduces the bound for \(\mathrm{Sha}\) to four statements (G1)–(G4) about such a graded deformation ring: (G1) a class of \(\mathrm{Sha}\) gives a tangent vector of weight \(-1\) at the Eisenstein point; (G2) under hypotheses (M1)–(M5), the cotangent module of weight \(-1\), modulo the differentials of the Selmer equations, has length at most \(2\sum_\xi v(\xi(a))\), where \(a\) is a trace difference and \(\xi\) runs over representatives of the torus orbits of points with both upper and lower entries nonzero; (G3) the deformation problem is finite and these points are automorphic; (G4) a rigidity condition at these points. None of them is proved there; they are steps of its sketch towards the Selmer bound on windows, which in [Adr] is [Adr, Hyp. 17.11]; see the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58. Moreover (G3) is not a published theorem: it lies outside the hypotheses of the published automorphy lifting theorems for residually reducible representations, such as [ANT, Thm. 1.1]. We test (G2) on a model in which everything can be computed.

Let \(P \in \mathbb{Z}_p[u]\) be a polynomial with \(P(0) \ne 0\) and leading coefficient in \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\), and let \[A := \mathbb{Z}_p[y, z]/\bigl(y\, P(yz)\bigr) ,\] graded by giving \(y\) weight \(-1\) and \(z\) weight \(+1\); the relation is homogeneous of weight \(-1\). Think of \(y\) as an upper right entry and \(z\) as a lower left entry, so that \(u = yz\) plays the role of \(b \cdot c\). Let \(x \colon A \to \mathbb{Z}_p\) be the Eisenstein point \(y = z = 0\), with ideal \(I_x = (y, z)\). The cotangent module \(I_x/I_x^2\) is graded; write \((I_x/I_x^2)_{-1}\) for its part of weight \(-1\), the model for the dual of the Selmer group.

Tag 22WB

Problem 33.4.1. \(A_0 = \mathbb{Z}_p[u]/(u P(u))\) with \(u = yz\), and \((I_x/I_x^2)_{-1} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p/P(0)\mathbb{Z}_p\). For \(P(u) = p^r - u\), \(A_0\) is the toy Hecke algebra \(\mathbf{T}_r\) of Problem 33.1.4.

Tag 22WC

Problem 33.4.2. A point \((y, z) \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p^2\) of \(\operatorname{Spec} A\) has \(y = 0\), or else \(yz\) is a root of \(P\) and \(z \ne 0\). For each root \(u_0\) of \(P\), the points with \(yz = u_0\) form one orbit of the torus.

So the points with both entries nonzero, the two-signed points, correspond to the roots of \(P\); they are the cuspidal points. Let \(\mathbf{T}^0 := \mathbb{Z}_p[u]/(P(u))\), the cuspidal quotient of \(A_0\), and let \(I_\mathrm{Eis}:= u\mathbf{T}^0\), the image of the ideal of \(x\) in \(A_0\); as in Section 33.2, it is generated by the product of an upper and a lower entry.

Tag 22WD

Problem 33.4.3. \(\mathbf{T}^0\) is free over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\). For \(a \in \mathbb{Z}_p[u]\) with \(a(\xi) \ne 0\) at every root \(\xi\) of \(P\), \[\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbf{T}^0/a\mathbf{T}^0 = \sum_\xi v(a(\xi)) ,\] the sum over the roots of \(P\) in \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p\) with multiplicity, where \(v(p) = 1\).

Tag 22WE

Problem 33.4.4. Let \(a \in A_0\) vanish at \(x\), and let its image in \(\mathbf{T}^0\) be nonzero at every root of \(P\). Then \[\operatorname{length}(I_x/I_x^2)_{-1} = \operatorname{length}\mathbf{T}^0/I_\mathrm{Eis}\le \sum_\xi v(\xi(a)) ,\] where \(\xi(a)\) is the value of \(a\) at \(u = \xi\) and the sum is over the roots \(\xi\) of \(P\) in \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p\), with multiplicity; that is, over the torus orbits of two-signed points (Problem 33.4.2), each counted with the multiplicity of its root. Equality holds for \(a = u\).

Problems 33.4.1–33.4.4 prove the following.

Tag 22WF

Theorem 33.4.1. In the model \(A\), the length of the cotangent module of weight \(-1\) equals \(\operatorname{length}\mathbf{T}^0/I_\mathrm{Eis}\), and is at most \(\sum_\xi v(\xi(a))\) for every \(a\) as in Problem 33.4.4. Equality holds for \(a = u\).

For \(P = 691 - u\) the model has \(A_0 = \mathbb{Z}_{691}[x]/(x(x-691))\), the Hecke algebra of Problem 33.1.5, one two-signed orbit, with \(u = 691\), and a cotangent module of weight \(-1\) of length \(1 = \operatorname{length}\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q}, 11)\). In the model, (G1) and (G3) are built in: the cotangent module stands for the dual of the Selmer group, and every two-signed point is cuspidal. The content of the argument is to prove them for the actual deformation ring.

§33.5. The factor two Tag 22WG

In the model the comparison holds with the factor \(1\), while (G2) carries a factor \(2\) for a graded ring with many coordinates of each weight. Does the factor \(1\) survive when there are more coordinates? Traces have weight \(0\), so add a coordinate \(t\) of weight \(0\), and with it one relation of weight \(0\). Let \(P, R \in \mathbb{Z}_p[u, t]\) with \(P(0,0) \ne 0\) and \(R(0, 0) = 0\), and let \[B := \mathbb{Z}_p[y, z, t]/\bigl(y P(yz, t),\ R(yz, t)\bigr), \qquad S := \mathbb{Z}_p[u, t]/(P, R) ,\] graded as before with \(t\) of weight \(0\). Now \(x\) is the point \(y = z = t = 0\), with ideal \(I_x = (y, z, t)\), and \(S\) is the cuspidal quotient of \(B_0\).

Tag 22WH

Problem 33.5.1. \(B_0 = \mathbb{Z}_p[u,t]/\bigl(u P, R\bigr)\) with \(u = yz\), and \((I_x/I_x^2)_{-1} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p/P(0,0)\mathbb{Z}_p\). For \(R = t\), \(B\) is the model \(A\) with \(P(u, 0)\) in place of \(P\), provided \(P(u, 0)\) has leading coefficient in \(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times\).

Tag 22WJ

Problem 33.5.2. Let \(a \in (u, t) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_p[u,t]\). Then \(\operatorname{length}(I_x/I_x^2)_{-1} \le \operatorname{length}S/aS\). If \(S\) is free of finite rank over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) and \(a\) is nonzero at every point \(\xi\) of \(\operatorname{Spec} S\) over \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p\), then \(\operatorname{length}(I_x/I_x^2)_{-1} \le \sum_\xi v(\xi(a))\), where \(\xi\) is counted with multiplicity the length of the localization of \(S \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p\) at \(\xi\); equivalently, the \(\xi(a)\) are the eigenvalues of multiplication by \(a\) on \(S \otimes \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p\).

So the factor \(1\) survives whenever the cuspidal quotient is finite and free. When \(S\) is finite over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) but has torsion, and \(a\) is nonzero at every \(\xi\), \(\operatorname{length}S/aS\) exceeds \(\sum_\xi v(\xi(a))\) by \(\operatorname{length}S[a]\), and a bound by the values \(\xi(a)\) alone must be obtained otherwise.

The factor two is harmless in the argument: in [Adr] it yields a nonzero integer \(N\) such that, for every \(\ell\), a sum of lengths of Tate–Shafarevich groups at \(\ell\) is at most \(2 v_\ell(N)\), and only \(\sum_\ell 2 v_\ell(N)\log\ell = 2\log\lvert N\rvert\) is used. We do not know whether the factor two in (G2) can be replaced by \(1\) in general.
