Prerequisites.

Even unimodular lattices, \(E_8\), theta series and the Hecke operators \(T_n\) on modular forms of level one [SeA, Ch. V and VII]. Quadratic forms over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) and the number of their isotropic vectors (Problem 22.2.2). Gaussian binomial coefficients \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{a}{b}_{Q}\), which count the \(b\)-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb{F}_Q^a\) [Andr, Ch. 3]. From Chapter 21, Masses and the count of forms: Section 21.1, in particular Problems 21.1.1, 21.1.2 and 21.2.3. From Chapter 22, The Siegel–Weil formula in the smallest case: Problems 22.1.2 and 22.3.7. From Chapter 23, Definite theta lifts and their norms: Section 23.1. For Section 24.4, maximal orders in a definite quaternion algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}\) [Gr], [Pi].

A theta lift is a sum over classes of lattices. A Hecke operator on a class set is also a sum over lattices, namely over the neighbours of a lattice. How do the two interact? Eichler found that a theta series summed over the neighbours of a lattice is a Hecke transform of the theta series of the lattice, up to an explicit multiple of the identity [Ei52], [Ei73]; see [An] for Siegel modular forms and [Ra] for the general theta correspondence. We prove this in degree one, and check it on class sets with two elements, where the Hecke operator is a \(2 \times 2\) matrix.

§24.1. Neighbours Tag 22NJ

How does one move from a lattice to the other lattices of its genus? Kneser’s answer is to change it at one prime [Kn].

Let \(L\) be an even unimodular lattice of rank \(n = 2k\), with \(8 \mid n\), and let \(p\) be a prime. A \(p\)-neighbour of \(L\) is an even unimodular lattice \(M \subset L \otimes \mathbb{Q}\) with \([L : L \cap M] = [M : L \cap M] = p\). Let \(\mathcal{N}_p(L)\) be the set of \(p\)-neighbours.

Tag 22NK

Problem 24.1.1. Let \(p\) be odd. For \(v \in L \setminus pL\) with \(v \cdot v \equiv 0 \pmod{2p^2}\), let \(L_v := \{x \in L : x \cdot v \equiv 0 \pmod p\}\). Then \(M_v := L_v + \mathbb{Z}\, v/p\) is a \(p\)-neighbour of \(L\), and \(M_v\) depends only on the line \(\mathbb{F}_p \bar v \subset L/pL\). Every \(p\)-neighbour arises in this way, from exactly one isotropic line of \(L/pL\).

Tag 22NL

Problem 24.1.2. The quadratic space \(L/pL\) over \(\mathbb{F}_p\) is split, so that \[\lvert \mathcal{N}_p(L) \rvert = \frac{(p^k - 1)(p^{k-1} + 1)}{p - 1} .\] For \(L = E_8\) and \(p = 3, 5\) this is \(1120\) and \(19656\).

For \(p = 2\) the same count holds, if \(v\) is chosen with \(v \cdot v \equiv 0 \pmod 8\) [CL]; for \(E_8\) it is \(135\). Since \(E_8\) is the only even unimodular lattice of rank \(8\), all its neighbours are copies of \(E_8\). For \(n \ge 3\), Kneser proved that any two lattices of one genus are joined by a chain of \(p\)-neighbours [Kn].

Let \(\mathrm{Cl}\) be a class set, with \(\Gamma_L := \mathrm{O}(L)\). The neighbour operator on functions \(\varphi \colon \mathrm{Cl}\to \mathbb{C}\) is \((T_p\varphi)(L) := \sum_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} \varphi(M)\), and its matrix \(N_p(L, M) := \#\{M' \in \mathcal{N}_p(L) : M' \cong M\}\) is the Brandt matrix of the class set at \(p\). Let \(\langle \varphi, \psi \rangle := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}} \varphi(L)\overline{\psi(L)}/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\), as in Chapter 21.

Tag 22NM

Problem 24.1.3. \(N_p(L, M)/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert = N_p(M, L)/\lvert\Gamma_M\rvert\). Hence \(T_p\) is self-adjoint for \(\langle\,,\rangle\), and the constant function is an eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\lvert\mathcal{N}_p(L)\rvert\).

In rank \(16\) the class set is \(\{E_8 \perp E_8,\, D_{16}^+\}\), with \(\lvert\Gamma\rvert = 2 \cdot 696729600^2\) and \(2^{15}\cdot 16!\) [CoS]. By a machine enumeration of the \(32895\) two-neighbours of \(E_8 \perp E_8\), sorted by whether their roots span the lattice, \(14670\) of them are isometric to \(E_8 \perp E_8\) and \(18225\) to \(D_{16}^+\).

Tag 22NN

Problem 24.1.4. The Brandt matrix of the class set of rank \(16\) at \(p = 2\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} 14670 & 18225 \\ 12870 & 20025 \end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(32895\) and \(1800\).

The eigenvalue \(1800\) is \(15\,\tau(2) + (2^4 + 2^5 + \dots + 2^{10}) + 2^7\), where \(\tau(2) = -24\) is the coefficient of Ramanujan’s \(\Delta\). Chenevier and Lannes explain such identities by the Arthur parameters of the eigenvectors [CL]. In Section 24.2 we show that the theta series of degree one cannot see this eigenvector.

§24.2. Eichler’s commutation relation in degree one Tag 22NP

For \(L\) as in Section 24.1, let \(r_L(m) := \#\{x \in L : x \cdot x = 2m\}\), with \(r_L(m) := 0\) unless \(m \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\), and let \(\theta_L := \sum_m r_L(m)\, q^m\). It is a modular form of weight \(k\) for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) [SeA, Ch. VII], and \(T_{p^2}\) acts on its coefficients by \[a(m) \mapsto a(p^2m) + p^{k-1} a(m)\,[p \mid m] + p^{2k-2} a(m/p^2) .\] We compute \(\sum_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} r_M(m)\) by counting, for each vector, the neighbours that contain it. Let \(p\) be odd.

Tag 22NQ

Problem 24.2.1. Let \(x \in L \setminus pL\) with \(x \cdot x = 2m\). The number of \(M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)\) with \(x \in M\) is \((p^{2k-2} - 1)/(p - 1)\) if \(p \nmid m\), and \(1 + p(p^{k-1} - 1)(p^{k-2} + 1)/(p - 1)\) if \(p \mid m\).

Tag 22NR

Problem 24.2.2. The vectors of \(\bigcup_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} M \setminus L\) are the \(y/p\) with \(y \in L \setminus pL\) and \(y \cdot y \equiv 0 \pmod{2p^2}\), and each lies in exactly one neighbour.

Problems 24.2.1 and 24.2.2, together with \(x \in pL \subset M\) for every \(M\), give the following.

Tag 22NS

Theorem 24.2.1 (Eichler). For odd \(p\), \(\displaystyle \sum_{M \in \mathcal{N}_p(L)} \theta_M = T_{p^2}\theta_L + \frac{p^{2k-2} - p}{p - 1}\,\theta_L\).

Tag 22NT

Problem 24.2.3. Theorem 24.2.1 for \(L = E_8\) and \(p = 3\) reads \(1120 = 757 + 363\). The same identity of constant terms holds for \(p = 2\): \(135 = 73 + 62\).

Let \(\theta(\varphi) := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}} \varphi(L)\, \theta_L / \lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\) be the theta lift of a function on the class set. It is the analogue for these lattices of the lift of Chapter 23 to rank one in weight zero, with the vectors sorted by norm into a \(q\)-series instead of weighted by a Gaussian.

Tag 22NU

Problem 24.2.4. Let \(p\) be odd. Then \(\theta(T_p\varphi) = \bigl(T_{p^2} + \frac{p^{2k-2} - p}{p-1}\bigr)\theta(\varphi)\). If \(\theta(\varphi) \ne 0\) and \(T_p\varphi = \mu\varphi\), and \(\theta(\varphi)\) is an eigenform with \(T_p\theta(\varphi) = \lambda\theta(\varphi)\), then \(\mu = \lambda^2 - p^{k-1} + (p^{2k-2} - p)/(p-1)\).

So the theta lift intertwines \(T_p\) on the class set with a polynomial in \(T_p\) on modular forms; the explicit factor is the constant \((p^{2k-2}-p)/(p-1)\).

In rank \(16\), \(\theta_{E_8 \perp E_8} = \theta_{D_{16}^+}\) by Problem 22.1.2 of Chapter 22.

Tag 22NV

Problem 24.2.5. The eigenvector of Problem 24.1.4 with eigenvalue \(1800\) lies in the kernel of \(\theta\). For the constant function, the eigenvalue \(32895\) agrees with the formula of Problem 24.2.4 read at \(p = 2\), where we have not proved it: \(129^2 - 2^7 + (2^{14} - 2) = 32895\).

The two lattices are distinguished by their theta series of degree \(4\), and not before [CL]. A lift that kills an eigenvector says nothing about its eigenvalue; one needs a lift to a larger group, as in the Rallis inner product formula (Section 23.3).

§24.3. Hermitian lattices of rank five Tag 22NW

Let \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), let \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) be a prime, and let \(p = \pi\bar\pi\) in \(\mathcal{O}\). Let \(L\) be a unimodular Hermitian lattice of rank \(n\), as in Chapter 21, and let \(L^\sharp\) denote duals. Then \(L/\pi L\) is an \(n\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathcal{O}/\pi = \mathbb{F}_p\). For a hyperplane \(H \subset L/\pi L\) let \(L_H\) be its preimage in \(L\), and \[L'_H := L_H + \pi L_H^\sharp .\]

Tag 22NX

Problem 24.3.1. \(L'_H\) is unimodular and lies in the genus of \(L\), with \(L \cap L'_H = L_H\) of index \(p\) in both. Moreover \(L'_H \subset \bar\pi^{-1}L\), and \(L\) is obtained from \(L'_H\) by the same construction with \(\bar\pi\) in place of \(\pi\).

We call \(L'_H\) a \(\pi\)-neighbour of \(L\). There are \((p^n - 1)/(p-1)\) of them, and \(T_\pi\varphi(L) := \sum_H \varphi(L'_H)\) defines an operator on functions on \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\). By Problem 24.3.1 and the argument of Problem 24.1.3, \(T_{\bar\pi}\) is the adjoint of \(T_\pi\). Let \(r_L(m) := \#\{x \in L : h(x,x) = m\}\) and \(\theta_L := \sum_m r_L(m)\, q^m\), a modular form of weight \(n\) for \(\Gamma_0(4)\) with character \(\chi^n\), where \(\chi\) is the character modulo \(4\) [Iw, Ch. 10]. Since \(\chi(p) = 1\), \(T_p\) acts on such forms by \(a(m) \mapsto a(pm) + p^{n-1}a(m/p)\).

Tag 22NY

Problem 24.3.2. For \(x \in L\), the number of \(H\) with \(x \in L'_H\) is \((p^{n-1}-1)/(p-1)\) if \(x \notin \pi L\), and \((p^n - 1)/(p - 1)\) if \(x \in \pi L\). A vector \(x \notin L\) lies in some \(L'_H\) exactly when \(y := \bar\pi x \in L \setminus \bar\pi L\) and \(p \mid h(y,y)\), and then in exactly one.

Tag 22NZ

Problem 24.3.3. \(\displaystyle \sum_H \theta_{L'_H} = T_p\theta_L + \frac{p^{n-1} - p}{p - 1}\,\theta_L\). Hence, with \(\theta(\varphi) := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \varphi(L)\theta_L/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\), one has \(\theta(T_\pi\varphi) = \bigl(T_p + \frac{p^{n-1}-p}{p-1}\bigr)\theta(\varphi)\), and likewise for \(\bar\pi\).

Here the factor is linear in \(T_p\), because at a split prime the local group is \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Q}_p)\) and a \(\pi\)-neighbour is an edge of type one in the sense of Section 24.5.

Now take \(n = 5\), \(p = 5\) and \(\pi = 2 + \mathrm{i}\). By Problem 21.2.3, \(\mathrm{Cl}_5 = \{I_5, E \perp I_1\}\), with \(\lvert\Gamma\rvert = 122880\) and \(184320\), and \(r(1) = 20\) and \(4\).

Tag 22P2

Problem 24.3.4. The matrix of \(T_\pi\) on \(\mathrm{Cl}_5\) is \(\begin{pmatrix} 525 & 256 \\ 384 & 397 \end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(781\) and \(141\).

We checked this matrix by constructing all \(781\) neighbours of each lattice by machine. Let \(g\) be the cusp form called \(f\) in Chapter 22, \[g = \frac14\sum_{\alpha \in \mathcal{O}}\alpha^4 q^{\lvert\alpha\rvert^2} = \eta(\tau)^4\eta(2\tau)^2\eta(4\tau)^4 = q - 4q^2 + 16q^4 - 14q^5 - 64q^8 + 81q^9 + \cdots ;\] here \(\eta\) is Dedekind’s function. The form \(g\) spans the cusp forms of weight \(5\) for \(\Gamma_0(4)\) with character \(\chi\), and it has complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\). By Problem 22.3.7, \(\theta_{I_5} - \theta_{E \perp I_1} = 16\,g\).

Tag 22P3

Problem 24.3.5. The eigenvector of \(T_\pi\) with eigenvalue \(141\) is \(\varphi = (2, -3)\), and \(\theta(\varphi) = g/3840\).

Tag 22P4

Problem 24.3.6. \(a_5(g) = -14 = 2\operatorname{Re}\bigl((2+\mathrm{i})^4\bigr)\), and \(141 = -14 + 155\), as Problem 24.3.3 predicts.

Here the lift is injective, so the matrix of \(T_\pi\) is that of \(T_5 + 155\) on the span of \(\theta_{I_5}\) and \(\theta_{E \perp I_1}\). Neither theta series is an eigenform; the eigenforms are \(g\) and the lift of the constant function, with \(T_5\)-eigenvalues \(-14\) and \(626 = 781 - 155\).

§24.4. Brandt matrices Tag 22P5

The original setting of Eichler’s relation is a quaternion algebra, where the theta series of the norm forms of maximal orders give the modular forms of weight two and prime level [Ei73], [Pi], [Gr]. We follow Gross [Gr] in the smallest case with two classes.

Let \(B = (-1, -11)/\mathbb{Q}\), with \(\mathrm{i}^2 = -1\), \(j^2 = -11\) and \(k = \mathrm{i}j\); it is ramified exactly at \(11\) and \(\infty\). Let \(\mathcal{O}_1 := \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\mathrm{i}+ \mathbb{Z}\frac{1+j}{2} + \mathbb{Z}\frac{\mathrm{i}+ k}{2}\). There is one other class of maximal orders, represented by an order \(\mathcal{O}_2\) with \(\Theta_{\mathcal{O}_2} := \sum_{x \in \mathcal{O}_2} q^{N(x)} = 1 + 6q + 6q^3 + 24q^4 + 18q^5 + \cdots\). Let \(w_i := \lvert\mathcal{O}_i^\times\rvert/2\). Gross defines the Brandt matrices \(B(m)\), \(m \ge 1\), whose entries \(B_{ij}(m)\) count the elements of norm \(m\) in \(\mathrm{Hom}(I_j, I_i)\), divided by \(2w_j\), for left ideals \(I_i\) of a fixed maximal order with right orders \(\mathcal{O}_i\); he shows that \(B_{ii}(m) = r_{\mathcal{O}_i}(m)/2w_i\), that \(w_j B_{ij}(m) = w_i B_{ji}(m)\), that \(\sum_j B_{ij}(m) = \sigma(m)\) for \(11 \nmid m\), and that \(m \mapsto B(m)\) is the action of the Hecke operators \(T_m\) on \(M_2(\Gamma_0(11))\) [Gr]. For a prime \(\ell \ne 11\), \(B_{ij}(\ell)\) counts the left ideals \(J \subset I_i\) of the fixed order with \([I_i : J] = \ell^2\) and \(J \cong I_j\); there are \(\ell + 1\) such \(J\) in all, and they play the role of the \(\ell\)-neighbours of Section 24.1.

Tag 22P6

Problem 24.4.1. \(\mathcal{O}_1\) is an order, its norm form is \((a^2 + ac + 3c^2) + (b^2 + bd + 3d^2)\), and \(\mathcal{O}_1^\times = \{\pm1, \pm\mathrm{i}\}\). Thus \(\Theta_{\mathcal{O}_1} = \bigl(\sum_{a,c} q^{a^2+ac+3c^2}\bigr)^2\).

Eichler’s mass formula \(\sum_i 1/w_i = (11 - 1)/12\) then gives \(w_2 = 3\) [Gr].

Tag 22P7

Problem 24.4.2. \(B(2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(B(3) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(3, -2\) and \(4, -1\).

Tag 22P8

Problem 24.4.3. Let \(f := q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)^2(1 - q^{11n})^2 = q - 2q^2 - q^3 + 2q^4 + q^5 + \cdots\). Then \(\Theta_{\mathcal{O}_1} - \Theta_{\mathcal{O}_2} = -2f\), and \((1, -1)\,B(\ell) = a_\ell(f)\,(1, -1)\) for \(\ell = 2, 3\).

The eigenvalues \(-2\) and \(-1\) are the traces of Frobenius of the elliptic curve \(X_0(11)\) at \(2\) and \(3\).

§24.5. A split place Tag 22P9

In the chain of lifts of Adriazola’s reconstruction [Adr], the kernel of a lift at a split place is the indicator of \(xL \subset L'\) for a matrix \(x\), and the Hecke operators are sums over edges of lattices. The relation between them is a count of subspaces, of which Problems 24.3.2 and 24.3.3 are the case of rank one.

Let \(Q\) be a power of \(p\), and let \(W\) be the unramified extension of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\) with residue field \(\mathbb{F}_Q\). For lattices \(L'' \subset L\) in a \(W[1/p]\)-vector space, \((L'', L)\) is an edge of type \(i\) if \(pL \subset L'' \subset L\) and \(\dim L/L'' = i\). Let \(L \subset W[1/p]^N\) and \(L' \subset W[1/p]^{N+g}\) be lattices, let \(x\) be an \((N+g) \times N\) matrix with \(xL \subset L'\), and let \(\rho\) be the rank of \(x\) modulo \(p\) as a map \(L/pL \to L'/pL'\).

Tag 22PA

Problem 24.5.1. The number of edges \((L_1, L')\) of type \(i\) with \(xL \subset L_1\) is \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{N+g-\rho}{i}_{Q}\). The number of edges \((L, A_1)\) of type \(i\) with \(xA_1 \subset L'\) is \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{N-\rho}{i}_{Q}\).

Tag 22PB

Problem 24.5.2. As functions of \(x\) (with \(\mathbf 1_{L,L'}(x) := [xL \subset L']\)), \[\sum_{(L_1, L') \text{ of type } i} \mathbf 1_{L, L_1} = \sum_{l=0}^{i} Q^{(i-l)(g-l)} \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q} \sum_{(L, A_1) \text{ of type } i-l} \mathbf 1_{A_1, L'} .\] For \(N = 1\), \(i = 1\) and \(Q = p\) this is the first count of Problem 24.3.2.

Tag 22PC

Problem 24.5.3. For \(0 \le l \le g\), \(e_l(Q^{t/2}, Q^{t/2 - 1}, \dots, Q^{-t/2}) = Q^{-l(g-l)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q}\), where \(t = g - 1\), the arguments step by \(Q^{-1}\), and \(e_l\) is the elementary symmetric polynomial.

Summing Problem 24.5.2 against a kernel that depends on \(x\) only through \(\mathbf 1_{L,L'}(x)\) and on the lengths of the lattices through a power of \(p\), and moving the sum over edges at the source to the other variable as in Problem 24.1.3, gives a relation of the form \(E_{j+1}(X)\, K = K\, P(X)\, E_j(uX)\) between the generating series \(E(X) = \sum_i \mathcal E_i X^i\) of normalized Hecke operators at the target and at the source. Here \(u\) is a unit and \(P(X) = \prod_a (1 + aX)\) is scalar, with roots read off from Problem 24.5.3. This is the relation for one lift in [Adr].

Tag 22PD

Problem 24.5.4. Suppose that \(E_{j+1}(X)\,K_j = K_j\, P_{j+1}(X)\, E_j(X)\) modulo \(X^{r+1}\) for \(0 \le j < m\), with \(P_{j+1}(X) = \prod_{a \in x_{j+1}}(1 + aX)\), and where \(E_j(X) = \sum_i \mathcal E^{(j)}_i X^i\), and let \(Y\) be the union of the families \(x_1, \dots, x_m\) and \(\mathcal K := K_{m-1}\cdots K_0\). Then \(\mathcal K\, \mathcal E^{(0)}_r = \sum_{l=0}^r (-1)^l h_l(Y)\, \mathcal E^{(m)}_{r-l}\, \mathcal K\), where \(h_l\) is the complete symmetric polynomial.

So a Hecke operator at the source of a chain of lifts moves to the target as a polynomial in the Hecke operators there, with coefficients \(h_l(Y)\). Problem 24.2.4 is an instance with one lift. For divisibility along a chain, the valuations of these coefficients are what matter [Adr].

\(v/p\) has integral products with \(L_v\), and \((v/p) \cdot (v/p) \in 2\mathbb{Z}\). Compare discriminants: \([L : L_v] = p\). Conversely \(M \cap L = L_v\) for any \(v \in pM \setminus pL\). Use the Gaussian binomial theorem \[\prod_{s=0}^{g-1}(1 + Q^s z) = \sum_l Q^{l(l-1)/2}\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{g}{l}_{Q} z^l .\]

24.5.4

Work with power series in \(X\) modulo \(X^{r+1}\); the scalars commute with the operators, and \(\prod_{a \in Y}(1 + aX)^{-1} = \sum_l (-1)^l h_l(Y) X^l\).
