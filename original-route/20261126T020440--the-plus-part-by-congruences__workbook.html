Prerequisites.

Chapter 55, Section 55.1: the fields \(E=E(\psi,a,q,d)\), the plus part \(\mathrm{Sha}^+_\ell(E,e)\) and its length \(h^+_{\ell,e}(E)\). Chapter 30: \(W_e\), \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\) and Problem 30.2.7. Chapter 32, Section 32.5: the coefficient ring \(\mathcal{O}\), with ramification index \(e_\mathcal{O}\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\). Chapter 33: the length bound of Theorem 33.4.1 and its factor two (Section 33.5). Chapter 54: layers. Galois representations attached to modular forms, Bloch–Kato Selmer groups [BK], and Bertrand’s postulate [AZ, Ch. 2]. Throughout, \(\ell\) is an odd prime, \(N=\operatorname{lcm}(2^{a+2},N_\psi)qd\) is the modulus of \(E\), and \(v_\ell\) is normalized by \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\).

§56.1. Characters and cases

On the plus part no \(L\)-value is available (Problem 55.1.3). How can one bound it at all, uniformly in \(\ell\) and \(e\)? The answer in the formalization is a case analysis over \((\ell,e,\eta)\), with a different arithmetic argument in each case, and in the main case an integer built from automorphic forms in place of a Bernoulli number. First the reduction to characters. For a character \(\eta\) modulo \(N\) with values in \(\mathcal{O}\), let \(\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\) be the group of classes in \(\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)_\mathcal{O}\) that vanish also on the decomposition groups at the primes dividing \(N\). The character \(\eta\) is plus for \(e\) if \(\eta(-1)=(-1)^e\).

Lemma 56.1.1 (reduction to characters). If \(\ell\nmid[E:\mathbb{Q}]\) and \(e\ge1\), then \(e_\mathcal{O}\,h^+_{\ell,e}(E)\le\sum_\eta\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\), the sum over the characters \(\eta\) of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) that are plus for \(e\).

Status: proved in the Lean formalization; we have read the statement, not the proof. Without the extra local conditions it would follow from Problems 32.5.3 and 55.1.2.

Problem 56.1.1. \(\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\subseteq\mathrm{Sha}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)_\mathcal{O}\). Hence, if \(\ell\ge5\), \(e\ge2\) is even and \((\ell-1)\mid2e\), the summand of the trivial character vanishes.

Let \(o\) be the order of \(\psi\). Let \(p_K\) be the least prime \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) with \(p\ge7\) and \(p\nmid N_\psi\), let \(h_*\) be the class number of \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p_K})\), and let \(p_1\) be the least prime \(p\equiv1\) modulo the odd part of \(o\) with \(p\nmid2N_\psi p_K\). The set of bad primes of \(\psi\) is \[\mathcal{B}_\psi=\{3\}\cup\{\text{odd primes dividing }o\,N_\psi\,p_K\,h_*\,p_1\}.\] It depends only on \(\psi\). The role of \(p_K\) and \(h_*\) is presumably to supply the imaginary quadratic field of Section 56.2; we have not checked this, nor traced the role of \(p_1\).

Problem 56.1.2. For \(\psi=1\), \(\mathcal{B}_\psi=\{3,7\}\). For the quadratic character of conductor \(5\), \(\mathcal{B}_\psi=\{3,5,7\}\). For a character of order \(3\) and conductor \(7\), \(p_K=11\), \(h_*=1\), \(p_1=13\) and \(\mathcal{B}_\psi=\{3,7,11,13\}\).

Problem 56.1.3. Let \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\) and let \(\eta\) be a character of \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\). Then \(\ell\nmid[E:\mathbb{Q}]\), \(\ell\nmid o(\eta)\), and the component of \(\eta\) at \(\ell\) (its restriction to \((\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{v_\ell(N)})^\times\)) has order at most \(2\).

Let \(\chi_P(\eta)\) be the character obtained from \(\eta\) by removing its part of \(\ell\)-power order and then its component at \(\ell\). For \(0<e<t\) and \(\eta\) plus for \(e\), the formalization distinguishes:

  • \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\) and \((\ell-1)\nmid2e\);

  • \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\), \((\ell-1)\mid2e\) and \(\eta=1\);

  • \(e\ge2\), the order of \(\chi_P(\eta)\) is even, and \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) or \((\ell-1)\mid2e\);

  • \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\), \((\ell-1)\mid2e\), \(\eta\ne1\) and the order of \(\eta\) is odd;

  • \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\), \(e\ge2\) and the order of \(\chi_P(\eta)\) is odd;

  • \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) and \(e=1\).

Problem 56.1.4. Suppose \(2t+1<q\) and \(2t+1<d\). Then every triple \((\ell,e,\eta)\) as above lies in at least one of the cases I–V and Z. In Cases II and III with \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\), \(\ell\le2e+1<2t+1\).

Case Z vanishes by Problem 56.1.1. Cases II and III have \(\ell\) small, Case I has \(\ell\) arbitrary, and Cases II, IV and V with \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) involve finitely many \(\ell\).

§56.2. The congruence integer

In Case I the Bernoulli number of the minus part is replaced by an integer. Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field, \(w\) a prime of \(K\) above a prime \(p\ge7\) with \(p\nmid N\Delta_K\), \(Q=\lvert\mathcal{O}_K/w\rvert\), \(\ell\ne p\), \(\sigma\in G_K\) an arithmetic Frobenius at \(w\), and \(\Psi=\eta\chi_\ell^e\), a character of \(G_\mathbb{Q}\) that we also restrict to \(G_K\). Since \(\Psi\oplus1\oplus\Psi^{-1}\) is self-dual, \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma^{-1}\) have the same trace on it. For a root of unity \(z\) put \[t_z=Q^{-e}z+1+Q^ez^{-1}.\]

Problem 56.2.1. The trace of \(\sigma^{-1}\) on \(\Psi\oplus1\oplus\Psi^{-1}\) is \(t_z\) with \(z=\eta(\sigma)^{-1}\), and \(Q^et_z\) is an algebraic integer.

The representations that may be congruent to this Eisenstein point are three-dimensional representations \(\rho\) of \(G_K\) of two kinds, called admissible below: those attached to automorphic representations of a unitary group in three variables with weights \((e,0,-e)\) and bounded level, and those of the form \(1\oplus W\) with \(W\) attached to a newform of weight \(2e+1\). We have not transcribed the precise conditions.

Lemma 56.2.1 (one character). Let \(K\), \(\ell\), \(e\), \(N\) and \(\eta\) satisfy: \(e\ge1\), \(N\) even, \(\ell\nmid o(\eta)\), the component of \(\eta\) at \(\ell\) has order at most \(2\), \(\eta\) is plus for \(e\), \(\ell\nmid\Delta_Kh_K\), \((\Delta_K,N)=1\), \((\ell-1)\nmid2e\), and a further condition relating \(K\) and \(\eta\). Let \(\sigma\in G_K\) and \(t=\Psi(\sigma)+1+\Psi(\sigma)^{-1}\), and suppose \(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma)\ne t\) for every admissible \(\rho\). Then \(\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\) is finite, and there is a finite set \(X\) of pairwise non-isomorphic admissible \(\rho\) with \[\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\le2e_\mathcal{O}\sum_{\rho\in X}v_\ell\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma)-t\bigr).\]

Lemma 56.2.2 (the integer). For each \(K\) there are \(c\) and \(A\) with the following property. Let \(e,N\ge1\), \(p\), \(w\), \(Q\) be as above, and \((z_o)\) a finite family of roots of unity, stable under \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\). There is a nonzero integer \(M\) with \(\log\lvert M\rvert\le c\cdot\#(z_o)\cdot(N(e+1))^A\log p\) such that, for every odd \(\ell\ne p\) and all finite sets \(X_o\) of pairwise non-isomorphic admissible \(\rho\), one has \(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma^{-1})\ne t_{z_o}\) and \(v_\ell(M)\ge\sum_o\sum_{\rho\in X_o}v_\ell\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma^{-1})-t_{z_o}\bigr)\).

Status: both lemmas are proved in the Lean formalization; we have read their statements, not their proofs. The first is a bound for a Selmer group by trace differences at congruent points, of the kind modelled in Theorem 33.4.1, with the factor two of Section 33.5; its proof uses deformation rings, automorphy lifting and the input of Section 56.3. The second counts automorphic forms on a unitary group in three variables. Zhang describes both [Zh].

Here is why such an integer exists, in a model.

Problem 56.2.2. If the eigenvalues of \(\rho(\sigma^{-1})\) have absolute value \(1\) under an isomorphism \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\simeq\mathbb{C}\), then \(\operatorname{tr}\rho(\sigma^{-1})\ne t_z\).

Problem 56.2.3. Let \(a_1,\dots,a_D\) be algebraic numbers, permuted by \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), with \(Q^ea_s\) integral and \(\lvert a_s\rvert\le3\) in every complex embedding, and let \((z_o)\) be as in Lemma 56.2.2. Then \(M=\prod_o\prod_sQ^e(a_s-t_{z_o})\) is a nonzero integer, \(\log\lvert M\rvert\le D\cdot\#(z_o)\cdot(2e\log Q+1)\), and \(v_\ell(M)=\sum_{o,s}v_\ell(a_s-t_{z_o})\) for \(\ell\ne p\).

Problem 56.2.4. Fix an isomorphism \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\simeq\mathbb{C}\), and assume the hypotheses of Lemma 56.2.1, other than the trace condition, for every \(\eta\) in an orbit of characters under \(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\). Then Lemmas 56.2.1 and 56.2.2, applied to the family \((z_\eta)\) indexed by the \(\eta\) in this orbit, with \(z_\eta=\eta(\sigma)^{-1}\), give \(\sum_\eta\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\le2e_\mathcal{O}v_\ell(M)\) for \(\ell\ne p\).

Problem 56.2.5. Let \(p\ne p'\), \(M_p\), \(M_{p'}\) nonzero integers, and suppose that \(L_\ell\le2v_\ell(M_p)\) for odd \(\ell\ne p\) and \(L_\ell\le2v_\ell(M_{p'})\) for odd \(\ell\ne p'\). Then \(\sum_\ell L_\ell\log\ell\le2\log\lvert M_p\rvert+2\log\lvert M_{p'}\rvert\), the sum over odd \(\ell\).

Problem 56.2.6. The two least primes \(p<p'\) with \(p\ge7\) and \(p,p'\nmid30N\Delta_K\) satisfy \(\log p'\le C(1+\log(N\lvert\Delta_K\rvert))\), with \(C\) absolute.

§56.3. Kato’s vanishing

At the points \(1\oplus W\) the deformation argument must not see Selmer classes of \(W\) itself. Let \(\theta\) be the quadratic character of \(K\).

Problem 56.3.1. Let \(V\) be a two-dimensional representation with \(\det V=\theta\). Then \(\operatorname{Hom}(1,V)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}(V,1)\cong V\oplus(V\otimes\theta)\).

Problem 56.3.2. If \(\operatorname{tr}W\equiv\theta\Psi+\Psi^{-1}\) modulo the maximal ideal, then on \(G_K\) the trace of \(1\oplus W\) is congruent to \(t=\Psi+1+\Psi^{-1}\): the point \(1\oplus W\) is congruent to the Eisenstein point.

Lemma 56.3.1 (Kato). Let \(W\) be a two-dimensional \(\ell\)-adic representation of \(G_\mathbb{Q}\) that is a Tate twist of the representation of a newform of weight \(2e+1\), with \(\det W=\theta\), satisfying further conditions we do not transcribe. Then the Bloch–Kato Selmer groups \(H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},W)\) and \(H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},W\otimes\theta)\) vanish.

Status: Kato proved the finiteness of the Selmer groups of modular forms at the critical twists other than the central one [Ka, Thm. 14.2], which gives this vanishing at the relevant twists [Zh]. In the Lean formalization Lemma 56.3.1 is an input of the proof of Lemma 56.2.1; we have not read how it is established there.

Problem 56.3.1 says that the two components of an adjoint class at \(1\oplus W\) are classes for \(W\) and \(W\otimes\theta\), so Lemma 56.3.1 removes them.

§56.4. The plus part

Problem 56.4.1. Suppose each pair \((\ell,\eta)\) in Cases II and III with \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\) contributes \(\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\log\ell\le(\ell Ne)^C\), with \(C\ge1\); here \(e_\mathcal{O}=1\), since \(\ell\nmid[E:\mathbb{Q}]\) by Problem 56.1.3. Then the sum of these contributions over \(\ell\), \(\eta\) and \(1\le e<t\) is at most \((Nt)^{5C+4}\).

Problem 56.4.2. Suppose \(2t+1<q\) and \(2t+1<d\). Assume Lemma 56.1.1, with \(\mathcal{O}\) depending on \(\ell\), the bound of Problem 56.4.1, and for each \(e<t\) the bound \[\sum_\ell\log\ell\sum_\eta e_\mathcal{O}^{-1}\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\le(Nt)^C,\] the sum over the \(\ell\) in Case I. Then \[\sum_{\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi}\log\ell\sum_{1\le e<t}h^+_{\ell,e}(E)\le2(Nt)^{5C+4}.\]

In the formalization the bound for Case I comes from Problems 56.2.4–56.2.6; the bound \((\ell Ne)^C\) in Cases II and III from a statement about automorphic forms on the group \(\mathrm{GSp}_4\) of symplectic similitudes in four variables, whose content we have not traced; and Case V from a lifting argument for unitary groups that uses the nonvanishing of a Bernoulli number modulo \(\ell\); Zhang describes unitary groups over \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) here [Zh]. At \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\), Case IV and part of Case V are reduced to the fixed layers of Chapter 54. The hypothesis used there is a bound \(B\) on layers: for every \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\) and the layer \(L\) of degree \(\ell^{j(\ell)}\) over \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell N_\psi})\), \[\sum_{1\le e<t}h_{\ell,e}(L)\le B.\] The result is the following.

Theorem 56.4.1 (the plus part). Let \(\psi\) be primitive and \(j\) a function on primes. There are \(C\) and primes \(r_1,r_2\) such that the following holds. Let \(E=E(\psi,a,q,d)\) with \(q,d>\max(r_1,r_2,2t+1,N_\psi,o,p_K,h_*,p_1)\), \(t\ge2\), and suppose the bound \(B\) on layers holds. Then \[\sum_\ell\log\ell\sum_{1\le e<t}h^+_{\ell,e}(E)\le(Nt)^C+CB,\] the sum over odd primes \(\ell\).

Status: proved in the Lean formalization. For \(\ell\notin\mathcal{B}_\psi\) it is Problem 56.4.2 with the case bounds above. We have not read the treatment of \(\ell\in\mathcal{B}_\psi\), which uses a version of Lemma 56.1.1 with a loss term when \(\ell\mid[E:\mathbb{Q}]\). With \(B=\exp(x^{4\varepsilon/5})\) on the windows of the next chapter, Theorem 56.4.1 is the bound for \(P_+\) in part (I) of Theorem 52.4.2.

Problem 56.4.3. If \(e\) is even and \(\ell\nmid[E:\mathbb{Q}]\), then \(h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q})\le h^+_{\ell,e}(E)\).

What remains.

The theorem is a uniform weakening of Vandiver’s conjecture for the fields \(E\). For \(\psi=1\), \(a=2\), and \(q\), \(d\) the least admissible primes above \(\max(r_1,r_2,2t+1,7)\), which are of size polynomial in \(t\) by the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions, Problems 56.4.2 and 56.4.3 bound \(\sum_{\ell\ne3,7}\log\ell\sum_{e<t,\ e\text{ even}}h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q})\) by a polynomial in \(t\): the kind of bound asked for at the end of Chapter 30. Its proof is the part of the argument that we have been able to follow least: Lemmas 56.1.1, 56.2.1, 56.2.2 and 56.3.1, and the cases with \(\mathrm{GSp}_4\), are proved in the formalization by long arguments that we have not read.

In the Lean formalization 1. In [Lean] Lemma 56.1.1 is HV.redPrime; the group \(\mathrm{Sha}^{(N)}_\ell(\mathbb{Q},e;\eta)\) has length Vlen. The set \(\mathcal{B}_\psi\) is Bchi, with pK, hStar, pOne; the cases are Host.CaseI, …, Host.CaseV and CaseZ, with \(\chi_P(\eta)\) the character chiP. Lemma 56.2.1 is OneCharU_s, with hypotheses GD and admissible representations Adm3U_s or Adm2A; Lemma 56.2.2 is NumberU3U_s, with \(t_z\) the function eis3; Problem 56.2.4 is propGK_of_numberU3U_of_oneCharU, and Problem 56.2.5 is the passage from PropGK to CorGK. Lemma 56.3.1 is KatoTwoW. The case bounds are CountI, CountII, CountIII, CountIV, CountV, CountZ, the second and third from SymplecticFourImageDense; the bound on layers is LayerLe, and Theorem 56.4.1 is PointHV, from sumIn and sumOut.
