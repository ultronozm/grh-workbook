Prerequisites.

Chapter 14, Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime: formal groups, their logarithms and the invariant derivation \(D\) (Section 14.2), and the statement of Lubin–Tate theory with the model logarithm (Section 14.3, Problem 14.3.7). Chapter 16, The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime: the function \(E_p\) and Theorem 16.4.1. Chapter 17, The isotropic plane, Theorem 17.3.1 and Section 17.4; Chapter 19 is mentioned once, for orientation. Chapter 2, Problem 2.1.1. The ring \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}\) of integers of the unramified quadratic extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\), its Teichmüller roots of unity, and its Frobenius \(\sigma\), which satisfies \(\sigma(a) \equiv a^p \pmod p\). Legendre’s formula \(v_p(n!) = (n - s_p(n))/(p-1)\), where \(s_p(n)\) is the sum of the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\).

Chapter 17 found the gain at the odd levels only for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), because it needed a global element of the CM order acting by a non-unit on one eigenline. The argument needs the gain at every inert prime. What structure, visible at \(p\) alone, forces a theta kernel to be divisible by about \(p^{\alpha p/(p^2-1)}\), that is by \(p^{\sum_{i \text{ odd}} \alpha/p^i}\), after \(\alpha\) derivatives? In the argument a theta kernel at an inert prime has the form \(\exp(-\beta(x,y))\,U(x,y)\) with \(U\) integral, where \(x\) and \(y\) are parameters of a formal group of height two and \(\beta\) is a pairing, biadditive and semilinear for Frobenius up to series with integral exponential. This chapter classifies such pairings and derives the divisibility from the normal form, in the simplest setting: one variable \(x\) and one partner variable \(y\).

§18.1. The formal module of height two Tag 22H7

Throughout, \(p\) is an odd prime, \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_{p^2}\), \(K := \mathbb{Q}_{p^2}\), \(\sigma\) is the Frobenius of \(K\), and \(v\) is the valuation with \(v(p) = 1\). A power series over \(K\), or over a finite extension of \(K\), is integral if all its coefficients have valuation \(\ge 0\). Let \[\operatorname{lg}(X) := \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{X^{p^{2j}}}{p^j}, \qquad \operatorname{lg}^*(X) := \frac1p \operatorname{lg}(X^p) = \sum_{j \ge 0} \frac{X^{p^{2j+1}}}{p^{j+1}} .\] In the notation of Chapter 14, \(\operatorname{lg}= \ell_p\) is the model logarithm for the uniformizer \(p\) of \(\mathcal{O}\), with \(q = p^2\).

We use Hazewinkel’s functional-equation lemma [Ha], [Ha79] in the following case. Let \(f(X) \in K[[X]]\) satisfy \(f(X) - p^{-1} f(X^{p^2}) \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) and \(f(X) \equiv X\) modulo degree \(2\). Then: \(f^{-1}(f(X) + f(Y))\) has coefficients in \(\mathcal{O}\); if \(g(X) \in X K[[X]]\) also satisfies \(g(X) - p^{-1} g(X^{p^2}) \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\), then \(f^{-1}(g(X)) \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\); and for \(\alpha, \beta \in X\mathcal{O}[[X]]\) and \(r \ge 1\), \(\alpha \equiv \beta \pmod{p^r}\) if and only if \(f(\alpha) \equiv f(\beta) \pmod{p^r}\).

Let \(F(X,Y) := \operatorname{lg}^{-1}(\operatorname{lg}X + \operatorname{lg}Y)\) and \([\alpha](X) := \operatorname{lg}^{-1}(\alpha \operatorname{lg}X)\) for \(\alpha \in \mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22H8

Problem 18.1.1. \(F \in \mathbb{Z}_p[[X,Y]]\) and \([\alpha] \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\), so \(F\) is a formal \(\mathcal{O}\)-module. Modulo degree \(p^2 + 1\), \[F(X,Y) \equiv X + Y - \frac1p \sum_{0 < k < p^2} \binom{p^2}{k} X^k Y^{p^2-k} .\] For \(\zeta \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\zeta^{p^2-1} = 1\), \([\zeta](X) = \zeta X\) and \(F(\zeta X, \zeta Y) = \zeta F(X,Y)\).

Tag 22H9

Problem 18.1.2. \([p](X) \equiv X^{p^2} \pmod p\). So \(F\) has height two.

Tag 22HA

Problem 18.1.3. Let \(u\) be a root of unity of order \(2(p^2-1)\), so that \(u^{p^2-1} = -1\) and \(u \in \mathbb{Z}_{p^4}\). Then \(\ell_{-p}(uX) = u \operatorname{lg}(X)\). Hence for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) the formal group of \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) is isomorphic to \(F\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^4}\).

For a series \(f\) over \(K\) let \(f^\sigma\) be the series obtained by applying \(\sigma\) to the coefficients, and \(f^\phi(X) := f^\sigma(X^p)\), where \(X^p\) raises every variable to the power \(p\).

Tag 22HB

Problem 18.1.4. For \(f\) integral over \(\mathcal{O}\) in any number of variables, \(f^\phi \equiv f^p \pmod p\). In particular \(F(X^p, Y^p) \equiv F(X,Y)^p \pmod p\).

§18.2. Pairings and their normal form Tag 22HC

For series \(f\), \(g\) over \(K\) write \(f \simeq g\) if \(f - g\) has no constant term and \(\exp(f - g)\) is integral. The basic tool is Dwork’s lemma [Ko], in the following form [Adr, Lemma 9.1(i)], which has the same proof: for a series \(\Psi\) over \(K\) in finitely many variables without constant term, \[\exp\Psi \text{ is integral} \iff \Psi^\phi - p\Psi \in p\,\mathcal{O}[[X]] .\] For a series \(H\) in two variables let \(H^\mathrm{sw}(X,Y) := H(Y,X)\) and \[(d_F H)(X_0, X_1, X_2) := H\bigl(F(X_0,X_1), X_2\bigr) - H(X_0, X_2) - H(X_1, X_2),\] and for a series \(f\) in one variable let \(\partial_F f := f(F(X_0,X_1)) - f(X_0) - f(X_1)\). A pairing is a series \(\beta(X,Y)\) over \(K\) such that: (i) every monomial of \(\beta\) contains both variables; (ii) \(d_F\beta \simeq 0\) and \(d_F\beta^\mathrm{sw}\simeq 0\); (iii) \(\beta(\zeta X, Y) \simeq \beta(X, \zeta^p Y)\) for every \(\zeta \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\zeta^{p^2-1} = 1\). Condition (ii) says that \(\exp\beta\) is biadditive up to integral cocycles. Condition (iii) is semilinearity for Frobenius, since \(\zeta^p = \sigma(\zeta)\).

Tag 22HD

Problem 18.2.1. The relation \(\simeq\) is an equivalence relation, stable under sums and under substitution of integral series without constant term for the variables. If \(f \simeq g\), then \(d_F f \simeq d_F g\).

Tag 22HE

Problem 18.2.2. (i) \(\beta(\zeta X, Y) = \beta(X, \zeta^p Y)\) for all \(\zeta\) as in (iii) if and only if every monomial \(X^m Y^n\) of \(\beta\) has \(m \equiv pn \pmod{p^2-1}\). (ii) Let \(\beta\) be a pairing, \(N := p^2 - 1\), \(\zeta\) a primitive \(N\)th root of unity, and \(G := N^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \beta(\zeta^k X, \zeta^{-pk} Y)\). Then \(G\) is a pairing, \(G \simeq \beta\), and \(G\) satisfies the condition of (i).

For \(c, c' \in K\) let \[A(X,Y) := \sum_{0 \le i \le j} \frac{X^{p^{2i}}\, Y^{p^{2j+1}}}{p^{\,i+j+1}}, \qquad Q_{c,c'}(X,Y) := c\,A(X,Y) - c'\,A(Y,X) .\] In every monomial of \(A\) the exponent of \(Y\) is the exponent of \(X\) times an odd power of \(p\).

Tag 22HF

Problem 18.2.3. For all \(c, c'\), \(Q_{c,c'}(\zeta X, Y) = Q_{c,c'}(X, \zeta^p Y)\) for every \(\zeta\) as in (iii). No monomial of \(\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y)\) satisfies the congruence of Problem 18.2.2(i).

Tag 22HG

Problem 18.2.4. \(A(X^p, Y^p) = p\bigl(\operatorname{lg}^*(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y) - A(Y,X)\bigr)\). Hence \[Q_{c',c}(X^p, Y^p) - p\,Q_{c,c'}(X,Y) = p\bigl(c'\operatorname{lg}^*(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y) - c\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}^*(Y)\bigr).\]

Tag 22HH

Problem 18.2.5. If \(c \in K\) and \(Q_{c,\sigma c} \simeq 0\), then \(c = 0\).

Tag 22HJ

Problem 18.2.6. If \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), \(\beta\) is a pairing and \(\beta \simeq Q_{c,\sigma c}\), then \(p \cdot [X Y^p]\,\beta \in \mathcal{O}\) and \(p \cdot [XY^p]\,\beta \equiv c \pmod p\).

Is \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) itself a pairing? For \(c \ne 0\) it is not integral, and it is not biadditive on the nose. Problems 18.2.7–18.2.10 show that it is biadditive up to \(\simeq\).

Tag 22HK

Problem 18.2.7. Let \(\Phi\) be a series over \(K\) whose partial derivative \(\partial\Phi/\partial X_e\) is integral, let \(\varphi\) be a tuple of integral series without constant terms, and let \(V\) be an integral series without constant term. Then \(\Phi(\varphi + pV\mathbf{e}_e) - \Phi(\varphi) \in p\,\mathcal{O}[[X]]\), where \(\mathbf{e}_e\) is the unit vector of the variable \(X_e\).

Tag 22HL

Problem 18.2.8. \(\partial_F \operatorname{lg}= 0\), and \(\partial_F \operatorname{lg}^*\) is integral.

Tag 22HM

Problem 18.2.9. For \(c, c' \in \mathcal{O}\), the series \(\partial_X\bigl(Q_{c,c'}(X,Y) - c\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}^*(Y)\bigr)\) is integral.

Tag 22HN

Problem 18.2.10. For \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) is a pairing.

Conversely, every pairing has this form.

Tag 22HP

Theorem 18.2.1 ([Adr, Thm. 9.3]). For every pairing \(\beta\) there is a unique \(c \in \mathcal{O}\) with \(\beta \simeq Q_{c,\sigma c}\).

Problems 18.2.5 and 18.2.10 give the uniqueness and show that every \(Q_{c,\sigma c}\) occurs. For the existence, Adriazola replaces \(\beta\) by the average \(G\) of Problem 18.2.2, finds \(c\) from the coefficient of \(X\) in \(G\), as in Problem 18.2.6, and applies Dwork’s lemma to \(G - Q_{c,\sigma c}\), using Problem 18.2.4; we do not reproduce the argument. The exactly bilinear pairing \(\operatorname{lg}(X)\operatorname{lg}(Y)\) is excluded by semilinearity (Problem 18.2.3). So, up to \(\simeq\), the denominators of a pairing sit only on the monomials \(X^{p^{2i}} Y^{p^{2j+1}}\) and \(Y^{p^{2i}} X^{p^{2j+1}}\), at odd shifts.

§18.3. Odd shifts and the credit Tag 22HQ

How much do these denominators cost after \(\alpha\) derivatives? Let \(\varpi_p\) be a root of \(\varpi_p^{p^2-1} = p\), and let \(\mathcal{O}' := \mathcal{O}[\varpi_p]\), so that \(v(\varpi_p) = \rho\) with \[\rho := \frac{1}{p^2-1}, \qquad \gamma_p := \frac{p}{p^2-1} .\]

Tag 22HR

Problem 18.3.1. \(\gamma_p = \sum_{e \ge 0} p^{-(2e+1)}\) and \(\gamma_p + \rho = 1/(p-1)\). If \(n = \sum_k d_k p^k\) in base \(p\), then \[\gamma_p n - E_p(n) = \gamma_p \sum_{k \text{ even}} d_k + \rho \sum_{k \text{ odd}} d_k, \qquad v(n!) - \rho n = \gamma_p n - \frac{s_p(n)}{p-1} .\] Hence \(v(n!) - \rho n \le E_p(n) \le \gamma_p n\).

So a derivative of order \(n\) in a formal parameter gains \(v(n!)\), and after the rescaling \(x \mapsto \varpi_p x\) the gain is \(\gamma_p n\) up to the digits of \(n\); for the formal group of Chapter 16, a derivative of order \(n\) in the logarithm gains \(E_p(n)\) on integral series (Theorem 16.4.1). Both are the credit \(\gamma_p n = \sum_{i \text{ odd}} n/p^i\) up to digits.

Tag 22HS

Problem 18.3.2. In \(Q_{c,c'}(\varpi_p X, \varpi_p Y)\) the coefficient of \(X^{p^{2i}} Y^{p^{2j+1}}\) (\(i \le j\)) has valuation \(v(c) + (p^{2i} + p^{2j+1})\rho - (i+j+1)\). For \(i = j = 0\) this is \(v(c) + \frac1{p-1} - 1\); otherwise it is at least \(v(c) + \frac{1}{p-1} + p - 2\). The same holds for the coefficient of \(Y^{p^{2i}} X^{p^{2j+1}}\), with \(c'\) in place of \(c\).

Tag 22HT

Problem 18.3.3. If every coefficient of a series \(\Psi\) without constant term has valuation \(\ge 1/(p-1)\), then \(\exp\Psi\) is integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\). For every series \(h\) over \(\mathcal{O}'\) without constant term, \(\exp(\varpi_p^{p+1} h/p) = \sum_{k \ge 0} (h/p)^k\, g_k\) with \(g_k \in \mathcal{O}'\).

For a series \(h\) without constant term, let \(\mathcal{C}(h)\) be the set of series \(\sum_{k \ge 0} p^{-k} h^k g_k\) with \(g_k\) integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\), the sum being finite in each degree. It is stable under multiplication by integral series. Theorem 18.2.1 and Problems 18.3.2–18.3.3 prove the following.

Tag 22HU

Theorem 18.3.1. Let \(\beta\) be a pairing with \(\beta \simeq Q_{c,\sigma c}\), let \(U \in \mathcal{O}[[x,y]]\), and let \(\Theta := \exp(-\beta(x,y))\, U(x,y)\). Then \[\Theta(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y) \in \mathcal{C}(h), \qquad h := \sigma(c)\, x^p y - c\, x y^p .\]

§18.4. Two local estimates Tag 22HV

Fix \(\Theta\), \(\beta\), \(c\), \(h\) as in Theorem 18.3.1. We bound the mixed derivatives at \(0\) of \(\Theta\), of order \(a\) in \(x\) and \(w\) in \(y\), in two ways: in the formal parameters, with \(\partial_x^a \partial_y^w\), and in the logarithm coordinates, with \(D_x^a D_y^w\), where \(D_x := \operatorname{lg}'(x)^{-1}\partial_x\) is the invariant derivation of Chapter 14. Thus \((D_x^a D_y^w \Theta)(0) = a!\, w!\, [z^a t^w]\, \Theta(\operatorname{lg}^{-1} z, \operatorname{lg}^{-1} t)\). We call \(x\) the top and \(y\) its partner.

Tag 22HW

Problem 18.4.1. If \(G(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y) \in \mathcal{C}(h)\), then \[v\bigl((\partial_x^a\partial_y^w G)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w) - \min\Bigl(w,\ \Bigl\lfloor \frac ap \Bigr\rfloor + \Bigl\lfloor \frac wp \Bigr\rfloor\Bigr).\]

In the formal parameters the multiplication by \(p\) on the partner, \(y \mapsto [p](y)\), is not linear. In the logarithm coordinates it is \(t \mapsto pt\), and its effect on \(D_y^w\) is the factor \(p^w\).

Tag 22HX

Problem 18.4.2. \(\beta(x, [p]y) \simeq p\,\beta(x,y)\), and \(\exp\bigl(-p\,Q_{c,\sigma c}(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y)\bigr)\) is integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\).

Tag 22HY

Problem 18.4.3. If \(G(\varpi_p x, \varpi_p y)\) is integral over \(\mathcal{O}'\), then \(v\bigl((D_x^a D_y^w G)(0)\bigr) \ge v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w)\).

Applying Problem 18.4.1 to \(\Theta\), and Problem 18.4.3 to \(\Theta(x, [p]y)\), which is allowed by Problem 18.4.2, gives the following.

Tag 22HZ

Theorem 18.4.1. For all \(a, w \ge 0\): \[\begin{align*} \text{(A)}\quad & v\bigl((\partial_x^a\partial_y^w \Theta)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w) - \min\bigl(w,\ \lfloor a/p \rfloor + \lfloor w/p \rfloor\bigr), \\ \text{(B)}\quad & v\bigl(p^w (D_x^a D_y^w \Theta)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ v(a!) + v(w!) - \rho(a+w) . \end{align*}\]

By Problem 18.3.1, both right sides are at least \(\gamma_p a - w - (s_p(a) + s_p(w))/(p-1)\): the top earns the credit \(\gamma_p a = \sum_{i \text{ odd}} a/p^i\), and the partner pays at most one factor of \(p\) per derivative. In (B) the payment is the explicit factor \(p^w\), the effect of \([p]\) on the partner; in the argument this factor becomes a Hecke operator, whose elementary divisors are the loss computed in Chapter 19. The bounds use only the formal \(\mathcal{O}\)-module \(F\) and the semilinearity (iii) for the Teichmüller roots of unity, as in hypothesis (iii) of [Adr, Thm. 9.3], not any global element of a CM order.

How are the \(w\) factors of \(p\) spent? For \(\Theta = \exp(-Q_{c,\sigma c})\) and \(w < p\) they are spent once at each odd level.

Tag 22J2

Problem 18.4.4. Let \(c \in \mathcal{O}\), \(1 \le w < p\), \(e \ge 0\), and \(a := p^{2e+1} w\). Then \[\bigl(\partial_x^a \partial_y^w \exp(-Q_{c,\sigma c}(x,y))\bigr)(0) = \frac{a!\, \sigma(c)^w}{p^{(e+1)w}} ,\] of valuation \(v(a!) - (e+1)w\) when \(c\) is a unit. (For \(p = 3\), \(w = 1\), \(e = 1\): \(27!\,\sigma(c)/9\), of valuation \(11\).)

So the term \(x^{p^{2e+1}} y / p^{e+1}\) of \(A(y,x)\) charges \(w\) at each of the levels \(1, 3, \dotsc, 2e+1\); this is the charge of \(w\) at each odd level in the bound \(\sum_{i \text{ odd}} (\lfloor a/p^i \rfloor - w)_+\) of Theorem 17.3.1. The odd powers \(p^i\) are exactly those at which \(1 - \chi_d(p^i) = 2\) for \(p\) inert (Problem 2.1.1(ii)), and the credit \(\gamma_p\alpha\) at a top of weight \(\alpha\) is matched against them in [Adr].

In the argument, \(x\) and \(y\) are the parameters along a hyperbolic pair \(f, f'\) of the hermitian form at \(p\), as in Section 17.4, transported to \(F\) by the Lubin–Tate isomorphism [Adr, Prop. 8.11], as in Problem 18.1.3. For the CM curve used there and every prime \(p \ge 5\) inert in its CM field, Adriazola shows that the coboundary of the logarithm of the reduced theta function is a pairing [Adr, Thm. 8.12], that the theta kernels are \(c_0 \exp(-\sum \beta(x,y))\, U\) with \(U\) integral [Adr, Thm. 8.21], and, with many tops and partners and with minors in place of single derivatives, proves lower bounds of the kind of Theorem 18.4.1 [Adr, Thm. 9.6]; its part (iii), with an explicit power of \(p\) attached to the partners, corresponds to (B). That the constant \(c_0\) is integral is assumed there: in [Adr] this is [Adr, Hyp. 11.42, (N3)]; see the ledger in Section 58.7 of Chapter 58. The Lean formalization [Lean] does not assume it: it proves an integral kernel law of this shape for the kernels it uses, by a different route, in which the exponent is a torus average of a coboundary of the logarithm of the sigma function of one CM curve, and the integrality comes from a factorization of the fourth tensor power of the kernel into sigma functions.
