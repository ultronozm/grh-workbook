Prerequisites.

Weierstrass functions and theta functions of one variable, and \(\Delta(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau) = (2\pi)^{12}q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1 - q^n)^{24}\), \(q = e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau}\) [SeA]. Modular forms for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\), \(q\)-expansions and the valence formula [SeA, Ch. VII]. From Chapter 12: Sections 12.1, 12.2 and 12.3. From Chapter 6, The slope method: Section 6.3, and the definition of \(\mu_{\max}\) in Section 6.4. From Chapter 26: Theorem 26.2.1, Problems 26.1.5, 26.1.7 and 26.2.2, and Section 26.3.

In Chapter 26 the leading term of a covolume was an arithmetic self-intersection. For theta functions on an elliptic curve it vanishes, and a period takes its place. Where does the period come from, and how does it enter lattices of modular forms and of forms on a definite group?

§28.1. Theta functions on an elliptic curve Tag 22RT

Let \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}\) be a lattice, with \(\sigma\), \(\wp\) and the quasi-period map \(\eta\) as in Section 12.1. Let \(A(\Lambda)\) be the area of \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\), and \(\Delta(\Lambda) = g_2^3 - 27g_3^2\).

Tag 22RU

Problem 28.1.1. There is a unique \(s_2 \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(\eta(\lambda) = s_2\lambda + \pi\bar\lambda/A(\Lambda)\) for all \(\lambda \in \Lambda\).

Tag 22RV

Problem 28.1.2. The function \[F(z) := \lvert \sigma(z) \rvert \exp\Bigl( -\tfrac12 \operatorname{Re}(s_2 z^2) - \frac{\pi \lvert z \rvert^2}{2A(\Lambda)} \Bigr)\] is \(\Lambda\)-periodic, and \(F(z)/\lvert z \rvert \to 1\) as \(z \to 0\).

Tag 22RW

Problem 28.1.3. \(F(-z) = F(z)\) and \(F(2z) = \lvert\wp'(z)\rvert\, F(z)^4\).

Let \(E = \mathbb{C}/\Lambda\), and let \(\omega_E\) be its cotangent space at the origin. Since the fiber of \(\mathcal{O}([0])\) at the origin is the tangent space, \(\mathcal{O}([0]) \otimes \omega_E\) is trivial there. By Problem 28.1.2 it has a metric, trivial at the origin and with translation-invariant curvature, for which the canonical section (pulled back to \(\sigma\) on \(\mathbb{C}\)) tensored with \(dz\) has norm \(F\); this is the cubist metric of Moret-Bailly [MB1].

For a nonzero differential \(\omega = c\,dz \in \omega_E\), let \(\Lambda_\omega = c\Lambda\) be its period lattice, \(x_\omega := c^{-2}\wp\) and \(y_\omega := c^{-3}\wp'/2\), so that \(dx_\omega/2y_\omega = \omega\) and the Weierstrass equation of \((x_\omega, y_\omega)\) has discriminant \(\Delta(\Lambda_\omega)\). Let \(f_0 := 1\), \(f_2 := x_\omega\), \(f_3 := y_\omega\), \(f_4 := x_\omega^2\), and so on, with \(f_j\) of pole order \(j\); for \(k \ge 1\), \(f_0, f_2, \dots, f_k\) span the elliptic functions with poles of order at most \(k\) at \(\Lambda\) only. Put \[\lVert f\,\omega^{k} \rVert^2 := \frac{\lvert c \rvert^{2k}}{A(\Lambda)} \int_{\mathbb{C}/\Lambda} \lvert f \rvert^2 F^{2k} \, dA ,\] the \(L^2\) norm of a section of \((\mathcal{O}([0]) \otimes \omega_E)^{\otimes k}\), and let \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(\omega)\) be the Gram determinant of \(f_0\omega^k, f_2\omega^k, \dots, f_k\omega^k\).

For the next problem let \(\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\), let \(\vartheta\) be as in Section 12.2, and for \(0 \le a < k\) let \(\vartheta_{k,a}(z) := \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{\pi\mathrm{i}k\tau(n + a/k)^2 + 2\pi\mathrm{i}k z(n + a/k)}\).

Tag 22RX

Problem 28.1.4. The functions \(\vartheta_{k,a}\), \(0 \le a < k\), are orthogonal for the weight \(e^{-2\pi k (\operatorname{Im} z)^2/\operatorname{Im}\tau}\) on \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) with the measure \(dA/\operatorname{Im}\tau\), and each has norm squared \((2k\operatorname{Im}\tau)^{-1/2}\).

Jacobi’s derivative formula reads \(\lvert \vartheta'(\tfrac{1+\tau}{2}) \rvert = e^{\pi\operatorname{Im}\tau/4} (2\pi)^{-1/2} \lvert \Delta(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau) \rvert^{1/8}\) [MuT, Ch. I, §§13–14].

Tag 22RY

Problem 28.1.5. \(\mathrm{Gr}_1(\omega) = \lVert \omega \rVert^2 = \sqrt2\,\pi\, A(\Lambda_\omega)^{-1/2} \lvert \Delta(\Lambda_\omega) \rvert^{-1/4}\).

Tag 22RZ

Theorem 28.1.1. For every nonzero differential \(\omega\) on \(E\) and every \(k \ge 1\), \[-\tfrac12 \log \mathrm{Gr}_k(\omega) = \frac{k}{4}\log A(\Lambda_\omega) + \frac{k^2 + 2}{24} \log\lvert \Delta(\Lambda_\omega) \rvert + \frac{k}{4}\log\frac{k}{2\pi^2} .\]

For \(k = 1\) this is Problem 28.1.5; for general \(k\) it follows from Moret-Bailly’s key formula [MB1, Ch. VIII], [MB2], in the metrized form of Bost [Bo, Thm. 4.10(v)]. We have checked it numerically for \(k \le 6\).

Tag 22S2

Problem 28.1.6. \(A(c\Lambda) \lvert \Delta(c\Lambda) \rvert^{1/6} = A(\Lambda) \lvert \Delta(\Lambda) \rvert^{1/6}\). Moreover \(\mathrm{Gr}_k(c\,\omega) = \lvert c \rvert^{2N_k} \mathrm{Gr}_k(\omega)\) with \(N_k = (k^2 - k + 2)/2\), and the right side of Theorem 28.1.1 also changes by \(-N_k\log\lvert c \rvert\) when \(\omega\) is replaced by \(c\,\omega\).

Now let \(E\) be an elliptic curve over a number field \(K\) with a Weierstrass equation over \(\mathcal{O}_K\) of unit discriminant \(\Delta_E\), and \(\omega = dx/(2y + a_1x + a_3)\); a curve with complex multiplication has such an equation over a finite extension [ST], [Si]. Let \(\Lambda_\sigma\) be the period lattice of \(\omega^\sigma\) for \(\sigma \colon K \to \mathbb{C}\). The Faltings height, in Deligne’s normalization [De], is \[h_{\mathrm{F}}(E) := -\frac{1}{2[K:\mathbb{Q}]} \sum_\sigma \log A(\Lambda_\sigma) = -\frac{1}{2[K:\mathbb{Q}]} \sum_\sigma \log\bigl(A(\Lambda_\sigma)\lvert\Delta(\Lambda_\sigma)\rvert^{1/6}\bigr),\] the second form by the product formula; by Problem 28.1.6 it does not change under extension of \(K\). Let \(M_k\) be the \(\mathcal{O}_K\)-lattice of sections of \((\mathcal{O}([0]) \otimes \omega_E)^{\otimes k}\) on the Weierstrass model; a basis is formed by the monomials in the coordinates \(x, y\) of the model of pole orders \(0, 2, \dots, k\), multiplied by \(\omega^k\). The \(f_j\omega^k\) are obtained from this basis by a triangular change of basis over \(K\) with diagonal entries \(1\), which may be nonintegral at \(2\) and \(3\); so the two bases have the same Gram determinant at every embedding, and Theorem 28.1.1 gives:

Tag 22S3

Theorem 28.1.2. \(\displaystyle \frac{\deg M_k}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]} = -\frac{k}{2}\,h_{\mathrm{F}}(E) + \frac{k}{4}\log\frac{k}{2\pi^2}\), and hence \(\mu^\sharp(M_k) = -h_{\mathrm{F}}(E) + \tfrac12\log\dfrac{k}{2\pi^2}\).

The degree has no term in \(k^2\). Why? Let \(\bar L\) be \(\mathcal{O}([0]) \otimes \omega_E\) on the Weierstrass model, with the cubist metric at every embedding, and let \(I := \hat{c}_1(\bar L)^2/[K:\mathbb{Q}]\). Multiplication by \(2\) is finite and flat of degree \(4\), so \(\hat{c}_1([2]^*\bar M)^2 = 4\,\hat{c}_1(\bar M)^2\) for every metrized line bundle \(\bar M\); and there is an isomorphism \([2]^*\bar L \cong \bar L^{\otimes 4}\) which is an isometry [MB1], at the archimedean places the identity of Problem 28.1.3.

Tag 22S4

Problem 28.1.7. \(I = 0\).

So by Theorem 26.2.1, applied to the Weierstrass model as a scheme over \(\mathbb{Z}\) (regarding \(M_k\) as a lattice over \(\mathbb{Z}\) changes its degree by \(O(k)\)), the coefficient of \(k^2\) in \(\deg M_k\) must vanish, as Theorem 28.1.2 shows it does. What does the normalization of the metric contribute? Let \(\bar L_t\) be \(\bar L\) with all norms multiplied by \(e^{-t}\), and \(M_k(t)\) the lattice \(M_k\) with the norms of \(\bar L_t^{\otimes k}\). For the trivial bundle \(\bar{\mathcal{O}}(a)\) with \(\lVert 1 \rVert = e^{-a}\) at every embedding, \(\hat{c}_1(\bar M \otimes \bar{\mathcal{O}}(a))^2 = \hat{c}_1(\bar M)^2 + 2a[K:\mathbb{Q}]\deg M_K\), where \(\deg M_K\) is the degree on the generic fiber [GS].

Tag 22S5

Problem 28.1.8. \(\deg M_k(t)/[K:\mathbb{Q}] = \deg M_k/[K:\mathbb{Q}] + tk^2\). The isomorphism \([2]^*\bar L_t \cong \bar L_t^{\otimes 4}\) multiplies norms by \(e^{-3t}\), and computing \(\hat{c}_1([2]^*\bar L_t)^2\) in the two ways gives \(\hat{c}_1(\bar L_t)^2/[K:\mathbb{Q}] = 2t\), in agreement with Theorem 26.2.1.

A constant rescaling of the metric moves the leading term of the degree; the cubist metric is the one normalization for which multiplication by \(2\) is an isometry, and only for it does the \(k^2\) term vanish. The period takes the place of the leading term of Theorem 26.2.1.

Tag 22S6

Problem 28.1.9. Let \(E\) have complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), and let \(\Omega\) be as in Problem 12.3.7. Then \(h_{\mathrm{F}}(E) = -\tfrac12\log(\pi\Omega) = -2\log\Gamma(\tfrac14) + \tfrac12\log 4\pi = -1.31053\ldots\)

Tag 22S7

Problem 28.1.10. For \(E\) as in Problem 28.1.9, \(h_{\mathrm{F}}(E) = \tfrac12\, L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi) - \tfrac12\gamma - \log\pi\), where \(\chi\) is the character modulo \(4\). Hence \(\mu^\sharp(M_k) = \tfrac12\bigl(\log\tfrac{k}{2} + \gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\bigr)\).

So Euler’s constant and \(L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\), which enter the slope method through the heights at infinity (Section 7.4 of The slope inequality in the mean), also enter the slope of the lattice of forms whose dual is the target.

§28.2. Cusp forms of level one Tag 22S8

Let \(M_k(\mathbb{Z})\) be the lattice of modular forms of weight \(k\) for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\) with \(q\)-expansion in \(\mathbb{Z}[[q]]\), \(S_k(\mathbb{Z})\) its cusp forms, and \(\Delta(\tau) := q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1-q^n)^{24} = (2\pi)^{-12}\Delta(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau)\). The Petersson norm is \(\lVert f \rVert^2 := \int_{\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathcal{H}} \lvert f \rvert^2 y^{k-2} \, dx\,dy\).

Tag 22S9

Problem 28.2.1. \(S_k(\mathbb{Z}) = \Delta\cdot M_{k-12}(\mathbb{Z})\), and \(S_k(\mathbb{Z})\) is a lattice of rank \(\dim S_k\).

Tag 22SA

Problem 28.2.2. \(S_k(\mathbb{Z})\) has rank one exactly for \(k \in \{12, 16, 18, 20, 22, 26\}\), and then \(S_k(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}\,\Delta E_{k-12}\), where \(E_0 = 1\), \(E_4\), \(E_6\), \(E_8 = E_4^2\), \(E_{10} = E_4E_6\) and \(E_{14} = E_4^2E_6\) are the normalized Eisenstein series.

For example \(\lVert\Delta\rVert^2 = 1.035362\ldots \cdot 10^{-6}\) (Section 11.2 of Chapter 11), so \(\deg S_{12}(\mathbb{Z}) = 6.89037\ldots\) How fast do the slopes grow with \(k\)?

Tag 22SB

Problem 28.2.3. Every nonzero \(f \in S_k(\mathbb{Z})\) has \(\lVert f \rVert^2 \ge 3e^{-\pi k/3}/(\pi k)\). Hence \(\mu_{\max}(S_k(\mathbb{Z})) \le \pi k/6 + O(\log k)\).

Linear growth is also what Theorem 26.2.1 gives for the mean slope of the sections of powers of a line bundle with a smooth metric. For the Hodge bundle \(\omega\) the Petersson metric is singular at the cusp; its self-intersection was computed by Kühn [Kü]. Problem 28.2.3 does not use this self-intersection: it compares the Petersson norm with a norm on the half-strip \(y \ge 1\), as Problems 26.1.5 and 26.2.2 compare norms.

A form \(f\) gives the section \(f(\tau)(2\pi\mathrm{i}\,dz)^k\) of \(\omega^k\) on \(E_\tau = \mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau)\), and \(2\pi\mathrm{i}\,dz\) is the integral differential \(dt/t\) of the Tate curve [Ka]; the Petersson metric is \(\lVert dz \rVert^2 = y\).

Tag 22SC

Problem 28.2.4. The function \(y^6\lvert\Delta(\tau)\rvert\) is invariant under \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\). If \(\mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\) is the ring of integers of an imaginary quadratic field of class number one and \(E_\tau\) is as above, then \[h_{\mathrm{F}}(E_\tau) = -\tfrac{1}{12}\log\bigl(y^6\lvert\Delta(\tau)\rvert\bigr) - \log 2\pi .\] In particular \(\Delta(\mathrm{i}) = (\Omega/4\pi)^6\).

So the Faltings height is \(-\tfrac1{12}\) times the logarithm of the Petersson norm \(y^6\lvert\Delta(\tau)\rvert\) of \(\Delta\) at the point with complex multiplication, and the term \(-\log 2\pi\) is the change from \(dz\) to \(2\pi\mathrm{i}\,dz\). Such normalizations move terms linear in the weight, as the main terms are, and must be fixed once.

§28.3. The period on a definite group Tag 22SD

The lattice \(V_\lambda(L)_\mathbb{Z}\) of Section 26.3 is defined by \(L\), the homology of the torus \(L_\mathbb{C}/L\); the algebraic structure is defined by differentials. For \(L = I_n\) the torus is \(E^n\) with \(E = \mathbb{C}/\mathcal{O}\); since \(\mathcal{O}\) is a principal ideal domain, every \(L\) is free over \(\mathcal{O}\) and every \(L_\mathbb{C}/L\) is isomorphic to \(E^n\). Let \(\omega = c\,dz\) be the invariant differential of a Weierstrass equation for \(E\), let \(V_\lambda(L)^{\mathrm{alg}}\) be the image of the tensor powers of \(c^{-1}L\) (for \(L = I_n\), the \(\mathcal{O}\)-span of the \(\partial_k := c^{-1}e_k\)), and let \(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n)^{\mathrm{alg}}\) be the corresponding lattice of forms.

Tag 22SE

Problem 28.3.1. \(\mu\bigl(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n)^{\mathrm{alg}}\bigr) = \mu\bigl(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n)_\mathbb{Z}\bigr) + \lvert\lambda\rvert\log A(c\,\mathcal{O})\). For \(y^2 = x^3 - x\) and \(\omega = dx/2y\), \(A(c\,\mathcal{O}) = \varpi^2 = \pi\Omega/2\).

Over a field where \(E\) has an equation of unit discriminant, Problems 28.1.9 and 26.1.7 give instead an increase of \(\mu^\sharp\) by \(-2\lvert\lambda\rvert h_{\mathrm{F}}(E) = \lvert\lambda\rvert\log(\pi\Omega) = \lvert\lambda\rvert(\gamma + 2\log\pi - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi))\); the difference \(\lvert\lambda\rvert\log 2\) comes from the prime above \(2\). So the period enters the slope of the lattice of forms linearly in the weight. In Alpöge’s argument [Alp], [Zh] the lattices are of forms on unitary groups of signature \((p,q)\), and the period enters with coefficient \(p - q\) times the weight [Adr, Hyp. 15.58]. These groups are indefinite, and their Shimura varieties have positive dimension; Section 28.2, on \(X(1)\), is the closer model, and the definite case of this section is a model only. What the sizes do not account for are the denominators of maps that do not respect integral structures; the next part, from Chapter 29 on, measures them.

28.1.4

28.1.5

Both sides depend only on \((E, \omega)\), and change by the same factor when \(\omega\) is multiplied by a constant. So one may take \(\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\) and \(\omega = dz\). Write \(\sigma\) through \(\vartheta(z + \tfrac{1+\tau}{2})\) (Problem 12.2.3); the constant is fixed by \(\sigma'(0) = 1\). Section 28.2.

28.2.1

\(1/\Delta = q^{-1}\prod_{n \ge 1}(1-q^n)^{-24}\) has integral coefficients. For the rank, \(S_k\) has a basis of products of \(\Delta\), \(E_4\) and \(E_6\) [SeA], and \(f \mapsto (a_1, \dots, a_d)\), \(d = \dim S_k\), is injective by the valence formula.

28.2.2

\(\dim S_k\) is the dimension of the space of modular forms of weight \(k - 12\), which is spanned by a form with \(q\)-expansion \(1 + O(q)\) when it is one-dimensional.

28.2.3

The half-strip \(\lvert x \rvert \le \tfrac12\), \(y \ge 1\) lies in the fundamental domain. If the first nonzero coefficient of \(f\) is \(a_m\), integrate \(\lvert f \rvert^2\) over \(x\) by Parseval and use \(y^{k-2} \ge 1\). The valence formula gives \(m \le k/12\). For the slope, Minkowski’s theorem gives a nonzero vector of length at most \(\sqrt{s}\,\operatorname{covol}(V)^{1/s}\) in a lattice \(V\) of rank \(s\).

28.2.4

Use the invariant form of \(h_{\mathrm{F}}\) in Section 28.1, with \(\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}+ \mathbb{Z}\tau\); every conjugate of \(E_\tau\) is isomorphic to \(E_\tau\), since the class number is one. For \(\Delta(\mathrm{i})\), Problem 12.3.7.
