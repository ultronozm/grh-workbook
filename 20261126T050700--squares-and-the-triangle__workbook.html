§72.1. Why a triangle

Problem 72.1.1. With \(t = 5r\): \(2r < r + t\), but \(2(r + (j-1)t) > r + jt\) for every \(j \ge 2\). So the condition \(2n^\flat < n^\sharp\) of the inner product formula (Theorem 69.2.1) holds for the first lift \(Y_0 \to Y_1\) of the chain and for no later lift \(Y_{j-1} \to Y_j\).

The following identity would suffice if the Gram operators could be moved through the preceding lifts.

Problem 72.1.2. Let \(u_j \colon \mathcal V_{j-1} \to \mathcal V_j\) (\(1 \le j \le m\)) be linear maps of complex inner product spaces that have adjoints, and \(U_j := u_j \circ \dots \circ u_1\). Suppose that \(u_1^*u_1 = a_1 \mathcal E_1\) and that \(u_j^*u_j\,U_{j-1} = a_j\,U_{j-1} \mathcal E_j\) for \(2 \le j \le m\), with scalars \(a_j\) and endomorphisms \(\mathcal E_j\) of \(\mathcal V_0\). Then \(U_m^*U_m = a_1 \cdots a_m\, \mathcal E_1 \mathcal E_2 \cdots \mathcal E_m\).

Used in: Chapter 68

For \(2 \le j \le m\) the ranks of the chain are outside the range of the inner product formula (Problem 72.1.1). The proof applies the formula only on the first edge of each row of a triangle of lifts, and relates the resulting Gram forms by two square laws.

§72.2. The triangle and its two square laws

In this sheet a triangle of size \(m\) consists of inner product spaces \(H_{i,n}\) (\(i, n \ge 0\), \(i + n \le m\)); a space \(H_\circ\) with linear isometric embeddings \(H_\circ\to H_{i,0}\), through which we regard \(H_\circ\) as a subspace of each \(H_{i,0}\); linear maps \(\lambda_{i,n} \colon H_{i,n} \to H_{i,n+1}\) (lifts) and \(\delta_{i,n} \colon H_{i,n+1} \to H_{i+1,n}\) (descents) for \(i + n < m\); and positive integer weights \(g_0, g_1, \dots\). The weighted rows are \(\lambda_i^{(0)} := 1\) and \(\lambda_i^{(n+1)} := g_n \lambda_{i,n} \lambda_i^{(n)} \colon H_{i,0} \to H_{i,n+1}\). The square \((i,n)\), for \(i + n + 2 \le m\), has corners \(H_{i,n+1}\), \(H_{i,n+2}\), \(H_{i+1,n}\), \(H_{i+1,n+1}\). We ask:

  • for \(i < m\), \(x \in H_\circ\) and \(v \in H_{i,1}\): \(\langle v, \lambda_{i,0}x\rangle = \langle \delta_{i,0}v, x\rangle\); and \(\delta_{i,0}\lambda_{i,0}\) maps \(H_\circ\subseteq H_{i,0}\) injectively into \(H_\circ\subseteq H_{i+1,0}\);

  • the direct law at each square, with a scalar \(c_{i,n}\): \(g_n \lambda_{i+1,n}\delta_{i,n} = (g_{n+2}/\bar c_{i,n})\, \delta_{i,n+1}\lambda_{i,n+1}\) on \(H_{i,n+1}\), where a quotient by \(0\) means \(0\);

  • the adjoint law at each square, with a scalar \(c^*_{i,n}\): every \(v \in H_{i,n+2}\) has an \(x \in H_{i,n+1}\) with \(\langle \lambda_{i,n+1}y, v\rangle = \langle y, x\rangle\) for all \(y\), and \(\langle \delta_{i,n}x, y'\rangle = c^*_{i,n} \langle \delta_{i,n+1}v, \lambda_{i+1,n}y'\rangle\) for all \(y' \in H_{i+1,n}\).

Problem 72.2.1. Let \(m = 2\), every \(H_{i,n} = H_\circ= \mathbb C\) with the usual inner product and identity embeddings, \((g_0, g_1, g_2) = (2, 3, 5)\), and let the maps be multiplication by \(\lambda_{0,0} = 2\), \(\lambda_{1,0} = 3\), \(\lambda_{0,1} = 4\), \(\delta_{0,0} = 2\), \(\delta_{1,0} = 3\), \(\delta_{0,1} = 1\). Then (F) holds; the only square is \((0,0)\), its direct law holds exactly with \(c_{0,0} = 5/3\), and its adjoint law exactly with \(c^*_{0,0} = 8/3\).

Used in: Problem 72.2.2

Problem 72.2.2. In Problem 72.2.1, \(\lambda_0^{(2)} = 48\), and \(48^2 = 2304 = c_{0,0}c^*_{0,0}\,(g_0g_1^2/g_2)\,(g_0\lambda_{0,0}^2)(g_0\lambda_{1,0}^2)\).

In Problems 72.2.3–72.2.6 the triangle is arbitrary and satisfies (F), (D), (A). For \(i + n < m\) put \(c_i^{[n]} := c_{i,0}c_{i,1}\cdots c_{i,n-1}\) and \(c_i^{*[n]} := c^*_{i,0}\cdots c^*_{i,n-1}\) (empty products are \(1\)); Problems 72.2.3, 72.2.5 and 72.2.6 assume \(i + n < m\).

Problem 72.2.3. \(\lambda_{i+1}^{(n)}\delta_{i,0}\lambda_{i,0} = \dfrac{g_{n+1}}{g_1g_0\,\overline{c_i^{[n]}}}\,\delta_{i,n}\lambda_i^{(n+1)}\) on \(H_{i,0}\).

Problem 72.2.4. For \(i + n \le m\), \(\lambda_i^{(n)}\) is injective on \(H_\circ\). If \(H_\circ\ne 0\) and \(i + n < m\), then \(c_i^{[n]} \ne 0\).

Problem 72.2.5. For \(x \in H_\circ\) and \(v \in H_{i,n+1}\): \(\langle v, \lambda_{i,n}\lambda_i^{(n)}x\rangle = c_i^{*[n]}\,\langle \delta_{i,n}v, \lambda_{i+1}^{(n)}x\rangle\).

Problem 72.2.6. For \(x, y \in H_\circ\) and \(x' := \delta_{i,0}\lambda_{i,0}x \in H_\circ\): \(\langle \lambda_i^{(n+1)}x, \lambda_i^{(n+1)}y\rangle = c_i^{[n]}c_i^{*[n]}\,\dfrac{g_0g_1g_n}{g_{n+1}}\,\langle \lambda_{i+1}^{(n)}x', \lambda_{i+1}^{(n)}y\rangle\).

In the chain, \(H_{i,n}\) consists of forms on a Hermitian space of rank \(r + nt\) whose parameter has \(n\) blocks of length \(t\), labelled \(i+1, \dots, i+n\). In row \(0\) these spaces are \(Y_0, \dots, Y_m\); the first space of row \(i\) is \(Y_0\) with its form multiplied by \((-1)^i\). On labels, the lift \(\lambda_{i,n}\) adds block \(i+n+1\) and the descent \(\delta_{i,n}\) removes block \(i+1\). So the square \((i,n)\) removes block \(i+1\) and adds block \(i+n+2\); we say it carries the pair \((i+1, i+n+2)\).

Problem 72.2.7. As \((i,n)\) runs over the squares of a triangle of size \(m\), the pair \((i+1, i+n+2)\) runs over the pairs \(1 \le i' < j' \le m\), each once; there are \(m(m-1)/2\) squares. For \(m = 3\) the squares \((0,0), (0,1), (1,0)\) carry \((1,2), (1,3), (2,3)\).

§72.3. The triangle of the chain and its scalars

For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) let \(\mathfrak m := (\pi_M)\prod_{w \in Q_\vartheta} w\), so \(S_{\mathfrak m} = \mathcal M\cup Q_\vartheta\), and in this sheet \(\mathfrak R := \mathcal O_E/\mathfrak m\), both depending on \(\vartheta\); \(\mathfrak R \cong \prod_{w \in \mathcal M\cup Q_\vartheta} k_w\), as \(p_0, p_1\) split completely in \(E\) and \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\) is prime to \(M\). In the triangle of the chain, \(H_\circ= H(Y_0)\), the node \(H_{i,n}\) is a space of forms on a space of rank \(r + nt\), row \(0\) runs through \(Y_0, \dots, Y_m\), the lift \(\lambda_{i,n}\) is a theta lift built from the character \(\mu^0_{i+n+1}\vartheta^{i+n+1}\) (times a power of the splitting character \(\upsilon\), depending on the ranks) with the indicator of \(\binom{1}{0}\) modulo \(\mathfrak m\) as finite datum, and \(g_n(\vartheta) := \#\mathrm{GL}_{r+nt}(\mathfrak R)\). The descents are transposed theta lifts; in each row the proof chooses their polynomials at infinity and the finite datum of \(\delta_{i,0}\) so that \(\delta_{i,0}\) is adjoint to \(\lambda_{i,0}\) on \(H_\circ\). For a function \(p\) on matrices \(\eta\) of a fixed shape over \(\mathfrak R\), the next datum is the function on the matrices with one more block of rows and of columns, of size \(t\), that equals \(p(-\eta)\), divided by the number of possible blocks \(\eta'\), at \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\eta & \eta'\\ 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), and \(0\) at all other matrices; the finite datum of \(\delta_{i,n+1}\) is the next datum of that of \(\delta_{i,n}\).

Realization of the triangle. The proof shows (F), and (D), (A) at each square with functions \(c_{i,n}\), \(c^*_{i,n}\) of the \(m\)-fold twist, as follows. Theorem 71.5.1, on the sum of the two middle Hermitian spaces, gives a finite square law for the two composites around the square on point data; a finite summation turns it into (D) for a datum and its next datum, with \(\lvert c_{i,n}(\vartheta)\rvert^2 = C_{i,n}\,\mathrm{ex}_t(\mathcal M\cup Q_\vartheta, \nu_{i,n}(\vartheta))\), \(C_{i,n} \in \mathbb R\) independent of \(\vartheta\) and \(\nu_{i,n}(\vartheta)\) as in Problem 72.3.2. The proof also provides the adjoint vectors in (A), with \(c^*_{i,n} \doteq 1\) in the sense of Definition 72.3.1; one of its factors is the scalar of the reversed square, which combines an exchange with partners of equal rank with the inverse of the Fourier constant of Problem 72.3.5. Status: read from the proof. For joint harmonics see [Li90; Ho89], for the seesaw principle [Ku84].

Definition 72.3.1. For functions \(c\) from \(m\)-fold twists to \(\mathbb C\) and \(f\) to \(\mathbb R\), \(c \doteq f\) means: there are \(C' \in \mathbb R\) (possibly \(0\)) and \(e' \in \mathbb Z\) with \(\lvert c(\vartheta)\rvert^2 = C'(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e'} f(\vartheta)^2\) for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\).

Used in: Chapter 68; Chapter 73

The relation \(c \doteq f\) allows \(c\) to vanish identically; if \(c\) is nonzero at one twist, its zeros are exactly those of \(f\).

Definition 72.3.2. For \(1 \le i < j \le m\), \(\nu := \mu_j^0/\mu_i^0\) and an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \[\rho_{ij}(\vartheta) := \prod_{z=1}^{t} \frac{\lvert L^{\mathcal M\cup Q_\vartheta}\bigl(z, (\nu\vartheta^{j-i})^{-1}\bigr)\rvert}{\lvert L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu^{-1})\rvert} ,\] a quotient by \(0\) being \(0\).

Used in: Chapter 67; Chapter 68

The values \(L^S(z, \nu)\) in Definition 72.3.2 and in the exchange factors below are finite and nonzero, by the nonvanishing stated in Chapter 71, Section 71.2: the characters have weight \(\pm(j-i)\Delta \ne 0\) and a tame part (Problems 72.3.2 and 72.4.6).

Problem 72.3.1. In the square \((i,n)\) the Hermitian spaces underlying the middle corners \(H_{i,n+1}\), \(H_{i+1,n+1}\) have rank \(r + (n+1)t\) each, and those underlying the partners \(H_{i,n+2}\), \(H_{i+1,n}\) have ranks \(r + (n+2)t\) and \(r + nt\). So in Theorem 71.5.1, with \(V^0\) the orthogonal sum of the two middle Hermitian spaces, \(n_0 = 2(r + (n+1)t)\) and \(h = t\). In the reversed square the partners are the spaces underlying \(H_{i,n+1}\), \(H_{i+1,n+1}\), of equal rank, so \(h = 0\) and \(\mathrm{ex}_0 = 1\).

For the data, \(8m \le 16\sqrt x < x^{11} \le T(s) \le q_1 d\) (Definition 36.3.1, Problem 36.3.3) and \(q_1 d \mid q_2\) (Chapter 36).

Problem 72.3.2. Let \(\nu_{i,n}(\vartheta) := \bigl((\mu^0_{i+n+2}/\mu^0_{i+1})\vartheta^{n+1}\bigr)^{-1}\). It has weight \((n+1)\Delta\); moreover \((n+1)\Delta \ge r + (n+1)t\) and \(n_0 \le 2(n+1)\Delta\). Its tame part is \(\omega_w^{-(n+1)}\) at \(w \in \mathcal M\), \(\psi_w^{-(n+1)}\) at \(w \in Q_\vartheta\), trivial elsewhere, and nontrivial at every \(w \in \mathcal M\cup Q_\vartheta\).

Used in: Chapter 68

Problem 72.3.3. If \(c \doteq f\) and \(c' \doteq f'\), then \(cc' \doteq ff'\). For every square, \(c_{i,n} \doteq \rho_{i+1,\,i+n+2}\), with exponent \(e' = t^2\), and \(c^*_{i,n} \doteq 1\).

Used in: Chapter 73

The powers of \(\#\mathfrak R\) in the adjoint law come from finite Fourier transforms \(\hat F(\mathbf v') := \sum_{\mathbf v} F(\mathbf v)\,\mathbf e(\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf v'\mathbf v))\), with \(\mathbf e\) a primitive additive character of \(\mathfrak R\) (nontrivial on each field factor). For \(\mathfrak R = \prod_w k_w\), with primitive characters \(\mathbf e\) (additive) and \(\chi\) (multiplicative, \(0\) on nonunits: every \(a \ne 0\) has some \(\chi(1+ca) \notin \{0,1\}\)) there is \(G \in \mathbb C\) with \(\lvert G\rvert^2 = \#\mathfrak R^{t^2}\) such that every function \(F\) on \(t \times t\) matrices with \(F(k\mathbf v) = \chi(\det k)F(\mathbf v)\) for invertible \(k\) has \(\hat F(\mathbf v') = G\,\chi^{-1}(\det \mathbf v')\,F(1)\), with \(\chi^{-1}\) also extended by \(0\). Status: for the Gauss sum of a determinant character over a finite field see [Ko63]; for \(\mathfrak R = \prod_w k_w\) such an \(F\) vanishes off the invertible matrices and is a product over \(w\), so the identity follows from the case of fields. At a node of rank \(r+nt\), let \(\iota=\binom{1}{0}\) be the inclusion into rank \(r+(n+1)t\). The proof compares two sums over matrices \(x\), each against \(L((\iota\;x))\), where \((\iota\;x)\) denotes adjoining the columns of \(x\) to \(\iota\) and \(L(k\mathbf v)=\chi(\det k)L(\mathbf v)\) for invertible \(k\). The sum weighted by the Fourier transform of the conjugate of the next datum of \(p\) is \(G\,\#\mathfrak R^{(r+nt)t}\) times the sum weighted by the next datum of the Fourier transform of \(\bar p\): \(G\) comes from a corner block of size \(t\), and \(\#\mathfrak R^{(r+nt)t}\) from the sum over a free block of size \((r+nt)\times t\). The reversed square uses the inverse of \(G\,\#\mathfrak R^{(r+nt)t}\) (in the proof of (A)).

§72.4. The Gram identity of the chain

Definition 72.4.1. For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), let \(\mathcal R_\vartheta\) be the endomorphism of \(T\) \[\mathcal R_\vartheta := \mathcal R_1(\vartheta; Y_0)\,\mathcal R_2(\vartheta^2; Y_0) \cdots \mathcal R_m(\vartheta^m; Y_0) .\]

Used in: Chapter 68

Each factor preserves \(T\) (Corollary 70.4.1). In this sheet \(c_{\mathrm{mat}}(\vartheta) := \#\mathfrak R^{r(r+t)}\), the number of \((r+t) \times r\) matrices over \(\mathfrak R\). The inner product formula on the first lift of each row (Problem 72.1.1), with the point datum, gives: for \(i < m\) there is \(C_i \in \mathbb C\), independent of \(\vartheta\), with \[g_0\,\langle \lambda_{i,0}x, \lambda_{i,0}y\rangle = \frac{C_i}{c_{\mathrm{mat}}(\vartheta)}\,\langle x, \mathcal R_{i+1}(\vartheta^{i+1}; Y_0)\,y\rangle \qquad (x \in H(Y_0),\ y \in T).\] Status: read from the proof.

Problem 72.4.1. In a triangle of size \(m\) with (F), (D), (A), let \(T' \subseteq H_\circ\) be a subspace, \(\mathcal E_i\) endomorphisms of \(T'\) and \(C_i\) scalars with \(g_0\langle \lambda_{i,0}x, \lambda_{i,0}y\rangle = C_i\langle x, \mathcal E_i y\rangle\) for \(i < m\), \(x \in H_\circ\), \(y \in T'\). Then for \(i + n = m\), \(x \in H_\circ\) and \(y \in T'\) \[\langle \lambda_i^{(n)}x, \lambda_i^{(n)}y\rangle = c_{(i)}\,\frac{g_0g_1^n}{g_n}\,\langle x, \mathcal E_i\mathcal E_{i+1}\cdots\mathcal E_{m-1}\,y\rangle ,\] where \(c_{(i)}\) is the product of \(C_i, \dots, C_{m-1}\) and of all \(c_{i',n'}c^*_{i',n'}\) with \(i' \ge i\).

Problem 72.4.2. For the triangle of the chain there is \(c_\triangle \doteq \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}\) with \[\langle \lambda_0^{(m)}x, \lambda_0^{(m)}y\rangle = c_\triangle(\vartheta)\,\frac{g_0g_1^m}{g_m\,c_{\mathrm{mat}}^m}\,\langle x, \mathcal R_\vartheta y\rangle \qquad (x \in H(Y_0),\ y \in T).\]

On \(H(Y_0)\) we write \(\Theta_\vartheta\) for the lower chain with values in algebraic forms on \(Y_m\), and on \(T\) for its restriction, as in Chapter 37. Its target consists of algebraic forms of the prescribed weight on \(Y_m\) on which the final level \(K_{\mathrm{last}}(\vartheta) \subseteq K^\circ(Y_m)\) acts by its character (Definition 37.9.1): the elements of \(K^\circ(Y_m)\) that reduce to \(1\) at each \(w \in \mathcal M\) and to a block upper triangular matrix, with blocks \(r, t, \dots, t\), at each \(w \in Q_\vartheta\). The proof shows \([K^\circ(Y_m) : K_{\mathrm{last}}(\vartheta)] = \prod_{w \in \mathcal M}\#\mathrm{GL}_{r+mt}(k_w)\,\prod_{w \in Q_\vartheta}[\mathrm{GL}_{r+mt}(k_w) : \mathrm{Par}_w]\), with \(\mathrm{Par}_w\) the group of Problem 72.3.6 over \(k_w\), and that for \(x, y \in H(Y_0)\) \[\langle \Theta_\vartheta x, \Theta_\vartheta y\rangle = c_{\mathrm{wt}}\,[K^\circ(Y_m) : K_{\mathrm{last}}(\vartheta)]\,\langle \lambda_0^{(m)}x, \lambda_0^{(m)}y\rangle ,\] with \(c_{\mathrm{wt}}\) a positive integer independent of \(\vartheta\). Status: read from the proof.

Problem 72.4.3. \(\Theta_\vartheta\) is injective on \(H(Y_0)\), in particular on \(T\).

Used in: Chapter 37; Chapter 67; Chapter 68

Problem 72.4.4. For every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \[[K^\circ(Y_m) : K_{\mathrm{last}}(\vartheta)]\,\frac{g_0g_1^m}{g_m} = \bigl(c_{\mathrm{mat}}(\vartheta)\,c_{Q_\vartheta}\bigr)^m\,(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{-mrt - t^2m(m-1)/2}\] times a positive number independent of \(\vartheta\).

Problem 72.4.5. There is \(c\) with \(c \doteq \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}\) and \(\langle \Theta_\vartheta x, \Theta_\vartheta y\rangle = c(\vartheta)\,c_{Q_\vartheta}^m\,\langle x, \mathcal R_\vartheta y\rangle\) for all \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\) and \(x, y \in T\).

Problems 72.2.3–72.2.7, 72.3.3 and 72.4.1–72.4.5, with the realization of the triangle, the first-edge identity and the description of the last level, prove the following.

Theorem 72.4.1. There is a function \(c\) from \(m\)-fold twists to \(\mathbb C\) with \(c \doteq \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}\) such that for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) and all \(x, y \in T\), \[\langle \Theta_\vartheta x, \Theta_\vartheta y\rangle = c(\vartheta)\,c_{Q_\vartheta}^m\,\langle x, \mathcal R_\vartheta y\rangle .\]

Used in: Chapter 68: Problem 68.4.2

The data satisfy \(t \le \Delta\) and \(m\Delta \le 4b\) (as \(m\Delta \le 4\sqrt x\,(3x^5)^8 < x_1 < b\) by Definition 36.3.1).

Problem 72.4.6. \(\rho_{ij}(1) = 1\). The character \(\mu_j^0/\mu_i^0\) has weight \(-(j-i)\Delta\), with \(t \le (j-i)\Delta \le 4b\), and tame part \((\omega_w^{j-i})\), nontrivial exactly at the \(w \in \mathcal M\); so each \(P_z := (\mu_j^0/\mu_i^0, z)\), \(1 \le z \le t\), is a ramified critical pair (Definition 37.10.1), and \(\rho_{ij}(\vartheta) = \prod_{z=1}^t R_{P_z}(\vartheta^{j-i}, 1)\).

Used in: Chapter 68

Problem 72.4.6 identifies the unprimed ratios of Problem 68.5.1; Theorem 68.5.1, used in Chapter 67, bounds for a family with small ratios the height of the inverses of \((\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{i<j}\rho_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\), every \(e \in \mathbb Z\).
