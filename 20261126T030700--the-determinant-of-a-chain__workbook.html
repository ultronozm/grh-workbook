For the lower chain and the single lifts, fix the data of Chapter 36. Let \(m\) be the number of lifts, \(r\) the rank of \(Y_0\), \(t := 5r\), and \(x_1\) and \(b\) the scales of \(D\).

How does the determinant \(Z(\vartheta)\) of the chain change with the \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\)? By Theorem 68.4.1, its quotient by \(Z(1)\) is the product of the \(m\) quotients \(Z_j(\vartheta)/Z_j(1)\) for single lifts, times a correction factor: a product of pairwise ratios \(\rho_{ij}(\vartheta)\) of Hecke \(L\)-values, multiplied by \((\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e_Z/2}\) with an integer \(e_Z\) independent of \(\vartheta\), and raised to the power \(d_T\). The problems derive Theorem 68.4.1 using the Gram identity of one lift (Theorem 69.4.1), the comparison on good forms (Corollary 70.4.1), the Gram identity of the chain (Theorem 72.4.1), and the injectivity of the single lifts and the chain. Chapter 72 derives the Gram identity of the chain from three statements read from the proof: the realization of a triangle of lifts, the inner product formula on its first edges, and the comparison at the final level. The square laws in the realization use the exchange identity (Theorem 71.5.1, stated and not proved in Chapter 71). Section 68.1 treats the linear algebra in its simplest setting; Sections 68.2–68.4 assemble the theorem. Section 68.5 recalls the auxiliary forms of Chapter 73 (Theorem 73.4.1) and proves a height bound for the ratios (Theorem 68.5.1); both are used in the proof of Theorem 67.2.1.

§68.1. Volumes of maps

For a linear map \(u \colon \mathcal E \to \mathcal E'\) of finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces let \[Z(u) := \det\nolimits_{\mathcal E} (u^* u) ,\] the determinant on the domain \(\mathcal E\); we call it the volume of \(u\). Dimensions are complex dimensions, the determinant on the zero space is \(1\), and inner products are conjugate-linear in the first argument and linear in the second.

Problem 68.1.1. For every orthonormal basis \(e_1, \dots, e_d\) of \(\mathcal E\), \(Z(u) = \det\bigl(\langle u e_a, u e_b \rangle\bigr)_{a,b}\). The number \(Z(u)\) is real and \(\ge 0\), and \(Z(u) > 0\) if and only if \(u\) is injective.

Problem 68.1.2. Let \(\mathcal R\) be an endomorphism of \(\mathcal E\) and \(a \in \mathbb C\) with \(\langle ux, uy\rangle = a \langle x, \mathcal R y\rangle\) for all \(x, y \in \mathcal E\). Then \(Z(u) = a^{\dim \mathcal E} \det \mathcal R\).

Problem 68.1.3. If \(u_j \colon \mathcal E_{j-1} \to \mathcal E_j\), for \(1 \le j \le m\), are linear maps between spaces of one and the same dimension, then \(Z(u_m \circ \dots \circ u_1) = \prod_{j=1}^m Z(u_j)\).

When the dimension grows along the chain, equality can fail.

Problem 68.1.4. Let \(u_2 \colon \mathbb C^2 \to \mathbb C^2\) be \(\operatorname{diag}(1, 2)\), and let \(u_1, u_1' \colon \mathbb C\to \mathbb C^2\) be \(x \mapsto (x, 0)\) and \(x \mapsto (0, x)\). Then \(Z(u_1) = Z(u_1') = 1\) and \(Z(u_2) = 4\), while \(Z(u_2u_1) = 1\) and \(Z(u_2u_1') = 4\).

Nor is the volume of a composite determined by the volumes of its factors when the first one is not onto.

Problem 68.1.5. Let \(u_1 \colon \mathbb C\to \mathbb C^2\) be \(x \mapsto (x, x)\), and let \(u_2 = \operatorname{diag}(2, 3)\), \(u_2' = \operatorname{diag}(1, 6)\) on \(\mathbb C^2\). Then \(Z(u_1) = 2\) and \(Z(u_2) = Z(u_2') = 36\), while \(Z(u_2u_1) = 13\) and \(Z(u_2'u_1) = 37\).

For the lower chain restricted to \(T\), Theorem 72.4.1 gives an identity for its Gram form on \(T\). Problem 72.1.2 is a model of how such an identity can be propagated along a chain.

For \(a \ge 0\) divisible by \(4\), the two lifts from \(\mathrm U(1)\) of Chapter 23 used here have a one-dimensional source. The lift \(\Theta_{(a)}\) from \(\mathrm U(1)\) to \(\mathrm U(1)\) is multiplication by \(c_a/4\) (Problem 23.2.3), and by Problem 23.3.2 the lift \(\Theta_{(a)}\) from \(\mathrm U(1)\) to \(\mathrm U(2)\) satisfies \[\lVert \Theta_{(a)}\varphi \rVert^2 = \lambda_a \lVert \varphi \rVert^2 , \qquad \lambda_a := \frac{1}{128} \sum_j \binom{a}{j} c_j^2 c_{a-j}^2 .\] This finite calculation illustrates the inner product of a lift with itself; the strict rank inequality of the inner product formula (Theorem 69.2.1) first allows a source of rank \(1\) when the target has rank \(3\), the case with which Chapter 69 begins.

Problem 68.1.6. For \(4 \mid a\), the composite of the lift \(\mathrm U(1) \to \mathrm U(1)\) with the lift \(\mathrm U(1) \to \mathrm U(2)\) of Chapter 23, in degree \(a\), has volume \(\lambda_a c_a^2/16\), the product of the two volumes.

§68.2. One lift

For \(1 \le j \le m\) and a tame character \(\xi\), the single lift \(\hat u_{\xi,j}\) on \(H(W_j)\) and its restriction \(u_{\xi,j}\) to \(H_g(W_j)\) are those of Section 37.9; \(H(V)\) carries the Petersson product (Definition 37.4.1). We recall two statements. By Theorem 69.4.1, for every \(1 \le j \le m\) there is \(a_j \in \mathbb C\), \(a_j \ne 0\), such that for every tame character \(\xi\), \[\hat u_{\xi,j}^*\, \hat u_{\xi,j} = a_j\, c_{Q_\xi}\, \mathcal R_j(\xi; W_j)\] on \(H(W_j)\), with \(c_Q\) the twist factor of Definition 69.3.1 (a power of \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\xi\) times a product of factors \(1 - q_w^{-v}\) over \(w \in Q_\xi\), with \(c_\varnothing = 1\)) and \(\mathcal R_j(\xi; V)\) the Rallis operator of Definition 69.3.2 (a convergent sum of right translations by finite adelic representatives supported outside the places of \(\mathcal M \cup Q_\xi\) and their conjugates, with coefficients that do not depend on \(V\)). For the same \(j\) and \(\xi\), \(\hat u_{\xi,j}\) is injective (Chapter 69, Section 69.4). By Corollary 70.4.1, for \(1 \le j \le m\): \(\dim H_g(W_j) = d_T\), and for every tame character \(\xi\), \(\mathcal R_j(\xi; Y_0)\) maps \(T\) to itself, \(\mathcal R_j(\xi; W_j)\) maps \(H_g(W_j)\) to itself, and \[\det\bigl(\mathcal R_j(\xi; W_j) \mid H_g(W_j)\bigr) = \det\bigl(\mathcal R_j(\xi; Y_0) \mid T\bigr) .\]

Problem 68.2.1. For \(1 \le j \le m\) and every tame character \(\xi\), \(Z(u_{\xi,j}) = (a_j c_{Q_\xi})^{d_T} \det\bigl(\mathcal R_j(\xi; Y_0) \mid T\bigr)\). Moreover \(Z(u_{\xi,j}) > 0\).

Used in: Chapter 70

§68.3. Twists and the ratios

Tame characters \(\xi\), their sets \(Q_\xi\) and powers \(\xi^n\), twists and \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\) are as in Chapter 37; By definition, the tame power \(\xi^n\) has the same set \(Q_\xi\), so \(c_{Q_{\xi^n}} = c_{Q_\xi}\).

Problem 68.3.1. If \(\vartheta\) is an \(m\)-fold twist and \(1 \le n \le m\), its tame power \(\vartheta^n\) satisfies the twist condition.

For \(1 \le i < j \le m\) and an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \(\rho_{ij}(\vartheta)\) is the ratio of Definition 72.3.2: with \(\nu := \mu_j^0/\mu_i^0\), \[\rho_{ij}(\vartheta) = \prod_{z=1}^{t} \frac{\lvert L^{\mathcal M \cup Q_\vartheta}\bigl(z, (\nu\vartheta^{j-i})^{-1}\bigr)\rvert}{\lvert L^{\mathcal M}(z, \nu^{-1})\rvert} ,\] a nonnegative real number (a quotient by \(0\) being \(0\), as in Definition 72.3.2). These ratios enter the Gram identity of the chain through the exchange identity (Theorem 71.5.1). In the direct square for \(i < j\), the squared modulus of the exchange scalar is a constant independent of \(\vartheta\) times \(\mathrm{ex}_t\bigl(\mathcal M \cup Q_\vartheta, (\nu\vartheta^{j-i})^{-1}\bigr)\) (Definition 71.2.3 and Problem 72.3.2). There is one such square for each pair \(1 \le i < j \le m\).

By Problem 72.4.6, \(\rho_{ij}(1) = 1\) for \(1 \le i < j \le m\).

§68.4. The factorization

For an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), the lower chain \(\Theta_\vartheta\) of Section 37.9, restricted to \(T\), takes values in a space of algebraic level forms on \(Y_m\) with the class-sum inner product; it is injective (Problem 72.4.3). In the notation of Section 68.1, \(Z(\vartheta) = Z(\Theta_\vartheta|_T)\) and \(Z_j(\vartheta) = Z(u_{\vartheta^j, j})\). Let \(\mathcal R_\vartheta = \mathcal R_1(\vartheta; Y_0)\,\mathcal R_2(\vartheta^2; Y_0) \cdots \mathcal R_m(\vartheta^m; Y_0)\) be the endomorphism of \(T\) of Definition 72.4.1. By Theorem 72.4.1 there is a function \(c\) from \(m\)-fold twists to \(\mathbb C\) with \(c \doteq \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}\), that is (Definition 72.3.1), with a real number \(C'\) and an integer \(e'\) such that \(\lvert c(\vartheta)\rvert^2 = C'(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e'}\bigl(\prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}(\vartheta)\bigr)^2\) for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), and such that for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) and all \(x, y \in T\), \[\langle \Theta_\vartheta x, \Theta_\vartheta y\rangle = c(\vartheta)\,c_{Q_\vartheta}^m\,\langle x, \mathcal R_\vartheta y\rangle .\]

Problem 68.4.1. Let \(\Sigma\) be a set with a point \(1\), let \(\nu \colon \Sigma \to \mathbb R_{>0}\) and \(f \colon \Sigma \to \mathbb R_{\ge 0}\) with \(\nu(1) = f(1) = 1\), and let \(c \colon \Sigma \to \mathbb C\), \(C' \in \mathbb R\) and \(e' \in \mathbb Z\) satisfy \(\lvert c(x) \rvert^2 = C' \nu(x)^{e'} f(x)^2\) for all \(x\). If \(c(1) \ne 0\), then \(C' = \lvert c(1)\rvert^2\) and \(\lvert c(x)/c(1) \rvert = \nu(x)^{e'/2} f(x)\) for all \(x\).

Used in: Chapter 73

Problem 68.4.2. In Theorem 72.4.1, if \(c(1) = 0\) then \(T = 0\). For every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \[Z(\vartheta) = \Bigl( c(\vartheta) \Big/ \prod_{j=1}^m a_j \Bigr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m Z_j(\vartheta) ,\] and \(Z(\vartheta) > 0\).

Problem 68.4.3. If \(T \ne 0\), then for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), \[\frac{Z(\vartheta)}{Z(1)} = \Bigl( (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e'/2} \prod_{1 \le i < j \le m} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) \Bigr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{Z_j(\vartheta)}{Z_j(1)} .\] If \(T = 0\), both sides equal \(1\) (a real number to the power \(0\) is \(1\)).

The normalized determinant identity is the following.

Theorem 68.4.1. For the data of Chapter 36 there is an integer \(e_Z\) such that for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\):

  1. \(0 < Z(\vartheta)\);

  2. \(0 < Z_j(\vartheta)\) for every integer \(j\) with \(1 \le j \le m\);

  3. in \(\mathbb C\), \[\frac{Z(\vartheta)}{Z(1)} = \biggl( (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e_Z/2} \prod_{i=1}^{m} \prod_{j=i+1}^{m} \rho_{ij}(\vartheta) \biggr)^{d_T} \prod_{j=1}^m \frac{Z_j(\vartheta)}{Z_j(1)} .\]

Used in: Chapter 8; Chapter 23; Chapter 67; Chapter 69; Chapter 72

By definition \(Z(\vartheta)\) and \(Z_j(\vartheta)\) are squares of the real norm determinants of the corresponding maps, viewed in \(\mathbb C\). Injectivity gives their strict positivity in (a) and (b). The base of the power \(d_T\) in (c) is a real number, with \(e_Z/2\) a real exponent, and \(e_Z\) does not depend on \(\vartheta\). Problems 68.2.1 and 68.4.1–68.4.3 prove Theorem 68.4.1. One may take \(e_Z = e'\) when \(T \ne 0\). Thus the volume of the chain at a twist, relative to the trivial twist, is expressed through \(m\) volumes of single lifts, a power of \(\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta\), and \(m(m-1)/2\) ratios \(\rho_{ij}\) of \(L\)-values.

§68.5. Auxiliary forms

The proof of Theorem 67.2.1 extends a linearly independent family of target forms to a basis by auxiliary forms (Definition 73.4.1). By Theorem 73.4.1 there are an integer \(e_\gamma\) and a function \(\gamma\) from the \(m\)-fold twists to \(\mathbb C\), with \(\gamma(\vartheta) \ne 0\) for every \(\vartheta\), \(\gamma(1) = 1\) and \[\lvert\gamma(\vartheta)\rvert = (\operatorname{Nm}Q_\vartheta)^{e_\gamma/2} \prod_{0 \le i, j < m} \rho'_{ij}(\vartheta)\] for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\), such that \(T\) is spanned over \(\mathbb C\) by the forms that are auxiliary for \(\gamma\). Here \(\rho'_{ij}\), \(0 \le i, j < m\), are the turn ratios of Definition 73.2.1. Theorem 68.5.1 below is proved by Problems 68.5.1 and 68.5.2, with Problems 72.4.6 and 73.2.2.

Theorem 68.5.1 (small ratios). Let \(\theta\) be a family of \(m\)-fold twists with small ratios (Definition 79.1.2). For every integer \(e\), the families \[\iota \mapsto \Bigl((\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\Bigr)^{-1} \quad\text{and}\quad \iota \mapsto \Bigl((\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota})^{e/2}\prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\Bigr)^{-1}\] have height at most \(m^2tx_1\).

Used in: Problem 68.5.2; Chapter 67; Chapter 72; Chapter 73

By Problem 72.4.6 each \(\rho_{ij}\) is a product of \(t\) ratios \(R_P(\vartheta^{j-i}, 1)\) with \(P\) a ramified critical pair. By Problem 73.2.2, for \(0 \le i, j < m\) each \(\rho'_{ij}(\vartheta)\) is the product of the \(t\) ratios \(R_{P_z}(\vartheta^{j+1}, 1)\), \(1 \le z \le t\), with \(P_z := (\mu^0_{i+1}\mu^0_{j+1}, z)\) a ramified critical pair and \(1 \le j + 1 \le m\).

Problem 68.5.1. Let \(\theta\) be a family of \(m\)-fold twists such that, for every \(1 \le n \le m\) and every ramified critical pair \(P\), the ratios \(R_P(\theta_\iota^n, 1)\) are nonzero and the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^n, 1)^{-1}\) has height at most \(x_1\). Then the families \(\iota \mapsto \bigl(\prod_{1 \le i < j \le m}\rho_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\bigr)^{-1}\) and \(\iota \mapsto \bigl(\prod_{0 \le i, j < m}\rho'_{ij}(\theta_\iota)\bigr)^{-1}\) have height at most \(m^2tx_1\).

Used in: Chapter 72

Problem 68.5.2. Let \(\theta = (\theta_\iota)\) be a family of \(m\)-fold twists. Suppose that for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(\iota \mid \ell\) one has \(\ell \nmid \operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota}\). Then for every integer \(e\) the family \(\iota \mapsto (\operatorname{Nm}Q_{\theta_\iota})^{-e/2}\) has height at most \(0\). Deduce Theorem 68.5.1.
