Prerequisites.

Hermitian forms over fields; Witt’s extension theorem [Sch]. From Chapter 39: the Bessel subgroup \(R=\mathcal{U}\rtimes U(X)\) and an example of its character (Section 39.1, Problems 39.1.1 and 39.1.2), and the finite unfolding of Problem 39.2.1. From Chapter 10: the unfolding of Problem 10.1.1. From Chapter 23: Problem 23.1.5 and Section 23.4. From Chapter 52: Theorem 52.4.1 and Problem 52.2.4. The Weil representation of a unitary dual pair and the Heisenberg representation under it, at the level of their definitions [We], [Ku]. Matrices act on columns; the matrix of a map \(V\to V'\) has its rows indexed by the basis of \(V'\). In this sheet \(b\), \(d\), \(s\) are local letters (not the scale, the prime and the tower exponent).

The proof of Theorem 52.4.1 pairs both sides with a form on the last space of the lower chain and moves the lifts onto that form by transposition, as in Problem 52.2.4; what is left is a Bessel period of a theta function, integrated against the form. How does such a period pass through one theta lift? For the weights \(0\), the theta function expands in constant terms indexed by the classes of rational maps \(V\to V'\) modulo maps of degree \(\ge0\) (degrees are defined in Section 53.2, and the expansion is stated in Section 53.4). In the period against a Bessel function only the \(U(V')(F)\)-translates of the set of classes agreeing with the string map (Definition 53.2.3) contribute; the rational Bessel subgroup of the second space preserves this set, and each translate contributes one value of the transferred function. We first see the mechanism over \(\mathbb{F}_3\) and \(\mathbb{F}_9\).

§53.1. Theta against a character on a plane over \(\mathbb{F}_3\)

Let \(Q(v)=v_1^2+v_2^2\) on \(\mathbb{F}_3^2\), let \(\zeta_3:=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}\), and for a function \(\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_3^2\to\mathbb{C}\) and \(b\in\mathbb{F}_3\) put \((n(b)\phi)(v):=\zeta_3^{bQ(v)}\phi(v)\) and \(\theta(\phi):=\sum_v\phi(v)\). The operators \(n(b)\) commute with \((h\phi)(v):=\phi(h^{-1}v)\) for \(h\) in the orthogonal group \(O(Q)\). In this section \(\phi(v):=1+[v_1]+2[v_2]\), with \([\,\cdot\,]\in\{0,1,2\}\) the least residue.

Problem 53.1.1. The sums \(\sum_{Q(v)=1+j}\phi(v)\) are \(13\), \(22\), \(1\) for \(j=0,1,2\).

Problem 53.1.2. For every \(\phi\), \(\frac13\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}_3}\zeta_3^{-b}\,\theta(n(b)\phi)=\sum_{Q(v)=1}\phi(v)\). For the \(\phi\) above it is \(13\).

Used in: Problem 53.1.4

The left side is a period of \(b\mapsto\theta(n(b)\phi)\) against a character; only the \(v\) with \(Q(v)=1\) survive it.

Problem 53.1.3. \(O(Q)\) has \(8\) elements, the stabilizer of \(v_0:=(1,0)\) has \(2\), and the orbit of \(v_0\) is \(\{(\pm1,0),(0,\pm1)\}=\{Q=1\}\).

Problem 53.1.4. Let \(\mathrm{cf}(\phi)\) be the coefficient of Problem 53.1.2 and \(\operatorname{Stab}\) the stabilizer of \(v_0\). For \(h\in O(Q)\), \(\mathrm{cf}(h\phi)=\sum_{q\in O(Q)/\operatorname{Stab}}\phi(h^{-1}qv_0)\); for \(h=1\) this is \(\frac12\sum_{\gamma\in O(Q)}\phi(\gamma v_0)=13\). For every function \(z\) on \(O(Q)\), \[\sum_{h\in O(Q)}z(h)\,\mathrm{cf}(h\phi)=\sum_{h\in O(Q)}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lvert\operatorname{Stab}\rvert}\sum_{\gamma\in O(Q)}z(\gamma h)\Bigr)\phi(h^{-1}v_0).\]

At fixed \(h\), the coefficient \(\mathrm{cf}(h\phi)\) is the sum over \(O(Q)/\operatorname{Stab}\) of the function \(k\mapsto\phi(k^{-1}v_0)\) evaluated at \(k=q^{-1}h\); this is the finite model of the transfer. In the adelic period the unipotent group can be nonabelian, and the proof uses two choices of weights.

§53.2. Strings, grades and the string map

For the matrix definitions and the finite examples, let \(E\) be a field with an involution \(x\mapsto\bar x\) and fixed field \(F\), and let \(N\in F^\times\); all bases are finite.

Definition 53.2.1. A space with a string for \(N\) is a hermitian space \(V\) over \(E\) with a basis consisting of an orthonormal basis of a subspace \(X\) and vectors \(f_0,\dots,f_{s-1}\) orthogonal to \(X\), and with a constant \(c_V\in F^\times\), such that \(f_i\) pairs only with \(f_{s-1-i}\), to \(1\), or to \(-N\) if \(i=s-1-i\). We write \(J_V\) for the Gram matrix. \(X\) is the definite part, \(f_0,\dots,f_{s-1}\) is the string, and \(\operatorname{len}V:=s\) is its length.

Used in: Chapter 54

Definition 53.2.2. The grade of a vector of the basis of \(X\) is \(0\), and that of \(f_i\) is \(s-1-2i\). Let \(V'\) be a second space with a string, and let \(d\) be integer weights on the basis of \(V'\). For basis vectors \(e\) of \(V\) and \(e'\) of \(V'\), the entry of a map \(w\colon V\to V'\) in the row of \(e'\) and the column of \(e\) has degree \(d(e')-\operatorname{grade}(e)\), and \(w\) has degree \(\ge i\) if all its entries of degree \(<i\) vanish. For maps \(V\to V\) the weights are the grades.

Used in: Chapter 55

For the shape of Section 39.1 the string is \(v_1,\dots,v_\varrho,z,v'_\varrho,\dots,v'_1\); \(\mathcal{U}\) is the group of unitary \(n\) with \(n-1\) raising grades, and \(R\) that of the unitary \(g\) with \(gf_i-f_i\) in the span of the string vectors of larger grade for every \(f_i\) of grade \(\ge0\). In the adelic setting of the proof these conditions define \(\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_F)\) and \(R(\mathbb{A}_F)\) in the string basis. We use the same matrix conditions over finite fields.

Definition 53.2.3. Let \(\operatorname{len}V'=\operatorname{len}V-1\), or \(\operatorname{len}V=\operatorname{len}V'=0\). The string map \(\mathrm{st}\colon V\to V'\) kills the definite part and sends \(f_i\) to \(f'_i\) times a scalar, and to \(0\) if \(V'\) has no vector \(f'_i\). If \(\operatorname{len}V\) is odd, the scalar is \(1,\ -Nc_V,\ c_V\) for \(f_i\) of positive, zero, negative grade; if \(\operatorname{len}V\) is even, it is \(c_V,\ 1\) for positive, negative grade. For a map \(w\colon V\to V'\) put \(w^*:=J_V^{-1}\bar w^{t}J_{V'}\).

Used in: Chapter 39; Chapter 55

For the grades of \(V\) and \(V'\) the string map has degree \(\ge-1\); in the chain of the data it is \(\mathrm{st}_j\colon Y_{j-1}\to Y_j\).

The hook. In the finite examples of this section and the next, \(E=\mathbb{F}_9=\mathbb{F}_3[\mathrm{i}]\) with \(\mathrm{i}^2=-1\) and \(\bar{\mathrm{i}}=-\mathrm{i}\), \(N=1\), and \(V\), \(V'\) have no definite part, strings of lengths \(3\) and \(2\), and constants \(c_V=c_{V'}=1\): \(J_V=\operatorname{antidiag}(1,-1,1)\), \(J_{V'}=\operatorname{antidiag}(1,1)\), the grades are \(2,0,-2\) on \(V\) and \(1,-1\) on \(V'\), and \(\mathrm{st}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).

Problem 53.2.1. The unitary upper unitriangular matrices for \(J_V\) are the \(27\) matrices \(u=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a&b\\0&1&\bar a\\0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(b+\bar b=a\bar a\). They form \(\mathcal{U}\), and \(u-1\) has degree \(\ge2\) for each of them.

Problem 53.2.2. \(\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{st}^*\mathrm{st}\,u)=a-\bar a\), and for \(w=(v,0,0)\) with first column \(v\in\mathbb{F}_9^2\), \(\operatorname{tr}(w^*wu)=(\bar v^{t}J_{V'}v)\,b\).

§53.3. The survival test

Over the adeles, let \(E\) be a CM field with the CM type and Weil data of [GL], \(F\) its maximal totally real subfield and \(\mathbb{A}_E\) its adeles; \(\psi_E\) is the standard additive character of \(\mathbb{A}_E/E\), and \(j_E\in E^\times\), with \(\bar j_E=-j_E\), is the element given by the Weil data. In the proof the character used on the unitary \(n\) with \(n-1\) of degree \(\ge2\) is \(\psi_\mathcal{U}(n)=\psi_E\bigl(\tfrac12j_E\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{st}^*\mathrm{st}\,n)\bigr)\); the matrix \(\mathrm{st}^*\mathrm{st}\) is the shift of the string of \(V\) and depends on \(c_V\), and \(\psi_\mathcal{U}(nn')=\psi_\mathcal{U}(n)\psi_\mathcal{U}(n')\) for two such \(n,n'\). Over \(\mathbb{F}_9\) we use the same formulas with \(\psi_E(x)=\zeta_3^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_9/\mathbb{F}_3}x}\) and \(j_E=\mathrm{i}\) (and \(\frac12=-1\) in \(\mathbb{F}_3\)), and \(u\) runs over \(\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{F}_3)\).

Definition 53.3.1. Let \(d\) be weights on the basis of \(V'\). A rational map \(w\colon V\to V'\) survives for \(d\) if \[\psi_E\bigl(\tfrac12j_E\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{st}^*\mathrm{st}\,u)\bigr)=\psi_E\bigl(\tfrac12j_E\operatorname{tr}(w^*w\,u)\bigr)\] for every \(u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{A}_F)\) such that \(u-1\) has degree \(\ge2\) and \(w(u-1)\) has degree \(\ge1\) for the weights \(d\). A map \(w\) agrees with \(\mathrm{st}\) if \(w\) and \(\mathrm{st}\) have the same entry in the row of \(e'\) and the column of \(e\) whenever \(\operatorname{grade}(e)>0\) and \(\operatorname{grade}(e')<\operatorname{grade}(e)\).

The test is applied to classes of rational maps modulo the maps of degree \(\ge0\), through a chosen representative of each class; a class is determined by the entries of negative degree. For the weights \(0\) these are the columns of positive grade.

Problem 53.3.1. In the hook, take the weights \((0,0)\). The classes are represented by the \(81\) maps \(w=(v,0,0)\), and exactly \(32\) of them survive: the \(v\ne0\) with \(\bar v^{t}J_{V'}v=0\).

Used in: Problem 53.3.2

Problem 53.3.2. The unitary group \(U(V')(\mathbb{F}_3)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_9):\bar g^{t}J_{V'}g=J_{V'}\}\) has \(96\) elements, and their first columns are the \(32\) surviving vectors of Problem 53.3.1. The stabilizer of \((1,0)^{t}\) has order \(3\) and is the Bessel subgroup \(R'\) of \(V'\).

Problem 53.3.3. Take the weights \((1,-1)\), the grades of \(V'\). The classes that agree with \(\mathrm{st}\) are represented by the nine maps \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\0&c&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), \(c\in\mathbb{F}_9\), and exactly \(c=-1\) survives: the class of \(\mathrm{st}\).

The hook illustrates the two choices of weights in the proof. For the weights \(0\) the survivors are the translates by \(U(V')(\mathbb{F}_3)\) of the one class that agrees with \(\mathrm{st}\), \(v=(1,0)^{t}\), in bijection with \(U(V')(\mathbb{F}_3)/R'\) (\(96/3=32\)); for the grades of \(V'\) only the class of \(\mathrm{st}\) survives among the agreeing classes. Here is a finite model of how a failing map is killed.

Problem 53.3.4. Let \(H\) be a finite group and \(\mathfrak f\colon H\to\mathbb{C}\) a function with \(\mathfrak f(ux)=\zeta\mathfrak f(x)\) for all \(x\), for one \(u\in H\) and one \(\zeta\ne1\). Then \(\sum_{x\in H}\mathfrak f(x)=0\). If \(\mathfrak f\) is also left invariant under a subgroup \(S\), then \(\sum_{x\in S\backslash H}\mathfrak f(x)=0\).

§53.4. Constant terms and the transfer

Let \(V\), \(V'\) be spaces with strings for the same \(N\) over \(E\), with \(\operatorname{len}V'=\operatorname{len}V-1\) or \(\operatorname{len}V=\operatorname{len}V'=0\), each with an adelic frame (Section 37.3) in which its Gram matrix is the same diagonal matrix of signs at every infinite place and the identity at the finite places; matrices are written in the bases of Definition 53.2.1. Let \(R\), \(R'\) be the Bessel subgroups. Write \(\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_F\) for the groups and \(\mathbb{A}_E\) for the matrices. Put \([R]:=R(F)\backslash R(\mathbb{A})\) and \([U(V')]:=U(V')(F)\backslash U(V')(\mathbb{A})\), with the quotient measures of fixed Haar measures; \([R]\) is compact. Let \(\mathbb{W}:=\operatorname{Hom}(V,V')\), with the pairing \((y,y')\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(y'^*y)\). For each finite index set fix a continuous unitary character of the norm-one adeles of \(E\) trivial on the norm-one elements of \(E\); for the index sets of the bases of \(V\) and \(V'\) these give the Weil representation \(\omega\) of \(U(V)(\mathbb{A})\times U(V')(\mathbb{A})\) on the Banach space \(\mathcal{S}\) of data of [GL] (see [We], [Ku]), with the Heisenberg operators \(\mathrm{Heis}(y)\), \(y\in\mathbb{W}(\mathbb{A}_E)\), and the theta functional \(\theta\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{C}\). We use: \(\omega(g,h)\mathrm{Heis}(y)\omega(g,h)^{-1}=\mathrm{Heis}(hyg^{-1})\); \(\theta\circ\omega(\gamma,\gamma')=\theta\) for rational \(\gamma,\gamma'\); \(y\mapsto\theta(\mathrm{Heis}(y)\phi)\) is invariant under rational translations on every rational subspace of \(\mathbb{W}\) on which the pairing vanishes. Status: classical.

Let \(d\) be weights on the basis of \(V'\) that change sign under the involution of the basis fixing the definite part and exchanging \(f'_i\) and \(f'_{s'-1-i}\) (\(s'=\operatorname{len}V'\)), for instance \(d=0\) or the grading, and let \(\mathbb{W}^d_{>0}\) be the maps whose entries of degree \(\le0\) vanish. The pairing vanishes on \(\mathbb{W}^d_{>0}\). A map \(V'\to V'\) has degree \(\ge0\) for \(d\) if its entry in the row of \(e'\) and column of \(e''\) vanishes whenever \(d(e')<d(e'')\).

Definition 53.4.1. For a datum \(\phi\) and a map \(w\in\mathbb{W}(\mathbb{A}_E)\), define the constant term of \(\phi\) at \(w\) by the mean \[\mathrm{CT}^d_w(\phi):=\int_{\mathbb{W}^d_{>0}(E)\backslash\mathbb{W}^d_{>0}(\mathbb{A}_E)}\theta\bigl(\mathrm{Heis}(y)\mathrm{Heis}(w)\phi\bigr)\,dy\] for the probability Haar measure. We write \(\mathrm{CT}_w:=\mathrm{CT}^d_w\) when \(d\) is the grading of \(V'\).

In the proof, for rational \(w\), \(\mathrm{CT}^d_w\) depends only on \(w\) modulo rational maps of degree \(\ge0\). For the fixed spaces and weights \(d\) there is a constant \(C_d\ge0\) such that for every datum \(\phi\), \[\theta(\phi)=\sum_w\mathrm{CT}^d_w(\phi),\qquad \sum_w\lvert\mathrm{CT}^d_w(\phi)\rvert\le C_d\lVert \phi\rVert,\] the sums over the classes of rational maps modulo maps of degree \(\ge0\), with the norm of \(\mathcal{S}\). This is the Fourier expansion along \(\mathbb{W}^d_{>0}\) in a mixed model; compare [We]. Status: classical; the norm estimate is used in the form proved in the argument.

Definition 53.4.2. Let \(\phi\in\mathcal{S}\), and let \(\mathcal{W}\) be a continuous function on \(R(\mathbb{A})\), left invariant under \(R(F)\), with \(\mathcal{W}(nr)=\psi_\mathcal{U}(n)\mathcal{W}(r)\) for all \(n\in R(\mathbb{A})\) such that \(n-1\) has degree \(\ge2\). Define the transfer of \(\mathcal{W}\) on \(U(V')(\mathbb{A})\) by \[\mathcal{W}'(h):=\int_{[R]}\mathcal{W}(r)\,\mathrm{CT}_\mathrm{st}\bigl(\omega(r,h)\phi\bigr)\,dr .\]

Used in: Theorem 53.4.1; Chapter 54; Chapter 55

In the proof \(\mathcal{W}'\) is continuous and left \(R'(F)\)-invariant. For each class \(w\) choose a rational representative \(\tilde w\), and put \(\mathcal Q^d_w(h,r):=\mathcal{W}(r)\,\mathrm{CT}^d_{\tilde w}(\omega(r,h)\phi)\); a class survives, or agrees with \(\mathrm{st}\), if \(\tilde w\) does. In the proof, each \(\mathcal Q^d_w\) is jointly continuous in \((h,r)\), and there is a constant \(A_d\ge0\) such that \(\sum_{w\in\Sigma}\lvert\mathcal Q^d_w(h,r)\rvert\le A_d\lVert\omega(1,\delta h)\rVert\) for every \(h\in U(V')(\mathbb{A})\), \(r\in R(\mathbb{A})\), set \(\Sigma\) of classes and \(\delta\in U(V')(F)\) of degree \(\ge0\) for \(d\); and if \(u\in R(\mathbb{A})\), \(u-1\) has degree \(\ge2\) and \(\tilde w(u-1)\) has degree \(\ge1\), then \[\mathcal Q^d_w(h,ur)=\frac{\psi_E\bigl(\frac12j_E\operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{st}^*\mathrm{st}\,u)\bigr)}{\psi_E\bigl(\frac12j_E\operatorname{tr}(\tilde w^*\tilde w\,u)\bigr)}\,\mathcal Q^d_w(h,r).\] The ratio is \(1\) for all such \(u\) exactly when \(\tilde w\) survives.

Problem 53.4.1. Let \(\gamma\in R(F)\) and \(\gamma'\in U(V')(F)\), and let \(\gamma'\) have degree \(\ge0\) for the weights \(d\) (no condition when \(d=0\)). Then \(\mathcal Q^d_{\gamma'w\gamma^{-1}}(\gamma'h,\gamma r)=\mathcal Q^d_w(h,r)\).

We use special cases of two facts on discrete groups. Let \(G\) be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff group with a left Haar measure, \(\Gamma\subset G\) a discrete subgroup acting on a countable set \(\mathcal X\), quotients by discrete subgroups carrying the induced measures, and \(\mathfrak k_{\mathfrak x}\) (\(\mathfrak x\in\mathcal X\)) measurable functions on \(G\) with \(\mathfrak k_{\gamma\mathfrak x}(\gamma g)=\mathfrak k_{\mathfrak x}(g)\). If \(\sum_{\mathfrak x}\lvert\mathfrak k_{\mathfrak x}\rvert\) is integrable on \(\Gamma\backslash G\), then \(\mathfrak k_{\mathfrak x}\) is integrable on \(\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(\mathfrak x)\backslash G\) and \(\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{\mathfrak x'\in\Gamma\mathfrak x}\mathfrak k_{\mathfrak x'}=\int_{\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(\mathfrak x)\backslash G}\mathfrak k_{\mathfrak x}\) [Bou]. And if \(\mathfrak f\) is continuous, integrable on \(\Gamma\backslash G\) and left \(\Gamma\)-invariant, with \(\mathfrak f(ug)=\zeta\mathfrak f(g)\) for all \(g\), one \(u\in G\) and one \(\zeta\ne1\) of modulus one, then \(\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}\mathfrak f=0\); Problem 53.3.4 is the finite case. Status: the first is classical; for the second we use the scalar, integrable case of the twisted-integral lemma in the proof.

Problem 53.4.2. Let \(d\) be \(0\) or the grading of \(V'\), and let the class \(w\) fail the test for \(d\). Then for every \(h\), \(\int_{[R]}\sum_{w'\in w R(F)}\mathcal Q^d_{w'}(h,r)\,dr=0\).

Let \(w_+\) and \(\mathrm{st}_+\) be the matrices of the columns of positive grade of \(w\) and of \(\mathrm{st}\). In the proof, if a rational \(w\) survives for \(d=0\), then \(w_+\) has linearly independent columns and \(\bar w_+^{t}J_{V'}w_+=\bar{\mathrm{st}}_+^{t}J_{V'}\mathrm{st}_+\); Problem 53.3.1 illustrates this. Witt’s theorem: if \(w_1,w_2\) are matrices over \(E\) of the same shape, with linearly independent columns and \(\bar w_1^{t}Jw_1=\bar w_2^{t}Jw_2\) for a nondegenerate hermitian \(J\), then \(gw_1=w_2\) for some invertible square \(g\) over \(E\) with \(\bar g^{t}Jg=J\) [Sch]. Status: classical.

Problem 53.4.3. Let \(w\) be a rational map with \(w_+\) of linearly independent columns and \(\bar w_+^{t}J_{V'}w_+=\bar{\mathrm{st}}_+^{t}J_{V'}\mathrm{st}_+\). Then \(\gamma'w\) agrees with \(\mathrm{st}\) for some \(\gamma'\in U(V')(F)\).

Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be the set of classes for the weights \(0\) that agree with \(\mathrm{st}\). In the proof, if \(w\) agrees with \(\mathrm{st}\), \(\gamma\in R(\mathbb{A})\) and \(\gamma'\in U(V')(\mathbb{A})\), then \(\gamma'w\gamma\) agrees with \(\mathrm{st}\) if and only if \(\gamma'\in R'(\mathbb{A})\); in particular \(\mathcal{C}\) is stable under \(w\mapsto w\gamma\), \(\gamma\in R(F)\). Problem 53.3.2 illustrates this.

Problem 53.4.4. Let a group \(H\) act on a set \(\mathcal X\), let \(S\subseteq H\) be a subgroup and \(\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal X\) a subset such that for \(\mathfrak x\in\mathcal{C}\), \(g\mathfrak x\in\mathcal{C}\) if and only if \(g\in S\). Then \((q,\mathfrak x)\mapsto q_\mathrm{rep}\mathfrak x\), for any choice of representatives \(q_\mathrm{rep}\), is a bijection from \((H/S)\times\mathcal{C}\) onto the union of the translates of \(\mathcal{C}\).

The second selection takes place inside the period: in the proof, for every \(h\), \[\int_{[R]}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal Q^0_w(h,r)\,dr=\mathcal{W}'(h).\] For each datum, the sum of the constant terms over \(\mathcal{C}\) equals the sum over the classes agreeing with \(\mathrm{st}\) for the grading of \(V'\); every surviving representative of one of those lies in the class of \(\mathrm{st}\); \(\mathrm{st}\) survives, and \(R(F)\) fixes its class. Problem 53.3.3 illustrates this.

Problem 53.4.5. For every \(h\in U(V')(\mathbb{A})\), the series on the right converges absolutely and \[\int_{[R]}\mathcal{W}(r)\,\theta\bigl(\omega(r,h)\phi\bigr)\,dr=\sum_{q\in U(V')(F)/R'(F)}\mathcal{W}'(q_\mathrm{rep}^{-1}h).\]

Problem 53.4.6. Let \(\Gamma'\subseteq\Gamma\) be discrete subgroups of \(G\) as above, \(\mathfrak f\) a continuous left \(\Gamma'\)-invariant function and \(z\) a continuous left \(\Gamma\)-invariant function on \(G\), with \(\sum_{q\in\Gamma/\Gamma'}\lvert\mathfrak f(q_\mathrm{rep}^{-1}h)z(h)\rvert\) integrable on \(\Gamma\backslash G\). Then \(\mathfrak fz\) is integrable on \(\Gamma'\backslash G\) and \[\int_{\Gamma\backslash G}\sum_{q\in\Gamma/\Gamma'}\bigl(\mathfrak f(q_\mathrm{rep}^{-1}h)\,z(h)\bigr)\,dh=\int_{\Gamma'\backslash G}\mathfrak f(h)z(h)\,dh .\]

The height of \(h\in U(V')(\mathbb{A})\) is the reciprocal of the first minimum (the length of a shortest nonzero lattice vector), cut off at \(1\), of the lattice of the space moved by \(h\). In the proof, for every \(g_0\) and every large integer \(e\), each \(h\) has a \(\gamma'\in U(V')(F)\) with \(\lVert\omega(g_0,\gamma'h)\rVert\le2^e\) times the \(e\)-th power of the height of \(h\); we use \(g_0=1\). This growth estimate is proved by reduction theory; compare [KR]. A function \(z\) on \(U(V')(\mathbb{A})\) is automorphic if it is continuous, left invariant under \(U(V')(F)\) and invariant under right translation by a neighbourhood of \(1\) in the finite adelic group, and rapidly decreasing if for every integer \(b\ge0\), \(\lvert z(h)\rvert\) times the \(b\)-th power of the height of \(h\) is bounded. For each rapidly decreasing \(z\), in the proof, \(\sum_{w\in\Sigma}\lvert\mathcal Q^0_w(h,r)z(h)\rvert\le C'\) for one constant \(C'\), every \(h\), \(r\) and every set \(\Sigma\) of classes, and \(\sum_q\lvert\mathcal{W}'(q_\mathrm{rep}^{-1}h)z(h)\rvert\le C'\operatorname{vol}[R]\), and \([U(V')]\) has finite volume. Problems 53.4.1–53.4.6, with the facts stated in this section, prove the following.

Theorem 53.4.1. With \(E\), the frames, \(\omega\) and \(\mathcal{S}\) as in this section, let \(V\), \(V'\) be hermitian spaces over \(E\), each the orthogonal sum of a definite part and a string for the same \(N\), with \(\operatorname{len}V'=\operatorname{len}V-1\) or \(\operatorname{len}V=\operatorname{len}V'=0\); let \(R\), \(R'\) be their Bessel subgroups and \(\mathrm{st}\) the string map. Let \(\phi\in\mathcal{S}\), \(\mathcal{W}\) be as in Definition 53.4.2, and \(z\) be an automorphic, rapidly decreasing function on \(U(V')(\mathbb{A})\). Then \(\mathcal{W}'z\) is integrable on \(R'(F)\backslash U(V')(\mathbb{A})\) and \[\int_{[R]}\mathcal{W}(r)\int_{[U(V')]}\theta\bigl(\omega(r,h)\phi\bigr)\,z(h)\,dh\,dr=\int_{R'(F)\backslash U(V')(\mathbb{A})}\mathcal{W}'(h)\,z(h)\,dh .\]

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 54

What has been proved.

A Bessel period of a theta function, paired with a form on \(V'\), is the integral of the transfer against the form over \(R'(F)\backslash U(V')(\mathbb{A})\). Used as given: the facts on \(\omega\), \(\mathrm{Heis}\) and \(\theta\) stated at the start of Section 53.4, the expansion of \(\theta\), the statements marked “in the proof”, Witt’s theorem and the facts on discrete groups. If \(c_V=c_{V'}\), then in the proof, for each \(h\), the function \(r'\mapsto\mathcal{W}'(r'h)\) on \(R'(\mathbb{A})\) satisfies the hypothesis on \(\mathcal{W}\) for \(V'\): the transfer is again a Bessel function, on a string of length \(\max(\operatorname{len}V-1,0)\).
