Prerequisites.

Chapter 21, Masses and the count of forms: the class sets \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\), the groups \(\Gamma_L\), the representations \(V_\lambda(L) = \mathbb{S}_\lambda(L_\mathbb{C})\), the notation \(e^\nu\) in \(\operatorname{Sym}^a\), and the spaces of forms \(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n)\) with their norm (Sections 21.1 and 21.3). From Chapter 11: Theorem 11.3.1 and Problems 11.3.7, 11.3.1 and 11.3.4. From Chapter 15: Section 15.1 and Problem 15.1.3. Stirling’s formula.

Since a definite unitary group has a finite class set, a theta lift between two such groups is a finite matrix. What are its entries, and how large is it? We compute both in the smallest cases.

§23.1. The theta kernel Tag 22MP

How does one map forms on one class set to forms on another? The idea is to sum over all homomorphisms between the two lattices, with a Gaussian weight.

Let \(L\) and \(L'\) be positive definite unimodular Hermitian lattices over \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) of ranks \(n\) and \(n'\). For \(x \in \operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(L, L')\) let \(x^* \in \operatorname{Hom}(L'_\mathbb{C}, L_\mathbb{C})\) be its adjoint, and let \(h(x,y) := \operatorname{tr}(y^* x)\). Let \[\Theta_{L,L'}(Z) := \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(L,L')} e^{-\pi h(x,x) + \operatorname{tr}(Z x)}, \qquad Z \in \operatorname{Hom}(L'_\mathbb{C}, L_\mathbb{C}) .\] For a polynomial \(P\) in the entries of \(x\), let \(P(\partial)\) be the differential operator obtained by replacing \(x_{\beta\alpha}\) with \(\partial/\partial Z_{\alpha\beta}\). For a weight \(\lambda\) define \[\vartheta_\lambda(L, L') := \sum_{x \in \operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(L,L')} \mathbb{S}_\lambda(x)\, e^{-\pi h(x,x)} \colon V_\lambda(L) \to V_\lambda(L'),\] and, for the class sets \(\mathrm{Cl}_n\) and \(\mathrm{Cl}_{n'}\), define the lift \(\Theta_\lambda \colon \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n) \to \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n')\) by \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:lift}\tag{1} (\Theta_\lambda \varphi)(L') := \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \frac{1}{\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert}\, \vartheta_\lambda(L, L')\, \varphi(L) . \end{equation}\] In bases, \(\Theta_\lambda\) is a finite matrix whose block in row \(L'\) and column \(L\) is \(\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert^{-1}\vartheta_\lambda(L,L')\), restricted from \(V_\lambda(L)^{\Gamma_L}\) to \(V_\lambda(L')^{\Gamma_{L'}}\). For a matrix coefficient \(P\) of \(\mathbb{S}_\lambda\), the series \(\sum_x P(x)\, q^{h(x,x)}\) is a classical theta series with polynomial coefficients, and the corresponding entry of \(\vartheta_\lambda\) is its value at \(q = e^{-\pi}\).

Thus \(\Theta_{L,L'}\) is a theta function of the Hermitian lattice \(\operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(L,L')\), which is unimodular of rank \(nn'\) by Problem 23.1.1 below. Chapter 12, The Fock model and theta intertwiners, treats one-variable theta functions of self-dual lattices with semicharacters; for commensurable lattices whose semicharacters agree on their intersection it defines an intertwiner, and for the neighbours of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) studied there the composition scalar is a root of unity given by a Gauss sum. The problems of this chapter use only the series itself.

How large are these entries?

Tag 22N3

Problem 23.2.6. \(\log c_a^2 \le a \log a - a(1 + \log 2\pi) + O(\log a)\), where \(\log 0 := -\infty\).

Numerically the bound is attained up to \(O(\log a)\): for \(a = 40, 80, \dots, 240\) the left side exceeds the right side without the \(O(\log a)\) by between \(1.7\) and \(4.8\). The scale \(2\pi\) belongs to the Gaussian; the rational part \(F^{(a)}(0)\) of \(c_a\) sees instead the period. By Problem 11.3.7, \(\varpi^2/2\pi = \Omega/4\) with \(\Omega = 2\varpi^2/\pi\), and by Theorem 11.3.1, \(\log \Omega = \gamma + \log \pi - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\). Combining these with Problems 23.2.1 and 23.2.6 gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:F-size}\tag{2} \log F^{(a)}(0)^2 \le a \log a - a\Bigl(1 + \gamma + \log\frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{L'(1,\chi)}{L(1,\chi)}\Bigr) + O(\log a) . \end{equation}\] This is how Euler’s constant and \(L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)\) enter the linear term of a size bound.

§23.3. The Gaussian norm and the norm of the lift Tag 22N4

The size of an entry is one thing; the size of the lift is measured by its operator norm. What is \(\lVert \Theta_{(a)} \rVert\) in the smallest case?

Let \(\mathcal{F}_m\) be the space of entire functions on \(\mathbb{C}^m\) with \[\lVert f \rVert^2 := \pi^{-m}\int_{\mathbb{C}^m} \lvert f(z) \rvert^2 e^{-\lvert z\rvert^2} \, dA(z) < \infty .\]

For \(m = 1\) this is the norm of Chapter 5, in which the monomials are orthogonal and \(\lVert z^a \rVert^2 = a!\) (Problem 5.4.4); since the measure and the inner product of two monomials factor over the variables, the monomials \(z^\nu\) are orthogonal in \(\mathcal{F}_m\) with \(\lVert z^\nu \rVert^2 = \nu!\). By Problems 21.3.2 and 5.4.4, sending \(e^\nu\) to \(z^\nu\) identifies \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^m)\) with the homogeneous polynomials of degree \(a\) in \(\mathcal{F}_m\), with \(\lVert e^\nu \rVert^2 = \lVert z^\nu \rVert^2/a!\). In this normalization the size of a derivative is controlled as follows.

Tag 22N6

Problem 23.3.1. If \(\Theta(Z) = \sum_\nu b_\nu Z^\nu\) and \(P = \sum_\nu P_\nu x^\nu\), then \[\lvert P(\partial)\Theta(0) \rvert^2 \le \lVert P \rVert_{\mathcal{F}}^2 \sum_{\nu \in \operatorname{supp} P} \nu!\, \lvert b_\nu \rvert^2 .\]

What is the squared norm of the lift from \(\mathrm{U}(1)\) to \(\mathrm{U}(2)\)?

Tag 22N7

Problem 23.3.2. For \(\Theta_{(a)} \colon \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1) \to \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2)\) and \(\varphi \ne 0\), \[\frac{\lVert \Theta_{(a)}\varphi \rVert^2}{\lVert \varphi \rVert^2} = \frac{1}{128} \sum_{x, y \in \mathcal{O}^2} h(x,y)^a e^{-\pi(\lvert x\rvert^2 + \lvert y\rvert^2)} = \frac{1}{128} \sum_j \binom{a}{j} c_j^2 c_{a-j}^2 .\] For \(a = 4\) and \(a = 8\) it equals \(\tfrac14 \vartheta_0^4 (\varpi/2\pi)^8\) and \(1296\, \vartheta_0^4 (\varpi/2\pi)^{16}\).

Used in: Chapter 68; Chapter 69; Chapter 76

The middle expression is a theta sum over \(\operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(I_1 \oplus I_1, I_2)\), the lattice of pairs \((x, y)\). This is the structure of the Rallis inner product formula [Ra]. More generally, the diagonal kernel of \(\Theta'_\lambda\Theta_\lambda\) is a stabilizer-weighted sum over \(L'\in\mathrm{Cl}_{n'}\) of theta series on the doubled lattice \(\operatorname{Hom}_\mathcal{O}(L\oplus L,L')\). In the convergent range the Siegel–Weil formula identifies the corresponding adelic theta integral with an Eisenstein series [We65], and the doubled theta sum is the construction behind the inner product formula of [Ra]. For \(4 \mid a\) the source \(\mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1)\) is a line, and Problem 23.3.2 computes the scalar directly.

The two smallest lifts satisfy the following norm estimate.

Tag 22N8

Theorem 23.3.1. For the lifts \(\Theta_{(a)} \colon \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1) \to \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(n')\) with \(n' = 1, 2\), one has \(\lVert \Theta_{(a)} \rVert^2 = c_a^2/16\) and \(\lVert \Theta_{(a)} \rVert^2 = \tfrac{1}{128}\sum_j \binom{a}{j} c_j^2 c_{a-j}^2\) respectively, and in both cases \[\log \lVert \Theta_{(a)} \rVert^2 \le a \log a - a(1 + \log 2\pi) + O(\log a) .\]

Used in: Chapter 75

So the squared norm of the lift is at most a power of \(a\) times one factorial, \(\lVert z^a \rVert^2 = a!\), times \((2\pi)^{-a}\). Numerically, for \(n' = 2\) and \(a = 40, 80, \dots, 240\), the left side exceeds the right side without the \(O(\log a)\) by an amount growing from \(1.5\) to \(5.1\), roughly like \(2\log a - 5.8\).

What kind of number is \(c_a^2/16\)? For \(4 \mid n\) the function \(\upsilon_n((\alpha)) := \alpha^n\) is well defined on the nonzero ideals of \(\mathcal{O}\), since the units of \(\mathcal{O}\) are fourth roots of unity; it is a Hecke character. For \(\Re s > n/2 + 1\) let \[L(\bar\upsilon_n, s) := \sum_{\mathfrak{a} \ne 0} \overline{\upsilon_n(\mathfrak{a})}\, \mathrm{N}\mathfrak{a}^{-s} = \frac14 \sum_{\alpha \in \mathcal{O}}{}' \bar\alpha^n \lvert\alpha\rvert^{-2s} .\] For \(n \ge 4\) the series converges at \(s = n\), where it equals \(\frac14\sum'_\alpha \alpha^{-n} = (2\varpi)^n H_n/(4\cdot n!)\) by Problem 11.3.1.

§23.4. The seesaw Tag 22N9

A seesaw computes one period in two ways: lift to a large group and restrict to a subgroup, or restrict first and lift between smaller groups [Ku]. In the definite lattice model, take an orthogonal decomposition \(L' = L_1 \perp L_2\).

Tag 22NA

Problem 23.4.1. \(\Theta_{L, L_1 \perp L_2}(Z_1, Z_2) = \Theta_{L, L_1}(Z_1)\, \Theta_{L, L_2}(Z_2)\).

Here is the smallest case. Write \(I_2 = Y \perp \mathcal{O}z\) with \(Y = \mathcal{O}e_1\) and \(z = e_2\), and let \(0 \le j \le a\) be integers with \(4 \mid j\) and \(4 \mid a - j\). For \(\psi \in \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(2)\) and \(\xi \in \mathcal{A}_{(j)}(1)\), viewed as a form on the class set of \(Y\), define the period of \(\psi\) along \(Y\) by \[P(\psi, \xi) := \frac{1}{\lvert\Gamma_Y\rvert} \bigl\langle \psi(Y \perp \mathcal{O}z),\; \xi(Y)\, e_2^{a-j} \bigr\rangle .\] On the other side let \(\theta_z := \vartheta_{(a-j)}(\mathcal{O}z, I_1)\, e_2^{a-j} \in \operatorname{Sym}^{a-j}(\mathbb{C})\), the vector obtained from \(\mathcal{O}z\) alone. Since \(4\mid a\), we identify each vector of \(\operatorname{Sym}^a((I_1)_\mathbb{C})\) with the unique form in \(\mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1)\) taking that value at \(I_1\).

§23.5. Chains Tag 22NC

How do kernels and norms behave under composition?

Tag 22ND

Problem 23.5.1. The composite \(\mathcal{A}_\lambda(n) \to \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n') \to \mathcal{A}_\lambda(n'')\) of two lifts is \(\varphi \mapsto \bigl(L'' \mapsto \sum_{L \in \mathrm{Cl}_n} \lvert\Gamma_L\rvert^{-1} K(L, L'')\,\varphi(L)\bigr)\), with the kernel \[K(L, L'') = \sum_{L' \in \mathrm{Cl}_{n'}} \frac{1}{\lvert\Gamma_{L'}\rvert}\, \vartheta_\lambda(L', L'')\, \vartheta_\lambda(L, L') .\]

Used in: Chapter 52; Chapter 69
Tag 22NE

Problem 23.5.2. In the bases \(e^\mu\) of \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^n)\) and \(\operatorname{Sym}^a(\mathbb{C}^{n'})\), every entry of \(\vartheta_{(a)}(I_n, I_{n'})\) lies in \(\vartheta_0^{nn'} (\varpi/2\pi)^a\, \mathbb{Q}\).

So for fixed \(n\), \(n'\) and \(a\), the entries of the standard kernel have the common factor \(\vartheta_0^{nn'}(\varpi/2\pi)^a\). For a term of the composite kernel passing through standard lattices of ranks \(n_0, \dots, n_m\), the product of its kernel entries lies in \(\vartheta_0^{\sum_{j=1}^m n_{j-1}n_j}(\varpi/2\pi)^{ma}\,\mathbb{Q}\); the class sums add the weights \(1/\lvert\Gamma_L\rvert\).

Tag 22NF

Problem 23.5.3. Let \(S \subset R\) be commutative rings and \(D_1, \dots, D_m \in R\). Suppose that in a composite of \(m\) maps \((T_j f)(i') = \sum_i h_j(i)\, K_j(i, i')\, f(i)\), with finite sums and \(h_j(i) \in R\), all \(K_j(i,i')\) and all starting values lie in \(S\), and that \(D_j h_j(i) \in S\) for all \(i\). Then \(D_1 \cdots D_m\) times every final value lies in \(S\).

How large is a chain? It is measured by the volume \(\det(u^*u)\) of the map \(u\) it defines.

Tag 2368

Problem 23.5.4. Let \(u_j \colon H_{j-1} \to H_j\), \(1 \le j \le m\), be linear maps between complex inner product spaces of the same finite dimension, and \(u := u_m \circ \dots \circ u_1\). Then \(\det(u^*u) = \prod_j \det(u_j^*u_j)\). For \(4 \mid a\) the space \(\mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1)\) is a line, and for the chain of \(m\) lifts \(\Theta_{(a)} \colon \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1) \to \mathcal{A}_{(a)}(1)\) this is \((c_a^2/16)^m\).

Tag 2369

Problem 23.5.5. Let \(u(\omega) \colon H \to H'\) be linear maps of finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces, depending on a parameter \(\omega\), with \(\langle u(\omega)x, u(\omega)y\rangle = a(\omega)\langle S(\omega)x, y\rangle\) for all \(x, y \in H\), where \(a(\omega) \in \mathbb{C}\) and \(S(\omega) \in \operatorname{End}(H)\). Then \(\det(u(\omega)^*u(\omega)) = a(\omega)^{\dim H}\det S(\omega)\). Hence, if \(\det(u(1)^*u(1)) \ne 0\) and \(a(\omega) = c\, b(\omega)\) with \(c\) independent of \(\omega\) and \(b(1) \ne 0\), the ratio \(\det(u(\omega)^*u(\omega))/\det(u(1)^*u(1))\) equals \((b(\omega)/b(1))^{\dim H}\det S(\omega)/\det S(1)\) and does not involve \(c\).

For the chains of the proof see Chapter 37. Theorem 68.4.1 compares the determinant of the chain from \(Y_0\) to \(Y_m\) with the determinants of the single lifts from \(W_j\) to \(V_j\). Problem 23.5.4 gives a model for composition, and Problem 23.5.5 explains how division by the value at the trivial twist cancels the common constant.

Beyond the smallest cases a chain needs more. The class sets grow with the rank (Section 21.2), and in the proof the norm of a lift comes from Rallis’s inner product formula (Theorem 69.2.1) in place of Problem 23.3.2. For the moments \(c_a\) the rational factors are the derivatives \(F^{(a)}(0)\); Problem 15.5.3 gives a one-variable obstruction at inert primes, and Theorem 16.3.1 gives a divisibility bound for the normalized two-variable moments \(t(a,w)\) of Section 15.1 when \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). Constants need not be exact: one seesaw constant works for all twists (Theorem 52.4.1), and constants independent of the twist cancel in the ratio of Theorem 68.4.1.

In the upper-bound argument of the proof, the seesaw transfers a size bound for the composite lift to the lower chain on Poincaré images (Theorem 52.4.1). In the lower-bound argument, the norms of single lifts enter the factorization of the ratio of chain determinants (Theorem 68.4.1).
