Prerequisites.

Complete local noetherian rings and Hensel’s lemma; the Artin–Rees lemma [AM, Cor. 10.10]; lengths of modules. From Chapter 27, Odd Tate twists: the groups \(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\), \(G_p\), \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e)\), Problem 27.1.1 and Theorem 27.1.2. From Chapter 30, Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes: Problem 30.1.1, the congruence (1), the lattices of Section 30.2 (Problems 30.2.1–30.2.4), the inputs (D), (I), (O) of Section 30.3 with Problems 30.3.1 and 30.3.3, Section 30.4, in particular Problems 30.4.6–30.4.8, and Section 30.5.

Section 30.4 of Chapter 30 observed that the Hecke algebra sees a Galois representation only through its traces. The Hecke algebra on the Eisenstein and cuspidal eigenspaces in Problem 31.1.6 has two components. In general the traces in a Hecke algebra need not be realized by a representation over that algebra. What structure do traces alone carry, and how do they measure \(\mathrm{Sha}\)? The structure is a pseudocharacter; the ideal of products \(b \cdot c\) of Chapter 30 can be read off from the traces; and the quotient by it carries extension classes, which bound the congruence from above by the length of a Selmer group. In the eigenform example, the cocycle obeys every linear relation among the matrices \(U(g)-1\) for the twist \(U=\chi^{1-k}\rho_f\) (Problem 31.4.1). Theorem 31.4.1 realizes such cocycles by equivariant maps on submodules of families of matrices. Throughout, \(p\) is an odd prime.

§31.1. Pseudocharacters of dimension two Tag 22VN

Let \(G\) be a group and \(R\) a commutative ring in which \(2\) is invertible. A pseudocharacter of dimension \(2\) of \(G\) with values in \(R\) is a pair \((T, D)\) of a function \(T \colon G \to R\) and a homomorphism \(D \colon G \to R^\times\) such that \(T(1) = 2\) and, for all \(g, h \in G\), \[T(gh) = T(hg), \qquad T(gh) + D(g)\, T(g^{-1}h) = T(g)\, T(h) .\] By [Ch, Lemma 1.9] such pairs are the same as Chenevier’s determinants of dimension \(2\). The function \(T\) is then a pseudocharacter of dimension \(2\) in the sense of Taylor [Ta] and Rouquier [Ro], and conversely every such \(T\) arises in this way [Ch, Prop. 1.29]; see also [BC]. The notion is due to Wiles in dimension two and to Taylor in general [BC].

Tag 22VP

Problem 31.1.1. For every homomorphism \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(R)\), \((\operatorname{tr}\rho, \det\rho)\) is a pseudocharacter. In particular, so is \((\psi_1 + \psi_2, \psi_1\psi_2)\) for any two characters \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G \to R^\times\).

Traces of products need not see every entry.

Tag 22VQ

Problem 31.1.3. If \((T, D)\) is a pseudocharacter, then \(D(g) = \tfrac12\bigl(T(g)^2 - T(g^2)\bigr)\) and \(T(g^{-1}) = D(g)^{-1} T(g)\) for all \(g \in G\).

So \(D\) is determined by \(T\), and we often write \(T\) for the pair.

Tag 22VR

Problem 31.1.4. Let \(R \subseteq R_1 \times R_2\) be a subring in which \(2\) is invertible, and let \((T_i, D_i)\) be pseudocharacters with values in \(R_i\). If \((T_1(g), T_2(g)) \in R\) for every \(g\), then \(((T_1, T_2), (D_1, D_2))\) is a pseudocharacter with values in \(R\).

The first example of a pseudocharacter that is not visibly a trace is the one carried by a Hecke algebra with one Eisenstein and one cuspidal point. Let \(G\) be profinite, let \(\chi \colon G \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times\) be a continuous character whose reduction \(\bar\chi\) is not trivial, and let \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)\) be a continuous representation with \(\operatorname{tr}\rho(G) \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_p\), \(\operatorname{tr}\rho \equiv 1 + \chi \pmod p\) and \(\operatorname{tr}\rho \ne 1 + \chi\). Put \[e := \min_{g \in G} v_p\bigl(\operatorname{tr}\rho(g) - 1 - \chi(g)\bigr), \qquad \mathcal{T}(g) := \bigl(1 + \chi(g), \operatorname{tr}\rho(g)\bigr) \in \mathbb{Z}_p\times \mathbb{Z}_p.\]

Tag 22VS

Problem 31.1.5. The \(\mathbb{Z}_p\)-subalgebra of \(\mathbb{Z}_p\times \mathbb{Z}_p\) generated by the \(\mathcal{T}(g)\) is \(\mathbf{T}_e := \{(u, v) : u \equiv v \pmod{p^e}\}\), and \(x \mapsto (0, p^e)\) induces \(\mathbb{Z}_p[x]/(x(x - p^e)) \cong \mathbf{T}_e\). The function \(\mathcal{T}\) is a pseudocharacter with values in \(\mathbf{T}_e\).

Used in: Problem 31.2.7

The ring \(\mathbf{T}_e\) is the toy Hecke algebra: \(x = 0\) is the Eisenstein point, \(x = p^e\) the cuspidal point, and \(e\) measures how close they are. It occurs in nature.

Tag 22VT

Problem 31.1.6. The \(\mathbb{Z}_{691}\)-subalgebra of \(\mathrm{End}(M_{12}(\mathbb{Q}) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_{691})\) generated by the Hecke operators \(T_n\), \(n \ge 1\), on modular forms of weight \(12\) and level \(1\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb{Z}_{691}[x]/(x(x - 691))\).

§31.2. The reducibility ideal Tag 22VU

Let \(R\) be a complete local noetherian ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) and residue field \(\mathbb{F}\) of characteristic \(\ne 2\) (so \(2 \in R^\times\)), and let \((T, D)\) be a pseudocharacter of \(G\) with values in \(R\) such that \(T \equiv \chi_1 + \chi_2 \pmod{\mathfrak m}\) for two distinct characters \(\chi_1, \chi_2 \colon G \to \mathbb{F}^\times\). Fix \(\sigma_0 \in G\) with \(\chi_1(\sigma_0) \ne \chi_2(\sigma_0)\).

Tag 22VV

Problem 31.2.1. The polynomial \(X^2 - T(\sigma_0)X + D(\sigma_0)\) has roots \(\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \in R\) with \(\lambda_i \equiv \chi_i(\sigma_0) \pmod{\mathfrak m}\), and \(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 \in R^\times\).

Define \(a, d \colon G \to R\) by \[a(g) := \frac{T(\sigma_0 g) - \lambda_2 T(g)}{\lambda_1 - \lambda_2} , \qquad d(g) := T(g) - a(g) .\]

Tag 22VW

Problem 31.2.2. Suppose that \(T = \operatorname{tr}\rho\) for \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}_2(R)\) with \(\rho(\sigma_0) = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2)\), and write \(\rho = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a' & b \\ c & d' \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Then \(a = a'\), \(d = d'\), and \(a(gh) - a(g)a(h) = b(g)c(h)\).

So \(a\) and \(d\) are the diagonal entries of a representation that need not exist, and the defect of \(a\) from being multiplicative stands in for the products \(b(g)c(h)\).

Tag 22VX

Problem 31.2.3. \(a \equiv \chi_1\) and \(d \equiv \chi_2 \pmod{\mathfrak m}\). In particular \(a(g) \in R^\times\).

Tag 22VY

Problem 31.2.4. \(D(g) = a(g)T(g) - a(g^2)\) for every \(g \in G\).

Define the reducibility ideal \(I \subseteq R\) as the ideal generated by the elements \(a(gh) - a(g)a(h)\), \(g, h \in G\).

Tag 22VZ

Problem 31.2.5. Modulo \(I\), \(a\) and \(d\) are characters \(G \to (R/I)^\times\), \(T \equiv a + d\) and \(D \equiv ad\).

Tag 22W2

Problem 31.2.6. Let \(J \subsetneq R\) be an ideal, and let \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G \to (R/J)^\times\) be characters with \(\psi_i \equiv \chi_i \pmod{\mathfrak m}\) and \(T \equiv \psi_1 + \psi_2 \pmod J\). Then \(\psi_1 \equiv a\) and \(\psi_2 \equiv d \pmod J\), and \(I \subseteq J\).

Problems 31.2.5 and 31.2.6 prove the following; in particular \(I\) does not depend on \(\sigma_0\). Over a henselian local ring in which \(d!\) is invertible, Bellaïche and Chenevier give the corresponding reducibility ideal for each partition of the pairwise distinct, absolutely irreducible residual constituents of a \(d\)-dimensional pseudocharacter [BC, Prop. 1.5.1].

Tag 22W3

Theorem 31.2.1. \(I\) is the smallest ideal \(J\) of \(R\) such that \(T\) is congruent modulo \(J\) to a sum of two characters lifting \(\chi_1\) and \(\chi_2\).

For a representation \(\rho\) over a discrete valuation ring as in Section 30.2, Problems 31.2.2 and 30.4.6 give \(I = \pi^{m+n}\mathcal{O}\), where \(m + n\) is the length of the segment of stable lattices. Return to the toy Hecke algebra.

Tag 22W4

Problem 31.2.7. In Problem 31.1.5, suppose that \(\rho\) is irreducible, and let \(m + n\) be the length of its segment of stable lattices (Problem 30.2.3, with \(\chi_1 = 1\), \(\chi_2 = \bar\chi\)). Then \(m + n \ge e\), and the reducibility ideal of \(\mathcal{T}\) is \(\{0\} \times p^{m+n}\mathbb{Z}_p= p^{m+n-e}\, x\mathbf{T}_e\).

Define the Eisenstein ideal of a pseudocharacter \(T\) with respect to two given characters \(\psi_1, \psi_2 \colon G \to R^\times\) as the ideal \(I_\mathrm{Eis}\) generated by the \(T(g) - \psi_1(g) - \psi_2(g)\). By Theorem 31.2.1, \(I \subseteq I_\mathrm{Eis}\) whenever the \(\psi_i\) lift the \(\chi_i\). In the setting of Chapter 30 the two ideals coincide.

Tag 22W5

Problem 31.2.8. Let \(k\), \(p\), \(f\), \(\lambda\) be as in Section 30.3, and let \(m + n\) be the length of the segment of stable lattices of \(\rho_f\). Then \[m + n = \min_{\ell \ne p} v\bigl(a_\ell(f) - 1 - \ell^{k-1}\bigr) ,\] the minimum over primes \(\ell \ne p\). So the reducibility ideal of \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_f\) equals its Eisenstein ideal for \((1, \chi^{k-1})\).

For \(\Delta\) and \(p = 691\) both sides are \(1\) (Problem 30.4.8). When \(K = \mathbb{Q}_p\), in the toy Hecke algebra built from \(\rho = \rho_f\) and \(\chi^{k-1}\) we get \(e = m + n\), and the reducibility ideal is \(x\mathbf{T}_e\): the Eisenstein component \(\mathbf{T}_e/x\mathbf{T}_e = \mathbb{Z}_p\) is the largest quotient on which \(\mathcal{T}\) is reducible.

§31.3. The quotient by the reducibility ideal and \(\mathrm{Ext}^1\) Tag 22W6

Ribet’s lemma produced a class modulo \(\pi\). The reducibility ideal produces a class modulo \(I\), which generates a copy of \(\mathcal{O}/I\). Keep the notation of Section 30.2, with \(\chi_1 \ne \chi_2\), and write \(\rho\) in the basis \(\pi^m e_1, e_2\) of \(L_m\) as \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Then the values of \(b\) generate \(\mathcal{O}\), the values of \(c\) generate \(I = \pi^{m+n}\mathcal{O}\), and by Problem 31.2.5 the reductions \(\psi_1, \psi_2\) of \(a, d\) modulo \(I\) are characters. Put \(\psi := \psi_1\psi_2^{-1}\).

Tag 22W7

Problem 31.3.1. \(\kappa(g) := b(g)\,d(g)^{-1} \bmod I\) is a cocycle on \(G\) with values in \((\mathcal{O}/I)(\psi)\).

Tag 22W8

Problem 31.3.2. The \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule of \(H^1(G, (\mathcal{O}/I)(\psi))\) generated by the class of \(\kappa\) is isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}/I\).

Thus the quotient \(\mathcal{O}/I\) embeds in a group of extensions of \(\psi_2\) by \(\psi_1\). For two distinct residual characters over a henselian local ring \(R\), Bellaïche and Chenevier construct an injection from \(\operatorname{Hom}_R(B,R/I)\) to the algebraic extension group of \(\psi_2\) by \(\psi_1\), where \(B\) is the upper right module of a chosen Cayley–Hamilton quotient [BC, Thm. 1.5.5]; for the continuous extension group under additional hypotheses, see [BC, Prop. 1.5.10(ii)]. Now take \(G = G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\), and \(f\), \(\lambda\), \(\mathcal{O}\) as in Problem 31.2.8, so that \(\psi \equiv \chi^{1-k} \pmod I\). As in Section 30.5, \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\) is the group of classes in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, (K/\mathcal{O})(1-k))\) that vanish on \(G_p\). Use the coefficient embedding \((\mathcal{O}/I)(1-k)\hookrightarrow(K/\mathcal{O})(1-k)\), \(\bar a\mapsto a/\pi^{m+n}+\mathcal{O}\).

Tag 22W9

Problem 31.3.3. The class of \(\kappa\) vanishes on \(G_p\), and its image in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, (K/\mathcal{O})(1-k))\) generates a submodule isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}/I\). Hence \(\operatorname{length}_\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \otimes \mathcal{O}\bigr) \ge m + n\).

Used in: Problem 31.4.1; Chapter 29; Chapter 83

This is the direction of Ribet and of Mazur and Wiles [MW], for one eigenform: a congruence of exponent \(m + n\) produces a cyclic piece of \(\mathrm{Sha}\) of that length. For \(\Delta\) it gives \(\operatorname{length}\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q}, 11) \ge 1\), and Theorem 27.1.2 gives equality. Read the other way, Problem 31.3.3 bounds a congruence by a Selmer group: \(m + n \le \operatorname{length}_\mathcal{O}\bigl(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \otimes \mathcal{O}\bigr)\). In the proof, a Selmer length contributes, in this direction, to the upper bound for the cost of clearing a projection; this is the step of Chapter 80, Denominators of projections to lifts, that this chapter serves. In Problem 31.3.3 the class already lies in the kernel of restriction to \(G_p\), so its full length contributes to the lower bound for \(\mathrm{Sha}\).

§31.4. Cocycles that obey the relations Tag 237P

The proof works with cocycles obeying the simultaneous relations below, and Problem 31.4.1 gives an example. Let \(\Lambda\) be a commutative ring, \(G\) a group, \(X\) a finite set, and, for \(i \in X\), let \(U_i \colon G \to M_q(\Lambda)\) be homomorphisms of monoids; let \(\varrho \colon G \to M_s(\Lambda)\) be another, and \(\alpha \in \Lambda\). Put \(\mathcal{N} := M_q(\Lambda)^X\), on which \(g\) acts by \((m_i)_i \mapsto (U_i(g)m_i)_i\), and \(U(g) - 1 := (U_i(g) - 1)_i \in \mathcal{N}\). A map \(\gamma \colon G \to (\Lambda/\alpha)^s\) is a cocycle for \(\varrho\) if \(\gamma(gh) = \gamma(g) + \varrho(g)\gamma(h)\). It obeys the relations among the \(U(g) - 1\) if, for every finitely supported \(\theta \colon G \to \Lambda\), \[\sum_{g} \theta(g)\,\bigl(U(g) - 1\bigr) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sum_{g} \theta(g)\,\gamma(g) = 0 .\] If all \(U_i\) are trivial, only \(\gamma = 0\) obeys them.

Tag 237Q

Problem 31.4.1. In the setting of Problem 31.3.3, put \(\Lambda := \mathcal{O}\), \(\alpha := \pi^{m+n}\), \(U := \chi^{1-k}\rho_f\) in the basis \(\pi^m e_1, e_2\) of \(L_m\) (one representation, \(q = 2\)) and \(\varrho := \chi^{1-k}\) (\(s = 1\)). Then \(\kappa(g) = \chi(g)^{1-k} b(g) \bmod I\), and \(\kappa\) is a cocycle for \(\varrho\) that obeys the relations among the \(U(g) - 1\).

From now on let \(\gamma\) be a cocycle for \(\varrho\) that obeys the relations among the \(U(g) - 1\), and let \(\mathcal{M} \subseteq \mathcal{N}\) be the \(\Lambda\)-span of the \(U(g) - 1\), \(g \in G\).

Tag 237R

Problem 31.4.2. There is a unique \(\Lambda\)-linear map \(\phi \colon \mathcal{M} \to (\Lambda/\alpha)^s\) with \(\phi(U(g) - 1) = \gamma(g)\) for all \(g\). The module \(\mathcal{M}\) is stable under \(G\), and \(\phi(g y) = \varrho(g)\phi(y)\) for \(y \in \mathcal{M}\).

Used in: Chapter 83

So the graph of \(\phi\) is a \(G\)-stable submodule of \(\mathcal{N} \times (\Lambda/\alpha)^s\), with \(g\) acting on the second factor through \(\varrho\), on which the first projection is injective. It is usually too small: even over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), \(\mathcal{M}\) need not contain \(p^j\mathcal{N}\) for any \(j \ge 0\). Let \(\Lambda\) be noetherian. By the Artin–Rees lemma [AM, Cor. 10.10], applied to the submodule \(\mathcal{M}\) of the finitely generated module \(\mathcal{N}\), there is \(\nu \ge 1\) with \(\mathcal{M} \cap \alpha^\nu \mathcal{N} \subseteq \alpha\mathcal{M}\).

Tag 237S

Problem 31.4.3. Let \(\mathcal{M} \cap \alpha^\nu \mathcal{N} \subseteq \alpha\mathcal{M}\). Then \[Y := \bigl\{ \bigl(y + \alpha^\nu v,\ \phi(y)\bigr) : y \in \mathcal{M},\ v \in \mathcal{N} \bigr\}\] is a \(G\)-stable submodule of \(\mathcal{N} \times (\Lambda/\alpha)^s\), the first projection is injective on \(Y\), and \(\bigl(U(g) - 1 + \alpha^\nu v, \gamma(g)\bigr) \in Y\) for all \(g \in G\) and \(v \in \mathcal{N}\).

Used in: Problem 31.4.4
Tag 237T

Problems 31.4.2 and 31.4.3 prove the following theorem, which is stated as in the proof.

Tag 237U

Theorem 31.4.1. Let \(\Lambda\) be a commutative noetherian ring, \(\alpha \in \Lambda\), \(G\) a group, \(X\) a finite set, and \(U_i \colon G \to M_q(\Lambda)\), \(i \in X\), and \(\varrho \colon G \to M_s(\Lambda)\) homomorphisms of monoids. Let \(\gamma \colon G \to (\Lambda/\alpha)^s\) be a cocycle for \(\varrho\) that obeys the relations among the \(U(g) - 1\). Then there are \(\nu \ge 0\) and a \(G\)-stable \(\Lambda\)-submodule \(Y\) of \(M_q(\Lambda)^X \times (\Lambda/\alpha)^s\), with \(g\) acting by \(\bigl((m_i)_i, w\bigr) \mapsto \bigl((U_i(g)m_i)_i, \varrho(g)w\bigr)\), such that the first projection is injective on \(Y\) and \(\bigl((U_i(g) - 1)_i + \alpha^\nu v, \gamma(g)\bigr) \in Y\) for all \(g \in G\) and all \(v \in M_q(\Lambda)^X\).

Used in: Chapter 82; Chapter 83

If \(\Lambda\) is a discrete valuation ring and \(\alpha \ne 0\), the first projection identifies \(Y\) with a lattice in \(M_q(\Lambda)^X\): its image contains \(\alpha^\nu M_q(\Lambda)^X\).
