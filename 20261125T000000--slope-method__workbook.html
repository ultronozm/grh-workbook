Prerequisites.

Linear algebra over \(\mathbb{C}\): Hermitian and positive semidefinite matrices, and Hadamard’s inequality, in the form \(\det A \le a_{11} \cdots a_{nn}\) for a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix \(A = (a_{ik})\), or \(\lvert \det(v_1, \dots, v_n) \rvert \le \lVert v_1 \rVert \cdots \lVert v_n \rVert\) for vectors. Dedekind domains, the absolute norm, the product formula [Ne, Ch. I and Ch. III], and the unique extension of the \(\ell\)-adic absolute value to an algebraic closure \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) of \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\) [Ne, Ch. II, §4]. Transcendence bases [La, Ch. VIII, §1], and two consequences: \(\mathbb{C}\) and \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) are isomorphic fields, both being algebraically closed of characteristic \(0\) and of transcendence degree the continuum over \(\mathbb{Q}\); and an isomorphism between two subfields of \(\mathbb{C}\) that are finitely generated over \(\mathbb{Q}\) extends to an automorphism of \(\mathbb{C}\). Legendre’s formula \(v_p(n!) = \sum_{e \ge 1} \lfloor n/p^e \rfloor\), Stirling’s formula, and the von Mangoldt function \(\Lambda\). No earlier chapter is used. The next chapter, A twist for each prime, continues this one.

§5.1. Divisibility against size Tag 226J

How can a divisibility statement bound a sum over primes? A binomial coefficient illustrates Chebyshev’s factorial method [Ch]. Let \(\theta(x) := \sum_{p \le x} \log p\). In the next two problems let \(n\) be a nonnegative integer.

Tag 226K

Problem 5.1.1. Every prime \(p\) with \(n < p \le 2n\) divides \(\binom{2n}{n}\), and \(\binom{2n}{n} \le 4^n\). Hence \(\theta(2n) - \theta(n) \le n \log 4\).

Used in: Chapter 14

The principle is the following. If a nonzero integer \(\Delta\) is divisible by \(p^{e_p}\) for each prime \(p\) in a set \(S\), where the \(e_p\) are nonnegative integers, then \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:det}\tag{1} \sum_{p \in S} e_p \log p \le \log \lvert \Delta \rvert . \end{equation}\]

For \(\Delta = n!\) and \(e_p = v_p(n!)\), with \(S\) the primes up to \(n\), both sides of (1) are known exactly.

Tag 226L

Problem 5.1.2. \(\log n! = \sum_{r \le n} \Lambda(r) \lfloor n/r \rfloor\).

By Stirling’s formula the left side is \(n\log n - n + O(\log n)\). Without the floor function the right side would be \(n \sum_{r \le n} \Lambda(r)/r\). Removing the floors overestimates the right side by \(\sum_{r \le n} \Lambda(r) \{n/r\}\), an error of order \(n\) by Chebyshev’s bounds, the scale of the linear term in Stirling’s formula; so this estimate cannot see \(\psi(n) - n\), where \(\psi(x) := \sum_{r \le x} \Lambda(r)\). For a finer comparison one can seek divisibility bounds with a continuous weight on prime powers, such as \((x/p^e - 1)_+\), where \(t_+ := \max(t, 0)\), and a size estimate whose error is smaller than its linear term.

The number \(\Delta\) will be a minor of a matrix.

Tag 226M

Problem 5.1.3. Let \(A \in M_r(\mathbb{Z})\) with \(\det A \ne 0\), and suppose that the \(j\)th column \(A_j\) is divisible by the positive integer \(g_j\). Then \[g_1 \cdots g_r \le \lvert \det A \rvert \le \lVert A_1 \rVert \cdots \lVert A_r \rVert .\] For the columns \((6, 6)\) and \((10, 20)\), with \(g_1 = 6\) and \(g_2 = 10\), the three numbers are \(60\), \(60\) and \(60\sqrt{10}\).

Used in: Problem 5.4.6

The same principle holds over a number field \(K\), where the product formula accounts for the size of a number at every place. At a finite place \(v\) write \(\mathcal{O}_v\), \(\mathfrak{p}_v\) and \(q_v\) for the valuation ring, its maximal ideal and the size of its residue field, and let \(\lvert x \rvert_v := q_v^{-\operatorname{ord}_v x}\). At an archimedean place use the usual modulus. Let \(n_v := 2\) at complex places and \(n_v := 1\) at the other places. Then \(\sum_v n_v \log \lvert x \rvert_v = 0\) for \(x \in K^\times\). Hence, if \(\gamma \in K^\times\) satisfies \(\lvert \gamma \rvert_v \le \exp(b_v - g_v)\) at every place, where \(b_v\) and \(g_v\) are real and vanish for almost all \(v\), then \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:scalar}\tag{2} \sum_v n_v g_v \le \sum_v n_v b_v . \end{equation}\] Typically \(\gamma\) is a minor of a matrix, \(b_v\) bounds it through the sizes of the entries, and \(g_v\) records an improvement from a congruence. The minor is chosen once over \(K\), but the basis in which one proves the congruence may vary with \(v\).

§5.2. A minor of a jet evaluation map Tag 226N

Where does a minor with many prime factors come from? One source is Frobenius. Throughout this section \(p\) denotes a prime. Let \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), so that a prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is inert and \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]/p\) is the field with \(p^2\) elements.

Tag 226P

Problem 5.2.1. If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(u \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), then \(\bar u \equiv u^p \pmod p\).

Thus complex conjugation is the Frobenius at every inert prime. Let \(u_1, \dots, u_I \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), let \(\mathcal{T} \subset \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^2\) be a finite set of \(s\) exponents, and let \(J\) be a positive integer. On the \(K\)-span of the monomials \(X^a Y^b\), \((a,b) \in \mathcal{T}\), consider the jet evaluation map \[\mathrm{ev} \colon f \longmapsto \bigl( (\partial_X^j f)(u_i, \bar u_i) \bigr)_{1 \le i \le I,\ 0 \le j < J} .\] Its matrix has entries in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\). Let \(g := X^p - Y\).

Tag 226Q

Problem 5.2.2. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). For \(f \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}][X, Y]\), \(u \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and integers \(j, k \ge 0\), the number \(\bigl(\partial_X^j (g^k f)\bigr)(u, \bar u)\) is divisible by \(p^k\).

Used in: Chapter 7: Problem 7.2.2
Tag 226R

Problem 5.2.3. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), and suppose that \((a - p, b + 1) \in \mathcal{T}\) whenever \((a, b) \in \mathcal{T}\) and \(a \ge p\). Then every \(s \times s\) minor of the matrix of \(\mathrm{ev}\) is divisible by \(p^{N_p}\), where \(N_p := \sum_{(a,b) \in \mathcal{T}} \lfloor a/p \rfloor\). The set \(\mathcal{T} = \{(a,b) : a + 2b \le N\}\) satisfies the hypothesis for every \(p\).

Used in: Theorem 5.2.1; Chapter 7: Problem 7.2.3

Let \(\beta := \max(1, \lvert u_1 \rvert, \dots, \lvert u_I \rvert)\), \(D := \sum_{\mathcal{T}} (a + b)\) and \(A_* := \max(\{1\} \cup \{a : (a,b) \in \mathcal{T}\})\). Every \(s \times s\) minor of the matrix of \(\mathrm{ev}\) has absolute value at most \(s!\,A_*^{s(J-1)}\beta^D\).

Tag 226S

Theorem 5.2.1. Suppose that \(\mathrm{ev}\) is injective, and let \(P\) be a set of primes \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) for which \(\mathcal{T}\) satisfies the hypothesis of Problem 5.2.3. Then \[\sum_{p \in P} \log p \sum_{(a,b) \in \mathcal{T}} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{a}{p} \Bigr\rfloor \le D \log\beta + \log s! + s(J-1)\log A_* .\]

Used in: Problem 5.4.10; Chapter 7

Over a finite field, Stepanov’s method for curves likewise compares the forced vanishing of an auxiliary function with a bound on its degree [Bom].

§5.3. Heights of families Tag 232Y

In Section 5.1 the size and the divisibility of one number were compared at the places of one field. The height bound in the Lean proof [GL] indexes its finite-place tests by pairs of a prime \(\ell\) and a field isomorphism \(\mathbb{C}\to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\). The complex number tested may vary with the isomorphism, even at a fixed prime. The height bound requires no number field as a parameter.

For a prime \(\ell\) let \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_\ell\) be the absolute value on \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) that extends the \(\ell\)-adic one, so that \(\lvert \ell \rvert_\ell = \ell^{-1}\). Write \(\iota \mid \ell\) for a field isomorphism \(\iota \colon \mathbb{C}\to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), put \(\lvert x \rvert_\iota := \lvert \iota(x) \rvert_\ell\) for \(x \in \mathbb{C}\), and let \(\bar\iota \mid \ell\) be the isomorphism \(x \mapsto \iota(\bar x)\). Let \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) be the group of all field automorphisms of \(\mathbb{C}\); most of them are not continuous. A family \(y\) assigns a complex number \(y_\iota\) to every isomorphism \(\iota \mid \ell\), for every prime \(\ell\).

Tag 232Z

Problem 5.3.1. Let \(\iota \mid \ell\). If \(x \in \mathbb{Q}\), then \(\lvert x \rvert_\iota = \lvert x \rvert_\ell\). If \(\ell \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(0 \ne x \in \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), then \(\lvert x \rvert_\iota = \ell^{-v}\), where \(\ell^v\) is the exact power of \(\ell\) dividing \(x\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\). If \(\ell = 5\), then exactly one of \(\lvert 2 + \mathrm{i}\rvert_\iota\) and \(\lvert 2 + \mathrm{i}\rvert_{\bar\iota}\) equals \(1/5\), and the other equals \(1\).

Used in: Chapter 6; Chapter 77

So the constant family \(2 + \mathrm{i}\) has different absolute values at different isomorphisms above one prime. The following notion bounds a family by one number. Let \(c \in \mathbb{R}\). A family \(y\) has height at most \(c\) if there are \(H \in \mathbb{C}\), \(\alpha \in \mathbb{R}\) and real numbers \(h(\ell)\), one for each prime \(\ell\), such that

  1. \(H \ne 0\), and \(h(\ell) \ge 0\) for every prime \(\ell\);

  2. \(\lvert \sigma H \rvert \le e^\alpha\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\);

  3. \(\lvert H y_\iota \rvert_\iota \le \ell^{h(\ell)}\) for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(\iota \mid \ell\);

  4. \(\alpha + \sum_{\ell \in U} h(\ell) \log \ell \le c\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes.

The multiplier \(H\) and the bound \(\alpha\) at infinity are common to all \(\iota\), while \(y_\iota\) may depend on \(\iota\), even among the isomorphisms above one prime. There is no condition on the sign of \(\alpha\). We write \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c\) when \(y\) has height at most \(c\).

Tag 2332

Problem 5.3.2. If the families \(y\) and \(y'\) have heights at most \(c\) and \(c'\), then the family \(\iota \mapsto y_\iota y'_\iota\) has height at most \(c + c'\). If \(y\) has height at most \(c\), then for every integer \(n \ge 0\) the family \(\iota \mapsto y_\iota^n\) has height at most \(nc\).

Used in: Chapter 6; Chapter 67; Chapter 68; Chapter 74; Chapter 79
Tag 2333

Problem 5.3.3. (a) Let \(e(\ell)\) be real numbers, of any sign, one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell) \le c\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes. If \(\lvert y_\iota \rvert_\iota \le e^{e(\ell)}\) for every \(\iota \mid \ell\), then \(y\) has height at most \(c\). (b) If \(H \ne 0\) and \(\lvert \sigma H \rvert \le e^c\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), then the constant family \(H^{-1}\) has height at most \(c\).

Used in: Chapter 68; Chapter 79

Can a height be negative? For the constant family \(1\), every admissible bound is nonnegative.

Tag 2334

Problem 5.3.4. If \(H \in \mathbb{C}\) is transcendental over \(\mathbb{Q}\), then for every prime \(\ell\) the numbers \(\lvert H \rvert_\iota\), \(\iota \mid \ell\), are unbounded. Hence a number \(H\) with \(\lvert H \rvert_\iota \le C\) for all \(\iota \mid 2\) is algebraic.

Tag 2335

Problem 5.3.5. If the constant family \(1\) has height at most \(c\), then \(c \ge 0\).

We now compare a height bound for a nonzero constant family with a uniform upper bound on the absolute values of its conjugates.

Tag 2336

Theorem 5.3.1. Let \(z \in \mathbb{C}\), \(z \ne 0\), and \(c, c' \in \mathbb{R}\). If the constant family \(z\) has height at most \(c\), and \(\lvert \sigma z \rvert \le e^{c'}\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), then \(-c \le c'\).

Used in: Problem 5.3.6; Chapter 6
Tag 2337

Problem 5.3.6. Let \(x \in \mathbb{Q}^\times\). The constant family \(x\) has height at most \(c\) if and only if \(c \ge -\log \lvert x \rvert\). For instance, the constant family \(2\) has height at most \(-\log 2\). If \(g = \prod_{p \in S} p^{e_p}\), with \(S\) a finite set of primes, divides the nonzero integer \(\Delta\), then the constant family \(\Delta\) has height at most \(-\log g\), and Theorem 5.3.1 then gives (1).

Tag 2338

Problem 5.3.7. Let \(S\) be a finite set of primes and \(N := \prod_{\ell \in S} \ell\). The family with \(y_\iota = \ell^{-1}\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell \in S\), and \(y_\iota = 1\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell \notin S\), has height at most \(c\) if and only if \(c \ge \log N\). For \(S = \{2, 3\}\) the least height is \(\log 6\).

§5.4. Index and covolume Tag 2339

The minors of Section 5.2 are Gaussian integers. In the proof of the general lattice theorem of Chapter 6, the size bound uses the covolume of the given vectors and the squared lengths of the completing vectors, both at the trivial twist. At each \(\iota \mid \ell\) the rows of one index matrix give combinations of the given vectors that are integral under every map allowed at \(\ell\). At each \(\iota\), the completing vectors must be integral under every map allowed at its prime after scaling by a nonzero scalar that may depend on the twist. Here we use a single map.

For \(\iota \mid \ell\) let \(\mathcal{O}_\iota := \{x \in \mathbb{C}: \lvert x \rvert_\iota \le 1\}\), a subring of \(\mathbb{C}\). To specify a lattice datum, give

  1. a complex vector space \(V\), a complex inner product space \(W\), with \(\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\) conjugate-linear in the first variable, and a linear map \(\Theta \colon V \to W\);

  2. for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(\iota \mid \ell\), an \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\)-submodule \(\Lambda_\iota \subseteq W\), such that \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) for all \(\varphi \in \Lambda_{\bar\iota}\) and \(\varphi' \in \Lambda_\iota\);

  3. a finite set \(I\), a linear map \(b \colon W \to \mathbb{C}^I\), and a matrix \(G \in M_I(\mathbb{Q})\), positive definite as a complex matrix, with \(\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle = b(\varphi)^* G\, b(\varphi')\) for all \(\varphi, \varphi' \in W\), where \({}^*\) is the conjugate transpose.

A vector \(y \in V\) is integral at \(\iota\) if \(\Theta y \in \Lambda_\iota\); these vectors form an \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\)-submodule \(M_\iota\) of \(V\). For the standard datum on \(\mathbb{C}^n\), take \(V = W = \mathbb{C}^n\), \(\Theta = b = \mathrm{id}\), \(G = 1\) and \(\Lambda_\iota = \mathcal{O}_\iota^n\).

Let \(S\) be a finite set, \(y = (y_s)_{s \in S}\) a family of vectors of \(V\), and \(\delta \in \mathbb{R}\). The family \(y\) has index at most \(\delta\) if there are real numbers \(e(\ell) \ge 0\), one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell) \log \ell \le \delta\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) some matrix \(u \in M_S(\mathcal{O}_\iota)\) satisfies \[\lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \ge \ell^{-e(\ell)} \quad\text{and}\quad \sum_{k \in S} u_{ik}\, y_k \in M_\iota \text{ for every } i \in S .\] The matrix may depend on \(\iota\). Every admissible \(\delta\) is nonnegative.

Tag 233A

Problem 5.4.1. The standard datum satisfies condition (ii). In it, a family of vectors with entries in \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) has index at most \(0\). On \(\mathbb{C}^1\), the family consisting of the one vector \(1/3\) has index at most \(\delta\) if and only if \(\delta \ge \log 3\).

Used in: Chapter 6
Tag 233B

Problem 5.4.2. If \(y = (y_s)_{s \in S}\) has index at most \(\delta\) and \(y' = (y'_s)_{s \in S'}\) has index at most \(\delta'\), then the family indexed by the disjoint union \(S \sqcup S'\) that restricts to \(y\) and \(y'\) has index at most \(\delta + \delta'\).

An index bound is usually proved one submodule at a time. For \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\)-submodules \(M, M'\) of \(V\) and \(z \in \mathcal{O}_\iota\), say that the colength of \(M'\) in \(M\) divides \(z\) if for every \(n \ge 0\) and all \(\Phi_1, \dots, \Phi_n \in M\) there is a matrix \(m \in M_n(\mathcal{O}_\iota)\) such that \(\det m\) divides \(z\) in \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\) and \(\sum_k m_{ik} \Phi_k \in M'\) for every \(i\). No inclusion between \(M\) and \(M'\) is assumed. This colength condition gives an upper bound for the index.

Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in V\), \(\delta \in \mathbb{R}\), and let \(e(\ell) \ge 0\), one for each prime \(\ell\), satisfy \(\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell) \log \ell \le \delta\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes. Suppose that for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) there are an integer \(j \ge 0\) and an \(\mathcal{O}_\iota\)-submodule \(M \subseteq V\) such that \(j \le e(\ell)\), every \(y_i\) lies in \(M\), and the colength of \(M_\iota\) in \(M\) divides \(\ell^j\). Then \(y\) has index at most \(\delta\).

Tag 227D

Problem 5.4.3. Let \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{Q}^n\) be a subgroup that contains \(\mathbb{Z}^n\) with finite index \(N\), and let \(y_1, \dots, y_n\) be a basis of \(\Lambda\). In the standard datum on \(\mathbb{C}^n\), the family \(y\) has index at most \(\log N\), and \(\det(y_i^* y_k)_{i,k} = N^{-2}\).

Now the size. For \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) and \(y \in V\) let \(v_\sigma(y) \in \mathbb{C}^I\) be the vector \(b(\Theta y)\) with \(\sigma\) applied to each coordinate, and let \(N(y, \sigma) := \operatorname{Re}\bigl(v_\sigma(y)^* G\, v_\sigma(y)\bigr)\). A family \(y = (y_s)_{s \in S}\) has covolume at most \(c\) if \[\bigl\lvert \det\bigl( v_\sigma(y_i)^* G\, v_\sigma(y_k) \bigr)_{i,k \in S} \bigr\rvert \le e^c \quad\text{for every } \sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}).\] For \(\sigma = \mathrm{id}\) the determinant is the Gram determinant of the vectors \(\Theta y_s\), the square of a volume. So the family of Problem 5.4.3 has covolume at most \(c\) exactly when \(c \ge -2\log N\). In the next example \(G\) differs from the identity when \(D \ge 3\). For \(f \in \mathbb{C}[z]\) let \[\lVert f \rVert^2 := \frac{1}{\pi} \int_\mathbb{C}\lvert f(z) \rvert^2 e^{-\lvert z \rvert^2} \, d A(z) .\]

Tag 233H

A lattice datum is a TwistedLattice with a single twist, allowed at every \(m \in \mathbb{N}\); index and covolume are TwistedLattice.IndexLe and TwistedLattice.CovolumeLe, Theorems 5.4.1 and 5.4.2 are TwistedLattice.indexLe_of_colengthDvd and TwistedLattice.covolumeLe_of_normSqAt_le, and Theorem 5.4.3 is the case of TwistedLattice.covolume_add_index_nonneg with this single twist, no completing vectors, the auxiliary scalar \(1\) and the three auxiliary bounds \(0\), for any inner product on \(V\) (file New/Reduction/Lattices/TwistedLattice.lean). The pairs \(\iota \mid \ell\) are PrimePadicIso in the height file. In the lattice file the integral vectors are TwistedLattice.integralVectors, the local matrices in the index bound satisfy PrimePadicIso.Carries, and the squared volumes in the general theorem of Chapter 6 are TwistedLattice.volume.

Theorem 5.4.3 compares the size and divisibility bounds for a single nonzero Gram determinant. In the general lattice theorem of Chapter 6, integrality at \(\iota \mid \ell\) is tested through every map allowed at \(\ell\), and covolume is measured using the map of the trivial twist. The theorem takes a choice of allowed twist for each \(\iota\) and a height bound for the family whose value at \(\iota\) is the squared volume of the trivial map divided by that of the chosen map.
