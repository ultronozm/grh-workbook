\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
Modules over a discrete valuation ring: a submodule of finite index in \(\Zp^n\) is free of rank \(n\), and a matrix over \(\Q\) can be brought to diagonal form by invertible matrices over \(\Zp\) \cite[Ch.~III, \S7]{La}.  From Chapter~\ref{sd-chap}, the principle that size is compared with divisibility.  From Chapter~\ref{da-chap}: the data (scales \(x < x_1 < b\), \(a = xb\), \(L = \log b\); the CM field \(E\) with its set \(\Phi\) of \(f\) infinite places; \(m\), \(k\), the weights \(\kappa\), the principal level \(M = p_0p_1\), with \(p_0 \ne p_1\) primes, and the prime level \(N\)), the amounts \(\hm\) and \(\rem\) and the unit \(U = faL^3\).  From Chapter~\ref{ob-chap}: the source forms, the group \(\Gb\), the idempotent \(e_0\), the target \(T\), tame characters, twists and the lower chain.  From Chapter~\ref{bp-chap}: the Poincar\'e map.  From Chapter~\ref{ss-chap}: the composite lift and the seesaw identity.  From Chapter~\ref{dc-chap}: the gain \(\gain(p)\), the set \(\Pi\) of primes \(p < x_1\) with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), and the divisibility of the composite lift.  From Chapter~\ref{d6-chap}: the size of the composite lift.  Chapter~\ref{tn-chap} treats small-rank examples of single definite lifts and their norms.

Theorem~A says that the ramified source is spanned by integral forms whose translated images have short lifts at the trivial twist.  It also bounds the index of every finite family of translated images of integral ramified forms in terms of the number of translates used.  The last chapter of this part uses both bounds.  The normalization multiplies by a positive rational number \(c_B\), with exponent \(-\lfloor\gain(p)-\err(p)\rfloor\) at each prime \(p\) in the product, including the level prime \(N\); the price of the shortness is paid in index.  We first do this with rational vectors, and then follow the exponents in the proof; the size of the composite lift is the subject of Chapter~\ref{d6-chap}.

\section{Index and colength over \texorpdfstring{\(\Z\)}{Z}}\label{sec:index}

Let \(\Zp\) be the ring of rationals with denominator prime to \(p\), and \(v_p\) the \(p\)-adic valuation, normalized by \(v_p(p) = 1\), with \(v_p(0) = +\infty\).  Let \(y = (y_i)_{i \in \mathcal{J}}\) be a finite family in \(\Q^n\).  A square matrix \(u = (u_{ik})_{i,k \in \mathcal{J}}\) over \(\Zp\) \emph{carries \(y\) with loss \(\delta_p\)} if every \(\sum_k u_{ik}y_k\) lies in \(\Zp^n\) and \(v_p(\det u) \le \delta_p\).  The family \emph{has index at most \(\delta\)} if there are real numbers \(\delta_p \ge 0\) with \(\sum_p \delta_p\log p \le \delta\) such that for every prime \(p\) some matrix carries \(y\) with loss \(\delta_p\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:one-sixth}
The family \((1/6)\) in \(\Q\) has index at most \(\log 6\), and not at most \(\delta\) for any \(\delta < \log 6\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:index-lattice}
A finite family \(y\) in \(\Q^n\) has index at most \(\delta\) if and only if \(\delta \ge \log\,[\Lambda_y : \Z^n]\), where \(\Lambda_y = \Z^n + \sum_i \Z y_i\).  For the family \(((\tfrac12, \tfrac12), (0, \tfrac14))\) in \(\Q^2\) one has \([\Lambda_y : \Z^2] = 8\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:indices-add}
If \(\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{J}' \sqcup \mathcal{J}''\) and the two subfamilies have index at most \(\delta'\) and \(\delta''\), then \(y\) has index at most \(\delta' + \delta''\).  Hence a family that is the union of \(h\) subfamilies of index at most \(\delta\) has index at most \(h\delta\).  For the families \((\tfrac12, \tfrac13)\) and \((\tfrac12, \tfrac12)\) in \(\Q\), split into singletons, the bound is \(\log 6\) and \(2\log 2\), and the index is \(\log 6\) and \(\log 2\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:colength}
Let \(W\) be a submodule of \(\Zp^k\) with \(\Zp^k/W\) of order \(p^j\).  For every finite family \((\varphi_i)_{i \in \mathcal{J}}\) in \(\Zp^k\) there is a square matrix \(u\) over \(\Zp\) with \(v_p(\det u) \le j\) and \(\sum_k u_{ik}\varphi_k \in W\) for every \(i\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:colength-index}
Let \(A\colon \Q^k \to \Q^n\) be linear.  Suppose that for every prime \(p\) there is a submodule \(W_p \subseteq \Zp^k\) of index \(p^{j_p}\) with \(A(W_p) \subseteq \Zp^n\), and \(j_p = 0\) for almost all \(p\).  Then every finite family in \(A(\Z^k)\) has index at most \(\sum_p j_p\log p\).
\end{problem}

\section{A model of Theorem A}\label{sec:model}

Let \(B\colon \Q^k \to \Q^n\) be linear; it stands for the chain of lifts.  Let \(\Pi_0\) be a finite set of primes, and suppose that for each \(p \in \Pi_0\) we are given a real number \(\gamma_p\) and a submodule \(W_p \subseteq \Zp^k\) of index \(p^{j_p}\) on which \(B\) is divisible: \(v_p(B\varphi) \ge \gamma_p\) coordinatewise for \(\varphi \in W_p\).  For \(p \notin \Pi_0\) put \(\gamma_p = j_p = 0\) and \(W_p = \Zp^k\), and suppose \(B(\Zp^k) \subseteq \Zp^n\) for these \(p\).  Put
\[
  g_p = \lfloor \gamma_p \rfloor, \qquad c_B = \prod_{p \in \Pi_0} p^{-g_p}, \qquad A = c_B B .
\]

\begin{problem}\label{pr:cB-local}
For every prime \(p\), \(v_p(c_B) = -g_p\) and \(A(W_p) \subseteq \Zp^n\).  Hence every finite family in \(A(\Z^k)\) has index at most \(\sum_p j_p \log p\), and \(\lvert A\varphi\rvert^2 = c_B^2\lvert B\varphi\rvert^2\) with \(2\log c_B \le -2\sum_{p \in \Pi_0} \gamma_p\log p + 2\sum_{p \in \Pi_0}\log p\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:model}
Let \(k = n = 2\), \(\Pi_0 = \{2, 3\}\) and \(B(\varphi_1, \varphi_2) = (4\varphi_1 + 6\varphi_2, 12\varphi_2)\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item With \(\gamma_2 = 1\), \(W_2 = \Z_{(2)}^2\) and \(\gamma_3 = 1\), \(W_3 = \{\varphi : 3 \mid \varphi_1\}\), one has \(c_B = 1/6\); the least \(\delta\) such that \((Ae_1, Ae_2)\) has index at most \(\delta\) is \(\log 3\), and \(\lvert Ae_1\rvert^2 = 4/9\), \(\lvert Ae_2\rvert^2 = 5\).
\item With \(\gamma_2 = 2\), \(W_2 = \{\varphi : 2 \mid \varphi_2\}\) and the same choice at \(3\), one has \(c_B = 1/12\); this least \(\delta\) is \(\log 6\), and \(\lvert Ae_1\rvert^2 = 1/9\), \(\lvert Ae_2\rvert^2 = 5/4\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:short-span}
The vectors of \(\Z^k\) with all coordinates in \(\{0, \pm1\}\) span \(\C^k\).  If \(T_1, \dots, T_f\) are commuting complex-linear isometries of \(\C^k\) preserving \(\Z^k\), the image of \(\prod_i(1 - T_i)\) is spanned by integral vectors of squared norm at most \(4^f k\).
\end{problem}

In Problem~\ref{pr:model}, increasing \(\gamma_2\) by one halves \(c_B\), shortens the image, and increases the index of the family by \(\log 2\).  For each prime \(p\) and each ring homomorphism \(\C \to \C_p\), the divisibility theorem of Chapter~\ref{dc-chap} gives a submodule of the integral ramified source forms of colength exponent at most \(\err(p)\) times the dimension of the full source.  On this submodule the composite lift has valuation at least \(\gain(p) - \err(p)\) for every twist of level prime to \(p\) and every chain datum for that twist.

\section{Theorem A}\label{sec:theorem-a}

Put \(\Sigma := \sum_{p \in \Pi}\gain(p)\log p\), as in Chapter~\ref{d6-chap}.  The composite lift \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\) of Chapter~\ref{ss-chap} is defined on all source forms; for a twist \(\vartheta\) it depends on a chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\), a finitely supported function on the translations.  Write \(G_f(V)\) for the group of finite adelic points of the unitary group of a space \(V\).  For a form \(F\) on \(X_0\) or on \(Y_m\) and \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), \(\lvert\sigma F(g)\rvert^2\) denotes the Weyl squared norm of the conjugate value.  By Theorem~\ref{d6-thm:size}, if \(\Psi\) is a chain datum for the trivial twist, \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), \(\varphi \in S\), \(c \in \R\), and \(\lvert\sigma\varphi(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for every \(g \in G_f(X_0)\), then for every \(y \in G_f(Y_m)\)
\[
  \lvert\sigma(B_{1,\Psi}\varphi)(y)\rvert^2 \le \exp\bigl(c + \hm - m\,\rem + 2\Sigma + U\bigr) .
\]
This is a statement at the trivial twist only.

The spaces \(S \supseteq \Sram\) of source forms and ramified source forms, the set \(\Sram^{\mathrm{int}}\) of ramified forms integral at every \(\iota\), the dimension \(D_X = \dim S\), the group \(\Gb\) and its action \(\rho\) on the principal forms \(H(Y_0)\), the target \(T\), tame characters \(\xi\), twists and \(m\)-fold twists \(\vartheta\) with their moduli \(Q_\vartheta\), and the lower chain \(\Theta_\xi\) are as in Chapter~\ref{ob-chap}; the Poincar\'e map \(\mathcal{P}\colon S \to H(Y_0)\) is that of Chapter~\ref{bp-chap}.  A form is integral at a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\) if all its values have gauge at most \(1\) at \(\eta\); integrality at \(\iota\) is integrality at the homomorphism induced by \(\iota\), and \(\Oc_\iota\) is the ring of \(z \in \C\) with \(\lvert\iota(z)\rvert_p \le 1\).  For \(Z > 0\) a form \(\varphi\) is \emph{\(Z\)-short} if it is integral at every \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\), for every prime \(p\), and \(\lvert\sigma\varphi(g)\rvert^2 \le Z\) for all \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\) and all \(g\).  For a form \(F\) on \(Y_m\) of specified level \(K\) and character, \(\lVert F\rVert_\sigma^2\) is the sum over the double classes at level \(K\) of \(\lvert\sigma F(g)\rvert^2\) at the chosen representatives \(g\), each weighted by the reciprocal of the order of its stabilizer.  For \(\lambda \in \C\) the squared norm of \(\sigma(\lambda F(g))\) is \(\lvert\sigma(\lambda)\rvert^2\) times that of \(\sigma F(g)\), and the gauge of \(\lambda F(g)\) at \(\eta\) is \(\lvert\eta(\lambda)\rvert_p\) times that of \(F(g)\).

The text uses five inputs.  Inputs~2 and~4 are theorems of the chapters named; input~1 is derived below from one statement read from the proof \cite{GL}; inputs~3 and~5 are read from the proof, and no chapter develops them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \(e^U\)-short forms in \(\Sram\) span \(\Sram\).  Problems~\ref{pr:translate-short} and~\ref{pr:ramify-short} derive this from the statement before them (compare Problem~\ref{pr:short-span}).
\item \emph{Seesaw.}  There is \(c_{\mathrm{ss}} \in \C^\times\) such that for every twist \(\vartheta\) there is a chain datum \(\Psi\) for \(\vartheta\) with \(\Theta_\vartheta(\mathcal{P}\varphi) = c_{\mathrm{ss}}B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\varphi\) for all \(\varphi \in \Sram\) (Theorem~\ref{ss-thm:seesaw}, at the point and the linear form that define \(\mathcal P\)).
\item \emph{Good part.}  Let \(z \in H(Y_0)\) and \(s \in \Gb\).  If \(\lvert\sigma(\Theta_1 z)(g)\rvert^2 \le e^c\) for all \(g \in G_f(Y_m)\), with \(c \in \R\) and a fixed \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), then \(\lVert\Theta_1(\#\Gb\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z)\rVert_\sigma^2 \le e^{x^{99} + c}\).  For every prime \(p\), every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon \C \to \C_p\), every tame character \(\xi\) and every \(\lambda \in \C\), if \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi z\) is integral at \(\eta\), so is \(\lambda\,\Theta_\xi(\#\Gb\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)z)\).  We use both estimates as inputs.
\item \emph{Divisibility.}  Theorem~\ref{dc-thm:divisibility}.  We write \(M_\eta\) for its module \(S_{\mathrm{ram},\eta}\) of forms in \(\Sram\) integral at \(\eta\), and \(j_{p,\eta}\) and \(S'_{p,\eta}\) for the integer \(l\) and the module \(S'\) of its part~(d) at \((p, \eta)\).  That the colength of \(S'_{p,\eta}\) in \(M_\eta\) divides \(p^{j_{p,\eta}}\) means (Section~\ref{sec:index}) that every finite family in \(M_\eta\) is carried into \(S'_{p,\eta}\) by a square matrix over \(\Oc_\eta\), the ring of \(z \in \C\) with \(\lvert\eta(z)\rvert_p \le 1\), whose determinant divides \(p^{j_{p,\eta}}\).
\item \(\dim \Sram \ge (1 - f/(N - 1))D_X\), and \(f/(N - 1) \le b^{-8}\).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover the data satisfy (Chapter~\ref{da-chap}) \(b \ge 2\), \(N \ge x_1\), \(\sum_{p \in \Pi \cup \{N\}}\log p \le b\) and \(x^{99} + 4U + 2b \le faL^4\).

For input~1 the proof \cite{GL} constructs, from bases of the lattice classes of \(X_0\) whose vectors are short at every complex embedding, source forms supported on one class, and obtains: \emph{the \(4^{-f}e^U\)-short forms in \(S\) span \(S\)}.  \emph{Status}: read from the proof.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:translate-short}
Let \(w \mid \pi_N\) be a prime of \(E\), \(T_w\) the operator of Section~\ref{ob-sec:source}, and \(Z > 0\).  If \(\varphi \in S\) is \(Z\)-short, then \((1 - T_w)\varphi\) is \(4Z\)-short.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:ramify-short}
There are \(f\) primes \(w \mid \pi_N\) of \(E\).  Hence \(\prod_{w \mid \pi_N}(1 - T_w)\) maps \(4^{-f}e^U\)-short forms to \(e^U\)-short forms, and the \(e^U\)-short forms in \(\Sram\) span \(\Sram\).
\end{problem}

\begin{definition}\label{def:normalized}
Fix \(\err\) as in input~4 and \(c_{\mathrm{ss}}\) as in input~2.  As in Section~\ref{sec:model}, put \(g_p = \lfloor\gain(p) - \err(p)\rfloor\) and \(c_B = \prod_{p \in \Pi \cup \{N\}}p^{-g_p}\), a positive rational number.  For \(s \in \Gb\) the \emph{normalized translate} \(A_s\colon \Sram \to T\) is
\[
  A_s\varphi = c_B\,c_{\mathrm{ss}}^{-1}\,\#\Gb\,\rho(e_0)\rho(s)\,\mathcal{P}\varphi .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:cB-general}
\(\log c_B \le -\Sigma + U + b\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:cB-padic}
For every prime \(p\) and every \(\iota\) above \(p\), \(\lvert\iota(c_B)\rvert_p = p^{g_p}\), and \(g_p = 0\) for \(p \notin \Pi \cup \{N\}\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:exponent-a}
\(U + (\hm - m\,\rem + 2\Sigma + U) + x^{99} + 2\log c_B \le \hm - m\,\rem + x^{99} + 4U + 2b\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:short-lifts}
Every \(e^U\)-short \(\varphi \in \Sram\) lies in \(\Sram^{\mathrm{int}}\) and satisfies \(\lVert\Theta_1 A_s\varphi\rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp(\hm - m\,\rem + faL^4)\) for all \(s \in \Gb\) and \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:dim-ramified}
\(D_X \le 2\dim \Sram\).
\end{problem}

Vectors of \(T\) integral at \(\iota\), and the index of a finite family in \(T\), are those of the target lattice (Definition~\ref{ob-def:target-lattice}): \(y \in T\) is integral at \(\iota\) above \(p\) if \(\Theta_\vartheta y\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) of level prime to \(p\), and a family has index at most \(\delta\) if matrices over the rings \(\Oc_\iota\), with determinants of total weighted valuation at most \(\delta\), carry it into the integral vectors.  This is the carrying condition of Section~\ref{sec:index} at each \(\iota\), with one matrix carrying the family into the vectors integral for all allowed twists at once.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:integral-image}
Let \(\iota\) lie above \(p\), and let \(\eta\) be the homomorphism induced by \(\iota\).  Then \(A_s\varphi\) is integral at \(\iota\) for every \(\varphi \in S'_{p,\eta}\) and every \(s \in \Gb\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:index-one-translate}
For each \(s \in \Gb\), every finite family in \(A_s(\Sram^{\mathrm{int}})\) has index at most \(\dim \Sram \cdot 2U\).
\end{problem}

The proof \cite{GL} establishes the following two statements.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem A]\label{thm:A}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The complex span of the set of \(\varphi \in \Sram^{\mathrm{int}}\) such that
\[
  \lVert\Theta_1 A_s\varphi\rVert_\sigma^2 \le \exp\bigl(\hm - m\,\rem + faL^4\bigr) \quad\text{for all } s \in \Gb \text{ and } \sigma \in \Aut(\C)
\]
is \(\Sram\).
\item[(b)] Let \(J \subseteq \Gb\) be finite, \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), and \(y_1, \dots, y_n \in T\).  If each \(y_i\) lies in \(A_s(\Sram^{\mathrm{int}})\) for some \(s \in J\), then \((y_1, \dots, y_n)\) has index at most \(\#J\cdot\dim \Sram\cdot 2faL^3\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Neither part asks the forms to be independent or the images to span; Theorem~B of Chapter~\ref{bp-chap} supplies few translates whose images span almost all of \(T\).  In the exponent of~(a) the gains have disappeared and the remainder appears with the factor \(m\).  The last chapter of this part compares this upper bound with a lower bound in which the main amount \(\hm\) appears without the remainder.

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:theorem-a}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:translate-short}] \(z_w\) is an integral unit scalar: translation by it changes neither the gauges nor the set of values \(\sigma\varphi(g)\).  Then \(\lvert u - u'\rvert^2 \le 4\max(\lvert u\rvert^2, \lvert u'\rvert^2)\), and the gauge is ultrametric.
\item[\ref{pr:ramify-short}] \(N\) splits completely in \(E\) and \([E:K] = f\).  Problem~\ref{d5-pr:ram-image}: \(\Sram\) is the image of the product, so it is spanned by the images of a spanning set.

\item[\ref{pr:cB-padic}] Which factors of \(c_B\) can have nonzero valuation at \(p\)?  Compare with the support conditions on the gain and error.
\item[\ref{pr:exponent-a}] Problem~\ref{pr:cB-general}.
\item[\ref{pr:short-lifts}] The seesaw at the trivial twist, Theorem~\ref{d6-thm:size} with \(c = U\), the good part, and Problem~\ref{pr:exponent-a}.
\item[\ref{pr:dim-ramified}] Input~5.
\item[\ref{pr:integral-image}] Fix an \(m\)-fold twist of level prime to \(p\).  Compare the gauge bound from the seesaw and divisibility with the local absolute value of \(c_B\), then use the good-part input.
\item[\ref{pr:index-one-translate}] As in Problem~\ref{pr:colength-index}, with \(\delta_p = D_X\err(p)\); then Problem~\ref{pr:dim-ramified}.
\end{description}
