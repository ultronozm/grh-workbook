§16.1. The isotropic plane Tag 22GF

The function \(F\) does not gain by itself (Problem 15.5.3). Why should \(\Theta\) along the directions \(e_\pm = (1, \pm\zeta)\) do better? They are the eigenlines of an action of \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\) on \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]^2\). Let \[\rho(\zeta) := \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ \mathrm{i}& 0 \end{pmatrix},\] let \(\sigma_+\) be the identity of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\) and \(\sigma_-\) the automorphism \(\zeta \mapsto -\zeta\), and let \(H(Z, W) := Z_1 \bar W_1 + Z_2 \bar W_2\).

Tag 22GG

Problem 16.1.1. \(\rho\) extends to a ring homomorphism \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta] \to M_2(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}])\), and \(\rho(\bar a) = \rho(a)^*\), the conjugate transpose.

Tag 22GH

Problem 16.1.2. \(\rho(a) e_\pm = \sigma_\pm(a) e_\pm\), and \(H(e_+, e_-) = 0\).

For each prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), fix an embedding \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}_p(\mathrm{i})\) extending the embedding of \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) given by \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}]\), and use it for \(v_p\) and for reduction modulo \(p\). Let \(\sigma_p\) be the nonidentity automorphism of \(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mathrm{i})/\mathbb{Q}_p\).

Now let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). The Frobenius \(\zeta \mapsto \zeta^3\) generates the decomposition group of \(p\) in \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\), with fixed field \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})\), \(\sqrt{-2} := \zeta + \zeta^3\). Since \(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]\) is a principal ideal domain, \(p = x^2 + 2y^2\) for integers \(x\), \(y\).

Tag 22GJ

Problem 16.1.3. After replacing \(y\) by \(-y\) if necessary, \(a := x + y\sqrt{-2}\) lies in \(p\mathcal{O}\), while \(\bar a = x - y\sqrt{-2}\) is a unit of \(\mathcal{O}\). Moreover \(\sigma_-(a) = \bar a\).

Tag 22GK

Problem 16.1.4. If \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), then \(v_p(a) = v_p(\bar a)\) for every \(a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\).

On Gaussian coefficients \(\sigma_p\) agrees with complex conjugation. The local form \(H_p(z, w) := z_1 \sigma_p(w_1) + z_2 \sigma_p(w_2)\) on \(\mathcal{O}^2\) reduces to the hermitian form \(h(z, w) := z_1 w_1^p + z_2 w_2^p\) on \(\mathbb{F}_{p^2}^2\). Let \(\bar e_\pm\) be the reductions of \(e_\pm \in \mathcal{O}^2\). Write \(\widetilde{\zeta}\) for the reduction of \(\zeta\) modulo \(p\).

Tag 22GL

Problem 16.1.5. \(h(\bar e_+, \bar e_+) = h(\bar e_-, \bar e_-) = 1 + \widetilde{\zeta}^{p+1}\) and \(h(\bar e_+, \bar e_-) = 1 - \widetilde{\zeta}^{p+1}\). So for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) both lines are isotropic and \(h\) pairs them, while for \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) they are anisotropic and orthogonal.

We call \(e_-\) the partner of \(e_+\). In terms of embeddings, \(\sigma_- = c \circ \operatorname{Frob}\circ \sigma_+\), where \(c\) is complex conjugation and \(\operatorname{Frob}(\zeta) = \zeta^p\); for \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), by contrast, \(c \circ \operatorname{Frob}\circ \sigma_+ = \sigma_+\). The derivatives along \(e_+\) will gain, and those along the partner \(e_-\) will pay for it.

§16.2. Integrality on an eigenline Tag 22GM

Let \(P(s) := \Theta(s e_+) = F(s)\, F(\zeta s)\). Since \(F\) is even, \(\Theta(t e_-) = P(t)\), and \(t(n, 0) = P^{(n)}(0)\). Write \(P(s) = \hat P(\operatorname{sl}s)\) with \(\hat P \in \mathbb{Q}[[X]]\). We show that \(\hat P\) is \(p\)-integral for every prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), unlike \(F\).

Let \(L\) be the period lattice of \(\operatorname{sl}\) and \(\eta\) the quasi-period map of \(\Lambda\). Since \(\eta(\mathrm{i}\omega) = -\mathrm{i}\eta(\omega)\), Legendre’s relation gives \(\eta(\omega) = (\pi/\operatorname{vol}\Lambda)\, \bar\omega\), and with the law \(\sigma(u + \omega) = \pm e^{\eta(\omega)(u + \omega/2)}\sigma(u)\) [Si2], \[F(u + \omega) = \exp\bigl(c\, \bar\omega u + \tfrac{c}{2} \lvert\omega\rvert^2\bigr) F(u), \qquad \omega \in \Lambda,\quad c := \frac{2\pi}{\operatorname{vol}\Lambda} .\]

Tag 22GN

Problem 16.2.1. For \(\gamma \in L^2\), \(\Theta(Z + \gamma) = \exp\bigl(c H(Z, \gamma) + \tfrac{c}{2}H(\gamma, \gamma)\bigr)\Theta(Z)\). For \(a \in \mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\), \(a \ne 0\), the function \[F_a(Z) := \frac{\Theta(\rho(a) Z)}{\Theta(\rho(\bar a) Z)}\] is \(L^2\)-periodic with \(F_a(0) = 1\). It descends to a rational function on \(E \times E\) defined over \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\).

Tag 22GP

Problem 16.2.2. On \(\mathbb{C}/L = E(\mathbb{C})\), the zeros of \(F\) are the two points at infinity of \(y^2 = 1 - X^4\), each with multiplicity two.

Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be \(E \times E\) over \(\mathcal{O}\), an abelian scheme on which \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\) acts through \(\rho\), and write \(\hat F_a(X_1, X_2)\) for the expansion of \(F_a\) in \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}Z_1\), \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}Z_2\).

Tag 22GQ

Problem 16.2.3. \(\hat F_a \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\).

Now let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), and let \(a\) be as in Problem 16.1.3.

Tag 22GR

Problem 16.2.4. \(\hat P([a]X) = \hat F_a(X, [\zeta]X)\, \hat P([\bar a]X)\).

Tag 22GS

Problem 16.2.5. The coefficients of \(\hat P\) lie in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\).

Problems 16.2.1–16.2.5 and Theorem 15.4.1 prove the following.

Tag 22GT

Theorem 16.2.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). Then \(\hat P \in \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[[X]]\), and \(v_p(t(n, 0)) = v_p(P^{(n)}(0)) \ge E_p(n)\) for every \(n \ge 0\).

Used in: Chapter 15

The induction in Problem 16.2.5 is the argument Bannai and Kobayashi use for the Kronecker theta function at ordinary primes [BK, Prop. 2.14], where one element of the CM order serves for all coordinates at once. Here \(\mathcal{A}\) is supersingular, but on the line \(\mathbb{C}e_+\) the element \(a\) acts by a non-unit and \(\bar a\) by a unit. At \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) there is no such element (Problem 16.1.4), and at \(p = 7\) the conclusion fails.

§16.3. The cross term Tag 22GV

Theorem 16.2.1 accounts for the derivatives along each axis. What remains is a factor equal to \(1\) on both axes. Throughout, \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}s\), \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}t\), and \(P_n := P^{(n)}(0)\).

Tag 22GW

Problem 16.3.1. \(J(s,t) := \partial_s\partial_t \log\Theta(s e_+ + t e_-) = 2\operatorname{sl}^2(s + t) - 2\mathrm{i}\, \operatorname{sl}^2\bigl(\zeta(s - t)\bigr)\), and \(J \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\).

Let \(M(s,t) := \int_0^s \int_0^t J\), and \(M_{\alpha\beta} := \partial_s^\alpha \partial_t^\beta M(0,0)\).

Then \[t(a,w) = \sum P_{\lvert S_0 \rvert}\, P_{\lvert T_0 \rvert} \prod_{B \in \pi} M_{\lvert B \cap S \rvert, \lvert B \cap T \rvert},\] the sum over subsets \(S_0 \subseteq S\), \(T_0 \subseteq T\), and partitions \(\pi\) of \((S \smallsetminus S_0) \sqcup (T \smallsetminus T_0)\) into blocks each meeting both \(S\) and \(T\).

Tag 22GZ

Problem 16.3.4. Each term in Problem 16.3.3 has at most \(w\) blocks and valuation at least \(\lfloor a/p \rfloor - w\).

Tag 22H2

Tag 22H3

Problem 16.3.6. Let \(\alpha_1, \dotsc, \alpha_k\) be positive integers and \(a_0\) a nonnegative integer such that \(a_0 + \sum_{b=1}^k \alpha_b = a\). Let \(n_i := \#\{b : p^i \mid \alpha_b\}\). Then \[v_p\Bigl(\frac{a!}{a_0!\, \alpha_1! \dotsm \alpha_k!}\Bigr) + E_p(a_0) + \sum_{b=1}^k E_p(\alpha_b - 1) \ \ge\ \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{a}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor - n_i\Bigr)_+ ,\] where \(x_+ := \max(x, 0)\).

Problems 16.3.2–16.3.6, Theorem 16.2.1 and the symmetry \(t(a,w) = t(w,a)\) of Problem 15.1.5 give the following theorem.

Tag 22H4

Theorem 16.3.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\). For all integers \(a, w \ge 0\), \[v_p\bigl(t(a,w)\bigr) \ \ge\ \max\Biggl( \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{a}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor - w\Bigr)_+ ,\ \sum_{\substack{i \ge 1 \\ i \text{ odd}}} \Bigl(\Bigl\lfloor \frac{w}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor - a\Bigr)_+ \Biggr) .\]

Exercise

Let \(M = s^p t/p\) and let \(w \ge 0\) be an integer. Show that \(\partial_s^{pw}\partial_t^w e^{M}(0,0) = (pw)!/p^w\), with valuation \(v_p(w!)\). Deduce that this valuation is \(0\) for \(0 \le w < p\). Show also that for every \(G \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\), with \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}s\) and \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}t\), the valuation of \(\partial_s^{pw}\partial_t^w G(0,0)\) is at least \(w\).

§16.4. Questions Tag 22H5

Theorem 16.3.1 does not extend to \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\): it fails at \(p = 7\) (Problem 16.2.6). What fails is the choice of directions: \(e_\pm\) are global eigenlines of \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta]\), and they are isotropic modulo \(p\) only for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) (Problem 16.1.5). For every prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the local form \(H_p\) on \(\mathcal{O}^2\) admits a basis \(f, f'\) with \(H_p(f, f) = H_p(f', f') = 0\) and \(H_p(f, f') = 1\). If \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), neither line \(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mathrm{i})e_\pm\) contains a nonzero isotropic vector. Theorem 16.3.1 concerns the two global eigenlines.

Let \(A\) be an abelian variety with complex multiplication by the ring of integers of a CM field \(K\), with CM type \(\Phi\) (one embedding \(K \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}\) from each complex-conjugate pair), and let \(\Theta\) be a normalized theta function on its universal cover. Fix a prime \(p\) and an embedding \(\jmath : \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \hookrightarrow \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p\), so that \(\jmath \circ \phi\) determines a prime of \(K\) above \(p\). The eigenlines \(\mathbb{C}e_\phi\) (\(\phi \in \Phi\)) replace \(e_\pm\); the isotropic plane uses \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\), \(\Phi = \{\sigma_+, \sigma_-\}\). The argument of Section 16.2 on \(\mathbb{C}e_\phi\) needs an element \(a\) of the CM order with \(\phi(a)\) a non-unit and \(\phi(\bar a)\) a unit, and such an element exists exactly when complex conjugation does not fix the prime of \(K\) determined by \(\phi\). In the first sum of Theorem 16.3.1, the subtracted order is \(w\), the order along \(e_-\). For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\), \(e_-\) is the partner direction at every odd level \(i\), since \(c \circ \operatorname{Frob}^i \circ \sigma_+ = \sigma_-\). For a general CM type, suppose \(p\) is unramified in the normal closure of \(K\), and let \(\operatorname{Frob}\) be the arithmetic Frobenius at the chosen prime of that closure. At a level \(i\) with \(\operatorname{Frob}^i \circ \phi \notin \Phi\), is the order subtracted from the order along \(e_\phi\) the order along \(e_{c \circ \operatorname{Frob}^i \circ \phi}\)?

Among the odd supersingular primes for \(E\), the choice of a global CM element in Problem 16.1.3 fails exactly for \(p \equiv 7 \pmod 8\). What derivative estimates hold in local hyperbolic coordinates at these primes? Let \(A_5\) be the Jacobian of \(y^2 = x^5 - 1\), with CM by \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)\). Among odd primes \(p \ne 5\), the supersingular primes for \(A_5\) are exactly those with \(p \not\equiv 1 \pmod 5\), by the Shimura–Taniyama formula for the slopes of a Jacobian with complex multiplication [LMPT]. At these primes complex conjugation is in the decomposition group of every prime above \(p\), so there is no element of the CM order separating a prime from its conjugate. For this Jacobian at such a prime, what choices of normalized theta function, eigenline direction and formal parameter from an integral model give a restriction with \(p\)-integral coefficients? What valuation bound then holds for the derivatives in that direction at the origin?

Which step this chapter serves.

This chapter gives a two-variable model for the partner terms in the divisibility estimate of the proof. Theorem 17.5.1 of Chapter 17, Pairings on a formal group of height two, bounds the valuation of jets along products of minors for series in the prescribed normal form and at the prescribed scale. Write \(r_e(\tau)\) for the partner of the infinite place \(\tau\) at level \(e\), as defined there. Among the columns of height less than the number of rows, let \(a_\tau\) count the columns of height one at \(\tau\), and let \(a'_\tau\) count the other columns there. In the positive-level sum for the predicted divisibility, the summand at \(\tau\) and \(e\) contains the positive part \(\bigl(a_\tau/p^e - a_{r_e(\tau)} - a'_{r_e(\tau)}\bigr)_+\). The sum runs over all infinite places and over odd positive \(e\) if \(p\) is inert in \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), or all positive \(e\) if \(p = d\). Its weight is \(1\) if \(p\) is inert and \(1/2\) if \(p = d\). The order \(w\) along \(e_-\) in the first sum of Theorem 16.3.1 plays the analogous role. Theorem 17.5.1 assumes that the prime \(p\) does not split in \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), with no separate congruence condition on \(p\) modulo \(8\). Theorems 16.2.1 and 16.3.1 give the estimates for the workbook moments.
