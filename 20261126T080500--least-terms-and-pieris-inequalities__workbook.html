For small exceptional primes, the proof of Theorem A(b) uses a near-surjectivity bound for scaled Hecke operators modulo prime powers. Chapter 63, A Hecke operator is nearly onto (D4 below), realizes these operators on edges. For suitable variable weights, the root lemma bounds the exponent defect (the signed weighted sum in Definition 63.3.1) of a term attaining a finite least order across translates by determinant-one matrices acting in a fixed ordered pair of rows. Order combines monomial weight with coefficient divisibility. The proof counts zeros of an auxiliary polynomial with multiplicity in the style of Stepanov [Bo]. Pieri’s inequalities say that the defect, a linear expression in the exponents, is nonpositive for each set of variables, called a letter. For one letter, the fraction of the exponent simplex allowed is the reciprocal of the number of full flags, chains of subspaces with one of each dimension. D4 uses the corresponding volume fraction to bound the dimension of the space of forms with bounded defects. A related zero count bounds the sum of the dimensions of the spaces of deep forms, whose translates have only terms of sufficiently large total exponent in designated variables (Section 63.2). The exercises establish Pieri’s inequalities for least terms in the one-letter setting and in D4’s two-letter model over \(\mathbb{F}_{25}\).

Prerequisites.

Multiplicity of a zero of a binary form on the projective line. Chapter 63 gives the matrix action on several sets of variables by means of a Frobenius cycle (Definition 63.1.1), the formula for the defect (Definition 63.3.1), and the model used here over \(\mathbb{F}_{25}\).

We start with binary forms to see how a root count can restrict the least exponent under linear changes of variables.

§64.1. One letter

Let \(F\ne0\) be a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(\mu\) in two variables \(\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\). For a matrix \(g=(g_{ij})\), replace variable \(j\) by the linear combination of the variables with coefficients \(g_{ij}\). This substitution is the column action, and the resulting polynomial \(g\cdot F\) is a translate of \(F\). The first problems bound the least exponent of \(\mathsf{y}\) across translates and give a form attaining the bound.

Problem 64.1.1. The sum of the multiplicities of \(F\) at the \(q+1\) rational points of \(\mathbb{P}^1\) is at most \(\mu\). At some rational point the multiplicity is at most \(\mu/(q+1)\).

Problem 64.1.2. As \(g\) runs through \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_q)\), the least exponent of \(\mathsf{y}\) in \(g\cdot F\) runs through the multiplicities at the rational points. If \(\mathsf{x}^{\mu-b}\mathsf{y}^b\) occurs in some translate and \(b\) is least among all terms of all translates, then \(\mu-b\ge qb\).

Used in: Problem 64.1.3, Problem 64.1.4

Problem 64.1.3. The form \[F=\mathsf{x}^q\mathsf{y}-\mathsf{x}\mathsf{y}^q=\mathsf{y}\prod_{v\in\mathbb{F}_q}(\mathsf{x}-v\mathsf{y})\] is fixed by \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_q)\) and attains equality in Problem 64.1.2. For \(q=3\) its square is \(\mathsf{x}^6\mathsf{y}^2+\mathsf{x}^4\mathsf{y}^4+\mathsf{x}^2\mathsf{y}^6\).

Problem 64.1.4. Take a translate \(g\cdot F\) in which the minimum \(b\) of Problem 64.1.2 occurs. The polynomial \((g\cdot F)(\mathsf{T},1)\) has degree \(\mu-b\) and is divisible by \((\mathsf{T}-v)^b\) for every \(v\in\mathbb{F}_q\). In particular \(qb\le\mu-b\).

We now bound the least exponents in more variables and use the bound to count coefficients that determine a form. Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer and let \(F\) be a nonzero form of degree \(\mu\) in \(z_0,\dots,z_{n-1}\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\), with the same column action. Row \(j\) means the index of \(z_j\). Compare exponent vectors \(\alpha\) by reading \(\alpha_{n-1},\alpha_{n-2},\dots,\alpha_0\) in order, with the smaller entry at the first difference coming first. Write \(z^\alpha\) for the monomial with exponents \(\alpha\) and \([z^\alpha]F\) for its coefficient.

Problem 64.1.5. A least exponent among all terms of all \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\)-translates satisfies the one-letter Pieri inequalities \(\alpha_j\ge q\alpha_{j+1}\) for \(0\le j<n-1\).

Used in: Problem 64.1.8, Problem 64.1.9

Problem 64.1.6. Under a lower triangular invertible matrix, the least exponent of a nonzero form stays the same, and its coefficient is multiplied by a nonzero scalar.

Problem 64.1.7. The number of full flags in \(\mathbb{F}_q^n\) is \(\prod_{j=1}^n\frac{q^j-1}{q-1}\). The same number counts the right cosets of the lower triangular subgroup in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\).

Used in: Problem 64.1.8

Problem 64.1.8. The coefficients at exponents satisfying Problem 64.1.5, in translates by representatives of the right cosets in Problem 64.1.7, determine \(F\). Hence \(\binom{\mu+n-1}{n-1}\) is at most the number of flags times the number of exponents of degree \(\mu\) satisfying Problem 64.1.5. For \((q,n,\mu)=(3,2,4),(2,3,7),(3,3,5)\), the triples (dimension, exponent count, flag count) are \((5,2,4),(36,4,21),(21,2,52)\), respectively.

Used in: Problem 64.1.9

Problem 64.1.9. The part of the simplex \(\alpha\ge0,\ \sum_j\alpha_j\le1\) satisfying the inequalities of Problem 64.1.5 has the volume share \[\prod_{j=1}^n\frac{q-1}{q^j-1}.\] The volume share is \(1/(q+1)\) for \(n=2\) and \(1/52\) for \(n=3,\ q=3\). For \(n=2\) the count in Problem 64.1.8 is at most the dimension plus \(q\): \[(q+1)\bigl(\lfloor \mu/(q+1)\rfloor+1\bigr)-(\mu+1)\le q.\]

With two sets of variables, the next bound will involve the exponents in both sets at once.

§64.2. Two letters over \(\mathbb{F}_{25}\)

In the action of Definition 63.1.1, the map \(\iota_s\) gives the matrix coefficients for letter \(s\), and the row index specifies a variable within the letter. We continue to write \(z^\alpha\) and \([z^\alpha]F\) for a monomial and its coefficient.

Definition 64.2.1. Fix an ordered pair of distinct rows \(a,a'\). In an even letter \(s\), \(\mathsf{x}_s,\mathsf{y}_s\) have rows \(a,a'\), respectively. In an odd letter, the rows are \(a',a\), respectively. To compute the defect \(d_i(\alpha)\) for this pair, use the rows of \(\mathsf{y}_s,\mathsf{x}_s\) as \(u_s,\bar u_s\) in Definition 63.3.1. A block is a group of consecutive rows. Pieri’s inequalities on a block require \(d_i(\alpha)\le0\) at every letter \(i\) for each adjacent pair \(a,a+1\) in the block.

We now enumerate the small-degree pairs that satisfy both inequalities in D4’s model. Reduce D4’s model to \(\mathbb{F}_{25}\). There are two letters and two rows (\(N_0=n=2\)), with \(P=5\), \(q=25\), and maps \(\iota_0(v)=v,\ \iota_1(v)=v^5\). For rows \(0,1\), the variables \((\mathsf{x}_0,\mathsf{y}_0,\mathsf{x}_1,\mathsf{y}_1)\) are \((z_0,z_1,z'_1,z'_0)\) in D4. A term of bidegree \((\mu,\mu)\) has total degree \(\mu\) in each letter. Write \(b,c\) for its exponents on \(\mathsf{y}_0,\mathsf{y}_1\). For the pair \((b,c)\), the defects of Problem 63.3.2 are \[24 d_0=26b+10c-6\mu,\qquad 24 d_1=10b+26c-6\mu.\]

Problem 64.2.1. The pairs \((b,c)\) with both defects nonpositive are \((0,0)\) alone for \(0\le\mu\le4\), the three pairs \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\) for \(\mu=5\), and those three together with \((1,1)\) for \(\mu=6\).

Used in: Problem 64.3.6

To ask which pairs can occur as least terms, we need a way to compare terms by weight and to track those weights under substitution.

Definition 64.2.2. Fix an element \(\varpi\) of a commutative coefficient ring. The order \(\operatorname{ord}_{\varpi}(h)\) of a coefficient \(h\) is the supremum of the nonnegative integers \(j\) for which \(\varpi^j\) divides \(h\). Assign a real weight \(\mathrm{wt}(z)\) to each variable \(z\). The monomial \(z^\alpha\) has weight \(\mathrm{wt}\cdot\alpha:=\sum_z\mathrm{wt}(z)\alpha(z)\). The Gauss order \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathrm{wt}} F\) is the least of \(\operatorname{ord}_{\varpi}([z^\alpha]F)+\mathrm{wt}\cdot\alpha\) over the terms of \(F\), and is \(+\infty\) for \(F=0\). A bound \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathrm{wt}} F\ge t\) requires each coefficient \([z^\alpha]F\) to be divisible by \(\varpi^{\max(0,\lceil t-\mathrm{wt}\cdot\alpha\rceil)}\).

In the field model \(\varpi=0\), so the Gauss order is the least weight of a term with nonzero coefficient. Use weights \(0\) on \(\mathsf{x}_0,\mathsf{x}_1\) and weights in \([1/2,1]\) on \(\mathsf{y}_0,\mathsf{y}_1\). Adding the same weight to both variables of a letter adds a constant to the weight of every term of a fixed bidegree. We find the least term of an invariant form and then give weight bounds for substitutions.

Problem 64.2.2. The invariant from Problem 63.2.3 is \[(\mathsf{x}_0^5\mathsf{y}_1+\mathsf{y}_0^5\mathsf{x}_1)(\mathsf{x}_0\mathsf{y}_1^5+\mathsf{y}_0\mathsf{x}_1^5).\] Its pairs \((b,c)\) are \((0,6),(1,1),(5,5),(6,0)\). The least term is \((1,1)\), where \(d_0=d_1=0\).

Used in: Problem 64.3.5

Problem 64.2.3. Over the field, give real weights to the variables of a source and a target polynomial ring. Suppose a substitution sends each source variable to a polynomial of Gauss order at least the source variable’s weight. Then every polynomial has Gauss order at least as large after substitution as before. Suppose also that each variable’s image is the sum of a main image and extra summands, with the main image meeting the same lower bound and each extra summand exceeding the source weight by at least \(\varepsilon>0\). The difference between the polynomial obtained from all summands and the polynomial obtained from the main images has Gauss order at least the original order plus \(\varepsilon\).

To bound the defects of a least term, we extract a polynomial in one variable whose zeros can be counted.

§64.3. The auxiliary polynomial

Let \(F\) be a form of bidegree \((\mu,\mu)\) in the field model. Take \(z^\alpha\) with nonzero coefficient in \(F\) and assume that its weight is least among all terms of all \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_{25})\)-translates of \(F\). We first treat the defect at letter \(0\).

Definition 64.3.1. The substitution \(\mathsf{S}\) in the two letters is given by \[\begin{align} \mathsf{x}_0&\longmapsto\mathsf{T}^{25}\mathsf{x}_0, & \mathsf{y}_0&\longmapsto\mathsf{x}_0,\\ \mathsf{x}_1&\longmapsto\mathsf{T}^{125}\mathsf{x}_1-\mathsf{T}^5\mathsf{y}_1,& \mathsf{y}_1&\longmapsto\mathsf{y}_1-\mathsf{x}_1 . \end{align}\] In \(\mathsf{S}(F)\), regard \(\mathsf{T}\) as a coefficient variable. Let \(\mathrm{aux}_\alpha(\mathsf{T})\) be the coefficient of the monomial obtained from \(z^\alpha\) by replacing \(\mathsf{y}_0\) by \(\mathsf{x}_0\). Assign zero weights \(25,5\) to \(\mathsf{y}_0,\mathsf{y}_1\) and pole weights \(25,125\) to \(\mathsf{x}_0,\mathsf{x}_1\). The unlisted entries of each list are zero. Summing each list of weights with the exponents of \(z^\alpha\) gives \[\mathrm{zo}(\alpha):=25b+5c,\qquad \mathrm{po}(\alpha):=25(\mu-b)+125(\mu-c).\]

Used in: Problem 64.3.1

The next problems relate the degree and zero multiplicities of \(\mathrm{aux}_\alpha\) to the defect.

Problem 64.3.1. Give \(\mathsf{T}\) weight \(-\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{y}_0)/25\), and raise the weight of each output variable by its pole weight times \(\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{y}_0)/25\). In each substitution in Definition 64.3.1, the first summand then has the weight of the input variable, and any second summand exceeds the input weight by at least \(1/20\). The degree of \(\mathrm{aux}_\alpha\) is \(\mathrm{po}(\alpha)\) and its leading coefficient is \([z^\alpha]F\).

Problem 64.3.2. For \(v\in\mathbb{F}_{25}\) let \(g_v=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&-v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Under \(g_v\) followed by \(\mathsf{S}\) and then \(\mathsf{T}\mapsto\mathsf{T}+v\), the images of \(\mathsf{x}_0,\mathsf{y}_0,\mathsf{x}_1,\mathsf{y}_1\) are, in order, \[-\mathsf{x}_0,\qquad \mathsf{T}^{25}\mathsf{x}_0,\qquad \mathsf{y}_1-\mathsf{x}_1,\qquad -\mathsf{T}^{125}\mathsf{x}_1+\mathsf{T}^5\mathsf{y}_1 .\]

Used in: Problem 64.3.3

Problem 64.3.3. Give \(\mathsf{T}\) weight \(\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{y}_0)/25\), and lower the weight of each output variable by its zero weight times \(\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{y}_0)/25\). The images in Problem 64.3.2 have Gauss order at least the original weights of the respective inputs. Consequently \((\mathsf{T}-v)^{\mathrm{zo}(\alpha)}\) divides \(\mathrm{aux}_\alpha(\mathsf{T})\) for every \(v\in\mathbb{F}_{25}\).

Problem 64.3.4. The orders satisfy \(25\mathrm{zo}(\alpha)\le\mathrm{po}(\alpha)\), equivalently \(d_0(\alpha)\le0\). Also \(d_1(\alpha)\le0\).

Problem 64.3.5. For the invariant of Problem 64.2.2, \[\mathsf{S}(F)=\mathsf{x}_0^6\,\mathsf{x}_1^5\,\mathsf{y}_1\,(\mathsf{T}^{25}-\mathsf{T})^{30}.\] For \((b,c)=(1,1)\), the auxiliary polynomial has degree \(750=\mathrm{po}(\alpha)\) and multiplicity \(30=\mathrm{zo}(\alpha)\) at each point of \(\mathbb{F}_{25}\).

In the field model, depth is \(b+c\), so \(\mathrm{Deep}_1\) is the space of forms whose \((0,0)\) coefficient vanishes in every \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_{25})\)-translate (Definition 63.2.1). The next problem computes the dimension of \(\mathrm{Deep}_1\) in small degrees and identifies the possible least terms in degree \(5\).

Problem 64.3.6. (i) Verify by computer that for \(\mu=5,6\) the linear map recording the coefficients at the pairs in Problem 64.2.1 in each of the \(26\) translates from Problem 63.1.1 has rank \(36,49\), respectively. The maps recording just the \((0,0)\) coefficient have rank \(26\) in both degrees. Thus \(\dim\mathrm{Deep}_1=10,23\), as in Problem 63.2.4.

(ii) For \(0\le\mu\le4\), \(\mathrm{Deep}_1=0\). If \(0\ne F\in\mathrm{Deep}_1\) and \(\mu=5\), a term attaining the least weight among all \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_{25})\)-translates has pair \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\), with \((\mathrm{po},\mathrm{zo})\) equal to \((725,25)\) or \((625,5)\), respectively.

Used in: Chapter 63

The general root lemma extends the bound on defects to cycles with more variables and to coefficients in a ring.

§64.4. The root lemma

Theorem 64.4.1. Let a Frobenius cycle of length \(N_0\ge1\) from \(W'\) to \(O\) be given as in Definition 63.1.1, allowing any commutative coefficient ring \(O\). Fix distinct rows \(a,a'\) and real weights such that \(\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{y}_s)-\mathrm{wt}(\mathsf{x}_s)\in[1/2,1]\) for every \(s\). Let \(F\) be a polynomial over \(O\), \(\alpha\) an exponent, and \(l\ge0\) an integer. Suppose \(\varpi^{l+1}\) does not divide \([z^\alpha]F\) and every translate by \(\mathrm{SL}_2(W')\) in rows and columns \(a,a'\) has Gauss order at least \(l+\mathrm{wt}\cdot\alpha\). Then at every letter \(i\), with \([s-i]\) the least nonnegative residue modulo \(N_0\), \[q\alpha(\mathsf{y}_i)+\sum_{s\ne i}P^{[s-i]}\alpha(\mathsf{y}_s) \ \le\ \sum_s P^{[s-i]}\alpha(\mathsf{x}_s).\] Equivalently, \(d_i(\alpha)\le0\) for the ordered pair \(a,a'\).

Used in: Chapter 63

Problems 64.3.1–64.3.4 prove the theorem on forms of bidegree \((\mu,\mu)\) in the model with \(l=0\).

The general root lemma is taken as given.

Section 63.3 of D4 divides the rows into two blocks of sizes \(r_0\) and \(n-r_0\). The dimension estimate concerns forms whose translates by matrices acting separately on the two blocks have zero coefficient wherever the defect for the pair \((r_0-1,r_0)\) exceeds \(3t_0\) at some letter. To bound the dimension, the proof packs a family of these forms into a single polynomial and selects a term in a block diagonal translate of that polynomial. The term has nonpositive defect at every letter along every adjacent pair within either block. The proof divides its exponent vector by the packing scale and then adds a shift. It counts lattice points in the resulting degree range, with coordinates for all rows and letters and nonpositive defect at every letter along every adjacent pair, including the boundary pair. The divisibility condition on the average in the same section uses the pair \((r_0-1,r_0)\) at the boundary between the blocks.
