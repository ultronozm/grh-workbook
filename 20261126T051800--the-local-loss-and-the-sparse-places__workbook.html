Prerequisites.

Local fields: decomposition groups, the degree \([E_v : \mathbb{Q}_\ell] = e_vf_v\) [SLF, Ch. II, §2, Cor. 1 to Prop. 3], and Frobenius elements [SLF, Ch. IV]; Legendre’s formula \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(n!) = \sum_{i \ge 1}\lfloor n/\ell^i\rfloor\). From Chapter 36: Definition 36.3.1 (the sparse set \(\Phi_b\), \(l_0(s)\)), Problems 36.1.1, 36.1.2, 36.2.1 and 36.5.4, and the budget \(B\) of Definition 36.4.1; the construction of \(\Phi_b\) in Chapter 4. Theorem 31.4.1. From Chapter 80: coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\) and normalized lengths (Section 80.1), Definitions 80.1.6, 80.1.7 and 80.1.1. Chapter 34 defines crystalline cocycles and states their rank and local duality.

The clearing bound \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell\) of Definition 80.1.1, which controls the denominators of projections onto lifts in Chapter 80, contains the term \(\mathrm{loc}_\ell\), counted per unit of rank. For a main summand of rank \(r'\) the allowance is \(r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell\) (Problem 80.1.3), for imposing the crystalline relations on the torsion rows above \(\ell\). Why is this price a fixed multiple of the valuation of one integer, and why is the sum of \(\mathrm{loc}_\ell\log\ell\) over all primes \(\ell\) only of size \(f(m/k)\,b\log b\), far below \(B\)? The bound at one place above \(\ell\) is taken from the proof. What this sheet proves is the arithmetic around it: the sum over the places and rows, the integer whose valuation determines the allowance, the sparse places that keep the small primes cheap, and the sum over \(\ell\). For a prime \(\ell\) and a positive integer \(n\) we write \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(n)\) for the exponent of \(\ell\) in \(n\), the valuation \(\operatorname{val}\) at \(\ell\).

§82.1. The loss at one place

We use the data and the notation of Chapter 36: the field \(E\) with \(f\) infinite places, the scales, \(m\), \(r\), \(k = r + 1 - m\), \(t = 5r\), \(M = p_0p_1\), the gap \(\Delta = 2r^8\), so \(\Delta/2 = r^8\).

Definition 82.1.1. Let \(\mathrm{Int}_b\) be the product of the integers \(i\) with \(\max(0, b - 30(\Delta/2)^3) < i \le b + 30(\Delta/2)^3\). For a prime \(\ell\) put \[\mathrm{crys}_\ell := \operatorname{val}_\ell\bigl((2b)!\,\mathrm{Int}_b^{(\Delta/2)^3}\bigr), \qquad \mathrm{defect}_\ell := 10^6\,\frac mk\,\bigl(b\,\operatorname{val}_\ell(M) + \mathrm{crys}_\ell\bigr),\] and \(\mathrm{loc}_\ell := 4f\,\mathrm{defect}_\ell\).

Used in: Problem 82.2.1, Problem 82.2.2; Chapter 83

Fix a prime \(\ell\), an isomorphism \(\iota\colon \mathbb{C}\simeq \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), and \(G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}:= \mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell)\). For a place \(v \mid \ell\) of \(E\) choose an embedding \(\jmath\colon E \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) inducing \(v\), and let \(\Gamma_v \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\) be the subgroup fixing \(\jmath(E)\), the Galois group of the completion \(E_v\); thus \([G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}: \Gamma_v] = [E_v : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]\). In the proof of the denominators theorem, the congruence cocycles obey the cocycle rule and the linear relations among the families \((U_x(g)-1)_x\) of Chapter 31. Theorem 31.4.1 realizes such a cocycle with torsion coefficients as the second projection of an integral cocycle on a graph lattice. Above \(\ell\) the proof realizes the restricted cocycle as the second projection of a crystalline cocycle on a graph lattice in the chosen class. The defect on the Tate dual (Definition 82.1.2) bounds the cost of imposing the crystalline relations of the target row on the torsion cocycles. The next problem illustrates the index calculation for a finite perfect pairing.

Problem 82.1.1. Let \(F_1 \times F_2 \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\) be a perfect pairing of finite abelian groups and \(F_0 \subseteq F_1\) a subgroup. Its orthogonal \(F_0^\perp \subseteq F_2\) has index \(\#F_0\). For \(F_1 = F_2 = \mathbb{Z}/9\) with \(\langle y_1, y_2\rangle = y_1y_2/9\) and \(F_0\) generated by \(3\), \(F_0^\perp = \{0, 3, 6\}\), of index \(3\): one equation costs length \(1\) on either side.

For a lattice with continuous action of \(\Gamma_v\), write \(Z_f(\cdot)\) for its integral crystalline cocycles. We use the following three inputs. (i) Local duality in index form. Use the divisibility of indices after Theorem 34.2.1, with \(F_v=E_v\). (ii) Rank of crystalline cocycles. Theorem 34.1.1 gives the integral rank formula, with \([E_v:\mathbb{Q}_\ell]=[G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}:\Gamma_v]\). Problem 34.1.5 gives the product of the orders of the reductions of \(Z_f(L)\) and \(Z_f(L^*(1))\). (iii) The bound (1) below is read from the rewritten proof. Its local estimates use an integral frame theorem for crystalline lattices (Section 34.3, where a basis over the chart ring also freely generates the associated module over \(A_{\mathrm{inf}}\)). For the underlying module theory see [Ki], [BMS].

Definition 82.1.2. Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be a coefficient ring, \(\mathcal C\) a class of \(\mathcal{O}\)-lattices with continuous \(\Gamma_v\)-action, and \(L'\) such a lattice. In this sheet let \(\mathcal C^\perp(L')\) be the \(\mathcal{O}\)-module of maps \(c\colon \Gamma_v \to L'\) such that for every \(\tilde L \in \mathcal C\), every \(n \ge 0\), every pairing \(\langle\,,\rangle\colon \tilde L \times L' \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n(1)\) obtained from an \(\mathcal{O}\)-linear form \(\psi\colon \tilde L \otimes_{\mathcal{O}} L' \to \mathcal{O}(1)\) whose coboundary \(g \mapsto g\psi - \psi\) is \(\ell^n\) times an integral crystalline cocycle, followed by a \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\)-linear map \(\mathcal{O}\to \mathbb{Z}_\ell\) and reduction modulo \(\ell^n\), and every \(y \in Z_f(\tilde L)\), the cochain \((g, h) \mapsto \langle y(g), g\,c(h)\rangle\), with values in the roots of unity of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\), is the coboundary of a locally constant cochain \(\Gamma_v \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell^\times\). Define the dual defect of \(L'\) for \(\mathcal C\) as the length of \(Z_f(L')/(Z_f(L') \cap \mathcal C^\perp(L'))\). For an \(\mathcal{O}\)-lattice \(L\), let \(L^\vee(1):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}}(L,\mathcal{O}(1))\) with the induced \(\Gamma_v\)-action. The dual defect at \(v\) of \(L\) is the dual defect of \(L^\vee(1)\) for \(\mathcal C\), in normalized length.

Fix \(v,\jmath\) as above and a family \(\mathrm{HT}\) admissible for \((r',t')\) in the sense of Definition 80.1.6. For all sufficiently large coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\), take any local \(\mathcal{O}\)-lattices with a main row \(L_0\) of rank \(r'\) and rows \(L^{(e)}\) indexed by \(0\le e<t'\). For every \(g\in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\), the jump multisets at the coefficient embedding \(z\mapsto g(z)\) are \(\mathrm{HT}_{\iota^{-1}g^{-1}\jmath}\) for \(L_0\) and \(\{e\}\) for \(L^{(e)}\). In the application \(L_0\) is the restriction of a lattice of a main-summand-like representation and \(L^{(e)}=\mathcal{O}(-e)\).

Choose a filter \(F\) on \(\Gamma_v\), a predicate \(K_s\) on \(\mathcal{O}[X]\) for each \(s\in\Gamma_v\), and a finite set \(U_\gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}\) for each coefficient embedding \(\gamma\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\). Let \(\mathcal C\) consist of the lattices \(Y\) whose periods fixed by almost all elements for the supremum of \(F\) and the neighborhood filter of \(1\) span \(B_{\max}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}Y\) over \(B_{\max}\), whose jumps at \(\gamma\) lie in \(U_\gamma\), and with the following property. For each inertia element \(s\), let \(T_s\) be the \(B_{\max}\)-linear endomorphism of \(B_{\max}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}Y\) agreeing with the diagonal action of \(s\) on the periods fixed by some neighborhood of \(1\) in \(\Gamma_v\). Require \(P(T_s)=0\) for every \(P\in\mathcal{O}[X]\) satisfying \(K_s\), with coefficients acting through \(Y\). Require each \(u\in U_\gamma\) to occur as a jump of some row at \(\gamma\).

In the crystalline case take \(F=\top\) and every \(K_s\) empty. In the tame case require \(\ell\in\{p_0,p_1\}\). Write \(A=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le j\le r}(\ell^j-1)\). For each inertia element \(s\), require that \(K_s\) hold for \(X^A-1\) and for some monic \(P_s\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell[X]\) of degree at most \((r+t)r\), viewed over \(\mathcal{O}\). Under either set of hypotheses, every indexed row \(L\) belonging to \(\mathcal C\) has normalized dual defect at \(v\) at most \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:dual-defect}\tag{1} [G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}: \Gamma_v]\,\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{O}}(L)\,\mathrm{defect}_\ell / t_L, \qquad t_L = 1 \text{ for the main row},\quad t_L = t \text{ for a row indexed by } e. \end{equation}\]

Problem 82.1.2. \(\sum_{v \mid \ell} [G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}: \Gamma_v] = [E : \mathbb{Q}] = 2f\). In \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) (a model, with \(1\) in place of \(f\)) the pairs \((e_v, f_v)\) are \((1, 2)\) above \(3\) and \((1, 1), (1, 1)\) above \(5\); the sum is \(2\) both times.

Problem 82.1.3. Let \(r',t'\) be integers with \(r'\ge1\) and \(0\le t'\le t\). The bounds (1) for the main row and the \(t'\) character rows, summed over the rows and the places \(v \mid \ell\), total at most \(4fr'\,\mathrm{defect}_\ell = r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell\).

For the comparison of crystalline colengths and dual defects, see [GL]. Problem 82.1.3 bounds the sum of the numerical estimates for those defects. The resulting allowance \(r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell\) is the local term in the inequality \(\mathrm{sel} + 2t'\,\mathrm{ram} + r'\mathrm{loc}_\ell + \varepsilon_\ell + n_\ell \le r'\mathrm{cl}_ell\) of Problem 80.1.3.

§82.2. The crystalline loss of one prime

The local estimates use both the factorial and interval contributions to \(\mathrm{crys}_\ell\).

Problem 82.2.1. \(\lfloor 2b/\ell\rfloor \le \mathrm{crys}_\ell\), so \(2b < \ell(\mathrm{crys}_\ell + 1)\). If \(\ell\) divides an integer of the interval of Definition 82.1.1, then \(\mathrm{crys}_\ell \ge (\Delta/2)^3\).

Problem 82.2.2. Formally take \(\Delta/2 = 1\) and \(b = 32\) (no data have these values). The integer of Definition 82.1.1 is \(64!\prod_{3 \le i \le 62} i\), and its valuations at \(\ell = 2, 3, 5, 31, 61, 67\) are \(119, 58, 28, 4, 2, 0\).

Used in: Problem 82.4.1

The bound (1) is proved by cases, which we only describe. If the class is tame (so \(\ell \in \{p_0, p_1\}\)), the cost is paid by the term \(b\,\operatorname{val}_\ell(M)\), as \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(M) \ge 1\).

In the crystalline case write \(H=\Delta/2\), \(u=\mathrm{crys}_\ell\), \(G=\lfloor a/x_1\rfloor\), and \(K_0=\lfloor2u/(kt)\rfloor\). For the lift index \(0\le j\le m\) supplied by the main weights, put \(\delta=j\Delta+H-2t\). The low window, the interval of jumps centered at \(0\), has radius \(H^2+1\). The two sides have far windows of opposite signs and width \(4t\), with unsigned start \(b+\delta\) at thick embeddings (those over \(\Phi_b\), as specified in Section 82.3) and \(G+\delta\) elsewhere.

For \(\ell\le kt\), the chart estimate uses sparsity, widens the low window to radius \(2G\), and gives the annihilating power \(\ell^{10^4 K_0(\lfloor\log_\ell H^3\rfloor+1)}\). For \(\ell>kt\) and \(u\ge H^3\), the power \(\ell^{10^4 K_0m}\) suffices. In both cases (ii) bounds the number of generators of the quotient. For \(\ell>kt\) and \(u<H^3\), the interval part of Problem 82.2.1 excludes multiples of \(\ell\) near \(b\). Together with \(2b<\ell(u+1)\) and the data size inequalities, this gives the exclusions around zero and both signed pairs of starts needed for the next estimate, with \(\lambda=2(u+1)\).

Let \(\ell\ge5\), let the completion \(E_v\) be unramified, and use an unramified Kummer chart of residue degree \(f_v\) for \(\Gamma_v\). Choose \(\xi\in\mathcal{O}\) with \(\xi^{\ell^{f_v}-1}=1\). The letters \(i\) are the elements of \(\mathbb F_{\ell^{f_v}}^\times\). A coefficient embedding \(\gamma\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\) lies above \(i\) if \(\gamma(\xi)\) is the Teichmüller root of \(i\). Take integers \(\lambda,\lambda_1\ge0\) and \(\lambda_0\ge1\). For every letter \(i\) choose a nonnegative integer \(\mathrm{fs}_i\) and a sign \(\mathrm{sg}_i=\pm1\), with \(\mathrm{fs}_i+\lambda_1+\lambda_0\le(\ell-3)\lambda\) and \(6(\lambda+\lambda_0+\lambda_1)<\mathrm{fs}_i\). Require that \(\ell\) divide no nonzero integer within distance \(6(\lambda+\lambda_0+\lambda_1)\) of \(0\), \(\mathrm{fs}_i\) or \(-\mathrm{fs}_i\), for every \(i\).

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the class of crystalline lattices with jumps in given finite sets \(\mathcal J_\gamma\), and let \(L'\) be crystalline with jumps in finite sets \(\mathcal J'_\gamma\). At each embedding \(\gamma\) above \(i\), require \(\mathcal J_\gamma\subseteq[-\lambda_0,\lambda_0]\cup-\mathrm{sg}_i[\mathrm{fs}_i,\mathrm{fs}_i+\lambda_1]\) and \(\mathcal J'_\gamma\subseteq[-\lambda_0,\lambda_0]\cup\mathrm{sg}_i[\mathrm{fs}_i,\mathrm{fs}_i+\lambda_1]\). The dual defect of \(L'\) for \(\mathcal C\) vanishes if at every \(\gamma\) both sets avoid \([-\lambda,0)\), or if at every \(\gamma\) the set \(\mathcal J'_\gamma\) lies in \([-\lambda_0,0]\). For every integer \(n_{\mathrm{low}}\ge0\), if at most \(n_{\mathrm{low}}\) jumps of \(L'\), counted with multiplicity, lie in \([-\lambda_0,\lambda_0]\) at each embedding, the normalized defect is at most \(11 f_v\bigl(n_{\mathrm{low}}(\lambda+1)+\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{O}}(L')(\lambda_1+1)\bigr)\).

Problem 82.2.3. For \(\ell=101\), \(\lambda=\lambda_0=\lambda_1=1\), and \(\mathrm{fs}_i=42\) for every chart letter \(i\), the height, separation and divisibility hypotheses of the preceding estimate hold.

§82.3. Thick letters

For \(\ell\le kt\), the estimate uses the spacing of the places of weight \(b\), together with either an unramified chart with \(\ell\ge7\) or a constant chart sign and a bound on the ramification index. Fix \(\jmath\colon E \to \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\). For \(g \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\) let \(\sigma_g := \iota^{-1}\circ g^{-1}\circ \jmath\), a complex embedding of \(E\), with place \([\sigma_g] \in \Phi\). For a complex embedding \(\sigma\) its sign \(s_\sigma = \pm 1\) for the CM type (as in Definition 80.1.6) is given by \(\sigma(\sqrt{-d}) = s_\sigma\sqrt{-d}\), with \(K \subset \mathbb{C}\) as in Chapter 36; if \(\zeta \in \mathbb{C}\) is the image of \(\zeta_{q_1}\) under the fixed embedding \(E \to \mathbb{C}\) over \(K\), the class of \([\sigma]\) in \((\mathbb{Z}/q_1)^\times\) is the \(u\) with \(\sigma(\zeta_{q_1}) = \zeta^{s_\sigma u}\).

Definition 82.3.1. Call \(\sigma_g\) thick if its place lies in \(\Phi_b\). For all sufficiently large \(\mathcal{O}\), the proof chooses a chart and a primitive \((\ell^{f_v}-1)\)-th root \(\xi\in\mathcal{O}\). A chart letter \(i\) is thick if its Teichmüller root equals \(g(\xi)\) for some \(g\in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\) with \(\sigma_g\) thick.

Problem 82.3.1. Let \(\zeta_\jmath := \jmath(\zeta_{q_1})\), nonnegative integers \(h,n\), and \(g, g' \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\) with \((g'g^{-1})(\zeta_\jmath^{\ell^h}) = \zeta_\jmath^{\ell^{h+n}}\). For every \(q' \mid q_1\) coprime to \(\ell^h\), the class of \([\sigma_g]/[\sigma_{g'}]\) modulo \(q'\) is \(s_{\sigma_g}s_{\sigma_{g'}}\,\ell^n\). For \(\ell = 2\) and \(31\) in place of \(q'\), the powers of \(\ell\) form the cycle \(1, 2, 4, 8, 16\), which does not contain \(-1\).

Used in: Problem 82.3.3

A lift \(\mathrm{Fr}_\ell \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\) of the Frobenius of the residue field raises every root of unity of order prime to \(\ell\) to its \(\ell\)-th power. So the embeddings \(\sigma_{\mathrm{Fr}_\ell^ng}\), \(n \in \mathbb{Z}\), form a cycle along which the classes of the places move by \(\pm\ell^{-1}\) modulo the prime-to-\(\ell\) part of \(q_1\). The sparse condition of Definition 36.3.1(3) is designed for this cycle.

Problem 82.3.2. Let \(\ell \le kt\), let \(\ell^h\) be the largest power of \(\ell\) dividing \(q_1\), and let \(i,j\) be integers with \(1 \le i, j \le \lfloor \log_\ell (\Delta/2)^3\rfloor\). Then \(\lfloor\log_\ell(\Delta/2)^3\rfloor \le 12s\), so \(i + j < l_0(s)\). If \(g_0, g_1, g_2 \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\), \(g_2g_0^{-1}\) raises \(\zeta_\jmath^{\ell^h}\) to the power \(\ell^{i+j}\), \(g_2g_1^{-1}\) raises it to the power \(\ell^j\), and \(\sigma_{g_0}\), \(\sigma_{g_2}\) are thick, then \(\sigma_{g_1}\) is not thick.

For \(\ell\le kt\), three thick embeddings along this Frobenius orbit cannot occur at successive positive gaps of at most \(\lfloor\log_\ell(\Delta/2)^3\rfloor\). The local estimate uses the corresponding exclusion for thick chart letters under \(i\mapsto i^\ell\).

Problem 82.3.3. In the cycle of Problem 82.3.1, call a subset allowed if it contains no three elements \(\sigma,\tau,\rho\), possibly repeated, with \(\tau/\sigma\equiv\pm2^i\) and \(\rho/\sigma\equiv\pm2^j\) modulo \(31\) for \(0<i<j<5\). The set \(\{1,2\}\) is allowed, \(\{1,2,8\}\) is forbidden, and every allowed subset has at most two elements. With \(l_0(s)\ge81\) in place of \(5\), the empty set is the only allowed subset of this cycle.

For \(\ell \nmid q_1d\), the field \(E \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1d})\) is unramified at \(\ell\), and \(\ell\ge13\) since every prime below \(13\) divides \(q_0\). At those places \(g(\xi)\) determines \(\sigma_g\) and hence its sign. For \(\ell\mid q_1d\) the thick embeddings have one sign, so the proof can use a constant sign on the chart. The next problems treat the two cases of \(\ell\mid q_1d\).

Problem 82.3.4. If \(\ell \mid q_1\), then \(s_{\sigma_g} = s_{\sigma_{g'}}\) for all \(g, g' \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}\); in particular two thick embeddings have the same sign.

Problem 82.3.5. \(d^2 \equiv 1\) and \(d \not\equiv \pm1 \pmod{q_0}\), so \(d^n \not\equiv -1 \pmod{q_0}\) for every \(n \ge 0\). Hence two thick embeddings over \(\ell = d\) have the same sign. For \(q_0 = 18480\) and \(d = 9239\), \(d^2 \bmod q_0 = 1\).

§82.4. The sum over the primes

We now sum the local losses over primes with weights \(\log\ell\).

Problem 82.4.1. For an integer \(n \ge 1\) and a finite set \(P_0\) of primes, \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0} \operatorname{val}_\ell(n)\log\ell \le \log n\). For the integer of Problem 82.2.2 the sum over \(\ell \le 67\) is \(401.34123\ldots\), its logarithm.

Problem 82.4.2. \(\mathrm{Int}_b \le (b + 30(\Delta/2)^3)^{60(\Delta/2)^3}\). For data, \[b\log M + \log\Bigl((2b)!\,(b + 30(\Delta/2)^3)^{60(\Delta/2)^6}\Bigr) \le 26\, b\log b .\]

Problem 82.4.3. For every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0}\mathrm{defect}_\ell\log\ell \le 26\cdot 10^6\,(m/k)\,b\log b\).

Problems 82.4.1–82.4.3 give the following theorem.

Theorem 82.4.1. For all data and every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, \[\sum_{\ell \in P_0}\mathrm{loc}_\ell\log\ell \le 4\cdot10^6 e^{180}\, f\,\frac mk\, b\log b .\]

Used in: Problem 82.4.4; Chapter 80; Chapter 85

The proof states the theorem with the constant \(e^{180}\), which it obtains by a general comparison of the sizes of the data.

Problem 82.4.4. The right side of Theorem 82.4.1 is at most \(16\cdot10^6 e^{180}\,B/(\log b)^2\).

So the local loss uses a fraction \(O((\log b)^{-2})\) of the budget \(B\). The exercises establish the row sum, the factorial and interval bounds, the spacing and signs of thick embeddings, and Theorem 82.4.1. They take (i)–(iii) and the chart estimate in the case analysis as given. With its five stated comparisons, Problem 80.1.7 combines the bounds for the terms of \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell\) and the extra valuation \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(C_1)\) defined there. Over every finite set of primes the weighted sum of \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell+\operatorname{val}_\ell(C_1)\) is at most \(B\).
