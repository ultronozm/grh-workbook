We present the arguments of Alpöge's [[https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf][proof]] of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions as a series of exercise sheets, each introducing one ingredient in its simplest setting.  The sheets follow the proof in the form of the [[https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean][rewritten Lean proof]] of L. Sernau, which is shorter than the [[https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis][Lean code]] first released and proves many steps by other arguments.  The presentation is inspired by the problem sequences of Onishchik and Vinberg, /Lie Groups and Algebraic Groups/ (Springer, 1990).  The sheets are drafts written and refereed by AI agents (Claude and Codex).

*Status, 26 November 2026.*  The collection is being extended to cover the complete proof and is updated continuously ([[https://github.com/ultronozm/grh-workbook/blob/main/STATUS.md][what changed]]).  Parts I–V and the side series are stable apart from small corrections.  The proof itself now occupies four Parts.  Part VI fixes the data and all the objects, and deduces the theorem from Theorems A, B and C.  Part VIII now proves Theorem A from its inputs, each developed in a sheet, except two bounds (the projection to good forms; the dimension of the ramified forms) and the theorem on elementary divisors used at exceptional primes, which are stated from the Lean code.  Part IX develops the determinant of a chain and the cost of one lift, up to the auxiliary forms and one operator identity stated from the Lean code; its last two inputs, the denominators of projections and the twisting theorem, are stated with all their definitions and not proved here, apart from the local loss and a lemma on roots of unity.  The second moment behind Theorem B (Part VII) is stated from the Lean code and is being developed.

*How the sheets fit together.*  The [[file:flowchart.html][flowchart]] shows which chapters depend on which, with sample seminar plans.  The proof bounds one sum over primes from above.  For a modulus $q_0$ and a prime $d$ let $R_{d,q_0}(y)$ be the sum of $\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_d(n))(1/n - 1/y)$ over $n \le y$, $n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}$, less its main term.  If for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and every prime $d$ of a suitable progression $R_{d,q_0}(y) \le y^{-1/2 + \varepsilon}$ for all large $y$, then no $L(s, \psi)$ with $\psi$ modulo $q_0$ vanishes in $\Re s > 1/2$ (Part I).  The bound comes from one determinant, whose size is compared with its divisibility (Part II): the Gram determinant of a family of automorphic forms under a chain of $m \approx \sqrt{x}$ theta lifts.  /Theorem A/: integral forms whose normalized translates have short lifts span the ramified source, because derivatives of theta functions with complex multiplication are highly divisible at primes that do not split (Parts III and IV), and families of their translates have bounded index.  /Theorem B/: the images under a few projected translates of the Poincaré map span almost all of the target (Part II, [[file:20261125T010000--rank-lemma-and-zero-estimate__workbook.html][/Few translates span/]] and [[file:20261125T010500--poincare-series-and-near-orthogonality__workbook.html][/Poincaré series and near orthogonality/]]; the count of the target and the main term of the second moment are modelled in [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][/Masses and the count of forms/]] and [[file:20261125T015500--siegel-weil-in-the-smallest-case__workbook.html][/The Siegel–Weil formula in the smallest case/]]).  /Theorem C/: a family that spans almost all of the target cannot have small covolume and small index together.  Sizes are read at the trivial twist and integrality at a twist chosen for each prime (Part II, [[file:20261126T030100--a-twist-for-each-prime__workbook.html][/A twist for each prime/]]).  The determinant of the chain changes under a twist as the product of the determinants of the single lifts, up to ratios of Hecke $L$-values (Part IX, [[file:20261126T030700--the-determinant-of-a-chain__workbook.html][/The determinant of a chain/]]).  The cost of a single lift is the size of its derivatives less their guaranteed divisibility, because that divisibility is attained (Part III, [[file:20261126T030200--the-bound-is-attained__workbook.html][/The bound is attained/]]), up to denominators of projections, which a suitable twist keeps small (Part V and [[file:20261126T031000--twists-with-few-congruences__workbook.html][/Twists with few congruences/]]).  Comparing the bounds gives an upper bound for $m$ times the remainder of the prime sum.  Parts I–V treat each ingredient in its simplest setting.  Parts VI–IX state each in the generality of the proof, as a declaration of the Lean proof named in a remark, and deduce the theorem.  The spaces, forms and twists of Parts VI–IX are defined once, in [[file:20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.html][/Spaces, forms and twists/]]; the chapters of Parts I–V treat simplest cases and point forward to the chapter of Parts VI–IX that states the general result.

*Notation.*  Each sheet defines the notation it uses; the following conventions are common to all sheets.  Throughout, $q_0$ and $d$ are the parameters and $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$, with character $\chi_K$; $E$ is the CM field of the data, with $f$ infinite places, among them the $a$-places $\Phi_a$ and the $b$-places $\Phi_b$, and $\kappa(\tau)$ is the weight of a place.  The scales are $x < x_1 < b$, with $a = xb$; $m$ is the number of lifts, $r$ and $k = r + 1 - m$ are the ranks of the target and of the source, and $t = 5r$: the ranks of the spaces $Y_j$ increase by $t$, those of the $X_j$ by $t + 1$.  $M = p_0p_1$ and the prime $N$ are the levels.  The main amount is $h_{\mathrm{main}} = mk\,h_{\mathrm{dir}}$, the remainder is $\mathrm{rem}$, and $U = fa\log^3 b$ is the unit of the errors.  A twist is $\vartheta$; a family of twists $\theta = (\theta_\iota)$ has one member for each isomorphism $\iota\colon \mathbb{C} \simeq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$.  $S \supseteq S_{\mathrm{ram}}$ are the source forms and the ramified ones, $T$ the target forms, of dimension $d_T$.  The diagram $\beta$ gives the weights of the forms; $\operatorname{val}$ is the valuation with $\operatorname{val}(p) = 1$ after a ring map to $\mathbb{C}_p$.  The remainder $R_{d,q}$ of the prime sum is as in [[file:20261125T001000--zeros-to-ramp-sums__workbook.html][/From zeros to a one-sided bound/]].  The numbers are defined in [[file:20261126T030300--the-data__workbook.html][/The data/]], the spaces, forms and twists in [[file:20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.html][/Spaces, forms and twists/]]; a chapter that uses one of these letters otherwise says so.

* Part I. The target
- [[file:20261125T001000--zeros-to-ramp-sums__workbook.html][1. From zeros to a one-sided bound]] \\
  An upper bound for a sum over primes excludes zeros: for $\zeta$, and for Dirichlet $L$-functions through one-sided bounds for a sum over primes in a progression with weight $1 - \chi_d$, for every prime $d$ of a class.
- [[file:20261125T001100--inert-primes-and-estimate-b__workbook.html][2. Inert primes and the choice of $d$]] \\
  What the weight $1 - \chi_d$ counts, and a prime $d$ for which the twists by $\chi_d$ of all characters of a given modulus have no zero in a given box.
- [[file:20261125T005000--galois-fourier-and-averaging__workbook.html][3. The places, the weights and the remainder]] \\
  Prime sums over the places of $E$ with two weights; the total remainder is an exact combination of $R_{q_0}(x)$, $R_{q_0}(x_1)$ and $R_1(x_1)$.
- [[file:20261125T005500--columns-from-a-sphere__workbook.html][4. Places from a sphere]] \\
  The places of weight $b$: dense, and sparse along multiplication by small primes, after Behrend.

- [[file:20261126T030100--a-twist-for-each-prime__workbook.html][6. A twist for each prime]] \\

- [[file:20261125T007000--stepanov-without-multiplicity__workbook.html][7. Stepanov without multiplicity]] \\

- [[file:20261125T003000--the-chain-and-the-square-root__workbook.html][8. The chain and the square root]] \\
  Stepanov's method, a chain of $m$ maps in place of an order of vanishing, and why $m \approx \sqrt{x}$.
- [[file:20261125T010000--rank-lemma-and-zero-estimate__workbook.html][9. Few translates span]] \\
  A second moment over a finite group bounds how many translates of one map are needed to span almost all of the target.
- [[file:20261125T010500--poincare-series-and-near-orthogonality__workbook.html][10. Poincaré series and near orthogonality]] \\
  A model for one input of Theorem B: Poincaré series of small index are nearly orthogonal, by Petersson's formula and a bound for Bessel functions.

* Part III. Divisibility at inert primes
- [[file:20261124T223000--elliptic-curves-for-the-proof__workbook.html][11. Complex tori, theta functions and periods]] \\
  Weierstrass and theta functions on the lemniscatic curve, and its period.
- [[file:20261125T009500--fock-model-and-theta-intertwiners__workbook.html][12. The Fock model and theta intertwiners]] \\
  Theta functions of a self-dual lattice with a fixed semicharacter form a line; intertwiners between compatible lattices compose up to a Gauss sum.
- [[file:20261124T223100--complex-multiplication-and-inert-reduction__workbook.html][13. Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime]] \\
  Formal groups with complex multiplication and the derivation $D^p$ at a supersingular prime.
- [[file:20261125T008000--one-cm-determinant__workbook.html][14. One CM determinant: Mertens modulo 4]] \\
  Mertens' theorem modulo 4 from determinants: a Gaussian Vandermonde for the split primes, a determinant of CM derivatives for the inert primes.
- [[file:20261124T214500--supersingular-theta-divisibility__workbook.html][15. The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime]] \\
  How divisible the invariant derivatives of integral series on the lemniscate are at an inert prime, in one variable, and why a theta function needs more.
- [[file:20261124T214600--the-isotropic-plane__workbook.html][16. The isotropic plane]] \\
  A second variable, along the isotropic eigenlines, gives a gain at every prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$.
- [[file:20261125T013700--pairings-on-a-formal-group-of-height-two__workbook.html][17. Pairings on a formal group of height two]] \\
  Frobenius-semilinear pairings on a formal group of height two, their normal form, and the general count of divisibility at a non-split prime.
- [[file:20261125T013500--hecke-nondegeneracy-on-the-tree__workbook.html][18. Hecke nondegeneracy on the tree]] \\
  Elementary divisors of a Hecke operator on the tree by Kummer's theorem, the colength of its image, and the exceptional primes where the proof needs it.
- [[file:20261126T030200--the-bound-is-attained__workbook.html][19. The bound is attained]] \\
  The exact rate of the least valuation of derivatives on one CM curve, with equality at powers of $p^2$ on the lemniscate; the sharpness on twelve copies that the proof uses is developed in Part IX.

* Part IV. Sizes, periods and counts
- [[file:20261125T009000--kronecker-limit-and-chowla-selberg__workbook.html][20. The Kronecker limit formula and Chowla–Selberg]] \\
  Kronecker's limit formula and Lerch's formula compute $L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)$ in two ways, and $e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)}$ is, up to an algebraic factor, the square of a CM period divided by $\pi^2$.
- [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][21. Masses and the count of forms]] \\
  The number of forms on a definite unitary group as an average over stabilizers, whose main term is a mass times a Weyl dimension.
- [[file:20261125T015500--siegel-weil-in-the-smallest-case__workbook.html][22. The Siegel–Weil formula in the smallest case]] \\
  Siegel's theorem: the mass-weighted average of theta series over a genus is an Eisenstein series, checked for even unimodular lattices and the rank-five class set of [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][/Masses and the count of forms/]]; the norm of a rank-one lift through averaged representation numbers.
- [[file:20261125T002100--definite-theta-lifts-and-norms__workbook.html][23. Definite theta lifts and their norms]] \\
  Theta lifts between definite unitary groups in the smallest cases: their entries and norms, the seesaw, and chains.
- [[file:20261125T016500--hecke-operators-and-theta-lifts__workbook.html][24. Hecke operators and theta lifts]] \\
  Eichler's commutation relation, $p$-neighbours and Brandt matrices.
- [[file:20261125T016700--divisibility-along-the-chain__workbook.html][25. Divisibility along the chain]] \\
  Carrying a gain along a chain of lifts by moving a Hecke operator through it, and the step of the proof that does this.

* Part V. Denominators and congruences
- [[file:20261125T004000--denominators-and-sha__workbook.html][26. Denominators and the Stickelberger element]] \\
  The index cost of a map that does not respect integral structures, and the Stickelberger element acting on class groups.
- [[file:20261125T004100--tate-twists-and-sha__workbook.html][27. Odd Tate twists]] \\
  Selmer classes of Tate twists, and the module of odd cocycles with prescribed ramification; at exponent zero it consists of homomorphisms from the class group.
- [[file:20261126T020430--the-minus-part-by-gauss-sums__workbook.html][28. Odd classes by Gauss sums]] \\
  One integer, from Stickelberger elements and Gauss sums, bounds the length of the module of odd cocycles at every $\ell$ and every twist in the range used.
- [[file:20261125T014100--congruence-modules__workbook.html][29. Congruence modules]] \\
  The cost of projecting to an eigenspace: in the lattice of all level-one forms of weight 12, the congruence module between $\Delta$ and the Eisenstein line is $\mathbb{Z}/691$.
- [[file:20261125T014500--eisenstein-congruences-and-selmer-classes__workbook.html][30. Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes]] \\
  Ribet's argument: a congruence with an Eisenstein series produces a Selmer class over $\mathbb{Q}$ and an unramified extension of a cyclotomic field.
- [[file:20261125T014700--pseudocharacters-and-the-length-bound__workbook.html][31. Pseudocharacters and the length bound]] \\
  Traces as pseudocharacters: a congruence gives a Selmer class, so Selmer lengths bound congruences; cocycles that obey the relations of a representation.
- [[file:20261125T011000--galois-representations-black-box__workbook.html][32. Black box: Galois representations of eigenforms]] \\
  The Galois representations attached to eigenforms on definite unitary groups, and the Hecke polynomial annihilating Frobenius at split spherical places away from $\ell$.

- [[file:20261126T030300--the-data__workbook.html][33. The data]] \\
  The field, the places, the scales, the ranks and the level primes; their sizes, the amounts that the proof compares, and that such data exist for every sufficiently large integer $x$.
- [[file:20261126T040100--spaces-forms-and-twists__workbook.html][34. Spaces, forms and twists]] \\

- [[file:20261126T031100--theorem-c-and-the-main-inequality__workbook.html][35. The main inequality]] \\
  Theorems A, B and C give the main inequality $m\,\mathrm{rem} \le fa\log^5 b$, and from it the one-sided bound and the theorem.

* Part VII. Theorem B: enough forms
- [[file:20261126T020210--bessel-poincare-series-and-their-gram-operators__workbook.html][36. Theorem B: enough forms]] \\
  Bessel–Poincaré series and their translates; a second moment shows that a few translates span almost all of the good target forms.

* Part VIII. Theorem A: short forms
- [[file:20261126T030600--theorem-a-short-forms__workbook.html][37. Theorem A: short forms]] \\
  Integral forms whose translated images have short lifts at the trivial twist span the ramified source, and families of their translates have bounded index.
- [[file:20261126T030400--the-seesaw-identity__workbook.html][38. The seesaw identity]] \\
  On ramified source forms, the lower chain applied to a Poincaré image equals one nonzero constant, the same for all twists, times the restricted lift along a definite chain.
- [[file:20261126T051200--transferring-a-bessel-period-through-a-theta-lift__workbook.html][39. Transferring a Bessel period through a theta lift]] \\
  How a Bessel period passes through one theta lift: constant terms indexed by classes of rational maps, strings and the string map, and the transferred Bessel function.
- [[file:20261126T051300--two-lifts-at-a-time__workbook.html][40. Two lifts at a time]] \\
  The transfers grouped in pairs: the string maps of two consecutive lifts fit into one isotropic map, and after each pair the function left is a lift between the definite spaces.
- [[file:20261126T051400--lattices-cuts-and-the-ramified-forms__workbook.html][41. Lattices, cuts and the ramified forms]] \\
  The integrals over the remaining outer variables as finite sums over lattices and their cuts; on ramified source forms the seesaw holds with one tensor in place of a chain datum.
- [[file:20261126T051500--the-seesaw-tensor-translated-vacua-gluing-and-the-unit__workbook.html][42. The seesaw tensor: translated vacua, gluing and the unit]] \\
  Fourier inversion on a residue module, gluing of pair data and a common denominator express the tensor in glued vacuum tensors, which gives the seesaw identity with one nonzero constant.
- [[file:20261126T030500--divisibility-of-the-composite-lift__workbook.html][43. Divisibility of the composite lift]] \\
  For each prime and each embedding, on a submodule of small colength, the composite lifts of level prime to that prime are divisible by the predicted gain up to an error; the errors are small in total.
- [[file:20261126T050100--the-count-congruences-directions-and-factorials__workbook.html][44. The count: congruences, directions and factorials]] \\
  The jets of a series in the normal form along products of minors: the credit of the factorials against the debit of the couplings, for two places and in general, with charges on the rows for the columns removed at marked places.
- [[file:20261126T050200--one-lift-at-a-small-non-split-prime__workbook.html][45. One lift at a small non-split prime]] \\
  The kernel of one lift is divisible by the stage bound of its diagram, which falls short of $\mathrm{gain}(p)/m$ only by the collision sum and a uniform error.
- [[file:20261126T050300--the-size-of-the-composite-lift__workbook.html][46. The size of the composite lift]] \\
  At the trivial twist the composite lift multiplies conjugate norms by at most $\exp(h_{\mathrm{main}} - m\,\mathrm{rem} + 2\sum_p \mathrm{gain}(p)\log p + U)$: one factorial for each row of each lift, and the level $N$ pays for the long rows.
- [[file:20261126T051600--the-level-prime-and-the-ramified-forms__workbook.html][47. The level prime and the ramified forms]] \\
  At the level prime $N$ the glued pairs of the composite lift carry poles; on ramified forms an operator at $N$ turns each pair into two direct lifts, and the loss is bounded on a submodule of small colength.
- [[file:20261126T051700--exceptional-primes-a-full-mark__workbook.html][48. Exceptional primes: a full mark]] \\
  At an exceptional prime the collisions exceed the unit of loss; a scaled Hecke operator of a full mark, applied before the chain, removes them at a bounded cost.

* Part IX. Theorem C: the lower bound
- [[file:20261126T040200--theorem-c__workbook.html][49. Theorem C]] \\
  A family spanning almost all target forms cannot have small covolume and small index together: the twisted lattice of the target, completed by auxiliary forms, with the cost of the single lifts and the factorization of the chain.
- [[file:20261126T030700--the-determinant-of-a-chain__workbook.html][50. The determinant of a chain]] \\
  The ratio of the Gram determinants of a chain of lifts at a twist and at the trivial twist factors into the same ratios for single lifts, ratios of Hecke $L$-values and a power of the norm of the modulus of the twist; auxiliary forms complete a family to a basis.
- [[file:20261126T050400--the-norm-of-one-lift__workbook.html][51. The norm of one lift]] \\
  The squared norm of a theta lift is a theta sum on the doubled space; by the Rallis inner product formula it is a scalar times a Hecke series, and the twist enters through one factor.
- [[file:20261126T050500--equal-multiplicities__workbook.html][52. Equal multiplicities]] \\
  One Hecke algebra acts on the good forms of two spaces of the same rank with different signs, with equal traces; hence equal dimensions, characteristic polynomials and joint eigenspaces.
- [[file:20261126T050600--the-exchange-identity__workbook.html][53. The exchange identity]] \\
  A theta integral over a large unitary group against one over a small group: removing a hyperbolic plane costs a product of partial Hecke $L$-values.
- [[file:20261126T050700--squares-and-the-triangle__workbook.html][54. Squares and the triangle]] \\
  The Gram operator of the chain of lifts on the target, from a triangle of lifts, the inner product formula on its first edges and the comparison at the last level.
- [[file:20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.html][55. The cost of one lift]] \\
  For a regular family of twists, one for each prime and each isomorphism above it, the ratios of the determinants of one lift at the trivial twist and at the twist have bounded height as a family.
- [[file:20261126T050800--compressions-and-false-lifts__workbook.html][56. Compressions and false lifts]] \\
  The Gram determinant of a lift at a twist, times the determinant of the compression of a comparison operator to the image of the lift, is a fixed number times a product of Hecke values; false lifts and clearing the projection.
- [[file:20261126T050900--hecke-values-as-norms-of-lifts__workbook.html][57. Hecke values as norms of lifts]] \\
  For a critical pair, the squared norm of a theta lift of a character from $\mathrm U(1)$ is a partial Hecke value, up to a power of the norm of the twist; the product of Hecke values of one lift.
- [[file:20261126T051000--the-bound-is-attained-at-many-places__workbook.html][58. The bound is attained at many places]] \\
  On twelve copies of the variables, one normalizing scalar makes a Taylor coefficient attain the divisibility predicted by the prime sum at every prime prime to the twist.
- [[file:20261126T051100--test-forms-and-the-height-of-a-hecke-ratio__workbook.html][59. Test forms and the height of a Hecke ratio]] \\
  Test forms bound the height of the inverse ratio of Hecke values for a main pair: the size from the jets at the trivial twist, the valuation from the attained divisibility; small ratios and regular families.
- [[file:20261126T030900--denominators-of-projections-to-lifts__workbook.html][60. Denominators of projections to lifts]] \\
  Denominators of projections of integral forms to lifts, bounded by local losses, Selmer lengths, odd classes and congruences between characters.
- [[file:20261126T051800--the-local-loss-and-the-sparse-places__workbook.html][61. The local loss and the sparse places]] \\
  The local loss in the clearing bound is the valuation of one integer at each prime; because the $b$-places are sparse, its sum over all primes is far below the budget.
- [[file:20261126T031000--twists-with-few-congruences__workbook.html][62. Twists with few congruences]] \\
  The twisting theorem: a twist for each prime and each isomorphism above it, with small Selmer lengths, inverse ratios of Hecke $L$-values of bounded height for its first $m$ powers, and denominators within a common budget.
- [[file:20261126T040300--roots-of-unity-for-many-polynomials__workbook.html][63. Roots of unity for many polynomials]] \\
  Finitely many polynomials, each small at some point, are small at once at roots of unity of $\ell$-power order, with a loss as small as one likes.
