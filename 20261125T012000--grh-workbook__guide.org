We present the arguments of Alpöge's [[https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf][proof]] of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions as a series of exercise sheets, each introducing one ingredient in its simplest setting.  The sheets follow the proof in the form of the [[https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean][rewritten Lean proof]] of L. Sernau, which is shorter than the [[https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis][Lean code]] first released and proves many steps by other arguments.  The presentation is inspired by the problem sequences of Onishchik and Vinberg, /Lie Groups and Algebraic Groups/ (Springer, 1990).  The sheets are drafts written and refereed by AI agents (Claude and Codex).

*How the sheets fit together.*  The proof bounds one sum over primes from above.  For a modulus $q_0$ and a prime $d$ let $R_{d,q_0}(y)$ be the sum of $\Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_d(n))(1/n - 1/y)$ over $n \le y$, $n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}$, less its main term.  If for every $\varepsilon > 0$ and every prime $d$ of a suitable progression $R_{d,q_0}(y) \le y^{-1/2 + \varepsilon}$ for all large $y$, then no $L(s, \psi)$ with $\psi$ modulo $q_0$ vanishes in $\Re s > 1/2$ (Part I).  The bound comes from one determinant, whose size is compared with its divisibility (Part II): the Gram determinant of a family of automorphic forms under a chain of $m \approx \sqrt{x}$ theta lifts.  /Theorem A/: integral forms whose normalized translates have short lifts span the ramified source, because derivatives of theta functions with complex multiplication are highly divisible at primes that do not split (Parts III and IV), and families of their translates have bounded index.  /Theorem B/: the images under a few projected translates of the Poincaré map span almost all of the target (Part II, [[file:20261125T010000--rank-lemma-and-zero-estimate__workbook.html][/Few translates span/]] and [[file:20261125T010500--poincare-series-and-near-orthogonality__workbook.html][/Poincaré series and near orthogonality/]]).  /Theorem C/: a family that spans almost all of the target cannot have small covolume and index together, because the cost of each lift is controlled by values of Hecke $L$-functions and their denominators (Parts IV and V).  Comparing the bounds gives an upper bound for $m$ times the remainder of the prime sum.  Parts I–V treat each ingredient in its simplest setting.  Part VI states each in the generality of the proof, as a declaration of the rewritten Lean proof named in a remark, and deduces the theorem.
A [[file:flowchart.html][flowchart]] shows how the chapters depend on each other, with sample seminar plans.

*Notation.*  Each sheet defines what it uses.  A few letters recur throughout: $p$ is a prime, usually not split in $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ (or $K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ in the simplest cases), and $d$ is an auxiliary prime, fixed in each datum; $\psi$ is the character whose $L$-function is treated; $R_{d,q}(y) = \sum_{n \le y,\, n \equiv 1 \bmod q} \Lambda(n)(1 - \chi_d(n))(1/n - 1/y) - \log y/\varphi(q) - c_{d,q}$ is the remainder of the prime sum modulo $q$, written $R_q$ when $d$ is fixed.  In the later chapters $E = K(\zeta_{q_1})$ is a CM field, while in the chapters on elliptic curves $E$ is an elliptic curve; $x < x_1 < b$ and $a = xb$ are the scales; $m$ is the number of lifts, $r$ and $k$ are ranks and $t = 5r$ is the step between ranks; $\iota$ is an isomorphism between $\mathbb{C}$ and $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell$.  The ramp sum $G(x) = \sum_n \Lambda(n)(x/n - 1)_+ - x\log x + (1 + \gamma)x$ occurs only in Part I and in [[file:20261125T003000--the-chain-and-the-square-root__workbook.html][/The chain and the square root/]].

* Part I. The target
- [[file:20261125T001000--zeros-to-ramp-sums__workbook.html][1. From zeros to a one-sided bound]] \\
  An upper bound for a sum over primes excludes zeros: for $\zeta$, and for Dirichlet $L$-functions through one-sided bounds for a sum over primes in a progression with weight $1 - \chi_d$, for every prime $d$ of a class.
- [[file:20261125T001100--inert-primes-and-estimate-b__workbook.html][2. Inert primes and the choice of $d$]] \\
  What the weight $1 - \chi_d$ counts, and a prime $d$ for which the twists by $\chi_d$ of all characters of a given modulus have no zero in a given box.
- [[file:20261125T005000--galois-fourier-and-averaging__workbook.html][3. The places, the weights and the remainder]] \\
  Prime sums over the places of $E$ with two weights; the total remainder is an exact combination of $R_{q_0}(x)$, $R_{q_0}(x_1)$ and $R_1(x_1)$.
- [[file:20261125T005500--columns-from-a-sphere__workbook.html][4. Places from a sphere]] \\
  The places of weight $b$: dense, and sparse along multiplication by small primes, after Behrend.

* Part II. The method
- [[file:20261125T000000--slope-method__workbook.html][5. Size against divisibility]] \\
  One determinant, from Chebyshev to heights of families, index and covolume.
- [[file:20261126T030100--a-twist-for-each-prime__workbook.html][6. A twist for each prime]] \\
  Sizes read at the trivial twist, valuations at a twist chosen for each prime and each isomorphism above it.
- [[file:20261125T003000--the-chain-and-the-square-root__workbook.html][7. The chain and the square root]] \\
  Stepanov's method, a chain of $m$ maps in place of an order of vanishing, and why $m \approx \sqrt{x}$.
- [[file:20261125T010000--rank-lemma-and-zero-estimate__workbook.html][8. Few translates span]] \\
  A second moment over a finite group bounds how many translates of one map are needed to span almost all of the target.
- [[file:20261125T010500--poincare-series-and-near-orthogonality__workbook.html][9. Poincaré series and near orthogonality]] \\
  A model for one input of Theorem B: Poincaré series of small index are nearly orthogonal, by Petersson's formula and a bound for Bessel functions.

* Part III. Divisibility at inert primes
- [[file:20261124T223000--elliptic-curves-for-the-proof__workbook.html][10. Complex tori, theta functions and periods]] \\
  Weierstrass and theta functions on the lemniscatic curve, and its period.
- [[file:20261125T009500--fock-model-and-theta-intertwiners__workbook.html][11. The Fock model and theta intertwiners]] \\
  Theta functions of a self-dual lattice with a fixed semicharacter form a line; intertwiners between compatible lattices compose up to a Gauss sum.
- [[file:20261124T223100--complex-multiplication-and-inert-reduction__workbook.html][12. Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime]] \\
  Formal groups with complex multiplication and the derivation $D^p$ at a supersingular prime.
- [[file:20261125T008000--one-cm-determinant__workbook.html][13. One CM determinant: Mertens modulo 4]] \\
  Mertens' theorem modulo 4 from determinants: a Gaussian Vandermonde for the split primes, a determinant of CM derivatives for the inert primes.
- [[file:20261124T214500--supersingular-theta-divisibility__workbook.html][14. The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime]] \\
  How divisible the invariant derivatives of integral series on the lemniscate are at an inert prime, in one variable, and why a theta function needs more.
- [[file:20261124T214600--the-isotropic-plane__workbook.html][15. The isotropic plane]] \\
  A second variable, along the isotropic eigenlines, gives a gain at every prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$.
- [[file:20261125T013700--pairings-on-a-formal-group-of-height-two__workbook.html][16. Pairings on a formal group of height two]] \\
  Frobenius-semilinear pairings on a formal group of height two, their normal form, and the general count of divisibility at a non-split prime.
- [[file:20261125T013500--hecke-nondegeneracy-on-the-tree__workbook.html][17. Hecke nondegeneracy on the tree]] \\
  Elementary divisors of a Hecke operator on the tree by Kummer's theorem, the colength of its image, and the exceptional primes where the proof needs it.
- [[file:20261126T030200--the-bound-is-attained__workbook.html][18. The bound is attained]] \\
  The exact rate of the least valuation of derivatives on one CM curve, with equality at powers of $p^2$ on the lemniscate, and the sharpness bound on twelve copies used in the proof.

* Part IV. Sizes, periods and counts
- [[file:20261125T009000--kronecker-limit-and-chowla-selberg__workbook.html][19. The Kronecker limit formula and Chowla–Selberg]] \\
  Kronecker's limit formula and Lerch's formula compute $L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)$ in two ways, and $e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)}$ is, up to an algebraic factor, the square of a CM period divided by $\pi^2$.
- [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][20. Masses and the count of forms]] \\
  The number of forms on a definite unitary group as an average over stabilizers, whose main term is a mass times a Weyl dimension.
- [[file:20261125T015500--siegel-weil-in-the-smallest-case__workbook.html][21. The Siegel–Weil formula in the smallest case]] \\
  The mass-weighted average of theta series over a genus is an Eisenstein series, for $E_8$, rank 16 and the class set of [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][/Masses and the count of forms/]].
- [[file:20261125T002100--definite-theta-lifts-and-norms__workbook.html][22. Definite theta lifts and their norms]] \\
  Theta lifts between definite unitary groups in the smallest cases: their entries and norms, the seesaw, and chains.
- [[file:20261125T016500--hecke-operators-and-theta-lifts__workbook.html][23. Hecke operators and theta lifts]] \\
  Eichler's commutation relation, $p$-neighbours and Brandt matrices.
- [[file:20261125T016700--divisibility-along-the-chain__workbook.html][24. Divisibility along the chain]] \\
  Carrying a gain along a chain of lifts by moving a Hecke operator through it, and the step of the proof that does this.

* Part V. Denominators and congruences
- [[file:20261125T004000--denominators-and-sha__workbook.html][25. Denominators and the Stickelberger element]] \\
  The index cost of a map that does not respect integral structures, and the Stickelberger element acting on class groups.
- [[file:20261125T004100--tate-twists-and-sha__workbook.html][26. Odd Tate twists]] \\
  Selmer classes of Tate twists, and the module of odd cocycles with prescribed ramification; at exponent zero it consists of homomorphisms from the class group.
- [[file:20261126T020430--the-minus-part-by-gauss-sums__workbook.html][27. Odd classes by Gauss sums]] \\
  One integer, from Stickelberger elements and Gauss sums, bounds the length of the module of odd cocycles at every $\ell$ and every twist in the range used.
- [[file:20261125T014100--congruence-modules__workbook.html][28. Congruence modules]] \\
  The cost of projecting to an eigenspace: in the lattice of all level-one forms of weight 12, the congruence module between $\Delta$ and the Eisenstein line is $\mathbb{Z}/691$.
- [[file:20261125T014500--eisenstein-congruences-and-selmer-classes__workbook.html][29. Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes]] \\
  Ribet's argument: a congruence with an Eisenstein series produces a Selmer class over $\mathbb{Q}$ and an unramified extension of a cyclotomic field.
- [[file:20261125T014700--pseudocharacters-and-the-length-bound__workbook.html][30. Pseudocharacters and the length bound]] \\
  Traces as pseudocharacters: a congruence gives a Selmer class, so Selmer lengths bound congruences; cocycles that obey the relations of a representation.
- [[file:20261125T011000--galois-representations-black-box__workbook.html][31. Black box: Galois representations of eigenforms]] \\
  The Galois representations attached to eigenforms on definite unitary groups, and the Hecke polynomial annihilating Frobenius at split spherical places away from $\ell$.

* Part VI. The proof
Each chapter of this part states its results in the form of the Lean proof, at the commit named under [[*Sources][Sources]], and says which statements were read from the code and which are derived here.
- [[file:20261126T030300--the-data__workbook.html][32. The data]] \\
  The field, the places, the scales, the ranks and the level primes; their sizes, and that they exist for every sufficiently large integer $x$.
- [[file:20261126T020210--bessel-poincare-series-and-their-gram-operators__workbook.html][33. Theorem B: enough forms]] \\
  Bessel–Poincaré series and their translates; a second moment shows that a few translates span almost all of the good target forms.
- [[file:20261126T030400--the-seesaw-identity__workbook.html][34. The seesaw identity]] \\
  On ramified source forms, the lower chain applied to a Poincaré image equals one nonzero constant, the same for all twists, times the restricted lift along a definite chain.
- [[file:20261126T030500--divisibility-of-the-composite-lift__workbook.html][35. Divisibility of the composite lift]] \\
  For each prime and each embedding, on a submodule of small colength, the composite lifts of level prime to that prime are divisible by the predicted gain up to an error; the errors are small in total.
- [[file:20261126T030600--theorem-a-short-forms__workbook.html][36. Theorem A: short forms]] \\
  Integral forms whose translated images have short lifts at the trivial twist span the ramified source, and families of their translates have bounded index.
- [[file:20261126T030700--the-determinant-of-a-chain__workbook.html][37. The determinant of a chain]] \\
  The ratio of the Gram determinants of a chain of lifts at a twist and at the trivial twist factors into the same ratios for single lifts, ratios of Hecke $L$-values and a power of the norm of the modulus of the twist.
- [[file:20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.html][38. The cost of one lift]] \\
  For a regular family of twists, one for each prime and each isomorphism above it, the ratios of the determinants of one lift at the trivial twist and at the twist have bounded height as a family.
- [[file:20261126T030900--denominators-of-projections-to-lifts__workbook.html][39. Denominators of projections to lifts]] \\
  Denominators of projections of integral forms to lifts, bounded by local losses, Selmer lengths, odd classes and congruences between characters.
- [[file:20261126T031000--twists-with-few-congruences__workbook.html][40. Twists with few congruences]] \\
  A twist for each prime and each isomorphism above it, such that the inverse ratios of Hecke $L$-values for its first $m$ powers have bounded height and the denominators stay within a common budget.
- [[file:20261126T031100--theorem-c-and-the-main-inequality__workbook.html][41. Theorem C and the main inequality]] \\
  Theorem C: a family spanning almost all target forms cannot have small covolume and index together; with Theorems A and B this bounds the prime sum and proves the theorem.
