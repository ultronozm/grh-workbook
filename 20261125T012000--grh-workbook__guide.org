We present the arguments of Alpöge's [[https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf][proof]] of the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions as a series of exercise sheets, each introducing one ingredient in its simplest setting.  The sheets were assembled from the [[https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis][Lean code]] and from the expositions listed under [[*Sources][Sources]] below.  The presentation is inspired by the problem sequences of Onishchik and Vinberg, /Lie Groups and Algebraic Groups/ (Springer, 1990).  The sheets are drafts written and refereed by AI agents (Claude and Codex).

*How the sheets fit together.*  Parts I–V take each ingredient in its simplest setting: one field, small rank, one prime or one scale.  Part VI states the ingredients in the generality the proof uses, as cited results, each pointing back to its simplest case and marked as classical, derived in Adriazola's reconstruction, or assumed there as a hypothesis.  The Lean formalization proves the final theorem; the corresponding steps of the reconstruction are hypotheses assumed there, and we have not checked that the intermediate statements of the formalization are these hypotheses (in several places they are known to differ).  The final chapter, [[file:20261125T021500--assembly-at-one-scale__workbook.html][/Assembly at one scale/]], assembles them into the inequality at one scale and records, for each input, whether it is classical, derived in the reconstruction, or a hypothesis there.  Part VI is the least developed part and will grow.
A [[file:flowchart.html][flowchart]] shows how the chapters depend on each other, with sample seminar plans.

*Notation.*  Each sheet defines what it uses.  A few letters recur throughout: $p$ is a prime, usually inert in $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ (or $K = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ in the simplest cases), and $q$, $d$ are the auxiliary primes; $G(x) = \sum_n \Lambda(n)(x/n - 1)_+ - x\log x + (1 + \gamma)x$ is the ramp sum less its main term, $G_\chi$ its twist by $\chi$, and $c_\chi = \frac{L'}{L}(1, \chi)$; $\psi$ is the character whose $L$-function is treated.  In the later chapters $E \supset K$ is a CM field, while in the chapters on elliptic curves $E$ is an elliptic curve.

* Part I. The target
- [[file:20261125T001000--zeros-to-ramp-sums__workbook.html][1. From zeros to a one-sided ramp sum]] \\
  Reduce the Riemann hypothesis for a Dirichlet $L$-function to an upper bound for a one-sided ramp sum over primes.
- [[file:20261125T001100--inert-primes-and-estimate-b__workbook.html][2. Inert primes and Estimate B]] \\
  Recover the ramp sum from ramp sums over odd powers of primes inert in an imaginary quadratic field.
- [[file:20261125T001500--real-characters-over-prime-moduli__workbook.html][3. Real characters over prime moduli]] \\
  Estimate B for the first auxiliary prime, from mean squares over prime moduli, Gallagher's density estimate and Page's theorem; what must be repeated for the second.
- [[file:20261125T005000--galois-fourier-and-averaging__workbook.html][4. Fourier analysis on the Galois group]] \\
  Expand weighted prime sums over the places of an abelian CM field in characters, and average over translates.
- [[file:20261125T005500--columns-from-a-sphere__workbook.html][5. Columns from a sphere]] \\
  How the distinguished columns are chosen: Behrend's construction gives a dense set that meets every short progression at most twice.

* Part II. The method
- [[file:20261125T000000--slope-method__workbook.html][6. The slope method]] \\
  Bound a sum over primes by comparing divisibility with size, from Chebyshev to Bost's slope inequality.
- [[file:20261125T000100--slope-inequality-in-the-mean__workbook.html][7. The slope inequality in the mean]] \\
  Use many maps into one target to replace its maximal slope by its mean slope.
- [[file:20261125T003000--the-chain-and-the-square-root__workbook.html][8. The chain and the square root]] \\
  Stepanov's method for $y^2 = f(x)$, and a chain of maps between lattices in place of an order of vanishing.
- [[file:20261125T007000--stepanov-without-multiplicity__workbook.html][9. Stepanov without multiplicity]] \\
  What the size and divisibility of one determinant say about primes, and about Siegel zeros.
- [[file:20261125T010000--rank-lemma-and-zero-estimate__workbook.html][10. The rank lemma and the zero estimate]] \\
  The rank hypothesis of the mean slope inequality from Gram matrices, equidistribution and a relative trace formula.
- [[file:20261125T010500--poincare-series-and-near-orthogonality__workbook.html][11. Poincaré series and near orthogonality]] \\
  The rank hypothesis as in the argument, from the Petersson formula and the decay of Bessel functions.

* Part III. Divisibility at inert primes
- [[file:20261124T223000--elliptic-curves-for-the-proof__workbook.html][12. Complex tori, theta functions and periods]] \\
  Weierstrass and theta functions on the lemniscatic curve, and its period.
- [[file:20261125T009500--fock-model-and-theta-intertwiners__workbook.html][13. The Fock model and theta intertwiners]] \\
  Theta functions of a lattice form a line; intertwiners between lattices compose up to a Gauss sum.
- [[file:20261124T223100--complex-multiplication-and-inert-reduction__workbook.html][14. Complex multiplication and reduction at an inert prime]] \\
  Formal groups with complex multiplication and the derivation $D^p$ at a supersingular prime.
- [[file:20261125T008000--one-cm-determinant__workbook.html][15. One CM determinant: Mertens modulo 4]] \\
  How much of $\log r!$ one determinant of CM values recovers from each prime.
- [[file:20261124T214500--supersingular-theta-divisibility__workbook.html][16. The invariant derivation at a supersingular prime]] \\
  How divisible the derivatives of a lemniscatic theta function are at an inert prime, in one variable.
- [[file:20261124T214600--the-isotropic-plane__workbook.html][17. The isotropic plane]] \\
  A second variable, along the isotropic eigenlines, gives a gain at every prime $p \equiv 3 \pmod 8$.
- [[file:20261125T013700--pairings-on-a-formal-group-of-height-two__workbook.html][18. Pairings on a formal group of height two]] \\
  Why the gain at an inert prime is $\alpha p/(p^2-1)$: Frobenius-semilinear pairings on the Lubin–Tate group and their normal form.
- [[file:20261125T013500--hecke-nondegeneracy-on-the-tree__workbook.html][19. Hecke nondegeneracy on the tree]] \\
  The loss that balances the gain: elementary divisors of a Hecke operator on symmetric powers, by Kummer's theorem.

* Part IV. Sizes, periods and counts
- [[file:20261125T009000--kronecker-limit-and-chowla-selberg__workbook.html][20. The Kronecker limit formula and Chowla–Selberg]] \\
  Kronecker's limit formula and Lerch's formula compute $L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)$ in two ways, and $e^{\gamma - L'(1,\chi)/L(1,\chi)}$ is, up to an algebraic factor, the square of a CM period divided by $2\pi i$.
- [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][21. Masses and the count of forms]] \\
  The number of forms on a definite unitary group, as a mass times a Weyl dimension.
- [[file:20261125T015500--siegel-weil-in-the-smallest-case__workbook.html][22. The Siegel–Weil formula in the smallest case]] \\
  The mass-weighted average of theta series over a genus is an Eisenstein series, for $E_8$, rank 16 and the class set of [[file:20261125T002000--definite-theta-lifts__workbook.html][/Masses and the count of forms/]].
- [[file:20261125T002100--definite-theta-lifts-and-norms__workbook.html][23. Definite theta lifts and their norms]] \\
  The entries and sizes of theta lifts between definite unitary groups in the smallest cases.
- [[file:20261125T016500--hecke-operators-and-theta-lifts__workbook.html][24. Hecke operators and theta lifts]] \\
  Eichler's commutation relation, $p$-neighbours and Brandt matrices.
- [[file:20261125T016700--divisibility-along-the-chain__workbook.html][25. Divisibility along the chain]] \\
  Carrying gains along a chain of lifts by inverting the one-step Hecke relation, and what it costs.
- [[file:20261125T006000--covolumes__workbook.html][26. Covolumes of lattices of sections]] \\
  Exact covolumes for polynomials and for forms on a definite group, against the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem.
- [[file:20261125T017500--chow-forms-and-effective-hilbert-samuel__workbook.html][27. Chow forms and an effective Hilbert–Samuel bound]] \\
  A lower bound for the covolume of sections from the Chow form and Hadamard's inequality.
- [[file:20261125T006500--covolumes-and-periods__workbook.html][28. Covolumes and periods]] \\
  How a period takes the place of the self-intersection for theta functions on an elliptic curve.

* Part V. Denominators and black boxes
- [[file:20261125T004000--denominators-and-sha__workbook.html][29. Denominators and the Stickelberger element]] \\
  The cost of a map that does not respect integral structures, measured by an eigenspace of a class group.
- [[file:20261125T004100--tate-twists-and-sha__workbook.html][30. Tate–Shafarevich groups of Tate twists]] \\
  The Galois cohomology groups that play this role for a Tate twist, and their orders.
- [[file:20261125T014100--congruence-modules__workbook.html][31. Congruence modules]] \\
  The denominator at the twisted places: the congruence module of $\Delta$ is $\mathbb{Z}/691$.
- [[file:20261125T014500--eisenstein-congruences-and-selmer-classes__workbook.html][32. Eisenstein congruences and Selmer classes]] \\
  Ribet's argument: a congruence with an Eisenstein series produces an unramified class.
- [[file:20261125T014700--pseudocharacters-and-the-length-bound__workbook.html][33. Pseudocharacters and the length bound]] \\
  The converse direction, bounding a Selmer group by the length of a Hecke algebra modulo its Eisenstein ideal.
- [[file:20261125T011000--galois-representations-black-box__workbook.html][34. Black boxes: Galois representations and local–global compatibility]] \\
  What a Galois representation remembers at $p \ne \ell$, and what an automorphic representation gives to compare it with.

* Part VI. The ingredients in general, and the assembly
These chapters state each ingredient in the generality the proof uses, as precisely cited results, each pointing back to its simplest case in the chapters above, with its status.  This part is under construction: for now it mostly records statements, and we expect to expand it substantially, with fuller accounts of the proofs drawn from the Lean code.  Some chapters, listed just after the Part VI chapter they develop and drawn below it in the [[file:flowchart.html][flowchart]], state one of its inputs in the form the Lean formalization proves, and outline that proof.
- [[file:20261126T000000--the-objects-of-the-construction__workbook.html][35. The objects of the construction]] \\
  The field and its places, the hermitian spaces and lattices, the chain of lifts, words, tops and nodes, and the period maps: the vocabulary of the chapters that follow, with the smallest instance written out.
- [[file:20261125T020000--the-analytic-frame__workbook.html][36. The analytic frame]] \\
  Windows, translates and the auxiliary characters, in general.
- [[file:20261125T020100--divisibility-at-inert-primes-in-general__workbook.html][37. Divisibility at inert primes, in general]] \\
  Theta kernels of hermitian lattices in Lubin–Tate coordinates, the valuation estimate, the chain and the Hecke image: the prime-sum and slope bounds in general.
- [[file:20261126T020110--the-normalized-kernel-law__workbook.html][38. The normalized kernel law]] \\
  The scalar in the kernel law, as the Lean formalization controls it: one theta function of one CM curve, normalized at the origin, and its fourth tensor power.
- [[file:20261126T020120--frames-at-odd-positions-and-unit-scalars__workbook.html][39. Frames at odd positions and the unit scalars]] \\
  Dual frames at the odd positions of a Frobenius orbit, and why the scalars normalizing the steps are units.
- [[file:20261126T020130--nondegeneracy-at-an-edge__workbook.html][40. Nondegeneracy at an edge]] \\
  The condition on one edge of the building that controls the Hecke image, as the Lean formalization states it, checked in the smallest model; why it needs the Frobenius twist.
- [[file:20261126T020140--the-colength-of-a-hecke-image__workbook.html][41. The colength of a Hecke image]] \\
  A bound for the colength of the image of a product of Hecke operators, linear in the depth and the denominator, from nondegeneracy at the edges.
- [[file:20261126T020150--gain-and-loss-at-an-inert-prime__workbook.html][42. Gain and loss at an inert prime]] \\
  The gain at each Frobenius orbit, the divisibility of the lifts on the Hecke image, and the bookkeeping between them, which the formalization proves on separate branches.
- [[file:20261125T020200--slopes-frames-and-bessel-poincare-series__workbook.html][43. Slopes, frames and Bessel–Poincaré series]] \\
  The slope inequality for the family of period maps, the frame theorem, and near orthogonality in general.
- [[file:20261125T020300--heights-periods-and-the-auxiliary-prime__workbook.html][44. Heights, the CM period and the auxiliary prime]] \\
  The height of each lift through its linear term, the CM period in general, and the count of forms.
- [[file:20261126T020310--counting-forms-and-the-critical-level__workbook.html][45. Counting forms and the critical level]] \\
  The two dimensions of the final pairing given exactly by the mass formula, and the auxiliary prime bounded below through critical levels.
- [[file:20261125T020400--local-terms-congruence-defects-and-selmer-windows__workbook.html][46. Local terms, congruence defects and Selmer windows]] \\
  The local terms of each lift, the congruence defects at the twisted places, and the Selmer hypothesis on windows, in general.
- [[file:20261126T020410--local-exponents-of-the-lifts__workbook.html][47. Local exponents of the lifts]] \\
  The local exponents given by formulas, and why their sizes are bounded: a congruence at the terminal prime, rows at the level primes, Minkowski's bound and orbit losses at inert primes.
- [[file:20261125T021500--assembly-at-one-scale__workbook.html][48. Assembly at one scale]] \\
  The final inequality at a single scale $x$: each term of the error traced to the estimate that pays for it, the choice $m \approx x^{1/2}$, and what must hold uniformly in a window.

* Side series
One short series stands outside the sequence.
- S. The sign-only method.  These two sheets follow the proof written out in [[https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00789v1][Alderliefste's deposit]], which reports that it was produced by an AI model and that no human has checked it.  The weak Lefschetz statement that the sheets quote from that text has not yet been compared with Katz's appendix to /Affine cohomological transforms, perversity, and monodromy/ (1993), its published source.  The series is a model: the proof of the Riemann hypothesis for $L$-functions does not use it.
