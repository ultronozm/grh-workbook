The small-twists theorem gives a single character whose first \(m\) powers bound the normalized Selmer lengths and the valuations of the Stickelberger system evaluations. To use the small-twists theorem in the proof of Theorem 85.1.2, we need to supply Selmer data for the representations and a bound for each datum. We compute the global term in rank one, bound the three terms for a general lattice, then combine the Selmer data with the systems of the Hecke ratios.

Prerequisites.

Use the data of Chapter 36, the chosen places, tame characters and critical pairs of Chapter 37, and the main-summand-like condition, auxiliary twists and Selmer bound of Definitions 80.1.7, 80.1.5 and 80.1.9. Use the Selmer datum, its three terms, characters for \(Q\) and torsion levels from Chapter 87, and the crystalline cocycles and their rank from Chapter 34. We use Theorems 85.1.1 and 89.3.1. For local reciprocity we use that local units map onto the abelianized inertia image, in either Frobenius convention. The inverse avatar uses the convention of Definition 80.1.5. We use elementary divisors over a discrete valuation ring [La]. Write \(\operatorname{len}_{\mathcal O}\) for ordinary module length and \(e_{\mathcal O}=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O}(\ell)\), so normalized length is ordinary length divided by \(e_{\mathcal O}\). Use \(G_E\), \(g^c\), the cyclotomic character \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) and coefficient rings as in Section 80.1, with \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O}(0)=+\infty\).

§90.1. Rank-one calculations Tag 23BV

The first three problems measure how long a character and its conjugate dual agree modulo powers of \(3\). For the rank-one calculation let \(E\) be any CM field, let \(\ell=3\), and let \(\mathcal O\) contain \(\mathbb Z_3[\mathrm i]\). Give the rank-one lattice \(\mathcal O\) the action of a continuous character \(\chi\colon G_E\to\mathcal O^\times\).

Tag 23BW

Problem 90.1.1. For \(0\ne a\in\mathcal O\), use the conjugate additive dual of Definition 87.3.1. After choosing an \(\mathcal O\)-linear identification of the additive dual of \(\mathcal O/a\mathcal O\) with \(\mathcal O/a\mathcal O\), the equivariant maps to the conjugate dual correspond to the elements of \(\mathcal O/a\mathcal O\) killed by every difference \[\chi(g)-\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g^c)\chi((g^c)^{-1}),\qquad g\in G_E.\]

Used in: Problem 90.1.2
Tag 23BX

Problem 90.1.2. Let \(j_0\ge 1\) be an integer and suppose \(\chi(g^c)\chi(g)=\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{-(j_0-1)}\) for every \(g\in G_E\). The supremum, over \(0\ne a\in\mathcal O\), of the normalized lengths of the modules in Problem 90.1.1 is \(\min_{g\in G_E}\operatorname{val}_3(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{j_0}-1)\), with \(\operatorname{val}_3(0)=+\infty\).

Tag 23BY

Problem 90.1.3. For \(j_0=1,2,3,4,6\), show that the minimum of \(\operatorname{val}_3(u^{j_0}-1)\) over \(u\in\mathbb Z_3^\times\) is respectively \(0,1,0,1,2\). At \(u=7\) the values are \(1,1,2,1,2\), and at \(u=19\) they are \(2,2,3,2,3\).

Problem 90.1.3 computes the minimum in Problem 90.1.2 when \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(G_E)=\mathbb Z_3^\times\). The cyclotomic minimum is of the same form as the number \(\nu_\ell\) in Zhang’s account of the Selmer bound for finite-order twists [Zh].

The next two problems measure the length of a module of rank-one cocycles over \(\mathbb Q_7\). We use the classical presentation of the tame quotient by a generator \(s\) of tame inertia and an arithmetic Frobenius lift \(\mathrm{Fr}\), with relation \(\mathrm{Fr}\,s=s^q\,\mathrm{Fr}\), where \(q\) is the residue-field size [SLF]. In residue characteristic \(p\), wild inertia is pro-\(p\) and the finite quotients of tame inertia have order prime to \(p\).

Tag 23BZ

Problem 90.1.4. Over \(\mathbb Q_7\) take the rank-one module \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), with wild inertia acting trivially, \(s\) acting by \(4\) and \(\mathrm{Fr}\) by \(u\in\{1,4,7\}\). The pairs \((z(s),z(\mathrm{Fr}))\) of continuous cocycle values are exactly the solutions of \[(1-4^7)z(\mathrm{Fr})=(1+4+\cdots+4^6-u)z(s) \quad\text{in }\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z.\]

Used in: Problem 90.1.5
Tag 23C2

Problem 90.1.5. For each \(u\) in Problem 90.1.4 there are \(27\) pairs, giving length \(3=2+\operatorname{val}_3(4-1)\) over \(\mathbb Z_3\).

The next problem bounds the length of the restrictions of global cocycles to a decomposition group.

Tag 23C3

Problem 90.1.6. For any prime \(\ell\), let \(L\) be an \(\mathcal O\)-lattice with continuous \(G_E\)-action. Fix a finite place \(v\nmid\ell\) of residue characteristic \(p\) and a decomposition group \(G_v\) at \(v\). Suppose some positive integer \(n_v\) prime to \(p\) satisfies \(L(h^{n_v})=1\) for all inertia elements \(h\) in \(G_v\), where \(L(g)\) denotes the matrix of the action. Suppose also that \(L(h)=1\) for every inertia element \(h\) in some neighborhood of \(1\) in \(G_v\). For \(0\ne a\in\mathcal O\) let \(C_v(a)\) be the module of restrictions to \(G_v\) of locally constant global cocycles with coefficients in \(L/aL\). For every inertia element \(\delta\) in \(G_v\), prove \[\begin{align} \operatorname{len}_{\mathcal O} C_v(a)&\le \operatorname{len}_{\mathcal O}(L/aL)+\operatorname{len}_{\mathcal O}\ker\bigl(L(\delta)-1\mid L/aL\bigr)\\ &\le \operatorname{len}_{\mathcal O}(L/aL)+\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O}\det\bigl(L(\delta)-1\bigr). \end{align}\]

Tag 23C4

Problem 90.1.7. For multiplication by \(6\) on \((\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^2\), the kernel has \(9\) elements and length \(2=2\operatorname{val}_3(6)\) over \(\mathbb Z_3\).

§90.2. The three terms of a datum Tag 23C5

We now bound the global term, the sum of the local terms over the places dividing \(M\) prime to \(\ell\), and the two determinant valuations that make up the inertia term.

Tag 23C6

Problem 90.2.1. Let \(L\) be a Galois lattice of positive rank \(r'\) whose action matrices span \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O}(L)\). Suppose \(g\) and \((g^c)^{-1}\) act by scalars \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha'\). For each \(0\ne a\in\mathcal O\), on the equivariant maps \(L/aL\to (L/aL)^{*c}\) of Definition 87.3.1, evaluation at a basis vector is injective, and every map is killed by \(\alpha-\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g^c)\alpha'\). Deduce that the supremum defining the global term is at most \(r'\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O}(\alpha-\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g^c)\alpha')\).

Used in: Problem 90.2.2

Fix \(\iota\colon\mathbb C\simeq\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) and a main-summand-like \(R\) of rank \(r'\) for \((t',\mathrm{HT})\). In the next problems take a lattice \(L\) of \(R\) over a coefficient ring large enough for clause (e) of Definition 80.1.7. Take \(w_0\in\mathcal M\) with \(w_0\nmid\ell\), and take \(\Sigma_0\) to be the places dividing \(\ell MN\), with \(g_0=s_{w_0}\) the tame generator at the fixed place above \(w_0\). Use the three terms of Definition 87.3.1 for \(L\) with \(\Sigma(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\) the places dividing \(\ell M\). As in Chapter 80, \(n_\ell=\operatorname{val}_\ell(\mathcal N)\).

Tag 23C7

Problem 90.2.2. If \(g\) acts by \(\alpha\) on \(L\), show that \((g^c)^{-1}\) acts by \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{\max(t'-1,0)}\alpha\). For such a \(g\) with \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)=p_i^{r'}\) for some \(i\in\{0,1\}\), show that the scalar difference in Problem 90.2.1 is \(\alpha(1-p_i^{r'(\max(t'-1,0)+1)})\). Prove that \(\mathrm{glob}/e_{\mathcal O}\le r'n_\ell\).

Tag 23C8

Problem 90.2.3. At most \(4f\) places divide \(M\), and \(\mathrm{plc}/e_{\mathcal O}\le 4fr'n_\ell\).

Tag 23C9

Problem 90.2.4. Show that the normalized determinant valuations at \(g_0\) and \((g_0^c)^{-1}\) in the definition of \(\mathrm{inr}\) are each at most \(r'n_\ell\). Deduce \(\mathrm{inr}/e_{\mathcal O}\le 2r'n_\ell\) and \((\mathrm{glob}+\mathrm{plc}+\mathrm{inr})/e_{\mathcal O}\le(4f+3)r'n_\ell\).

§90.3. The local conditions and the lattice Tag 23CA

The remaining conditions for a Selmer datum are a scalar inertia action and a bound on the ranks of the crystalline cocycles.

Tag 23CB

Problem 90.3.1. Show that some inertia element at a place dividing \(M\) prime to \(\ell\) acts on \(L\) by a scalar \(\lambda\) with \(\lambda^2-1\in\mathcal O^\times\), and that its conjugate inverse acts by the same scalar. To test the square-unit condition at \(\ell=3\), show that no unit whose residue belongs to \(\mathbb F_3^\times\) satisfies \(\lambda^2-1\in\mathcal O^\times\), and that \(\mathrm i\) satisfies the condition in \(\mathbb Z_3[\mathrm i]\).

For a lattice in \(R\), the coefficient-ring form of the crystalline rank formula (Theorem 34.1.1) uses the labeled jumps of clause (b). At \(w\mid\ell\), the finitely generated \(\mathcal O\)-module \(\mathcal Z_w=Z_f(L|_{G_w})\) has rank \[r'+\sum_{\sigma\mapsto w}\#\{u\in\mathrm{HT}_\sigma:u<0\},\] counting multiplicities. Here \(\sigma:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) runs over the embeddings inducing \(w\) through \(\iota\), and \(G_w\) is the fixed decomposition group at \(w\). Status: the labeled formula is read from the proof; not proved here.

Tag 23CC

Problem 90.3.2. Show that \(\sum_{w\mid\ell}(\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal O}\mathcal Z_w-r')\le fr'\). With \(\mathcal Z_w\) as the modules of local cocycles, check the conditions of Definition 87.3.1 for \(L\).

A continuous \(\ell\)-adic representation of \(G_E\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) has a lattice over every sufficiently large coefficient ring, meaning every coefficient ring containing one fixed ring. Status: read from the proof; not proved here.

Problems 90.2.2–90.3.2, with existence of lattices, prove the following.

Tag 23CD

Theorem 90.3.1. Let \(R\) be main-summand-like of rank \(r'\) and \(w_0\in\mathcal M\) be prime to \(\ell\). For all sufficiently large \(\mathcal O\), some lattice of \(R\) over \(\mathcal O\) carries a Selmer datum \(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}\) for \((\Sigma_0,s_{w_0})\), where \(\Sigma_0\) consists of the places dividing \(\ell MN\). The set \(\Sigma(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\) consists of the places dividing \(\ell M\), the local relations are those of the crystalline cocycles, and \(\mathrm{bd}(T_{\mathrm{Sel}})\le (4f+3)r'n_\ell\).

Used in: Problem 90.4.1; Chapter 85: Problem 85.1.6

§90.4. One character for all bounds Tag 23CE

To apply the small-twists theorem at a common torsion level, choose \(w_0\) above one of \(p_0,p_1\) different from \(\ell\). The set of chosen places above each \(p_i\) is nonempty and their residue-field sizes are \(p_i\) (Section 37.1). Use the same \(\Sigma_0\) and \(g_0\) for the representations and the systems of Theorem 89.3.1. There are finitely many critical pairs (see the hint for Problem 90.4.2).

Tag 23CF

Problem 90.4.1. Given a finite family of main-summand-like representations, show that there is one coefficient ring over which each representation has a lattice with a datum as in Theorem 90.3.1, and that \(c_{\mathrm{tor}}=(4f+3)rn_\ell+2\) is a torsion level for the family of data. In the numerical model \(\ell=3\), \(r=e_{\mathcal O}=1\) and \(c_{\mathrm{tor}}=2\), the residue-field inequality asks for more than \(5\) elements.

Used in: Problem 90.4.2; Chapter 85: Problem 85.1.6

Two facts connect the character and length bounds of Theorem 85.1.1 to the twists used in Theorem 85.1.2.

  • The return from Galois characters to tame characters. For \(Q\) admissible for \(\Sigma_0\), every character for \(Q\) is the inverse avatar of a weight-zero tame character of modulus \(Q\). Status: read from the proof; not proved here.

  • Transport of crystalline relations. Let \(\mathcal O'\supseteq\mathcal O\) be coefficient rings and let a character over \(\mathcal O'\) be trivial on the fixed decomposition groups above \(\ell\). Every crystalline relation on an \(\mathcal O\)-lattice, with coefficients mapped to \(\mathcal O'\), holds on the integral crystalline cocycles of the extended, twisted lattice. Status: read from the proof; not proved here.

The last two problems compare the two Selmer modules and account for the loss in passing from a system to a ratio. Write \(\mathrm{tw}_\ell=(4f+3)rn_\ell+2^{-\ell}\) as in Definition 80.1.1.

Tag 23CG

Problem 90.4.2. Let \(Q\) and \(\chi\) be given by Theorem 85.1.1 for \(\varepsilon=2^{-\ell}\), the data of Problem 90.4.1 and the systems for all ramified critical pairs and signs. Show that \(\chi\) is the inverse avatar of a tame character \(\vartheta\) of modulus \(Q\), and that for \(1\le j\le m\), \(\vartheta^j\) ramifies at every \(w\in Q\) and has inverse avatar \(\chi^j\). For each datum, each such \(j\) and each sufficiently large coefficient ring \(\mathcal O'\), take \(L_{\mathrm{tw}}\) to be the datum’s lattice extended to \(\mathcal O'\) and twisted by \(\chi^j\). Show that \(\mathrm{Sel}(L_{\mathrm{tw}},\gamma(T_{\mathrm{Sel}}))\) of Definition 80.1.9 is a submodule of the module of Definition 87.3.2 for the same datum and twist. Deduce clause 2 of Theorem 85.1.2.

Used in: Problem 90.4.3; Chapter 85
Tag 23CH

Problem 90.4.3. Prove \(4\operatorname{val}_\ell(\#k_{w_0}-1)\le(4f+3)rn_\ell\). Show that \(\vartheta\) from Problem 90.4.2 is an \(m\)-fold twist as in Section 37.6 and that each power \(\vartheta^j\) for \(1\le j\le m\) is auxiliary with ramification at most \(2^{-\ell}\). Show that each ratio valuation in clause 3 of Theorem 85.1.2 is at most \(4\operatorname{val}_\ell(\#k_{w_0}-1)+2^{-\ell}\). Deduce clauses 1 and 3 of that theorem.

Used in: Chapter 85

Taken as given.

We used the tame presentation, elementary divisors, the labeled crystalline rank formula, existence of lattices, the return from Galois characters to tame characters and transport of crystalline relations. The small-twists theorem and the systems of the ratios supply the two inputs to Problems 90.4.2 and 90.4.3, which finish the deduction of Theorem 85.1.2.
