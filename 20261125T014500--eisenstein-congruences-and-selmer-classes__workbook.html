Prerequisites.

Modular forms of level \(1\), Eisenstein series, \(\Delta\) and the Hecke operators [SeA, Ch. VII]; Serre writes the weight as \(2k\) and numbers the Bernoulli numbers differently. Complete discrete valuation rings and Hensel’s lemma. The Chebotarev density theorem. From Chapter 26, Denominators and the Stickelberger element: the eigenspaces \(A^{(i)}\) of Section 26.2 and Theorem 26.2.2. From Chapter 27, Odd Tate twists, with \(\ell = p\): the groups \(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\), \(G_p\), \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e)\), Problems 27.1.4 and 27.2.1, Theorem 27.1.1, and the Euler characteristic formula stated before Problem 27.1.7. From Chapter 29, Congruence modules: Problem 29.2.2. Galois representations attached to modular forms are stated in Section 30.3 with references.

Chapter 27 computed the length of \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e)\) for positive odd \(e\) from the theorem of Mazur and Wiles, which was used as a black box. How does one produce classes in \(\mathrm{Sha}\), and how does one bound them? Ribet’s answer to the first question [Ri] is that a cusp form congruent to an Eisenstein series gives a Galois representation that is reducible modulo \(p\), and a good choice of lattice turns it into an unramified class. The subgroup of \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q},k-1)\) killed by \(p\) has a deformation-theoretic interpretation as a space of tangent vectors at the reducible Eisenstein representation. We work with level \(1\), and the example to keep in mind is \(k = 12\), \(p = 691\). Throughout, \(p\) is an odd prime, \(\chi\) is the \(p\)-adic cyclotomic character and \(\omega\) is its reduction modulo \(p\).

§30.1. The congruence Tag 22UQ

For even \(k \ge 4\) let \(\sigma_{k-1}(n) := \sum_{d \mid n} d^{k-1}\), and normalize the Eisenstein series as \[E_k := 1 - \frac{2k}{B_k} \sum_{n \ge 1} \sigma_{k-1}(n)\, q^n ,\] so that \(E_k\) has rational coefficients and constant term \(1\) [SeA, Ch. VII, §4]. Let \(\Delta = q\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{24} = \sum \tau(n) q^n\). Since \(B_{12} = -691/2730\), the least common denominator of the coefficients of \(E_{12}\) is \(691\). By Problem 29.2.2 of Chapter 29, \(691\,(E_4^3 - E_{12}) = 432000\,\Delta\).

Used in: Problem 30.1.3
Tag 22UT

Problem 30.1.3. With \(g\) as in Problem 30.1.2, for every prime \(\ell\) and every \(n \ge 1\), \[a_n(T_\ell\, g) \equiv (1 + \ell^{k-1})\, a_n(g) \pmod p .\]

The cusp forms with coefficients in \(\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\) form a lattice stable under the Hecke operators. By the lifting lemma of Deligne and Serre [DeS, Lemme 6.11], Problem 30.1.3 gives a normalized eigenform \(f\) of weight \(k\) and level \(1\), and a prime \(\lambda \mid p\) of the field generated by its coefficients, such that \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:congruence}\tag{1} a_\ell(f) \equiv 1 + \ell^{k-1} \pmod \lambda \qquad \text{for every prime } \ell . \end{equation}\] In particular \(a_p(f) \equiv 1\): \(f\) is ordinary at \(\lambda\) (its \(p\)-th Fourier coefficient is a \(\lambda\)-adic unit). For \(k = 12\) we may take \(f = \Delta\), and \(\tau(691) \equiv 1 \pmod{691}\).

§30.2. Lattices Tag 22UU

A congruence between \(f\) and an Eisenstein series says that a Galois representation is reducible modulo \(\lambda\). Which reduction one sees depends on a lattice. Let \(K\) be a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_p\) with ring of integers \(\mathcal{O}\), uniformizer \(\pi\), valuation \(v\) with \(v(\pi) = 1\), and residue field \(\mathbb{F}\). Let \(G\) be a profinite group and \(\rho \colon G \to \mathrm{GL}(V)\) a continuous irreducible representation on a \(2\)-dimensional \(K\)-space. A lattice is a free \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule of rank \(2\) that spans \(V\).

§30.3. The unramified class Tag 22V2

We state three inputs. Let \(f\) be a normalized cuspidal Hecke eigenform of weight \(k\ge2\) and level \(1\), let \(\lambda \mid p\) be a prime of the field of its coefficients, and let \(K\) be the completion at \(\lambda\).

  • There is a continuous \(\rho_f \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(K)\) with \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_f(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell) = a_\ell(f)\) and \(\det \rho_f = \chi^{k-1}\), for every prime \(\ell \ne p\), where \(\mathrm{Frob}_\ell\) denotes arithmetic Frobenius [De].

  • \(\rho_f\) is irreducible [Ri2].

  • If \(a_p(f)\) is a \(\lambda\)-adic unit, then \(\rho_f|_{G_p}\) stabilizes a line on which the inertia group \(I_p\) acts by \(\chi^{k-1}\), and \(G_p\) acts on the quotient by an unramified character [Wi, Thm. 2.1.4].

Chapter 32, Black box: Galois representations of eigenforms, states the analogue of (D) as a black box, over CM fields; (I) and (O) are not part of it. Its Section 32.3 shows in the simplest case, the eigenform \(f\) of weight \(2\) and level \(11\), where \(\rho_f\) comes from: it is the rational \(p\)-adic Tate module of \(J_0(11)\), and the Eichler–Shimura relation gives the analogue of (D).

From now on \(k\) is even with \(4 \le k \le p - 3\), \(p\) divides \(B_k\) (equivalently, since \(k < p\), the numerator of \(B_k/k\)), and \(f\), \(\lambda\) are as in (1).

Tag 22V6

Theorem 30.3.1 (Ribet). Let \(k\) be even with \(4 \le k \le p-3\), and suppose that \(p \mid B_k\). Then \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, k-1) \ne 0\), and \(A^{(p-k)} \ne 0\).

This is the converse to Herbrand’s theorem [Ri]; see also [Wa, Thm. 6.18]. For \(k = 12\) and \(p = 691\) the input is \(\Delta\). There is an unramified cyclic extension of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{691})\) of degree \(691\), Galois over \(\mathbb{Q}\), with conjugation action on its relative Galois group through \(\omega^{-11} = \omega^{679}\). This proves nonvanishing of \(\mathrm{Sha}_{691}(\mathbb{Q},11)\). Congruences with Eisenstein series are measured by the Eisenstein ideal of the Hecke algebra, which Mazur introduced in weight \(2\) and prime level [Ma]. In this range Theorem 27.1.2, a consequence of the main conjecture of Iwasawa theory, proved by Mazur and Wiles [MW], gives \(\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q},k-1)=v_p(B_k/k)\).

§30.4. Tangent vectors at the Eisenstein point Tag 22V7

Ribet goes from a modular form to a class. Here a congruence produces a nonzero class. How does a class in \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q},k-1)[p]\) give a first-order deformation? Let \(\rho_0 := 1 \oplus \omega^{k-1} \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\), the reduction of the representation \(1 \oplus \chi^{k-1}\) of the Eisenstein series \(E_k\); we call it the Eisenstein point. Let \(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon]\) be the ring with \(\varepsilon^2 = 0\). Call a continuous homomorphism \(\rho_\varepsilon \colon G_{\mathbb{Q},p}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon])\) a lift of \(\rho_0\) if it reduces to \(\rho_0\) modulo \(\varepsilon\). Let \(\operatorname{ad}\rho_0\) be the space \(M_2(\mathbb{F}_p)\) with \(\sigma\) acting by conjugation by \(\rho_0(\sigma)\). See [Mz] for the general theory.

Tag 22V8

Problem 30.4.1. Writing \(\rho_\varepsilon = (1 + \varepsilon M)\rho_0\) identifies the lifts of \(\rho_0\), up to conjugation by matrices \(1 + \varepsilon X\), with \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \operatorname{ad}\rho_0)\).

The torus \(T = \{\mathrm{diag}(1, t)\}\) commutes with \(\rho_0\), and acts on lifts by conjugation.

Tag 22V9

Problem 30.4.2. \(\operatorname{ad}\rho_0 = \mathbb{F}_p E_{11} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p E_{22} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{1-k}) E_{12} \oplus \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{k-1}) E_{21}\), where \(E_{ij}\) are the matrix units, and \(T\) acts on these four summands, and on their \(H^1\), with weights \(0\), \(0\), \(-1\), \(+1\).

A cocycle \(\kappa\) with nonzero class in \(H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \mathbb{F}_p(\omega^{1-k}))\) gives the nonsplit \(\rho_\kappa = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \kappa\omega^{k-1} \\ 0 & \omega^{k-1} \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). For \(t \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times\), conjugating by \(\mathrm{diag}(t,1)\) replaces \(\kappa\) by \(t\kappa\), so all the \(\rho_{t\kappa}\) are isomorphic; at \(t = 0\) one reaches \(\rho_0\), and to first order at \(t = 0\) the family is the lift \((1 + \varepsilon\kappa E_{12})\rho_0\), of weight \(-1\). This is the sense in which a class is a tangent vector at the Eisenstein point.

Which lifts are allowed? By (O), \(\rho_f\) has a line on which \(I_p\) acts by \(\chi^{k-1}\) with unramified quotient. Call a lift \(\rho_\varepsilon\) ordinary if \(\mathbb{F}_p[\varepsilon]^2\) has a \(G_p\)-stable direct summand \(L\) of rank \(1\), reducing to \(\mathbb{F}_p e_2\), on which \(I_p\) acts by \(\omega^{k-1}\), with \(I_p\) acting trivially on the quotient. Let \(t_{\mathrm{ord}} \subseteq H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q},p}, \operatorname{ad}\rho_0)\) be the set of classes of ordinary lifts, and \(t_{\mathrm{ord}}^{(j)}\) its part of weight \(j\).

§30.5. From the CM field to characters Tag 22VH

The two results above use Galois representations over the CM field Data.E of Chapter 36, The data. This field is abelian over \(\mathbb{Q}\) by the cyclotomic embedding Parameters.toCycloField [GL]. In this section \(E\) denotes an arbitrary abelian number field. We consider the strict Selmer group over \(E\) and its character components. Problem 27.2.1 treats the cyclotomic case.

Let \(E\) be an abelian number field with \(\Gamma := \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})\) of order prime to \(p\). Let \(\mathcal{O}:= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mu_{\lvert\Gamma\rvert}]\), let \(W := W_e \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\) with \(W_e = \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p(-e)\) as in Chapter 27, and let \(G_E \subseteq G_\mathbb{Q}\) be the absolute Galois groups. Let \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}\) be the group of classes in \(H^1(G_E, W)\) that vanish on the inertia group \(I_w\) of every place \(w \nmid p\) and on the decomposition group \(D_w\) of every \(w \mid p\). Since \(\mathcal{O}\) is free of finite rank over \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), continuous cohomology and the restriction maps commute with \(\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\), so \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}= \mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathcal{O}\), with \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)\) as in Chapter 27. For a character \(\psi \colon \Gamma \to \mathcal{O}^\times\), let \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e; \psi)_\mathcal{O}\) be defined in the same way from \(H^1(G_\mathbb{Q}, W \otimes \psi^{-1})\). Let \(\Gamma\) act on \(\mathcal{O}[\Gamma]\) by left multiplication, so that \(G_\mathbb{Q}\) acts on it through \(\Gamma\), and let \(\gamma \in \Gamma\) act on \(H^1(G_E, W)\) by \((\gamma c)(h) := \tilde\gamma\, c(\tilde\gamma^{-1} h \tilde\gamma)\), for any lift \(\tilde\gamma \in G_\mathbb{Q}\) of \(\gamma\).

Tag 22VL

Problem 30.5.3. \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}\cong \bigoplus_\psi \mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e; \psi)_\mathcal{O}\). The summand \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(\mathbb{Q}, e; \psi)_\mathcal{O}\), which comes from the summand \(W \otimes \psi^{-1}\) of Problem 30.5.1, is the part on which \(\Gamma\) acts through \(\psi\).

Every class killed by \(p\) in the \(\psi\)-part of \(\mathrm{Sha}_p(E, e)_\mathcal{O}\) lifts to \(H^1(G_\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{F}(\omega^{-e}\bar\psi^{-1}))\), with \(\mathbb{F}\) the residue field of \(\mathcal{O}\). As in §30.4, a cocycle representing this lift gives an upper-triangular deformation of \(1\oplus\omega^e\bar\psi\) over \(\mathbb{F}[\varepsilon]\), with \(\varepsilon^2=0\). The deformation allows ramification at \(p\) and at primes dividing the conductor of \(\psi\). When the global and local residual invariants vanish, the coefficient maps are injective there and the strict local condition passes to the lift, as in Problem 27.1.4. For \(E=\mathbb{Q}\) and positive odd \(e\), Theorem 27.1.2 gives the length \(v_p(\operatorname{num}(B_{e+1}/(e+1)))\). For \(E\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\), write the reduction of \(\psi\) as \(\omega^j\) after inflation to \(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q})\). If \(i\equiv-e-j\not\equiv0\pmod{p-1}\), Problem 27.2.2 identifies the subgroup killed by \(p\) in this summand with \(\operatorname{Hom}(A^{(i)},\mathbb{F}_p)\). For odd \(3\le i\le p-2\), Theorem 26.2.2 gives the order of \(A^{(i)}\) and its criterion for nonvanishing. The case \(i=1\) is discussed after Problem 26.2.5.
