This sheet illustrates the denominator in a model with two quaternionic ideal classes. In Chapter 29 the exponent of a congruence module gave the scalar denominator of a projection onto an eigenspace, and in Chapter 30 a congruence produced a Galois cohomology class. Section 81.1 measures a denominator by a clearing operator and shows why false lifts must be excluded; Section 81.2 computes the denominator for a class set with two elements, where it is \(5\); Section 81.3 explains the congruence behind the denominator and the costs on the Galois side that enter the clearing bound. §81.1. Clearing a projection

Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\) and valuation \(\operatorname{ord}\), normalized by \(\operatorname{ord}(\varpi) = 1\) for a uniformizer \(\varpi\); we use \(\operatorname{ord}(0) = +\infty\). Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional \(K\)-vector space with a lattice \(L\), and let \(V = \mathcal F \oplus \mathcal F'\) with projection \(\mathrm{pr}\colon V \to \mathcal F\) along \(\mathcal F'\). Write \(\mathrm{C}(L; \mathcal F) := \mathrm{pr}(L)/(L \cap \mathcal F)\), as in Chapter 29. An operator \(C_\ell \in \mathrm{End}_K(\mathcal F)\) (the subscript recalls the prime \(\ell\) of the later sections) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on a subset \(Y \subseteq L\) if \(C_\ell(\mathrm{pr}(y)) \in L\) for every \(y \in Y\). A scalar \(c\) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(L\) when \(c\,\mathrm{pr}(L) \subseteq L\); an operator may clear different directions of \(\mathcal F\) by different amounts, and we measure it by \(\operatorname{ord}(\det C_\ell)\).

Problem 81.1.1. \(C_\ell\) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(L\) if and only if \(C_\ell(\mathrm{pr}(L)) \subseteq L \cap \mathcal F\). The least value of \(\operatorname{ord}(\det C_\ell)\) over such \(C_\ell\) is \(\operatorname{len}_{\mathcal{O}} \mathrm{C}(L; \mathcal F)\).

An integral operator that is a nonzero scalar \(a\) on \(\mathcal F\) and \(0\) on \(\mathcal F'\) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(L\) as the scalar \(a\), so \(\operatorname{len}_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{C}(L; \mathcal F) \le \dim \mathcal F \cdot \operatorname{ord}(a)\); see Zhang [Zh, Lemma 5.1]. In part (b) of the next problem, an eigenvalue difference gives the exact denominator.

Problem 81.1.2. (a) The orthogonal projection of \(\mathbb{Q}^2\) onto the line through \((1,2)\) is \(\frac15\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), and, for \(c \in \mathbb{Q}\), \(c\) times it preserves \(\mathbb{Z}^2\) exactly when \(c \in 5\mathbb{Z}\). (b) Let \(s, c \in \mathbb{Z}\) with \(s \ge 1\), and \(T = \left(\begin{smallmatrix}\ell^s&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\). The projection onto the \(\ell^s\)-eigenline along \(\ker T\) is \(\ell^{-s}T\), and \(\ell^{c-s}T\) preserves \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2\) exactly when \(c \ge s = \operatorname{val}_\ell(\ell^s - 0)\).

The operators that clear \(\mathrm{pr}\) on a given \(Y\) form an \(\mathcal{O}\)-module; the definitions make sense for any valuation ring \(\mathcal{O}\) and any \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule \(L\) of \(V\). The next problem combines clearing operators that are diagonal in one basis.

Problem 81.1.3. Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be a valuation ring with infinite residue field and valuation \(\operatorname{ord}\), such as the ring of integers of \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell\) with \(\operatorname{ord}(\ell) = 1\), and let \(b_1, \dots, b_n\) be a basis of \(\mathcal F\). Let \(C_\ell^{(1)}, \dots, C_\ell^{(n)}\) clear \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(Y\), with \(C_\ell^{(j)} b_i = \lambda_{ji} b_i\). Then some \(C_\ell = \sum_j u_j C_\ell^{(j)}\) with \(u_j \in \mathcal{O}\) has \(C_\ell b_i = \mu_i b_i\) with \(\operatorname{ord}(\mu_i) \le \operatorname{ord}(\lambda_{ii})\) for every \(i\); it clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(Y\).

Now let \(K\) be a subfield of \(\mathbb{C}\) stable under complex conjugation, and \(\mathcal{O}\) a discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\). Let \(V\) carry a positive definite hermitian form \(\langle\,,\rangle\), linear in the first variable, let \(\mathcal F' = \mathcal F^\perp\), and let \(T\) be a self-adjoint operator on \(V\) with \(T(L) \subseteq L\), diagonalizable with eigenvalues in \(\mathcal{O}\), such that \(\mathcal F \subseteq \ker(T - \lambda)\). Call the vectors of \(\mathcal F^\perp\) with the eigenvalue of \(\mathcal F\), \[F_T := \mathcal F^\perp \cap \ker(T - \lambda) ,\] the false lifts for \(T\). Let \(\Pi(X) := \prod_\mu (X - \mu)\), the product over the eigenvalues \(\mu \ne \lambda\) of \(T\), each taken once.

Problem 81.1.4. For \(y \in L\) orthogonal to \(F_T\), \(\Pi(T)\,y = \Pi(\lambda)\,\mathrm{pr}(y)\). Hence the scalar \(\Pi(\lambda)\) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on the \(y \in L\) orthogonal to \(F_T\), and the determinant of the scalar \(\Pi(\lambda)\) acting on \(\mathcal F\) has valuation \(\dim \mathcal F \cdot \operatorname{ord}(\Pi(\lambda))\).

Used in: Problem 81.2.2

The cost \(\operatorname{ord}(\Pi(\lambda)) = \sum_\mu \operatorname{ord}(\lambda - \mu)\) counts congruences between the eigenvalue of \(\mathcal F\) and the other eigenvalues. The restriction to vectors orthogonal to the false lifts cannot be dropped.

Problem 81.1.5. Let \(V = K^2\) with \(\langle x, y \rangle = x_1\bar y_1 + x_2\bar y_2\), \(L = \mathcal{O}^2\), \(T = \lambda\cdot 1\), \(\mathcal F = K(1,1)\), and suppose \(\operatorname{ord}(2) > 0\). Then \(F_T = K(1,-1)\), no unit \(c \in \mathcal{O}^\times\) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(L\), and \(c = 1\) clears it on the \(y \in L\) orthogonal to \(F_T\).

§81.2. Two classes

We take the class set of Section 24.4 of Chapter 24: the two left ideal classes of a maximal order in the quaternion algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}\) ramified at \(11\) and \(\infty\), indexed by their right orders, with \(w_1 = 2\) and \(w_2 = 3\). Functions on the two classes are vectors \(\varphi = (\varphi(1), \varphi(2))\), with \[\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle := \frac{\varphi(1)\overline{\varphi'(1)}}{w_1} + \frac{\varphi(2)\overline{\varphi'(2)}}{w_2} ,\] and the Brandt matrix \(B(\ell)\), for a prime \(\ell \ne 11\), acts by \((B(\ell)\varphi)(i) = \sum_j B_{ij}(\ell)\varphi(j)\). Recall from that section that \(B(\ell)\) has integer entries, that \(w_jB_{ij}(\ell) = w_iB_{ji}(\ell)\) and \(\sum_j B_{ij}(\ell) = \ell + 1\), and that \(B(\ell)\) is the action of \(T_\ell\) on \(M_2(\Gamma_0(11))\), which is spanned by an Eisenstein series of \(T_\ell\)-eigenvalue \(\ell + 1\) and by the form called \(f\) there; we write \[f_{11} := q\prod_{n \ge 1}(1-q^n)^2(1-q^{11n})^2 = \sum_{n \ge 1} a_n q^n .\] By Problem 24.4.2, \(B(2) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\) and \(B(3) = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 2 \\ 3 & 1 \end{pmatrix}\). The constant function \(1\) plays the role of the lifts: \(\mathcal F\) is the line of constant functions, and \(\mathrm{pr}\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal F\). The integral functions are \(L := \mathbb{Z}^2\). To apply Section 81.1 over \(\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\), use the lattice \(\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}^2\) in \(\mathbb{Q}^2\). Over \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\) use the lattice \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2\) and the same bilinear formula without the bar.

Problem 81.2.1. \(B(\ell)\) is self-adjoint for \(\langle\,,\rangle\), \(B(\ell)1 = (\ell + 1)1\), and \(\varphi_{11} := (2, -3)\) spans \(\mathcal F^\perp\) and satisfies \(B(\ell)\varphi_{11} = a_\ell\varphi_{11}\).

Used in: Chapter 84: Problem 84.4.4

Problem 81.2.2. \(\mathrm{pr}(\varphi) = \frac15\bigl(3\varphi(1) + 2\varphi(2)\bigr)\cdot 1\), and \(5\,\mathrm{pr}= B(2) + 2 = B(3) + 1\). Hence \(\mathrm{pr}(L) = \frac15\mathbb{Z}\cdot 1\) and \(\mathrm{C}(L; \mathcal F) \cong \mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}\). The least value of \(\operatorname{val}_5(\det C_\ell)\) for \(C_\ell\) clearing \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(\mathbb{Z}_5^2\) is \(1\); for \(\ell \ne 5\) the identity clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2\). The scalar \(\Pi(\lambda)\) of Problem 81.1.4 is \(5\) both for \(T = B(2)\) and for \(T = B(3)\).

Used in: Chapter 84

What congruence do the Brandt eigenvalues satisfy?

Problem 81.2.3. \(B(\ell) - a_\ell = (\ell + 1 - a_\ell)\mathrm{pr}\). Hence \(a_\ell \equiv 1 + \ell \pmod 5\) for every prime \(\ell \ne 11\); for example \(a_{13} = 4\) (Problem 32.3.1).

The denominator \(5\) is the numerator of the mass \(1/w_1 + 1/w_2 = (11 - 1)/12 = 5/6\). The next problem gives the denominator at primes dividing none of the weights.

Problem 81.2.4. Let \(I\) be a nonempty finite set with positive integers \(w_i\), let \(\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle := \sum_i \varphi(i)\varphi'(i)/w_i\) on \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell^I\), let \(\mathcal F\) be the line of constant functions, and let \(\ell\) be a prime dividing no \(w_i\). Then \(\mathfrak m := \sum_i 1/w_i\) has \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(\mathfrak m) \ge 0\), and \(\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{Z}_\ell^I; \mathcal F) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\operatorname{val}_\ell(\mathfrak m)}\mathbb{Z}\).

Used in: Problem 81.2.5

For the quaternion algebra ramified at a prime \(p\) and \(\infty\) the mass is \((p-1)/12\), and Mazur showed that the index of his Eisenstein ideal is the numerator of \((p-1)/12\) [Ma]. There is also an integrality question for the pairing of a cleared form with an integral form.

Problem 81.2.5. In the setting of Problem 81.2.4, drop the condition on \(\ell\), and let \(n_0\) be a common multiple of the \(w_i\). If \(C_\ell\) clears \(\mathrm{pr}\) on \(Y \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_\ell^I\), then so does \(n_0C_\ell\), and \(\langle n_0C_\ell\mathrm{pr}(y), \varphi' \rangle \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell\) for \(y \in Y\) and \(\varphi' \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^I\). In the class set with two elements, \(6\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \in \mathbb{Z}\) for \(\varphi, \varphi' \in \mathbb{Z}^2\).

§81.3. Lifts and the classes they produce

Where does the congruence of Problem 81.2.3 come from? The constant function carries the Galois representation \(1 \oplus \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\), where \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is the \(5\)-adic cyclotomic character; its trace at an arithmetic Frobenius \(\mathrm{Fr}_\ell\) is \(1 + \ell\) for \(\ell \ne 5\). The form \(f_{11}\) carries the representation \(\rho_{11}\) of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\) on the rational \(5\)-adic Tate module \(T_5(X_0(11)) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_5\), whose trace at \(\mathrm{Fr}_\ell\) is \(a_\ell\) for \(\ell \nmid 55\) (Section 32.3 of Chapter 32). Let \(\omega\) be the reduction of \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) modulo \(5\).

Problem 81.3.1. The semisimplification of the reduction of \(\rho_{11}\) modulo \(5\) is \(1 \oplus \omega\), and \(\omega(c) = -1\) for every complex conjugation \(c\).

The representation \(\rho_{11}\) is irreducible: it is semisimple and its commutant is \(\mathbb{Q}_5\), by Faltings’ theorem [Fa], since \(X_0(11)\) has no complex multiplication (its \(j\)-invariant \(-122023936/161051\) is not an integer). Ribet’s lemma, Theorem 30.2.1, then gives a nonzero class with values in \(\mathbb{F}_5(\omega)\) or \(\mathbb{F}_5(\omega^{-1})\), an odd character.

In Chapter 80, \(E\) is a CM field with maximal real subfield \(E^+\). Suppose \(R\) is a main summand for a descent pair and an eigenvalue system in the sense of Definition 80.1.8. For every compatible pair twisted by a tame character \(\xi\), every eigenform lifting that system, and every representation attached to its Hecke polynomials, the trace of the normalized representation at \(g\in\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\) is \(\hat\xi(g)\operatorname{tr}R(g)+\sum_{0\le e<t'}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{-e}\). Here \(\hat\xi\) is the avatar of \(\xi^{-1}\), \(t'\) is the difference between the target and source ranks, and \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character. For an extension of \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e'}\) by \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) modulo \(\ell^\nu\), the extension cocycle takes values in \((\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu)(-a)\), where \(a = e - e'\). On cocycles \(z\) of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\) a complex conjugation \(c\) acts by \(z^c(g) := z(cgc)\); call \(z\) odd if \(z^c = -z\) and even if \(z^c = z\).

Problem 81.3.2. Let \(\ell\) be odd and \(\mathcal{O}\) the ring of integers of a finite extension of \(\mathbb{Q}_\ell\). Every cocycle of \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E^+)\) with values in \((\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu)(-a)\) differs by a coboundary from one with \(z(c) = 0\). For such \(z\), the restriction to \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\) satisfies \(z^c = (-1)^a z\).

For odd \(\ell\) and odd \(a\), restriction from \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E^+)\) to \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\) therefore gives classes with odd representatives. Use the rank parameter \(r\) and the moduli \(q_1,d\) of Chapter 80, and write \(t = 5r\). The main-summand hypothesis gives \(t' \le t\). Since \(E \subseteq \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q_1d})\), Theorem 27.3.2 applies with \(N = q_1d\). It gives one positive integer \(X_{\mathrm{odd}}\) for all primes \(\ell\), all coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\) specified in that theorem, all \(0\le a<t\) and all \(\nu\ge0\). For \(a>0\), \(\operatorname{val}_{\mathcal{O}}(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\) bounds the \(\mathcal{O}\)-length of the module of odd cocycles with values in \((\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu)(-a)\) and the stated local conditions. For \(a=0\) it bounds the \(\mathcal{O}\)-length of the module of all continuous everywhere-unramified homomorphisms from \(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\) to \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\). The proof for positive \(a\) uses Gauss sums as in Chapter 28. For \(0<a<t'\) the proof controls the even cocycles by tests at auxiliary primes (Chapter 84). The cost of these tests enters the clearing bound of Chapter 80, which contains no separate term for the length of a module of even cocycles. The next problem uses Frobenius to test local vanishing of a Kummer class at primes where it is unramified.

Problem 81.3.3. Let \(\ell\) be an odd prime, \(u \in \mathbb{Q}^\times\) not an \(\ell\)-th power, and \(\mathrm{kum}_u\) the Kummer class of \(u\) in \(H^1(\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}), \mu_\ell)\). For a prime \(q \equiv 1 \pmod \ell\) with \(\operatorname{val}_q(u) = 0\), \(\mathrm{kum}_u\) is unramified at \(q\), and \(\mathrm{kum}_u(\mathrm{Fr}_q) = 0\), a condition independent of the choice of \(\mathrm{Fr}_q\), if and only if \(u^{(q-1)/\ell} \equiv 1 \pmod q\). There are infinitely many such \(q\) with \(\mathrm{kum}_u(\mathrm{Fr}_q) \ne 0\). For \(\ell = 3\) and \(u = 2\), \(q = 7\) detects \(\mathrm{kum}_2\) and \(q = 31\) does not.

Used in: Chapter 84

Ribet’s lemma needs an element on which the two characters differ (Problem 30.2.2). For the characters \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\), \(0 \le e < t\), with \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character, the cost of an element \(g\) is the valuations of the differences \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{a} - 1\), \(0 < a < t\). If \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)\) reduces to a generator of \(\mathbb{F}_\ell^\times\) and \(t \le \ell - 1\), these valuations are \(0\), by Problem 81.3.4. If \(g\) is an arithmetic Frobenius element at a chosen place \(w\) not dividing \(\ell\) (Section 37.1), then \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g) = \#k_w\) and these valuations are at most \(\operatorname{val}_\ell(\mathcal N)\), by Problem 81.3.5 with \(n = rt\): the integer \(\mathcal N\) of that section is a multiple of \(\#\mathrm{GL}_{rt}(k_w)\).

Problem 81.3.4. Let \(g \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\) reduce to a generator of \(\mathbb{F}_\ell^\times\). Then \(g^a - 1 \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times\) for \(0 < a < \ell - 1\).

Used in: Chapter 83

Problem 81.3.5. Let \(q\) be a prime power and \(1 \le a \le n\). Then \(q^a - 1\) divides \(\#\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q) = q^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^n (q^i - 1)\).

Used in: Chapter 83
