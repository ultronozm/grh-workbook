§46.1. One step on \(\mathbb{Z}/25\)

For \(\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathbb{Z}/q\to\mathbb{C}\) put \(\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(l):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/q}\mathfrak{f}(j)\,\mathrm{e}(-lj/q)\) (counting measure; Chapter 45 uses the mean). For a subgroup \(H\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/q\) let \(H^\perp:=\{l:\mathrm{e}(lh/q)=1\text{ for all }h\in H\}\).

Problem 46.1.1. \(\sum_{h\in H}\mathfrak{f}(h)=\frac{\#H}{q}\sum_{l\in H^\perp}\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(l)\). For \(H=5\mathbb{Z}/25\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/25\) one has \(H^\perp=5\mathbb{Z}/25\) and \(\#H/q=1/5\).

Used in: Problem 46.1.5

In this sheet \(w(j):=1+\cos(2\pi j/25)\) on \(\mathbb{Z}/25\), and \(H:=5\mathbb{Z}/25\).

Problem 46.1.2. \(\hat w(0)=25\), \(\hat w(\pm1)=25/2\), and \(\hat w(l)=0\) for \(l\ne0,\pm1\). Hence \(\sum_{h\in H}w(h)=5\), all of it from \(l=0\).

Problem 46.1.3. If \(\mathfrak{f}\ge0\) and \(\mathcal{Z}\subseteq H\), then \(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathfrak{f}(j)\le\frac{\#H}{q}\bigl(\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(0)+\sum_{l\in H^\perp,\,l\ne0}\lvert\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(l)\rvert\bigr)\).

In the model a head is a residue \(a\in\mathbb{Z}/25\), and a head followed by an entry \(j\) is the pair \((a,j)\). Put \[\mathcal{J}_2\bigl((a,j),(a',j)\bigr):=w(j)\,\mathrm{e}\bigl((a-a')j/25\bigr),\qquad \mathcal{J}_1(a,a'):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/25}\mathcal{J}_2\bigl((a,j),(a',j)\bigr).\] For a set \(\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/25\) of heads write \[\Sigma_1:=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{J}_1(a,a),\qquad \Sigma_2:=\sum_{j\in H}\sum_{a,a'\in\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{J}_2((a,j),(a',j)).\]

Problem 46.1.4. \(\mathcal{J}_1(a,a')=\hat w(a'-a)\), and \(\sum_j\mathrm{e}(-lj/25)\,\mathcal{J}_2((a,j),(a',j))=\hat w(a'-a+l)\). For each \(j\) the matrix \(\bigl(\mathcal{J}_2((a,j),(a',j))\bigr)_{a,a'\in\mathbb{Z}/25}\) is positive semidefinite of rank one.

Problem 46.1.5. For \(\mathcal{E}=\{0,1,7\}\): \(\Sigma_1=75\) and \(\Sigma_2=25\). In Problem 46.1.1 for \(j\mapsto\sum_{a,a'\in\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{J}_2((a,j),(a',j))\), the frequency \(l=0\) gives \(75\) from equal heads and \(25\) from the pairs \((0,1)\), \((1,0)\); \(l=\pm5\) give \(12.5\) each, from \((7,1)\) and \((1,7)\); \(l=\pm10\) give \(0\). Thus \(25=(75+25+25)/5\).

Used in: Chapter 45

Problem 46.1.6. For \(\mathcal{E}=\{0,7,14\}\), \(\Sigma_2=20=(75+0+25)/5\). For \(\mathcal{E}=\{0,1,\dots,6\}\), \(\Sigma_2=105=(175+150+200)/5\): together the two errors are twice the main term.

Problem 46.1.7. Let \(\mathcal{E}\ne\varnothing\), \(\varepsilon\ge\lvert\mathcal{J}_1(a,a')\rvert\) for all \(a\ne a'\) in \(\mathcal{E}\), and \(\varepsilon\ge\sum_{l\in H^\perp,\,l\ne0}\max_{a,a'\in\mathcal{E}}\lvert\hat w(a'-a+l)\rvert\). Then \(\Sigma_2\le\frac15\bigl(\Sigma_1+2\varepsilon(\#\mathcal{E})^2\bigr)\). For \(\mathcal{E}=\{0,1,7\}\) the least such \(\varepsilon\) is \(25\), and the bound is \(105\).

Heads in the proof carry weights.

Problem 46.1.8. On \(\mathbb{Z}/6\) let \(w'(j):=2+\cos(2\pi j/3)\), and give two heads \(\mathfrak{h}_1,\mathfrak{h}_2\) the weights \(1\) and \(1/2\) and the form \(\mathrm{wt}(\mathfrak{h})\mathrm{wt}(\mathfrak{h}')w'(j)\) at a common entry \(j\). Then \(\hat w'=12,3,3\) at \(l=0,2,4\) and \(\hat w'(l)=0\) otherwise. The sum over \(j\in\{0,3\}\) and all pairs of heads is \(13.5=5+4+4.5\): equal heads at \(l=0\), unequal heads at \(l=0\), and \(l=2,4\).

§46.2. Sieve systems and the recurrence

In the proof the heads are sequences of local units, the sums run over scaled images of elements of \(E^\times\), and integrating over a common entry gives the preceding form.

Definition 46.2.1. Let \(\mathsf{C}\) be a commutative ring with a measure, \(\psi\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathbb{C}\) a function, \(\mathsf{U}\) a topological space whose points are called entries, and \(u\mapsto u^\times\), \(\mathsf{C}\to\mathsf{U}\), and \(\mathrm{inv}\colon\mathsf{U}\to\mathsf{C}\) maps. A head is a sequence \(\mathfrak{h}=(\mathfrak{h}_0,\mathfrak{h}_1,\dots)\) of entries; \(\mathfrak{h}[i{:}u]\) is \(\mathfrak{h}\) with its entry at position \(i\) replaced by \(u^\times\). A sieve system of length \(n\) is a family \((\mathcal{J}_i)_{i\ge0}\) of complex functions on pairs of heads, of which the axioms use \(\mathcal{J}_0,\dots,\mathcal{J}_{n+1}\), with weights \(\mathrm{wt}_i(\mathfrak{h})\in\mathbb{R}\), a decay \(\mathrm{dec}\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathbb{R}\) and a real number \(c_{\mathrm{mass}}\), such that:

  1. \(\psi(0)=1\);

  2. for \(i\le n\), heads \(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}'\) and \(l\in\mathsf{C}\), the function \(u\mapsto\psi(lu)\,\mathcal{J}_{i+1}(\mathfrak{h}[i{:}u],\mathfrak{h}'[i{:}u])\) is integrable; its integral is \(\mathcal{J}_i(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}')\) if \(l=0\), and has modulus at most \(\mathrm{dec}(l)\mathrm{wt}_i(\mathfrak{h})\mathrm{wt}_i(\mathfrak{h}')\) if \(l\) is a unit;

  3. for every finite family of heads with a common entry at position \(n\), the matrix of the values of \(\mathcal{J}_{n+1}\) on its pairs is positive semidefinite;

  4. \(\mathcal{J}_{n+1}\) is continuous on pairs of heads (product topology);

  5. \(\lvert\mathcal{J}_0(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}')\rvert\le c_{\mathrm{mass}}\);

  6. for \(i<n\), if \(l:=\mathrm{inv}(\mathfrak{h}_i)-\mathrm{inv}(\mathfrak{h}'_i)\) is a unit, then \(\lvert\mathcal{J}_{i+1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}')\rvert\le\mathrm{dec}(l)\mathrm{wt}_{i+1}(\mathfrak{h})\mathrm{wt}_{i+1}(\mathfrak{h}')\).

In the model, (ii) at \(l=0\) defines \(\mathcal{J}_1\), Problem 46.1.4 gives (iii), and Problem 46.1.7 models the bounds of (ii) and (vi); its nonzero frequencies \(\pm5,\pm10\) are not units of \(\mathbb{Z}/25\), so the bound of (ii) at units does not apply to them.

The sieve system over the data. We use the admissible filling of the data over the CM field \(E\), with one chosen embedding \(\tau\) at each infinite place, as in Chapter 45. Let \(\mathsf{P}\) be a finite set of finite places of \(E\), \(E_{\mathsf P}:=\prod_{v\in\mathsf{P}}E_v\) with integers \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\), and \(\mathsf{C}:=\prod_\tau\mathbb{C}\times E_{\mathsf P}\), with Lebesgue measure on each \(\mathbb{C}\) and the Haar measure of \(E_{\mathsf P}\) for which \(\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\) has measure one. Let \(\delta_E\) be the generator of the different of \(E\) fixed in the proof, with \(\bar\delta_E=-\delta_E\), and \(\psi(u):=\prod_\tau e^{u_\tau-\bar u_\tau}\cdot\psi_f(-u_\mathsf{P}/\delta_E)\), where \(u_\mathsf{P}\) is read as a finite adele with components \(0\) outside \(\mathsf{P}\). The entries are the units of \(\mathsf{C}\); \(u^\times\) replaces each zero component of \(u\) at an infinite place by \(1\), and its component at \(\mathsf{P}\) by \(1\) if that is not a unit; and \(\mathrm{inv}(u):=\bigl((u_\tau^{-1})_\tau,\,M^{-1}u_\mathsf{P}^{-1}\bigr)\). Define the scales by \(R_{\tau i}:=\lvert M\tau(\delta_E)/(2\pi)\rvert^{2(\varrho-i)}/N\) for \(i\le\varrho\) (exponent \(0\) for \(i>\varrho\)); in the proof \(\lvert M\tau(\delta_E)/(2\pi)\rvert\ge1\) at every \(\tau\). For \(\mathfrak{e}\in E^\times\) let \(\mathrm{ent}_a(\mathfrak{e}):=\bigl((R_{\tau a}\tau(\mathfrak{e}))_\tau,\,\mathfrak{e}\bigr)\in\mathsf{C}\), a unit.

Definition 46.2.2. Let \(\mathcal{E}\subset E^\times\) be finite. A tuple \(\mathfrak{h}=(\mathfrak{e}_0,\dots,\mathfrak{e}_{i-1})\in\mathcal{E}^i\) is read as the head with entries \(\mathrm{ent}_a(\mathfrak{e}_a)\) for \(a<i\) and \(\mathrm{ent}_a(1)\) for \(a\ge i\); \((\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{e})\in\mathcal{E}^{i+1}\) is \(\mathfrak{h}\) followed by \(\mathfrak{e}\). For a sieve system \((\mathcal{J}_i)\) over \(\mathsf{C}\) put \(\Sigma_0:=\operatorname{Re}\mathcal{J}_0(\varnothing,\varnothing)\), \[\Sigma_{i+1}:=\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}'\in\mathcal{E}^i}\mathcal{J}_{i+1}\bigl((\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{e}),(\mathfrak{h}',\mathfrak{e})\bigr),\qquad \lambda_i:=\sum_{\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{E}^i}\mathrm{wt}_i(\mathfrak{h}),\qquad c_i:=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\hat{\mathcal O}_E)}{\prod_\tau R_{\tau i}^2}.\]

Used in: Chapter 45: Theorem 45.3.2

For \(l\in\mathsf{C}\) and an integrable \(\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathbb{C}\) write \(\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(l):=\int_{\mathsf{C}}\psi(lu)\mathfrak{f}(u)\,du\). Theorem 45.3.1 is used on \(\mathsf{C}\) in the following form. Let \(i\ge0\), let \(\mathcal{Z}\subset E^\times\) be finite with elements integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\), let \(\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathsf{C}\to\mathbb{R}\) be nonnegative and integrable and lower semicontinuous at the points \(\mathrm{ent}_i(\mathfrak{e})\), \(\mathfrak{e}\in\mathcal{Z}\), and let \(c_\mathfrak{f}\ge0\) with \(\lvert\hat{\mathfrak{f}}(l)\rvert\le\mathrm{dec}(l)c_\mathfrak{f}\) for every unit \(l\). If \(\varepsilon\) bounds \(\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Z}'}\mathrm{dec}\bigl(((2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\tau(y)/(\tau(\delta_E)R_{\tau i}))_\tau,y)\bigr)\) for every finite set \(\mathcal{Z}'\) of nonzero elements of \(E\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\), then \[\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in\mathcal{Z}}\mathfrak{f}\bigl(\mathrm{ent}_i(\mathfrak{e})\bigr)\le c_i\Bigl(\varepsilon c_\mathfrak{f}+\int_{\mathsf{C}}\mathfrak{f}\Bigr).\] Status: read from the proof; it is Theorem 45.3.1 for the function on the adeles that is \(\mathfrak{f}\) after the dilation by \(R_{\tau i}\) at the infinite places on the adeles integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\), and zero elsewhere; the factor \(c_i\) is the change of measure.

For the recurrence fix a sieve system \((\mathcal{J}_i)\) of length \(\varrho\) over \(\mathsf{C}\) with weights \(\mathrm{wt}_i\ge0\), a finite \(\mathcal{E}\subset E^\times\) with \(\mathfrak{e}\) and \(\mathfrak{e}^{-1}\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\) for \(\mathfrak{e}\in\mathcal{E}\), assume \(M^{-1}\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\) and \(\lvert M\tau(\delta_E)/(2\pi)\rvert\ge1\) at every \(\tau\), and let \(\varepsilon\ge0\) satisfy \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:eps}\tag{1} \sum_{y\in\mathcal{Z}'}\mathrm{dec}\Bigl(\bigl((c_\tau\tau(y)/R_{\tau a})_\tau,\,y\bigr)\Bigr)\le\varepsilon \end{equation}\] for every \(a\ge0\), every \(\mathbf c=(c_\tau)\in\prod_\tau\mathbb{C}\) with \(c_\tau=0\) or \(\lvert c_\tau\rvert\ge2\pi/\lvert\tau(\delta_E)\rvert\) at each \(\tau\), and every finite set \(\mathcal{Z}'\) of nonzero elements of \(E\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\). For \(i\le\varrho\) and \(u\in\mathsf{C}\) put \(\mathfrak{f}_i(u):=\sum_{\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}'\in\mathcal{E}^i}\mathcal{J}_{i+1}(\mathfrak{h}[i{:}u],\mathfrak{h}'[i{:}u])\), so that \(\Sigma_{i+1}=\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak{f}_i(\mathrm{ent}_i(\mathfrak{e}))\). By Fatou’s lemma and downward induction on \(i\), \(\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak{f}_i\) is lower semicontinuous at \(\mathrm{ent}_i(\mathfrak{e})\) for every \(\mathfrak{e}\in E^\times\) (read from the proof).

Problem 46.2.1. In a sieve system of length \(n\), for \(i\le n\) and finitely many heads with a common entry at position \(i\), the sum of \(\mathcal{J}_{i+1}\) over all their pairs is real and nonnegative. In particular \(\mathfrak{f}_i\ge0\).

Problem 46.2.2. For \(0\le i\le\varrho\), \(\int_{\mathsf{C}}\mathfrak{f}_i=\sum_{\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}'\in\mathcal{E}^i}\mathcal{J}_i(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}')\). For \(1\le i\le\varrho\), if \(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}'\in\mathcal{E}^i\) have distinct last entries \(\mathfrak{e},\mathfrak{e}'\), then \(y:=(\mathfrak{e}^{-1}-\mathfrak{e}'^{-1})/M\) is nonzero and integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\), the \(\mathrm{inv}\)-images of the entries at position \(i-1\) differ by \(\bigl((M\tau(y)/R_{\tau,i-1})_\tau,y\bigr)\), and \(\lvert\mathcal{J}_i(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}')\rvert\le\varepsilon\mathrm{wt}_i(\mathfrak{h})\mathrm{wt}_i(\mathfrak{h}')\). For \(0\le i\le\varrho\), \(\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathsf{C}}\mathfrak{f}_i\le\Sigma_i+\varepsilon\lambda_i^2\).

Problem 46.2.3. \(\lvert\hat{\mathfrak{f}}_i(l)\rvert\le\mathrm{dec}(l)\lambda_i^2\) for every unit \(l\) of \(\mathsf{C}\).

The change of measure with \(\mathfrak{f}=\operatorname{Re}\mathfrak{f}_i\), \(\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{E}\) and \(c_\mathfrak{f}=\lambda_i^2\) (the frequencies are (1) with \(c_\tau=2\pi\mathrm{i}/\tau(\delta_E)\)), together with Problems 46.2.1–46.2.3, proves the following.

Theorem 46.2.1. Under these hypotheses, for the data, \(\Sigma_{i+1}\le c_i\bigl(\Sigma_i+2\varepsilon\lambda_i^2\bigr)\) for \(0\le i\le\varrho\).

Used in: Chapter 45

Problem 46.2.4. In the lattice example \(E=\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), \(\operatorname{vol}(\hat{\mathcal O}_E)=1\), and \(\delta_E=2\mathrm{i}\) generates the different. With \(\mathsf{P}=\varnothing\), the scale \(2\) and \(\mathfrak{f}(z)=e^{-\lvert z\rvert^2}\), the change-of-measure factor is \(1/4\), and Poisson summation on the lattice gives \(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]}e^{-4\lvert\nu\rvert^2}=1.0746048\ldots=\frac\pi4\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]}e^{-\pi^2\lvert l\rvert^2/4}\). The term \(l=0\) is \(\pi/4=\frac14\int_\mathbb{C}e^{-\lvert z\rvert^2}dz\).

§46.3. The sum over the frequencies

Condition (1) asks for a uniform bound over all finite sets of nonzero numbers integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\). For \(\nu\in E\) let \(\mathfrak b_\nu:=\{a\in\mathcal{O}_E:a\nu\in\mathcal{O}_E\}\), the denominator ideal. For a unit \(z\) of \(E_{\mathsf P}\), \([\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:z\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]:=\prod_{v\in\mathsf{P}}\lvert z\rvert_v^{-1}\) (normalized absolute values) is the index when \(z\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\). In the proof, at a unit \(l\) of \(\mathsf{C}\), \[\mathrm{dec}(l)=c'\prod_\tau\lvert l_\tau\rvert^{-k_1}\min\bigl(1,\lvert l_\tau\rvert^{-1}\bigr)^6\cdot\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:l_\mathsf{P}\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]^{k_1/2}}{[\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\cap l_\mathsf{P}^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]^3},\qquad k_1:=k-(\varrho+1)^7\ge0,\] with \(c'=(c_{\mathrm{vol}}b^{2000(\varrho+1)^9})^fb^{(\varrho+1)^9f}\), where \(c_{\mathrm{vol}}>0\) is the volume ratio of the shape, and \(\mathrm{dec}(l)=0\) if some \(l_\tau=0\) (read from the proof).

Problem 46.3.1. For \(y\in E^\times\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\): \([\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:y\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]\le\prod_\tau\lvert\tau(y)\rvert^2\) and \(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak b_y\le[\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\cap y^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]\).

Problem 46.3.2. In \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) with \(\mathsf{P}=\{3\}\) and \(y=3^j\) for \(j\in\mathbb{Z}\): \([\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:y\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]=9^j\) and \([\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\cap y^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]=9^{\max(0,-j)}\). With the model exponent \(k_1=2\), the quotient of the two index powers in \(\mathrm{dec}\) is \(9^{-8},9^{-4},1,9\) at \(j=-2,-1,0,1\), while \([\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:y\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]\cdot\lvert y\rvert^{-2}=1\) for all \(j\).

Problem 46.3.3. If \(c,y\in\mathbb{C}^\times\) and \(k_1\ge0\), then \[\lvert cy\rvert^{-k_1}\min\bigl(1,\lvert cy\rvert^{-1}\bigr)^6\lvert y\rvert^{k_1}\le\lvert c\rvert^{-k_1}\max\bigl(1,\lvert c\rvert^{-1}\bigr)^6\max\bigl(1,\lvert y\rvert\bigr)^{-6}.\]

So for \(y\ne0\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\) and \(c_\tau\in\mathbb{C}^\times\), the decay at \(((c_\tau\tau(y))_\tau,y)\) is at most \(c'\prod_\tau\lvert c_\tau\rvert^{-k_1}\max(1,\lvert c_\tau\rvert^{-1})^6\) times \(\prod_\tau\max(1,\lvert\tau(y)\rvert)^{-6}\cdot\mathrm{N}\mathfrak b_y^{-3}\).

Problem 46.3.4. For a nonzero ideal \(\mathfrak b\), the \(\nu\in E\) with \(\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathfrak b_\nu\) lie in at most \(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak b\) classes modulo \(\mathcal{O}_E\). Two distinct elements \(\nu,\nu'\) of one class do not have \(\tau(\nu)\) and \(\tau(\nu')\) in the same square \([j/2,(j+1)/2)\times[j'/2,(j'+1)/2)\) for every \(\tau\).

Problem 46.3.5. If \(\lvert z-z_0\rvert<1\), then \(\max(1,\lvert z\rvert)^{-6}\le64\max(1,\lvert z_0\rvert)^{-6}\).

Problem 46.3.6. By machine, \(\sum_{z\in\frac12\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]}\max(1,\lvert z\rvert)^{-6}=19.1704\ldots\). A disc of radius at least \(1\) contains at most \(25\) times its squared radius points of \(\frac12\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), and, without the machine value, the sum over every finite subset of \(\frac12\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) is at most \(38\).

At \(s=2\) the Euler factor of the Dedekind zeta function \(\zeta_E\) at a prime \(p\) is at most \((1-p^{-2})^{-[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\), so the sum over the nonzero integral ideals is \(\sum_{\mathfrak b}\mathrm{N}\mathfrak b^{-2}=\zeta_E(2)\le\zeta(2)^{2f}\le e^{4f}\). Status: classical [Ne]. Problems 46.3.4–46.3.6 and this bound prove the following.

Theorem 46.3.1. Let \(E\) be a totally complex number field with \(f\) infinite places and a chosen embedding \(\tau\) at each, and \(\mathcal{Z}\subset E\) finite. Then \[\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}}\prod_\tau\max\bigl(1,\lvert\tau(\nu)\rvert\bigr)^{-6}\cdot\mathrm{N}\mathfrak b_\nu^{-3}\le(64\cdot38\cdot e^4)^f .\]

Used in: Problem 46.3.7; Chapter 45

Problem 46.3.7. By machine, for \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\): \(\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]}\max(1,\lvert\nu\rvert)^{-6}=5.6589\ldots\), and the \(\nu\) with \(\mathrm{N}\mathfrak b_\nu\le40\) and \(\lvert\nu\rvert\le12\) contribute \(6.966\ldots\) to the sum of Theorem 46.3.1 (the bound is \(1.3278\ldots\cdot10^5\)).

If some \(c_\tau=0\) in (1), every summand is \(0\). Otherwise \(\lvert c_\tau/R_{\tau a}\rvert^{-1}\le\lvert M\tau(\delta_E)/(2\pi)\rvert^{2\varrho+1}/(MN)\le b^{23(2\varrho+1)}\) in the proof, and Problems 46.3.1 and 46.3.3 and Theorem 46.3.1 bound the sum by \[c'(64\cdot38\cdot e^4)^fb^{138(2\varrho+1)f}\prod_\tau(\lvert M\tau(\delta_E)/(2\pi)\rvert^{2\varrho+1}/(MN))^{k_1}.\] With \(64\cdot38\cdot e^4\le b^3\) and similar size bounds the proof enlarges this to \(\varepsilon=c_{\mathrm{fr}}\) of Theorem 45.3.2.

§46.4. Heads, weights and the sieve

In the proof the weight of \(\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{E}^i\) is a product over its prefix products \(\delta_a:=\mathfrak{e}_0\cdots\mathfrak{e}_a\), \(a<i\). At \(\tau\) the factor is \(\max(1,\lvert z\rvert^{-1})^{k_2}\) if \(\lvert z\rvert\le b^{60(\varrho+1)^2}\) and \(0\) otherwise, where \(z\) is the product of the first \(a+1\) entries of the head at \(\tau\); at \(\mathsf{P}\) it is \([\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}:\delta_a\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}]^{k_2/2}\) if \(\delta_a\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathsf P}\) and \(0\) otherwise; here \(k_2:=(\varrho+1)^6\). A head is determined by its prefix products.

Problem 46.4.1. Take \(\mathcal{E}=\{1,2,1/2\}\subset\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\), \(\mathsf{P}\) the prime above \(2\), all scales \(1\), the cutoff \(2\) in place of \(b^{60(\varrho+1)^2}\) and the exponent \(2\) in place of \(k_2\). The heads of length two with nonzero weight are \((1,1),(1,2),(2,1),(2,1/2)\), with weights \(1,4,16,4\); so \(\lambda_2=25\).

In the proof, for \(0\le i\le\varrho\) and \(\mathcal{E}\) with elements integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\), the prefix products of a head of nonzero weight are algebraic integers of modulus at most \(b^{120(\varrho+1)^2}\) at every \(\tau\), and packing them, bounding each weight and bounding \(\prod_{a<i}c_a\) below gives \(\lambda_i^2\le c_{\mathrm{wt}}\prod_{a<i}c_a\), with \(c_{\mathrm{wt}}=b^{400(\varrho+1)^9f}\) (Status: read from the proof; not proved here). For the application the proof uses the Weil data and the places \(\mathcal D\) of Theorem 45.3.2 with a nonzero vector in the reduced Bessel span at each, that is, in the span of the Bessel tuple \((v_1,\dots,v_\varrho,z)\) modulo the chosen place or its conjugate (Definitions 43.2.1 and 43.2.7), and chooses \(\mathsf{P}\) stable under conjugation with \(M^{-1}\) integral outside \(\mathsf{P}\) and every element of \(\mathcal{E}\) a unit outside \(\mathsf{P}\). At each \(\tau\) the band limits of the Bessel system give a sieve system of length \(\varrho\) over \(\mathbb{C}\) with character \(e^{z-\bar z}\) and \(c_{\mathrm{mass}}=(1+b^{-10})/d_\tau\). Over \(E_{\mathsf P}\) the proof chooses an intermediate subgroup. For \(j\le\varrho\) it uses the subgroup’s floor form at \(j\), and for \(j>\varrho\) it uses the last-entry density at the \(\mathsf{P}\)-component of the chosen idele above \(N\). Dividing each form by the subgroup’s mass ratio gives a sieve system of length \(\varrho\) with \(c_{\mathrm{mass}}=(1+b^{-6})b^{-6k\#\mathcal D}\). Status: the local systems are read from the proof.
