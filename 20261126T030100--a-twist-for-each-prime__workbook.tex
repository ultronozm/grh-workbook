\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
Finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces; Hadamard's inequality and Fischer's inequality for positive semidefinite hermitian matrices \cite[\S7.8]{HJ}.  From Chapter~\ref{c5-chap}, \emph{Size against divisibility}: Sections~\ref{c5-sec:heights} and~\ref{c5-sec:index}, whose notation we keep: isomorphisms \(\iota \mid \ell\), \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_\iota\), \(\Oc_\iota\), \(\bar\iota\), families and their heights, lattice data, index and covolume.  Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first variable.  For vectors \(w_1, \dots, w_n\) in an inner product space let \(\Gamma(w) := \det(\langle w_i, w_k \rangle)_{i,k}\), with \(\Gamma(\varnothing) = 1\).

\medskip
Theorem~\ref{c5-thm:one} compares the size and the divisibility of one Gram determinant, both read through one map \(\Theta\).  In the rewritten Lean proof \cite{GL} the size of the Gram determinant of a basis is read through a fixed map \(\Theta_1\), and at each isomorphism \(\iota \mid \ell\) its divisibility is read through the map \(\Theta_{\xi_\iota}\) for a \emph{twist} \(\xi_\iota\) allowed at \(\ell\).  For injective maps the Gram determinant of a basis under \(\Theta_1\) is its Gram determinant under \(\Theta_{\xi_\iota}\) times a number \(Z(1)/Z(\xi_\iota)\) that does not depend on the basis.  This chapter works this out on \(\C^2\) and then states the general inequality.

\section{One twist on \texorpdfstring{\(\C^2\)}{C2}}\label{sec:toy}

A lattice datum asks for \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) with \(\varphi\) integral at \(\bar\iota\) and \(\varphi'\) integral at \(\iota\).  The two isomorphisms cannot be taken equal.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:one-place}
Some \(\iota \mid 5\) has \(\lvert 2 + \ii \rvert_\iota = 1\).  For such \(\iota\), \(v := (2 + \ii)^{-1}\) lies in \(\Oc_\iota\), and \(\langle v, v \rangle = 1/5\) does not.
\end{problem}

A \emph{twisted lattice} on a complex vector space \(V\), with twists in a set \(T\) that has a distinguished element \(1\), consists of the following data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each \(\xi \in T\), a complex inner product space \(W_\xi\) and a linear map \(\Theta_\xi\colon V \to W_\xi\).
\item A predicate on \(\mathbb{N}\times T\).  At a prime \(\ell\) it specifies the twists \emph{allowed} at \(\ell\).  In the toys take it to be false at nonprimes.
\item For each \(\xi \in T\) and each \(\iota \mid \ell\), an \(\Oc_\iota\)-submodule \(\Lambda_{\xi, \iota} \subseteq W_\xi\), such that \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) for all \(\varphi \in \Lambda_{\xi, \bar\iota}\) and \(\varphi' \in \Lambda_{\xi, \iota}\) whenever \(\xi\) is allowed at \(\ell\).
\item Rational coordinates on \(W_1\) alone: a finite set \(I\), a linear map \(b\colon W_1 \to \C^I\) and a matrix \(G \in M_I(\Q)\), positive definite as a complex matrix, with \(\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle = b(\varphi)^* G\, b(\varphi')\) for all \(\varphi, \varphi' \in W_1\).
\end{enumerate}
A vector \(y \in V\) is \emph{integral at \(\iota \mid \ell\)} if \(\Theta_\xi y \in \Lambda_{\xi, \iota}\) for every \(\xi\) allowed at \(\ell\); these vectors form an \(\Oc_\iota\)-submodule \(M_\iota \subseteq V\).  Sizes will be read through \(\Theta_1\) alone.

Throughout, \(p\) and \(r\) are distinct primes.  The \emph{toy} is the following twisted lattice on \(V = \C^2\) with \(T = \{1, \xi\}\): \(W_1 = W_\xi = \C^2\) with the standard inner product, \(\Theta_1 = \mathrm{id}\), \(\Theta_\xi = \diag(p^{-1}, 1)\); the twist \(1\) is allowed at every prime and \(\xi\) at every prime except \(r\); \(\Lambda_{1, \iota} = \Lambda_{\xi, \iota} = \Oc_\iota^2\) for every \(\iota\); \(I = \{1, 2\}\), \(b = \mathrm{id}\), \(G = 1\).  Let \(e_1, e_2\) be the standard basis.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:toy-lattice}
The toy is a twisted lattice.  Its integral vectors are \(M_\iota = p\Oc_\iota \oplus \Oc_\iota\) for \(\iota \mid p\), and \(M_\iota = \Oc_\iota^2\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\), \(\ell \ne p\).
\end{problem}

For a linear map \(u\colon U \to W\) between finite-dimensional inner product spaces, \(u^*\) is its adjoint, and \(u \circ w\) is the family \(u(w_1), u(w_2), \dots\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gram-comp}
If \(w_1, \dots, w_n \in U\) and \(n = \dim U\), then \(\Gamma(u \circ w) = \det(u^* u)\, \Gamma(w)\).
\end{problem}

The hypothesis \(n = \dim U\) cannot be dropped.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:not-basis}
In the toy, \(\Gamma(\Theta_\xi e_1) = p^{-2}\, \Gamma(\Theta_1 e_1)\) and \(\Gamma(\Theta_\xi e_2) = \Gamma(\Theta_1 e_2)\).
\end{problem}

Let \(Z(\xi) := \det(\Theta_\xi^* \Theta_\xi)\); in the toy \(Z(1) = 1\) and \(Z(\xi) = p^{-2}\).  Write \(\Gamma_\xi(w) := \Gamma(\Theta_\xi \circ w)\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:toy-gain}
In the toy, let \(w_1, w_2\) be a basis of \(\C^2\) with \(w_1, w_2 \in M_\iota\) for every \(\iota \mid p\).  Then \(\Gamma_1(w) = p^2\, \Gamma_\xi(w)\), and \(\lvert \Gamma_1(w) \rvert_\iota \le p^{-2}\) for every \(\iota \mid p\).
\end{problem}

Problem~\ref{pr:toy-gain} gives divisibility by \(p^2\) at every \(\iota\mid p\).  Section~\ref{sec:each} compares the gains from different choices of twist.

\section{Index, covolume and the inequality}\label{sec:general}

Fix a twisted lattice on \(V\) with twists \(T\).  Heights of families are those of Section~\ref{c5-sec:heights}.  We use two facts from there: Problem~\ref{c5-pr:height-mul}, that the product of families of heights at most \(a\) and \(a'\) has height at most \(a+a'\), and the \(m\)-th power of a family of height at most \(a\) has height at most \(ma\) for every integer \(m\ge0\); and the product formula for families, Theorem~\ref{c5-thm:product}: if \(z \in \C\), \(z \ne 0\), the constant family \(z\) has height at most \(c\), and \(\lvert \sigma z \rvert \le e^{c'}\) for every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), then
\[
  -c \le c' .
\]
Index and covolume are defined as in Section~\ref{c5-sec:index}, with two changes: \(M_\iota\) is now the module of vectors integral at \(\iota\) for every allowed twist, and sizes are read through \(\Theta_1\), with the coordinates \(b\) and the matrix \(G\) of \(W_1\).  Thus \(v_\sigma(y)\) is \(b(\Theta_1 y)\) with \(\sigma\) applied to each coordinate, \(N(y, \sigma) = v_\sigma(y)^* G\, v_\sigma(y)\), a real number \(\ge 0\) because \(G\) is positive definite as a complex matrix, so that the real part in Chapter~\ref{c5-chap} may be omitted, and \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) if \(\lvert \det(v_\sigma(y_i)^* G\, v_\sigma(y_k))_{i,k} \rvert \le e^c\) for every \(\sigma\).  A family \(y\) has index at most \(\delta\) if there is a function \(e\colon \mathbb{N} \to \R\), nonnegative everywhere, with \(\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell) \log \ell \le \delta\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) some \(u \in M_n(\Oc_\iota)\) \emph{carries \(y\) into \(M_\iota\) with loss} \(e(\ell)\): \(\sum_k u_{ik} y_k \in M_\iota\) for every \(i\), and \(\lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \ge \ell^{-e(\ell)}\).  The matrix may depend on \(\iota\) but not on the twist.  For the untwisted coordinates, the conjugate bound of Problem~\ref{c5-pr:cs-det} holds for every twisted lattice (\tl{CovolumeLe.norm\_map\_det\_gram\_le} in \cite{GL}), with the same proof: the modules \(\Lambda_{\xi,\iota}\) play no role.  If \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\), then
\[
  \lvert \sigma(\Gamma_1(y)) \rvert \le e^c \quad \text{for every } \sigma \in \Aut(\C).
\]

Let \(y_1,\dots,y_n\) and \(z_1,\dots,z_{n'}\) be vectors in \(V\).  In Problems~\ref{pr:val}--\ref{pr:index-height}, assume that \(V\) is a complex inner product space.  Write \(y \sqcup z\) for the family \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'}\), and \(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2\) for the complex number \(\bar\gamma\gamma\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:val}
Let \(\xi\) be allowed at \(\ell\), let \(\iota \mid \ell\), let \(w_1, \dots, w_m \in V\), and let \(u, u' \in M_m(\C)\) be such that \(\sum_k u_{ik} \Theta_\xi w_k \in \Lambda_{\xi, \iota}\) and \(\sum_k u'_{ik} \Theta_\xi w_k \in \Lambda_{\xi, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\).  Then
\[
  \lvert \det u' \rvert_{\bar\iota} \, \lvert \Gamma_\xi(w) \rvert_\iota \, \lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \le 1 .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:complete}
Let \(\xi\) be allowed at \(\ell\), let \(\iota \mid \ell\) and \(a \in \R\).  Suppose that \(u \in M_n(\Oc_\iota)\) carries \(y\) into \(M_\iota\) with loss \(a\), that \(u' \in M_n(\Oc_{\bar\iota})\) carries \(y\) into \(M_{\bar\iota}\) with loss \(a\), and that \(\gamma \in \C\) satisfies \(\gamma\, \Theta_\xi z_i \in \Lambda_{\xi, \iota} \cap \Lambda_{\xi, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\).  Then
\[
  \bigl\lvert (\lvert\gamma\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_\xi(y \sqcup z) \bigr\rvert_\iota \le \ell^{2a} .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:index-height}
Let \(y\) have index at most \(\delta\).  For each \(\iota \mid \ell\) let \(\xi_\iota \in T\) be allowed at \(\ell\) and \(\gamma_\iota \in \C\), with \(\gamma_\iota\, \Theta_{\xi_\iota} z_i \in \Lambda_{\xi_\iota, \iota} \cap \Lambda_{\xi_\iota, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\).  Then the family \(\iota \mapsto (\lvert\gamma_\iota\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_{\xi_\iota}(y \sqcup z)\) has height at most \(2\delta\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:fischer}
If \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\), and \(N(z_i, \sigma) \le e^{h}\) for every \(i\) and every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), then \(y \sqcup z\) has covolume at most \(c + n' h\).
\end{problem}

Now let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space.  For \(\xi \in T\) let \(Z(\xi)\) be the square of the absolute value of the determinant of \(\Theta_\xi\), as a map \(V \to \Theta_\xi(V)\), in orthonormal bases, when \(\Theta_\xi\) is injective, and \(Z(\xi) := 0\) otherwise.  When \(W_\xi\) is finite-dimensional, \(Z(\xi) = \det(\Theta_\xi^* \Theta_\xi)\), as in Section~\ref{sec:toy}.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:factor}
Let all \(\Theta_\xi\) be injective, let \(y \sqcup z\) be a basis of \(V\), and let \(\gamma \in \C\), \(\gamma \ne 0\).  Then \(\Gamma_1(y \sqcup z) \ne 0\), and for every \(\xi \in T\)
\[
  \Gamma_1(y \sqcup z) = (\lvert\gamma\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_\xi(y \sqcup z) \cdot (\lvert\gamma\rvert^{-2})^{n'} \cdot \frac{Z(1)}{Z(\xi)} .
\]
\end{problem}

Problems~\ref{pr:index-height}, \ref{pr:fischer} and~\ref{pr:factor}, together with the height bounds and the product formula of Chapter~\ref{c5-chap}, give the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:twist}
Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, \(T\) a set with a distinguished element \(1\), and \(L\) a twisted lattice on \(V\) with twists \(T\) whose maps \(\Theta_\xi\) are all injective.  Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'} \in V\) be linearly independent, with \(\dim V = n + n'\).  For each \(\iota \mid \ell\), for every prime \(\ell\), let \(\xi_\iota \in T\) be allowed at \(\ell\).  Let \(c, \delta, \cvol, \cshort, \cden \in \R\), and suppose that \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) and index at most \(\delta\).  Let \(\gamma\colon T \to \C\) with \(\gamma(\xi) \ne 0\) for every \(\xi\), and suppose that
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\gamma(\xi)\, \Theta_\xi z_i \in \Lambda_{\xi, \iota}\) for every \(i\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) for every prime \(\ell\), and every \(\xi\) allowed at \(\ell\);
\item \(N(z_i, \sigma) \le e^{\cshort}\) for every \(i\) and every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\);
\item the family \(\iota \mapsto (\lvert \gamma(\xi_\iota) \rvert^2)^{-1}\) has height at most \(\cden\);
\item the family \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\xi_\iota)\) has height at most \(\cvol\).
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  0 \le c + 2\delta + n'(\cshort + \cden) + \cvol .
\]
\end{theorem}

Hypothesis~(1) concerns every twist allowed at \(\ell\), including \(\xi_\iota\) when it differs from \(\xi_{\bar\iota}\).  To recover Theorem~\ref{c5-thm:one}, give \(V\) the inner product in which its given basis \(y\) is orthonormal, and take \(T=\{1\}\), with \(1\) allowed at every prime, \(n'=0\), \(\gamma=1\) and \(\cshort=\cden=\cvol=0\).

\section{A twist for each prime}\label{sec:each}

We first test Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} on the toy, then add a twist that is allowed at \(r\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:toy-check}
In the toy, let \(k \in \Z\) and \(s \ge 1\) an integer, and take \(n = n' = 1\), \(y_1 = p^k e_1\), \(z_1 = s^{-1} e_2\), \(\gamma(1) = \gamma(\xi) = s\), \(\xi_\iota = \xi\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell \ne r\), and \(\xi_\iota = 1\) for \(\iota \mid r\).  The hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} hold with
\[
  c = 2k \log p, \quad \delta = \max(1 - k, 0) \log p, \quad \cshort = -2\log s, \quad \cden = 2 \log s, \quad \cvol = -2 \log p ,
\]
and then \(c + 2\delta + n'(\cshort + \cden) + \cvol = 2\max(k - 1, 0) \log p\).  For \(p = 3\), \(k = -1\), \(s = 2\): \(c = -2\log 3\) and \(\delta = 2 \log 3\).  For the constant choice \(\xi_\iota = 1\), with the same \(\cshort\) and \(\cden\), the theorem gives only \(c + 2\delta \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

The \emph{second toy} adds to the toy a twist \(\eta\): \(T = \{1, \xi, \eta\}\), \(W_\eta = \C^2\), \(\Theta_\eta = \diag(1, r^{-1})\), \(\Lambda_{\eta, \iota} = \Oc_\iota^2\), and \(\eta\) is allowed at every prime except \(p\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:two-twists}
The second toy is a twisted lattice, with \(M_\iota = p\Oc_\iota \oplus \Oc_\iota\) for \(\iota \mid p\), \(M_\iota = \Oc_\iota \oplus r\Oc_\iota\) for \(\iota \mid r\), and \(M_\iota = \Oc_\iota^2\) otherwise.  For \(y = (p e_1, r e_2)\), \(n' = 0\), \(\xi_\iota = \xi\) for \(\iota \mid p\), \(\xi_\iota = \eta\) for \(\iota \mid r\), and \(\xi_\iota = 1\) otherwise, the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} hold with \(\gamma = 1\), \(\cshort = \cden = 0\), \(c = 2\log(pr)\), \(\delta = 0\) and \(\cvol = -2\log(pr)\), and the inequality is an equality.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:no-single}
In the second toy, suppose that \(\xi_\iota \in \{1, \xi\}\) for every \(\iota\).  If \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\xi_\iota)\) has height at most \(c''\), then \(c'' \ge -2\log p\).  The same holds with \(\eta\) and \(r\) in place of \(\xi\) and \(p\).
\end{problem}

So for \(y\) as in Problem~\ref{pr:two-twists}, a choice of twists with values in \(\{1, \xi\}\) or in \(\{1, \eta\}\) yields through Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} at most \(c \ge 2\log p\) or \(c \ge 2\log r\), while the choice that depends on the prime yields \(c \ge 2\log(pr)\), which is attained.

Under the other hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist}, a height bound of \(-B\) for \(\iota\mapsto Z(1)/Z(\xi_\iota)\) gives \(c+2\delta\ge B-n'(\cshort+\cden)\).  In the readable edition of \cite{GL} Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} is applied once, in the proof of Theorem~C (\tl{New.Data.integral\_forms\_long}; Chapter~\ref{c41-chap}).  There \(V\) is the space of good automorphic forms on \(Y_0\), and the twists are the \(m\)-fold twists of the datum; each has an automorphic character and a finite set \(Q_\xi\) of finite places of its CM field, and it is allowed at \(\ell\) precisely when \(\ell\) is prime to \(\prod_{w \in Q_\xi} \# k_w\), where \(k_w\) is the residue field at \(w\); these are the twists and the allowed predicate of \tl{New.Data.targetLattice}.  The number \(Z(\xi)\) is the Gram determinant of the chain of lifts on an orthonormal basis of \(V\) (Chapter~\ref{c37-chap}).  The proof bounds the height of \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\xi_\iota)\) by the bounds for single lifts of Chapter~\ref{c38-chap} and a height bound for the inverse scalar in the factorization of \(Z(1)/Z(\xi_\iota)\) (\tl{New.Data.heightLe\_chainDeterminant\_one\_div}).  The vectors \(z_i\) are auxiliary forms that complete \(y\) to a basis, and \(\gamma(\xi)\Theta_\xi z_i\) is integral at every \(\iota\) at which \(\xi\) is allowed.
