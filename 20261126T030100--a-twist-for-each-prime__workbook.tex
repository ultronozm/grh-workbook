\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
Finite-dimensional complex inner product spaces; Hadamard's inequality and Fischer's inequality for positive semidefinite hermitian matrices \cite[\S7.8]{HJ}.  From Chapter~\ref{c5-chap}, \emph{Size against divisibility}: Sections~\ref{c5-sec:heights} and~\ref{c5-sec:index}, whose notation we keep: isomorphisms \(\iota \mid \ell\), \(\lvert\cdot\rvert_\iota\), \(\Oc_\iota\), \(\bar\iota\), families and their heights, lattice data, index and covolume.  Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first variable.  For vectors \(w_1, \dots, w_n\) in an inner product space let \(\Gamma(w) := \det(\langle w_i, w_k \rangle)_{i,k}\), with \(\Gamma(\varnothing) = 1\).

\medskip
Theorem~\ref{c5-thm:one} compares the size and the divisibility of one Gram determinant, both read through one map \(\Theta\).  In the proof the size of the Gram determinant of a basis is read through a fixed map \(\Theta_1\), and at each isomorphism \(\iota \mid \ell\) its divisibility is read through the map \(\Theta_{\theta_\iota}\) for a \emph{twist} \(\theta_\iota\) allowed at \(\ell\).  For injective maps the Gram determinant of a basis under \(\Theta_1\) is its Gram determinant under \(\Theta_{\theta_\iota}\) times a number \(Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) that does not depend on the basis.  This chapter works this out on \(\C^2\) and then states the general inequality.

\section{One twist on \texorpdfstring{\(\C^2\)}{C2}}\label{sec:toy}

A lattice datum asks for \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) with \(\varphi\) integral at \(\bar\iota\) and \(\varphi'\) integral at \(\iota\).  The two isomorphisms cannot be taken equal.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:one-place}
Some \(\iota \mid 5\) has \(\lvert 2 + \ii \rvert_\iota = 1\).  For such \(\iota\), \(v := (2 + \ii)^{-1}\) lies in \(\Oc_\iota\), and \(\langle v, v \rangle = 1/5\) does not.
\end{problem}

A \emph{twisted lattice} on a complex vector space \(V\), with twists in a set \(\mathrm{Tw}\) that has a distinguished element \(1\), consists of the following data.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\), a complex inner product space \(W_\vartheta\) and a linear map \(\Theta_\vartheta\colon V \to W_\vartheta\).
\item A predicate on \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathrm{Tw}\).  At a prime \(\ell\) the predicate specifies the twists \emph{allowed} at \(\ell\).  In the toys take it to be false at nonprimes.
\item For each \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) and each \(\iota \mid \ell\), an \(\Oc_\iota\)-submodule \(\Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota} \subseteq W_\vartheta\), such that \(\lvert \langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle \rvert_\iota \le 1\) for all \(\varphi \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \bar\iota}\) and \(\varphi' \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) whenever \(\vartheta\) is allowed at \(\ell\).
\item Rational coordinates on \(W_1\) alone: a finite set \(I\), a linear map \(b\colon W_1 \to \C^I\) and a matrix \(G \in M_I(\Q)\), positive definite as a complex matrix, with \(\langle \varphi, \varphi' \rangle = b(\varphi)^* G\, b(\varphi')\) for all \(\varphi, \varphi' \in W_1\).
\end{enumerate}
A vector \(y \in V\) is \emph{integral at \(\iota \mid \ell\)} if \(\Theta_\vartheta y \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) for every \(\vartheta\) allowed at \(\ell\); these vectors form an \(\Oc_\iota\)-submodule \(M_\iota \subseteq V\).  Sizes will be read through \(\Theta_1\) alone.

Throughout, \(p\) and \(r\) are distinct primes.  The \emph{toy} is the following twisted lattice on \(V = \C^2\) with \(\mathrm{Tw} = \{1, \vartheta\}\): \(W_1 = W_\vartheta = \C^2\) with the standard inner product, \(\Theta_1 = \mathrm{id}\), \(\Theta_\vartheta = \diag(p^{-1}, 1)\); the twist \(1\) is allowed at every prime and \(\vartheta\) at every prime except \(r\); \(\Lambda_{1, \iota} = \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota} = \Oc_\iota^2\) for every \(\iota\); \(I = \{1, 2\}\), \(b = \mathrm{id}\), \(G = 1\).  Let \(e_1, e_2\) be the standard basis.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:toy-lattice}
The toy is a twisted lattice.  Its integral vectors are \(M_\iota = p\Oc_\iota \oplus \Oc_\iota\) for \(\iota \mid p\), and \(M_\iota = \Oc_\iota^2\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\), \(\ell \ne p\).
\end{problem}

For a linear map \(u\colon U \to W\) between finite-dimensional inner product spaces, \(u^*\) is its adjoint, and \(u \circ w\) is the family \(u(w_1), u(w_2), \dots\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gram-comp}
If \(w_1, \dots, w_n \in U\) and \(n = \dim U\), then \(\Gamma(u \circ w) = \det(u^* u)\, \Gamma(w)\).
\end{problem}

The hypothesis \(n = \dim U\) cannot be dropped.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:not-basis}
In the toy, \(\Gamma(\Theta_\vartheta e_1) = p^{-2}\, \Gamma(\Theta_1 e_1)\) and \(\Gamma(\Theta_\vartheta e_2) = \Gamma(\Theta_1 e_2)\).
\end{problem}

Let \(Z(\vartheta) := \det(\Theta_\vartheta^* \Theta_\vartheta)\); in the toy \(Z(1) = 1\) and \(Z(\vartheta) = p^{-2}\).  Write \(\Gamma_\vartheta(w) := \Gamma(\Theta_\vartheta \circ w)\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:toy-gain}
In the toy, let \(w_1, w_2\) be a basis of \(\C^2\) with \(w_1, w_2 \in M_\iota\) for every \(\iota \mid p\).  Then \(\Gamma_1(w) = p^2\, \Gamma_\vartheta(w)\), and \(\lvert \Gamma_1(w) \rvert_\iota \le p^{-2}\) for every \(\iota \mid p\).
\end{problem}

Problem~\ref{pr:toy-gain} gives divisibility by \(p^2\) at every \(\iota\mid p\).  Section~\ref{sec:each} compares the gains from different choices of twist.

\section{Index, covolume and the inequality}\label{sec:general}

Fix a twisted lattice on \(V\) with twists \(\mathrm{Tw}\).  Heights of families are those of Section~\ref{c5-sec:heights}.  We use two facts from there: Problem~\ref{c5-pr:height-mul}, that the product of families of heights at most \(a\) and \(a'\) has height at most \(a+a'\), and the \(m\)-th power of a family of height at most \(a\) has height at most \(ma\) for every integer \(m\ge0\); and the product formula for families, Theorem~\ref{c5-thm:product}: if \(z \in \C\), \(z \ne 0\), the constant family \(z\) has height at most \(c\), and \(\lvert \sigma z \rvert \le e^{c'}\) for every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), then
\[
  -c \le c' .
\]
Index and covolume are defined as in Section~\ref{c5-sec:index}, with two changes: \(M_\iota\) is now the module of vectors integral at \(\iota\) for every allowed twist, and sizes are read through \(\Theta_1\), with the coordinates \(b\) and the matrix \(G\) of \(W_1\).  Thus \(v_\sigma(y)\) is \(b(\Theta_1 y)\) with \(\sigma\) applied to each coordinate, \(N(y, \sigma) = v_\sigma(y)^* G\, v_\sigma(y)\), a real number \(\ge 0\) because \(G\) is positive definite as a complex matrix, so that the real part in Chapter~\ref{c5-chap} may be omitted, and \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) if \(\lvert \det(v_\sigma(y_i)^* G\, v_\sigma(y_k))_{i,k} \rvert \le e^c\) for every \(\sigma\).  A family \(y\) has index at most \(\delta\) if there is a function \(e\colon \mathbb{N} \to \R\), nonnegative everywhere, with \(\sum_{\ell \in U} e(\ell) \log \ell \le \delta\) for every finite set \(U\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) some \(u \in M_n(\Oc_\iota)\) \emph{carries \(y\) into \(M_\iota\) with loss} \(e(\ell)\): \(\sum_k u_{ik} y_k \in M_\iota\) for every \(i\), and \(\lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \ge \ell^{-e(\ell)}\).  The matrix may depend on \(\iota\) but not on the twist.  For the untwisted coordinates, the conjugate bound of Problem~\ref{c5-pr:cs-det} holds for every twisted lattice, with the same proof: the modules \(\Lambda_{\vartheta,\iota}\) play no role.  If \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\), then
\[
  \lvert \sigma(\Gamma_1(y)) \rvert \le e^c \quad \text{for every } \sigma \in \Aut(\C).
\]

Let \(y_1,\dots,y_n\) and \(z_1,\dots,z_{n'}\) be vectors in \(V\).  In Problems~\ref{pr:val}--\ref{pr:index-height}, assume that \(V\) is a complex inner product space.  Write \(y \sqcup z\) for the family \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'}\), and \(\lvert\gamma\rvert^2\) for the complex number \(\bar\gamma\gamma\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:val}
Let \(\vartheta\) be allowed at \(\ell\), let \(\iota \mid \ell\), let \(w_1, \dots, w_m \in V\), and let \(u, u' \in M_m(\C)\) be such that \(\sum_k u_{ik} \Theta_\vartheta w_k \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) and \(\sum_k u'_{ik} \Theta_\vartheta w_k \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\).  Then
\[
  \lvert \det u' \rvert_{\bar\iota} \, \lvert \Gamma_\vartheta(w) \rvert_\iota \, \lvert \det u \rvert_\iota \le 1 .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:complete}
Let \(\vartheta\) be allowed at \(\ell\), let \(\iota \mid \ell\) and \(a \in \R\).  Suppose that \(u \in M_n(\Oc_\iota)\) carries \(y\) into \(M_\iota\) with loss \(a\), that \(u' \in M_n(\Oc_{\bar\iota})\) carries \(y\) into \(M_{\bar\iota}\) with loss \(a\), and that \(\gamma \in \C\) satisfies \(\gamma\, \Theta_\vartheta z_i \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota} \cap \Lambda_{\vartheta, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\).  Then
\[
  \bigl\lvert (\lvert\gamma\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_\vartheta(y \sqcup z) \bigr\rvert_\iota \le \ell^{2a} .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:index-height}
Let \(y\) have index at most \(\delta\).  For each \(\iota \mid \ell\) let \(\theta_\iota \in \mathrm{Tw}\) be allowed at \(\ell\) and \(\gamma_\iota \in \C\), with \(\gamma_\iota\, \Theta_{\theta_\iota} z_i \in \Lambda_{\theta_\iota, \iota} \cap \Lambda_{\theta_\iota, \bar\iota}\) for every \(i\).  Then the family \(\iota \mapsto (\lvert\gamma_\iota\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_{\theta_\iota}(y \sqcup z)\) has height at most \(2\delta\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:fischer}
If \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\), and \(N(z_i, \sigma) \le e^{h}\) for every \(i\) and every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), then \(y \sqcup z\) has covolume at most \(c + n' h\).
\end{problem}

Now let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space.  For \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\) let \(Z(\vartheta)\) be the square of the absolute value of the determinant of \(\Theta_\vartheta\), as a map \(V \to \Theta_\vartheta(V)\), in orthonormal bases, when \(\Theta_\vartheta\) is injective, and \(Z(\vartheta) := 0\) otherwise.  When \(W_\vartheta\) is finite-dimensional, \(Z(\vartheta) = \det(\Theta_\vartheta^* \Theta_\vartheta)\), as in Section~\ref{sec:toy}.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:factor}
Let all \(\Theta_\vartheta\) be injective, let \(y \sqcup z\) be a basis of \(V\), and let \(\gamma \in \C\), \(\gamma \ne 0\).  Then \(\Gamma_1(y \sqcup z) \ne 0\), and for every \(\vartheta \in \mathrm{Tw}\)
\[
  \Gamma_1(y \sqcup z) = (\lvert\gamma\rvert^2)^{n'}\, \Gamma_\vartheta(y \sqcup z) \cdot (\lvert\gamma\rvert^{-2})^{n'} \cdot \frac{Z(1)}{Z(\vartheta)} .
\]
\end{problem}

Problems~\ref{pr:index-height}, \ref{pr:fischer} and~\ref{pr:factor}, together with the height bounds and the product formula of Chapter~\ref{c5-chap}, give the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:twist}
Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, \(\mathrm{Tw}\) a set with a distinguished element \(1\), and \(L\) a twisted lattice on \(V\) with twists \(\mathrm{Tw}\) whose maps \(\Theta_\vartheta\) are all injective.  Let \(y_1, \dots, y_n, z_1, \dots, z_{n'} \in V\) be linearly independent, with \(\dim V = n + n'\).  For each \(\iota \mid \ell\), for every prime \(\ell\), let \(\theta_\iota \in \mathrm{Tw}\) be allowed at \(\ell\).  Let \(c, \delta, \cvol, \cshort, \cden \in \R\), and suppose that \(y\) has covolume at most \(c\) and index at most \(\delta\).  Let \(\gamma\colon \mathrm{Tw} \to \C\) with \(\gamma(\vartheta) \ne 0\) for every \(\vartheta\), and suppose that
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\gamma(\vartheta)\, \Theta_\vartheta z_i \in \Lambda_{\vartheta, \iota}\) for every \(i\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) for every prime \(\ell\), and every \(\vartheta\) allowed at \(\ell\);
\item \(N(z_i, \sigma) \le e^{\cshort}\) for every \(i\) and every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\);
\item the family \(\iota \mapsto (\lvert \gamma(\theta_\iota) \rvert^2)^{-1}\) has height at most \(\cden\);
\item the family \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) has height at most \(\cvol\).
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  0 \le c + 2\delta + n'(\cshort + \cden) + \cvol .
\]
\end{theorem}

Hypothesis~(1) concerns every twist allowed at \(\ell\), including \(\theta_\iota\) when it differs from \(\theta_{\bar\iota}\).  To recover Theorem~\ref{c5-thm:one}, give \(V\) the inner product in which its given basis \(y\) is orthonormal, and take \(\mathrm{Tw}=\{1\}\), with \(1\) allowed at every prime, \(n'=0\), \(\gamma=1\) and \(\cshort=\cden=\cvol=0\).

\begin{leanremark}
\raggedright In the Lean proof \cite{GL} (file \tl{grh/src/New/Reduction/Lattices/TwistedLattice.lean}), a twisted lattice is \tl{New.TwistedLattice}; the integral vectors, carrying with loss, index, covolume, \(N(y,\sigma)\) and \(Z(\vartheta)\) are \tl{integralVectors}, \tl{New.PrimePadicIso.Carries}, \tl{IndexLe}, \tl{CovolumeLe}, \tl{normSqAt} and \tl{volume}.  Admissibility is tested only at primes.  The function \(e\) is nonnegative on all of \(\mathbb N\), and only its prime values occur in the sums and the carrying bounds.  Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} is \tl{New.\allowbreak{}TwistedLattice.\allowbreak{}covolume\_\allowbreak{}add\_\allowbreak{}index\_\allowbreak{}nonneg}, whose \tl{b\_volume}, \tl{b\_short}, \tl{b\_denom} are \(\cvol, \cshort, \cden\).
\end{leanremark}

\section{A twist for each prime}\label{sec:each}

We first test Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} on the toy, then add a twist that is allowed at \(r\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:toy-check}
In the toy, let \(k \in \Z\) and \(s \ge 1\) an integer, and take \(n = n' = 1\), \(y_1 = p^k e_1\), \(z_1 = s^{-1} e_2\), \(\gamma(1) = \gamma(\vartheta) = s\), \(\theta_\iota = \vartheta\) for \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell \ne r\), and \(\theta_\iota = 1\) for \(\iota \mid r\).  The hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} hold with
\[
  c = 2k \log p, \quad \delta = \max(1 - k, 0) \log p, \quad \cshort = -2\log s, \quad \cden = 2 \log s, \quad \cvol = -2 \log p ,
\]
and then \(c + 2\delta + n'(\cshort + \cden) + \cvol = 2\max(k - 1, 0) \log p\).  For \(p = 3\), \(k = -1\), \(s = 2\): \(c = -2\log 3\) and \(\delta = 2 \log 3\).  For the constant choice \(\theta_\iota = 1\), with the same \(\cshort\) and \(\cden\), the theorem gives only \(c + 2\delta \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

The \emph{second toy} adds to the toy a twist \(\vartheta'\): \(\mathrm{Tw} = \{1, \vartheta, \vartheta'\}\), \(W_{\vartheta'} = \C^2\), \(\Theta_{\vartheta'} = \diag(1, r^{-1})\), \(\Lambda_{\vartheta', \iota} = \Oc_\iota^2\), and \(\vartheta'\) is allowed at every prime except \(p\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:two-twists}
The second toy is a twisted lattice, with \(M_\iota = p\Oc_\iota \oplus \Oc_\iota\) for \(\iota \mid p\), \(M_\iota = \Oc_\iota \oplus r\Oc_\iota\) for \(\iota \mid r\), and \(M_\iota = \Oc_\iota^2\) otherwise.  For \(y = (p e_1, r e_2)\), \(n' = 0\), \(\theta_\iota = \vartheta\) for \(\iota \mid p\), \(\theta_\iota = \vartheta'\) for \(\iota \mid r\), and \(\theta_\iota = 1\) otherwise, the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} hold with \(\gamma = 1\), \(\cshort = \cden = 0\), \(c = 2\log(pr)\), \(\delta = 0\) and \(\cvol = -2\log(pr)\), and the inequality is an equality.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:no-single}
In the second toy, suppose that \(\theta_\iota \in \{1, \vartheta\}\) for every \(\iota\).  If \(\iota \mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) has height at most \(c''\), then \(c'' \ge -2\log p\).  The same holds with \(\vartheta'\) and \(r\) in place of \(\vartheta\) and \(p\).
\end{problem}

So for \(y\) as in Problem~\ref{pr:two-twists}, a choice of twists with values in \(\{1, \vartheta\}\) or in \(\{1, \vartheta'\}\) yields through Theorem~\ref{thm:twist} at most \(c \ge 2\log p\) or \(c \ge 2\log r\), while the choice that depends on the prime yields \(c \ge 2\log(pr)\), which is attained.

Under the other hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist}, a height bound of \(-A\) for \(\iota\mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) gives \(c+2\delta\ge A-n'(\cshort+\cden)\).  The proof of Theorem~C (Chapter~\ref{tc-chap}) contains the sole application of Theorem~\ref{thm:twist}.  There \(V\) is the space \(T\) of target forms, and \(\mathrm{Tw}\) is the set of \(m\)-fold twists.  A twist \(\vartheta\) with modulus \(Q_\vartheta\) is allowed at \(\ell\) when \(\ell\) is prime to \(\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\).  Its volume \(Z(\vartheta)\) is the Gram determinant of the images of an orthonormal basis under the chain of lifts (Chapter~\ref{c37-chap}).  The height of \(\iota\mapsto Z(1)/Z(\theta_\iota)\) is bounded by combining the bounds for the single lifts of Chapter~\ref{c38-chap} with a height bound for the inverse scalar in the chain factorization of Chapter~\ref{c37-chap}.  The vectors \(z_i\) are chosen from the auxiliary forms of Section~\ref{c37-sec:aux} to complete \(y\) to a basis, and each \(\gamma(\vartheta)\Theta_\vartheta z_i\) is integral at every \(\iota\) at which \(\vartheta\) is allowed.

\section*{Hints}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:one-place}] Problem~\ref{c5-pr:iota}.
\item[\ref{pr:toy-lattice}] Problem~\ref{c5-pr:index-basic} for the pairing condition.  At \(p\) the twist \(\vartheta\) is allowed; elsewhere \(p\) is a unit.
\item[\ref{pr:gram-comp}] Write \(\Gamma(w) = \lvert \det P \rvert^2\) with \(P\) the matrix of \(w\) in an orthonormal basis.
\item[\ref{pr:toy-gain}] Problem~\ref{pr:gram-comp}; for \(\Gamma_\vartheta(w)\), the pairing condition at \(\iota\) and \(\bar\iota\).
\item[\ref{pr:val}] Form the matrix of pairings of the rows formed with \(u'\) against those formed with \(u\), in that order.
\item[\ref{pr:complete}] Block diagonal matrices.
\item[\ref{pr:index-height}] Take the multiplier \(1\).  The index gives matrices at \(\iota\) and at \(\bar\iota\), which lie above the same prime.
\item[\ref{pr:fischer}] Fischer's inequality, then Hadamard's.
\item[\ref{pr:factor}] Problem~\ref{pr:gram-comp} with target \(\Theta_\vartheta(V)\) and its induced inner product.
\item[\ref{pr:toy-check}] Problem~\ref{pr:toy-lattice} for the index.  For \(\cvol\) take \(H = p^{-2}\).
\item[\ref{pr:two-twists}] For \(\cvol\) take \(H = (pr)^{-2}\).
\item[\ref{pr:no-single}] Compare with the constant family \(p^2\) at every \(\iota\).
\end{description}
