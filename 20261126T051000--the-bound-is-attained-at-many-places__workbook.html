At a prime \(p\) prime to the norm of the twist, a test form uses a Taylor coefficient on twelve copies of the variables; multiplied by \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}\prod_\tau\lambda(\tau)!\) and divided by one \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\) independent of \(p\), it must have valuation at most the divisibility \(s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}\) predicted by the prime sum. How can one normalization serve all primes, when the partners \([-p^e]\tau\) of a place (Chapter 36) lower the prediction?

§78.1. One curve, one partner

Recall \(\mu(n) \le \gamma_p n\), \(\gamma_p = p/(p^2-1)\), for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) (Theorem 19.2.1).

For \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), \(w \ge 0\) and \(G \in \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}][[X_1, X_2]]\) with \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}s\), \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}t\), the valuation of \(\partial_s^{pw}\partial_t^w G(0,0)\) is at least \(\mu(pw) + \mu(w)\) (Problem 19.4.2). A non-integral series does better.

Problem 78.1.1. \(\partial_s^{pw}\partial_t^{w} e^{s^pt/p}\big|_{(0,0)} = (pw)!/p^w\), of valuation \(v_p(w!)\). For \(p = 3\), \(w = 2\) this is \(80\), a unit, while for every \(G \in \mathbb{Z}_3[\mathrm{i}][[X_1, X_2]]\), with \(X_1 = \operatorname{sl}s\), \(X_2 = \operatorname{sl}t\), the valuation of \(\partial_s^6\partial_t^2 G(0,0)\) is at least \(2\).

For a prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 8\) and integers \(a, w \ge 0\), Theorem 16.3.1 gives in particular \(v_p(t(a,w)) \ge \sum_{i \ge 1,\ i \text{ odd}} (\lfloor a/p^i\rfloor - w)_+\) for the moments of Section 15.1, where \(w\) is the order of the derivative in the partner direction.

Problem 78.1.2. \(v_{11}(t(27,1)) = v_{11}(t(38,2)) = v_{11}(t(49,3)) = 1\), and in each case the bound of Theorem 16.3.1 at \(p = 11\) is \(1\): in these three cases the theta function itself attains the bound.

Problem 78.1.3. At \(p = 3\): \(v_3(t(a,1)) = 6, 6, 9\) for \(a = 11, 19, 27\), while the bound of Theorem 16.3.1 is \(2, 5, 8\).

So a fixed theta function can miss. For each \(p\) and \(\eta\) the proof chooses twelve sections, one on each copy of the variables \((z_\tau)_{\tau\in\Phi}\).

§78.2. What the partners take away

From now on we use the data (Definition 36.3.1). Fix a prime \(p\) and a ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\). Then \(c_p\), \(I_p\) and the local prime sum \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(y,\sigma) = c_p \sum_{e \in I_p} (y/p^e - \kappa([-p^e]\sigma))_+\) are those of Section 57.1. Let \(\gamma_p := c_p\sum_{e \in I_p} p^{-e}\) (values in Problem 19.3.1). The total local prime sum is \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:stot}\tag{1} s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}:= \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) . \end{equation}\] By Problem 19.4.1, \(s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}\le \gamma_p\sum_\tau \kappa(\tau)\). The difference is what the partners take away.

Problem 78.2.1. \(\gamma_p \sum_{\tau} \kappa(\tau) - s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}= c_p \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} \sum_{e \in I_p} \min\bigl(\kappa(\tau)/p^e,\ \kappa([-p^e]\tau)\bigr)\).

At an inert prime the partners enter the theta function through couplings. The proof uses a fixed curve: the lattice \(\Lambda = \Omega_0\mathcal{O}_K\), with \(\Omega_0\) as in Definition 59.4.3, with the point \(t\) of Section 19.3, written \(t_0\) here, and \(a_1\) and the series \(y\) as there; let \(\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)\) be the area of \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\). Let \(\sigma_\Lambda\) be the odd entire function with \(\sigma_\Lambda'(0) = 1\) and \(\sigma_\Lambda(z + l) = \epsilon_l \exp\bigl(\pi(\bar l z + \lvert l\rvert^2/2)/\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)\bigr)\sigma_\Lambda(z)\) for \(l \in \Lambda\), where \(\epsilon_l = 1\) on \(2\Lambda\) and \(-1\) elsewhere. In this sheet let \[\mathrm{th}(z) := e^{-\pi z\bar t_0/\operatorname{area}(\Lambda)}\, \frac{\sigma_\Lambda(z + t_0)}{\sigma_\Lambda(t_0)} ,\] We read its Taylor series in \(\mathbb{C}_p\) through \(\eta\). For linear forms \(\mu_i\) on \(\mathbb{C}_p^{\Phi}\) and integers \(c_i \ge 0\) (\(i\) in a finite set) put \(\mathrm{th}_{\mathrm{fr}}(z) := \prod_i \mathrm{th}(\mu_i z)^{c_i}\), and for odd \(e\) let \(\Gamma_e := \sum_{\tau} z_\tau^{p^e} z_{[-p^e]\tau}\) be the coupling of each place with its partner.

Normal form.

Let \(p\) be inert in \(K\), and let \((w_i,c_i)_i\) be a tight frame of length \(M\) with forms \(\mu_i\) (Definition 78.3.1 below), so that \(\mathrm{th}_{\mathrm{fr}}\) is formed with the frame’s own integers \(c_i\). The proof shows that there are \(\varpi \in \mathbb{C}_p\) with \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/(p^2-1)\), integral \(\mathfrak l_{e,j}\) (\(e\) odd, \(j \ge 0\)) with \(\mathfrak l_{e,0}\) a unit, and an integral series \(W\) in the \(z_\tau\), such that \[\mathrm{th}_{\mathrm{fr}}(\varpi z) = W(z) \exp\Bigl(\frac{\varpi^{p+1}}{p} \sum_{e\ \mathrm{odd}} \sum_{j \ge 0} \mathfrak l_{e,j}\, \Gamma_e^{\,p^j}\Bigr) .\] Status: read from the proof.

Problem 78.2.2. A two-variable model: \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})\), \(p = 13\), variables \(z_1, z_2\), \(\operatorname{val}\varpi = 1/168\) and \(\mathfrak l_{1,0}\) a unit. Then \(13\) is inert in \(K\), \(\operatorname{val}(\varpi^{14}/13) = -11/12\), and the coefficient of \(z_1^{13}z_2\) in \((\varpi^{14}/13)\mathfrak l_{1,0}(z_1/\varpi)^{13}(z_2/\varpi)\) is \(\mathfrak l_{1,0}/13\), of valuation \(-1\); times \(13!\,1!\) it has valuation \(0\), whereas the rate of one variable in degree \(14\) is \(\gamma_{13}\cdot 14 = 13/12\).

§78.3. Six copies along an isotropic vector

Definition 78.3.1. We define a tight frame of length \(M\) to consist of elements \(w_i \in E\) and integers \(c_i \ge 0\), \(i\) in a finite set, with \(\sum_i c_i\,\tau'(w_i)\overline{\tau(w_i)} = M\delta_{\tau'\tau}\) for \(\tau, \tau' \in \Phi\), each \(w_i\) lying in the lattice \(\mathfrak a_0 := E \cap \alpha_0\hat{\mathcal{O}}_E\), \(\alpha_0\) the finite idele of the Weil data fixed with \(E\) in the proof. At \(\eta\) its forms are \(\mu_i(z) := \sum_{\tau\in\Phi}\eta\bigl(c_{\mathrm{per}}\overline{\tau(w_i)}\bigr)z_\tau\), where \(c_{\mathrm{per}}\) is the period factor of Definition 59.4.3 (not the \(\Omega\) of Chapter 36). The twelve copies are the variables \(z_{\tau,i}\), \(\tau \in \Phi\), \(1 \le i \le 12\), one coordinate of \(\operatorname{Hom}(E, E^{12})\) at each infinite place and each of the twelve basis vectors; a multi-index on them is written \(\mathbf h= (h_{\tau,i})\), and \(n\) copies are the variables \(z_{\tau,i}\) with \(i \le n\).

Used in: Problem 78.3.1

Tight frames.

There is a tight frame of length \(M\) with \(c_i \ge 6fb\) for every frame index \(i\) such that for every prime \(p\) and every \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\) some form \(\mu_{i_0}\) has all its coefficients units. Status: read from the proof. Fix one; write \(\mathfrak o\) for the valuation ring of \(\mathbb{C}_p\), and \(\mathfrak o[[y_{\mathrm{fr}}]]\) for the ring of series \(G\bigl((y(\mu_i z))_i\bigr)\), \(G\) a power series over \(\mathfrak o\) in one variable for each \(i\).

Problem 78.3.1. For a primitive \(Q\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\) and embeddings \(\tau, \tau'\) of \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\), \(\sum_{j<Q}\tau'(\zeta^j)\overline{\tau(\zeta^j)} = Q\delta_{\tau'\tau}\), as in Definition 78.3.1 with \(c_i = 1\). For \(Q = 3\), \(v_j := (\zeta^j, \zeta^{2j})\): \(\sum_j v_jv_j^* = 3\cdot 1_2\).

Definition 78.3.2. Let \(n \in \mathbb{N}\), \(\nu\) a multi-index on \(\Phi\) and \(\rho \in \mathbb R\). We write \(\operatorname{lv}_n(\nu) \le \rho\) if there are \(H_1, \dots, H_n \in \mathfrak o[[y_{\mathrm{fr}}]]\) and multi-indices \(\nu_1 + \dots + \nu_n = \nu\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^n \operatorname{val}\bigl([z^{\nu_i}](H_i\,\mathrm{th}_{\mathrm{fr}})\bigr) \le \rho\).

Used in: Problem 78.5.2

The next problems use six scalings to make the product of the theta factors a unit of \(\mathfrak o[[y_{\mathrm{fr}}]]\).

Isotropic scalings.

Write \(\mathrm{th}= \exp(-g)\) with \(g(0) = 0\). For every \(\delta \in \mathbb{Z}_p\) the series \(\exp\bigl(\delta^2 g(z) - g(\delta z)\bigr)\) lies in \(\mathfrak o[[y]]\). Status: read from the proof. For \(0 \ne \alpha \in \mathcal{O}_K\), multiplication by \(\alpha\) is an isogeny of degree \(\alpha\bar\alpha\) of \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) (complex multiplication; Silverman, Advanced Topics in the Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves, Ch. II). For every \(\alpha \in \mathcal{O}_K\) the proof shows \(\mathrm{th}(\alpha z) = \mathrm{th}(z)^{\alpha\bar\alpha}v\) with \(v \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\), that is, \(\exp(\alpha\bar\alpha g(z) - g(\alpha z)) \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\); it then passes to \(p\)-adic limits of the pairs \((\alpha, \alpha\bar\alpha)\), among which are the pairs \((\delta, \delta^2)\), \(\delta \in \mathbb{Z}_p\).

Problem 78.3.2. \(1 + 70^2 = 29\cdot 13^2\); \((1, 70, 0, 0, 0, 0)\) is isotropic modulo \(13^2\); there is \(\delta_2 \in \mathbb{Z}_{13}\) with \(\delta_2 \equiv 70 \pmod{169}\) and \(\delta_2^2 = -1\). For every prime \(p\) there are \(\delta_2, \dots, \delta_5 \in \mathbb{Z}_p\) such that \(\delta := (1, \delta_2, \delta_3, \delta_4, \delta_5, 0)\) has \(\sum_{i=1}^6 \delta_i^2 = 0\).

Problem 78.3.3. If \(\delta \in \mathbb{Z}_p^6\) and \(\sum_i\delta_i^2 = 0\), then \(\prod_{i}\mathrm{th}(\delta_i z)\) lies in \(\mathfrak o[[y]]\) with constant term \(1\), and \(\prod_i \mathrm{th}_{\mathrm{fr}}(\delta_i z)\) is a unit of \(\mathfrak o[[y_{\mathrm{fr}}]]\).

Use the unscaled model of Section 61.3. Its one-variable series is \(\mathrm{th}_0=\exp(-g_0)\), and \(t_{\mathrm{for}}(u):=\wp_0'(u)/14=u+O(u^3)\) is its formal parameter. Write \(T(z_1,z_2)\) for the two-place product there.

Problem 78.3.4. At \(p=3\), let \(\delta_3^2=-2\) with \(\delta_3\equiv4\pmod 9\). The vector \((1,1,\delta_3,0,0,0)\) is isotropic. In the coordinate \(u\) its product is \[\mathrm{th}_0(u)^2\mathrm{th}_0(\delta_3 u) =1-\tfrac72 u^4+\tfrac{49}{20}u^6+\tfrac7{32}u^8+O(u^{10}).\] By machine the coefficients in \(t_{\mathrm{for}}\) are \(3\)-integral through degree \(43\), while \([u^4]\mathrm{th}_0=-1/12\).

Problem 78.3.5. At \(p=5\), the model product \(T\) has \(\partial_1^6 T(0)=0\). Let \(\delta_2^2=-1\) with \(\delta_2\equiv7\pmod{25}\). Then \(U(z):=T(z)T(\delta_2 z)\) is \(5\)-integral through degree eight and its terms of degree four are \(-\tfrac7{24}(z_1^4+z_2^4)-\tfrac74 z_1^2z_2^2\). Set \(H(z):=t_{\mathrm{for}}(\mu_1(z))^2\), where \(\mu_1(z)=(z_1+z_2)/2\). The prime \(5\) is inert in the CM field of the model, so its rate is \(5/(5^2-1)=5/24\). Then \(\operatorname{val}(6![z_1^6]H)=1\le 6\cdot(5/24)=5/4\). In the coefficient expansion of \(H=(H/U)U\), the products contributing to \([z_1^6]H\) from the splittings \(6=2+4\) and \(6=6+0\) both have valuation \(0\le 5/4-\operatorname{val}(6!)=1/4\).

Problem 78.3.6. If \(\mu_{i_0}\) has unit coefficients, then \(\operatorname{lv}_6(\nu) \le \gamma_p\lvert\nu\rvert - \sum_{\tau}\operatorname{val}(\nu_\tau!)\) for every \(\nu\).

Two more six-copy bounds are read from the proof. At an inert prime \(p\), integral combinations of the forms give each coordinate times a unit, and then \(\operatorname{lv}_6(\nu) \le 0\) for every \(\nu\). At an inert prime \(p > 2\), from the normal form: let \(\Phi_0 \subseteq \Phi\), odd positive integers \(e_\tau\) for \(\tau \in \Phi_0\) such that no \((\tau_1, e')\) with \(\tau_1 \in \Phi_0\), \(e' \le e_\tau\) an odd positive integer and \((\tau_1,e') \ne (\tau,e_\tau)\) has \([-p^{e'}]\tau_1 = [-p^{e_\tau}]\tau\), integers \(j_\tau \ge 0\), \(\nu' := \sum_{\tau\in\Phi_0} p^{e_\tau}j_\tau[\tau]\), \(\nu'' := \sum_{\tau\in\Phi_0} j_\tau[[-p^{e_\tau}]\tau]\) and \(J := \sum_{\tau\in\Phi_0} j_\tau\); then \[\operatorname{lv}_6(\nu' + \nu'') \le \frac{J}{p-1} - J - \frac{\lvert\nu' + \nu''\rvert}{p^2-1} - \operatorname{val}(\nu''!) ,\] where \([\tau]\) is the multi-index with a single \(1\) at \(\tau\) and \(\nu''! := \prod_\tau \nu''_\tau!\).

§78.4. Near pairs

We now describe the pairs used at non-split primes \(p \le k\). Let \(l_0(s)\) be as in Definition 36.3.1.

Definition 78.4.1. Let \(p\) be a prime. Call a pair \((\tau, e)\) with \(\tau \in \Phi\) near at \(p\) if \(e \in I_p\), \(2e < l_0(s)\), \(\tau \in \Phi_b\) and \([-p^e]\tau \in \Phi_b\); its start is \(\tau\) and its end is \([-p^e]\tau\).

A model: the group \((\mathbb{Z}/13)^\times\) in place of \(\Phi\), \(p = 3\) treated as inert, the cutoff \(2e < 3\), and the heavy set \(\{1, 10\}\) in place of \(\Phi_b\).

Problem 78.4.1. The iterates of \([-3]\) of orders \(0\) to \(4\) from \(1\) and from \(10\) are \((1, 10, 9, 12, 3)\) and \((10, 9, 12, 3, 4)\); the only near pair is \((1, 1)\), with end \(10\).

Problem 78.4.2. With weight \(27\) on \(\{1, 10\}\) and \(1\) elsewhere, \(\sum_{\tau}\sum_{e\ \mathrm{odd}} (\kappa(\tau)/3^e - \kappa([-3^e]\tau))_+ = 8\), the only nonzero term being \(\tau = 10\), \(e = 1\); the near pair \((1,1)\) contributes \(0\).

Problem 78.4.3. Let \(p \le k\) be a prime with \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\). (a) Two distinct near pairs at \(p\) have distinct starts and distinct ends, and the end of a near pair is never the start of one. (b) If \(p = d\), there are no near pairs. Hence the near pairs at a prime \(p \le k\) satisfy the hypothesis on \(\Phi_0\) in Section 78.3.

§78.5. Twelve copies and the theorem

On twelve copies the proof combines the two kinds of six-copy bounds: at a split prime, Problem 78.3.6 on six copies and the constant coefficient in degree zero on the other six; at an inert \(p > k\), every place coupled with its partner for \(e = 1\) on six copies and the span bound on six; at a non-split \(p \le k\), the near pairs on six copies and Problem 78.3.6 on six. For \(\lambda\colon\Phi\to\mathbb{N}\) let \(u := \lceil 6b/k\rceil\) and \[\Pi_\tau := \prod_{i=1}^{u}\bigl((\lambda(\tau) - u)_+ + i\bigr),\] a product of \(u\) consecutive integers (in this sheet \(\Pi_\tau\) is this product, not the set \(\Pi\) of small non-split primes); the reserved \(u\) pays for the rounding. The proof shows: if \(\kappa(\tau) \le \lambda(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau\), then at every prime \(p\) and every \(\eta\) \[\operatorname{lv}_{12}(\lambda) \le s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}+ 2\sum_\tau \operatorname{val}(\Pi_\tau) - \sum_\tau \operatorname{val}(\lambda(\tau)!) .\] Status: read from the proof. The loss added to \(s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}- \sum_\tau\operatorname{val}(\lambda(\tau)!)\) is zero at split primes, \(\sum_\tau\operatorname{val}(\Pi_\tau)\) at non-split \(p \le k\), and \(2\sum_\tau\operatorname{val}(\Pi_\tau)\) at inert \(p > k\).

Problem 78.5.1. \(12\cdot13\cdot14\cdot15 = 32760\) has \(v_{13} = 1\). A product of \(u\) consecutive integers has \(v_p \ge \lfloor u/p\rfloor\).

Problem 78.5.2. If \(\operatorname{lv}_n(\nu) \le \rho\), then the \(H_j\) in Definition 78.3.2 may be taken to be monomials \(\prod_i y(\mu_i z)^{e_{j,i}}\) with \(\sum_i e_{j,i} \le \lvert\nu\rvert\).

Let \(\mathfrak m\) be any modulus of \(E\) (Definition 71.2.1) with underlying ideal \((\pi_M)\). For \(y_0 \in (\mathcal{O}_E/\mathfrak m)^{12}\), let \(\mathrm{Th}_{y_0}\) be the theta function (the \(\mathrm{th}_\varphi\) of Definition 59.4.3; in this sheet \(\mathrm{th}\) is the theta function of the curve) of the datum on \(\operatorname{Hom}(E, E^{12})\) with the Gaussian at infinity, the indicator of \(y_0\) at the places dividing \(\mathfrak m\), and the vacuum at the other finite places. For a vector \(F\) on \(\operatorname{Hom}(E,E^{12})\) write \(\mathrm{Jet}(F)\) for its jet series at scale \(\pi_Mc_{\mathrm{jet}}\); for \(F\) with the Gaussian at infinity, as the datum of \(y_0\), this is the Taylor series at \(0\), in the twelve copies, of \(z \mapsto \mathrm{Th}_F(\pi_Mc_{\mathrm{jet}}z)\), as in Definition 59.4.3. Write \(\mathrm{Jet}_{y_0}\) for this series for the datum of \(y_0\). Two facts are read from the proof. First, if \(3e_{j,i} \le c_i\) for all copies \(j \le 12\) and all frame indices \(i\), the product over the twelve copies of the series \(\prod_i y(\mu_i z)^{e_{j,i}}\,\mathrm{th}(\mu_i z)^{c_i}\) is \(\eta\) applied to \(\mathrm{Jet}(F)\) for a vector \(F\) on \(\operatorname{Hom}(E, E^{12})\) whose values at finite arguments are integral at \(\eta\) and whose theta function is entire and fixed by the signed translations along \(\pi_M\mathfrak a_0^{12}\), with \(\mathfrak a_0\) as in Definition 78.3.1. Second, for such \(F\), finite Fourier inversion writes \(M^{12f}F\) as a finite sum \(\sum_j c_j\cdot(\text{translate of the vacuum})\) such that \(\lvert\eta(c_jv_*)\rvert \le 1\) for every value \(v_*\) of the vacuum at a finite argument, and each translate is a sum over \(y_0\) of roots of unity times the datum of \(y_0\). Status: both are read from the proof; for algebraic theta functions see [BK]. Here the integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(E,E^{12})\) is \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}\) times the vacuum (Section 37.8). Also read from the proof: every conjugate under \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) of a value of the integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(E, E^{12})\) at a finite argument has absolute value at most \(\exp(v_0q_1^2)^{12}\).

Problem 78.5.3. Let \(y_1, \dots, y_N \in \mathbb{C}_p\), \(c_1, \dots, c_N\) with \(\lvert c_i\rvert \le 1\), and \(\lvert\sum_i c_iy_i\rvert \ge \epsilon_0 > 0\). Then \(\lvert y_i\rvert \ge \epsilon_0\) for some \(i\). For any modulus \(\mathfrak m\) of \(E\) with underlying ideal \((\pi_M)\), let \(c_*\) be any value of the integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(E,E^{12})\) at a finite argument (with \(\operatorname{val}(0) = +\infty\)). If \(\operatorname{lv}_{12}(\lambda) \le \rho\) and \(c_i \ge 3\lvert\lambda\rvert\) for all \(i\), there are \(\mathbf h\) with \(\sum_i h_{\tau,i} = \lambda(\tau)\) and \(y_0\) with \(\operatorname{val}\eta\bigl(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}[z^{\mathbf h}]\mathrm{Jet}_{y_0}\bigr) \le \rho + \operatorname{val}\eta(M^{12f}c_*)\).

Fix a nonzero value \(c_*\) of the integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(E, E^{12})\) at a finite argument, before any prime is chosen, and put \(c_{\mathrm{att}}:= M^{12f}\,c_*\prod_\tau \Pi_\tau^2\). If \(\lambda(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau\), then \(\lvert\lambda\rvert \le f(b + x^{42}) \le 2fb\), so \(c_i \ge 6fb \ge 3\lvert\lambda\rvert\).

Problem 78.5.4. If \(\lambda(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau\), then for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\) \[\log\lvert\sigma(c_{\mathrm{att}})\rvert \le 12f\log M + 2fu\log(2b + u) + 12v_0q_1^2 \le \frac{fmb}{k} .\]

Problems 78.5.2, 78.5.3 and 78.5.4, with the bound on twelve copies (to which Problems 78.2.1, 78.3.6, 78.4.3 and 78.5.1 contribute), the tight frame and the two facts on theta functions, prove the following theorem.

Theorem 78.5.1. Let \(\lambda\colon\Phi\to\mathbb{N}\) satisfy \(\kappa(\tau) \le \lambda(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau\). There is \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\in \mathbb{C}\), \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\ne 0\), with \(\lvert\sigma(c_{\mathrm{att}})\rvert \le \exp(fmb/k)\) for every \(\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})\), such that for every modulus \(\mathfrak m\) of \(E\) (Definition 71.2.1) with underlying ideal \((\pi_M)\), every prime \(p\), and every ring homomorphism \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_p\), there are a multi-index \(\mathbf h= (h_{\tau,i})\) on the twelve copies with \(\sum_{i} h_{\tau,i} = \lambda(\tau)\) for every \(\tau\), and a class \(y_0 \in (\mathcal{O}_E/\mathfrak m)^{12}\), with \[\operatorname{val}\,\eta\Bigl(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}\,c_{\mathrm{att}}^{-1}\prod_{\tau\in\Phi}\lambda(\tau)!\cdot[z^{\mathbf h}]\,\mathrm{Jet}_{y_0}\Bigr) \le s^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\mathrm{fin},p}.\]

Used in: Chapter 19; Chapter 79

For fixed \(\lambda\) the same multiplier \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\) works for all the indicated \(\mathfrak m\), \(p\) and \(\eta\). The witnesses \(\mathbf h\) and \(y_0\) may depend on all three. Chapter 79 applies the theorem with \(\lambda\) the row \(\lambda_P\) of a critical pair main for \(j\) (Definitions 77.2.1 and 77.4.1), which satisfies the hypothesis by Problem 77.4.2.

Taken as given: the normal form, the isotropic scalings, the tight frame, the six-copy span and coupling bounds, the twelve-copy bound, the vacuum bound and the two facts on theta functions. The next sheet needs Theorem 78.5.1 and (1).
