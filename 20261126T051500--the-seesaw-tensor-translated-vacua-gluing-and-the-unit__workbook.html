The Weil representation over the finite adeles in its Schrödinger and lattice models [We], [MVW, Ch. 2]. As in Chapter 54, \(\mathbf 1_j(\xi)\) is the finite datum of the lift \(Y_{j-1}\to Y_j\); \(\omega^X_j\) is the finite Weil action on the data of the lift \(X_j\to X_{j+1}\).

This chapter is the last of the four that prove the seesaw identity, Theorem 52.4.1, which Theorem A uses. Theorem 55.4.1 gives it on automorphic forms, for ramified source forms, with the tensor \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\), a weighted sum over the cuts of evaluated pair data, in place of a chain datum. Which scalar and which common denominator suffice to express \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\) in glued vacuum tensors \(\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\)? Three local facts answer this: Fourier inversion on a residue module, the lattice model of the vacuum, and a neighbour lattice at \(N\). One complex unit (a nonzero scalar, as in Problem 52.5.5) then works for all twists.

Throughout, \(\xi\) is a tame character and \(q:=\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi\). A finite adelic vector is integral away from \(q\) if its entries are integral at every finite place not above a prime dividing \(q\).

§56.1. Residue modules and Fourier inversion

Definition 56.1.1. Let \((w_\gamma)_{\gamma\in\mathcal Z}\) be vectors of a complex vector space, \(\mathfrak d\ge1\) an integer and \(\mathfrak s\ge0\) real. A vector \(z\) is a combination of the \(w_\gamma\), \(\gamma\in\mathcal Z\), with denominator \(\mathfrak d\) and size \(\mathfrak s\) if \(z=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal Z}r_\gamma w_\gamma\) with finitely many nonzero \(r_\gamma\in\mathbb{C}\), \(\mathfrak d\,r_\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{[q]}\) for every \(\gamma\) and, if \(Q_\xi=\varnothing\), \(\sum_\gamma\lvert\sigma(r_\gamma)\rvert\le\mathfrak s\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{C}\).

Let \(\mathfrak m\subseteq\mathcal{O}_E\) be a nonzero ideal, \(\mathsf A_{\mathfrak m}:=(\mathcal{O}_E/\mathfrak m)^n\), \(\psi_{\mathfrak m}\) a primitive additive character of \(\mathcal{O}_E/\mathfrak m\), \(\mathfrak y\cdot\mathfrak b:=\sum_k\mathfrak y_k\mathfrak b_k\), and \(\chi_{\mathfrak b}(\mathfrak y):=\psi_{\mathfrak m}(\mathfrak y\cdot\mathfrak b)\). For the next problem alone, take the additive group \(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}\) and characters with values in the fifth roots of unity of \(\mathbb{F}_{11}\).

Problem 56.1.1. The class of \(3\) in \(\mathbb{F}_{11}\) has order \(5\), and \(\sum_{l=0}^{4}3^{\,l(j-2)}\) is \(0,0,5,0,0\) in \(\mathbb{F}_{11}\) for \(j=0,\dots,4\).

At the places of \(\mathfrak m:=(\pi_M)\prod_{w\in Q_\xi}w\) (a modulus prime to its conjugate) the datum \(\mathbf 1_j(\xi)\) is the operator of the Schrödinger model attached to the point mass at the class \(\mathfrak i\in \mathsf A_{\mathfrak m}\) of the inclusion matrix, \(n=\dim Y_{j-1}\dim Y_j\), applied to the vacuum \(\mathrm{vac}\); at the other finite places it is the vacuum. Here \(\psi_{\mathfrak m}(u\bmod\mathfrak m)=\psi_0(\varpi_\xi^{-1}u)\) for integral finite adeles \(u\), with \(\varpi_\xi\) the generator idele of \(\mathfrak m\) of Definition 55.2.6 and \(\psi_0\) the self-dual character of the finite adeles; a residue function is read on the reduction at the places of \(\mathfrak m\), and is zero off the integral box there. The operator of \(\chi_{\mathfrak b}\), on vectors fixed by the integral translations at the places of \(\mathfrak m\) and their conjugates (as \(\mathrm{vac}\) is), is the Heisenberg translation \(\mathrm{Heis}(t_{\mathfrak b})\), where \(t_{\mathfrak b}\) is the complex conjugate of the part at the places of \(\mathfrak m\) of \(\varpi_\xi^{-1}\tilde u_{\mathfrak b}\), \(\tilde u_{\mathfrak b}\) any integral lift of \(\mathfrak b\).

Problem 56.1.2. The point mass at \(\mathfrak i\) is \(\lvert \mathsf A_{\mathfrak m}\rvert^{-1}\sum_{\mathfrak b}\psi_{\mathfrak m}(-\mathfrak i\cdot\mathfrak b)\,\chi_{\mathfrak b}\). Hence \(\mathbf 1_j(\xi)=\lvert \mathsf A_{\mathfrak m}\rvert^{-1}\sum_{\mathfrak b}\psi_{\mathfrak m}(-\mathfrak i\cdot\mathfrak b)\,\mathrm{Heis}(t_{\mathfrak b})\mathrm{vac}\), and for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{C}\) the absolute values of the \(\sigma\)-conjugates of these coefficients sum to \(1\).

Problem 56.1.3. Each \(\bar\pi_Mt_{\mathfrak b}\) is integral away from \(q\), and \(\#(\mathcal{O}_E/\pi_M\mathcal{O}_E)^n\) divided by \(\lvert \mathsf A_{\mathfrak m}\rvert\) is \(q^{-n}\in\mathcal{O}^{[q]}\). So \(\mathbf 1_j(\xi)\) is a combination of the \(\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})\mathrm{vac}\), over the \({\mathfrak{z}}\) with \(\bar\pi_M{\mathfrak{z}}\) integral away from \(q\), with denominator \(\#(\mathcal{O}_E/\pi_M\mathcal{O}_E)^n\) and size \(1\).

§56.2. The lattice model

Let \(V\) be \(\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{j-1},Y_j)\) or \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_j,X_{j+1})\), with its standard lattice \(\Lambda\) in the finite adelic frame of Section 37.3, finite Heisenberg translations \(\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})\), Weil representation \(\omega\) and vacuum \(\mathrm{vac}\). For background on the Weil representation and its models see [We], [MVW, Ch. 2]; we use the following, in the normalization of [GL]. (L1) \(\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})\mathrm{vac}=\mathrm{vac}\) for \({\mathfrak{z}}\in\Lambda\), and the vectors of the finite part fixed by all these translations are the multiples of \(\mathrm{vac}\) (compare Problem 12.2.1). (L2) \(\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}}')=\zeta\,\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}}+{\mathfrak{z}}')\), where \(\zeta\) is a root of unity depending only on \(\sum_k\bar{\mathfrak{z}}'_k{\mathfrak{z}}_k\) and equal to \(1\) when this sum is \(0\). (L3) \(\omega(h)\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})=\mathrm{Heis}(h{\mathfrak{z}})\,\omega(h)\) for \(h\in U(V)(\mathbb A_f)\). (L4) \(\omega(h)\mathrm{vac}=c_h\sum_{{\mathfrak{z}}\in\Lambda/(\Lambda\cap h^{-1}\Lambda)}\mathrm{Heis}(h{\mathfrak{z}})\mathrm{vac}\) with \(c_h\ne0\); compare the intertwiner defined in Section 12.3 and evaluated in Problem 12.3.4. The scalar \(c_h\) is the Weil multiplier divided by its squared absolute value. If \(h\) is integral away from \(q\), then \(\lvert\eta(c_h)\rvert_\ell=1\) for every prime \(\ell\nmid q\) and every ring map \(\eta\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}_\ell\); if \(h\) is integral at every finite place, then \(c_h=1\). Status: we use the classical lattice model in the normalization of [GL], which proves (L1)–(L4) and the multiplier bounds.

Problem 56.2.1. Let \(h\in U(V)(\mathbb A_f)\). A vector \(f\) of the finite part with \(\mathrm{Heis}(h{\mathfrak{z}})f=f\) for all \({\mathfrak{z}}\in\Lambda\) is a multiple of \(\omega(h)\mathrm{vac}\).

Problem 56.2.2. Let \(h\in U(V)(\mathbb A_f)\) be integral away from \(q\), and let \(\bar\pi_M{\mathfrak{z}}_0\) be integral away from \(q\). Then \(\omega(h)\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}}_0)\mathrm{vac}\) is a combination of the \(\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})\mathrm{vac}\), over the \({\mathfrak{z}}\) with \(\bar\pi_M{\mathfrak{z}}\) integral away from \(q\), with denominator \(1\) and size \(1\).

The action \(\omega_j\) on the data of the lower chain is that of Problem 56.2.2 times a determinant-character factor, which at points integral away from \(q\) has \(\ell\)-adic norm \(1\) for \(\ell\nmid q\) and absolute value \(1\) under every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{C}\). So the moved data \(\mathbf 1_j(\xi)^y\) at points integral away from \(q\) keep the denominators of Problem 56.1.3 and size \(1\). If the two translations become integral away from \(q\) after multiplication by \(\bar\pi_M\), the join of their translated vacua is a root of unity times a translated vacuum on \(\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{2i+1},Y_{2i}^-\oplus Y_{2i+2})\), whose translation becomes integral away from \(q\) after multiplication by \(\pi_M\) on the first block and by \(\bar\pi_M\) on the second. Evaluation translates the joined datum by \(A_f\), takes its finite zero term along the isotropic part \(I\) of the splitting, and applies \(\boxtimes^{-1}\) on the definite spaces.

Problem 56.2.3. Let \(F\) be a nonarchimedean local field with integers \(\mathcal{O}_F\), \(W\) a finite-dimensional \(F\)-vector space with a nondegenerate symmetric or alternating bilinear form, \(L\subset W\) a lattice with \(L=\{x:\langle x,L\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{O}_F\}\), and \(I\subseteq W\) an isotropic subspace. Then the image of \(L\cap I^\perp\) in \(I^\perp/I\) is a lattice equal to its own dual.

Let \(L\) be the standard lattice of \(\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{2i+1},Y_{2i}^-\oplus Y_{2i+2})\) in its finite adelic frame. The proof identifies the reduced lattice with a finite adelic unitary image of the standard lattice in the quotient and deduces its self-duality for the pairing \((x,w)\mapsto\psi_0(\bar x^tw)\); Problem 56.2.3 is the analogue at one place. The image of \(L\cap I^\perp\) in \(I^\perp/I\), identified by the splitting with the finite adelic space of \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_{2i+1},X_{2i}^-\oplus X_{2i+2})\), is the zero-term lattice of pair \(i\). The zero term of the vacuum on the first Hom space is a number \(c_i'\ne0\) times the sum of the translates of the vacuum on the second by representatives of the zero-term lattice modulo its intersection with the standard lattice there.

§56.3. The neighbour at \(N\)

Which lattice is the zero-term lattice? Let \(P\) be the set of places \(w\mid\pi_N\) and \(\bar P\) the set of their conjugates; they are disjoint, with union the set of places above \(N\). Let \(\mathbf 1_P\) be the finite adele equal to \(1\) on \(P\) and \(0\) elsewhere, \(\pi_P\) the finite idele equal to \(\pi_N\) on \(P\), to \(\bar\pi_N^{-1}\) on \(\bar P\) and to \(1\) elsewhere, and \(u_{-1}\) a fixed unit of the integral finite adeles with \(u_{-1}\bar u_{-1}=-1\). For a pair \(i\) let \(e\), \(e''\) be the last basis vectors of \(X_{2i}\) and \(X_{2i+2}\) in \(X_{2i}^-\oplus X_{2i+2}\), and put \(e_P:=e+\bar u_{-1}\bar{\mathbf 1}_Pe''\). For columns write \(z^*:=\bar z^t\).

Problem 56.3.1. Over a commutative ring with involution, let \(z\) be a column with \(z^*z=1\), \(\delta\) a scalar and \(\gamma:=1+(\delta-1)zz^*\). Then \(\gamma z=\delta z\), \(\gamma z'=z'\) when \(z^*z'=0\), and \(\gamma^*\gamma=1\) if and only if \(\delta\bar\delta=1\). Moreover \(e_P^*e_P=1\) and \(\pi_P\bar\pi_P=1\).

Definition 56.3.1. Define the neighbour lattice of pair \(i\) as the image of the standard lattice of \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_{2i+1},X_{2i}^-\oplus X_{2i+2})\) under the matrix acting on the target as \(\gamma_P:=1+(\pi_P-1)e_Pe_P^*\) at the places above \(N\), and at the other finite places as the identity for \(i<\varrho\); for \(i=\varrho\), at these places it scales the last row of \(X_{m}\) by \(\varpi_M/\bar\varpi_M\) and fixes the other rows, \(\varpi_M\) the generator idele of \((\pi_M)\) of Definition 55.2.6.

For a local example take \(N=5\) and the split algebra \(\mathbb{Q}_5\times\mathbb{Q}_5\), with factors indexed by \(w\in P\) and \(\bar w\) and conjugation exchanging them; take \(\pi_N=(5,1)\), \(u_{-1}=(1,-1)\), \(\mathbf 1_P=(1,0)\), and keep the rows \(e\), \(e''\).

Problem 56.3.2. In this model \(e_P=\bigl((1,1),(0,1)\bigr)\), \(\pi_P=(5,\tfrac15)\), and the components of \(\gamma_P\) at \(w\) and \(\bar w\) are \(G=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}5&4\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(G'=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1/5&0\\-4/5&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), with \(G^tG'=1\).

To a residue modulo \(N\) at \(w\), with representative \(y\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}\), the proof attaches the translation supported at \(\bar w\) with entries \(u_{-1}\bar y/N\) in row \(e\) and \(-\bar y/N\) in row \(e''\), where \(\bar y/N\) is the conjugate of the adele equal to \(y/N\) at \(w\) and \(0\) elsewhere.

Problem 56.3.3. \(G\) is integral, and \(G'\mathbb{Z}_5^2+\mathbb{Z}_5^2=\mathbb{Z}_5^2+\mathbb{Z}_5\cdot\tfrac15(1,1)^t\); the five classes modulo \(\mathbb{Z}_5^2\) are those of \(\tfrac15(y,y)^t\), \(y=0,\dots,4\). The translation attached to \(y\) is \(-\tfrac15(y,y)^t\) at \(\bar w\), so one residue labels both rows.

Black box. For every pair \(i\) of the proof the zero-term lattice is the neighbour lattice; the lift of its columns is integral and orthogonal to the finite stacked map. We take the lattice equality and the two properties of the lift from [GL].

A representative of the neighbour lattice modulo its integral points is indexed by one residue modulo \(N\) for each basis vector of \(X_{2i+1}\) and each \(w\in P\), specifying both rows \(e\), \(e''\), and one class away from \(N\). Under the join on the definite spaces, \(\mathrm{Gl}_i\) corresponds to the sum of the Heisenberg operators of these residue translations. Let \(C_i\) be \(c_i'\) times the number of classes away from \(N\); it depends only on the data. The proof obtains: for \(n\) prime to \(N\) and \({\mathfrak{z}}\) on \(\operatorname{Hom}(Y_{2i+1},Y_{2i}^-\oplus Y_{2i+2})\) with \(\pi_M\) times its first block and \(\bar\pi_M\) times its second integral away from \(n\), the evaluation of \(\mathrm{Heis}({\mathfrak{z}})\mathrm{vac}\) is a finite sum of the vectors \(C_i\,\mathrm{Gl}_i(\mathrm{Heis}(t_1)\mathrm{vac}\otimes\mathrm{Heis}(t_2)\mathrm{vac})\), with \(\bar\pi_Mt_1\), \(\bar\pi_Mt_2\) integral away from \(n\), whose coefficients become integral away from \(n\) after multiplication by \(\#(\mathcal{O}_E/\pi_M\mathcal{O}_E)^{(n_{2i}+n_{2i+2})n_{2i+1}}\) and have \(\sigma\)-conjugates of absolute sum at most \(1\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{C}\).

§56.4. Invariance

Problem 56.4.1. Let \(\mathrm{sw}\) exchange the coordinates of \(\mathbb{Q}^2\). For \(v=(1,2)^t\) and \(v'=(3,-1)^t\), the sum of the matrices in the orbit of \(v{v'}^t\) under \(X\mapsto\mathrm{sw}\,X\,\mathrm{sw}^t\) is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&5\\5&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and is fixed by this action.

Problem 56.4.2. Let a group \(H\) act on finite sets \(\mathcal X_0,\dots,\mathcal X_{m-1}\) and linearly on a complex vector space; let \(\mathrm{mult}_j\colon\mathcal X_j\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\) be \(H\)-invariant and \(\mathcal E\) a function on \(\mathcal X_0\times\dots\times\mathcal X_{m-1}\) with \(\mathcal E(bc)=b\,\mathcal E(c)\) for \(b\in H\). Then \(\sum_c\prod_j\mathrm{mult}_j(c_j)^{-1}\mathcal E(c)\) is fixed by \(H\).

In the proof, let \(b_j\in K^X_j\) for \(j<m\) and \(b_m:=1\). For \(j<m\) the image of \(b_j\) in \(U(Y_j)(\mathbb A_f)\) lies in the finite Bessel subgroup, permutes \(\mathrm{Cut}_j(\xi)\) and keeps the multiplicities; and evaluation is equivariant: the evaluated pair datum at the translated points is \(\omega^X_{2i}(b_{2i},b_{2i+1})\otimes\omega^X_{2i+1}(b_{2i+1},b_{2i+2})\) applied to \(\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_i(\xi,y)\). By Problem 56.4.2, \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\) is fixed by \(\bigotimes_j\omega^X_j(b_j,b_{j+1})\) for all such \(b\).

§56.5. Denominators and sizes

Definition 56.5.1. Define the residue count by \(c_{\mathrm{res}}:=M^{fn_m^2}\).

Since \(q\) is prime to \(N\), the pair evaluation applies with \(n=q\). Thus for \(0\le i\le\varrho\) and points \(y\) integral away from \(q\), \(\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_i(\xi,y)\) is a combination of the vectors \(C_i\,\mathrm{Gl}_i(\mathrm{Heis}(t_1)\mathrm{vac}\otimes\mathrm{Heis}(t_2)\mathrm{vac})\), over \(t_1,t_2\) with \(\bar\pi_Mt_1\), \(\bar\pi_Mt_2\) integral away from \(q\), with denominator \(\#(\mathcal{O}_E/\pi_M\mathcal{O}_E)^{\mathfrak e_i}\) and size \(1\), where \(\mathfrak e_i:=\dim Y_{2i}\dim Y_{2i+1}+\dim Y_{2i+1}\dim Y_{2i+2}+(n_{2i}+n_{2i+2})n_{2i+1}\). The proof states this with the uniform denominator \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^4\).

Problem 56.5.1. Substituting \(1,2,3\) for the three \(Y\)-dimensions and for the three \(X\)-ranks in \(\mathfrak e_i\) gives \(16\le4\cdot3^2\). For the ranks of the proof, \(\mathfrak e_i\le4n_m^2\), so \(\#(\mathcal{O}_E/\pi_M\mathcal{O}_E)^{\mathfrak e_i}\) divides \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^4\).

Problem 56.5.2. Let \(z\) be a combination of the \(w_\gamma\), \(\gamma\in\mathcal Z\), with denominator \(\mathfrak d\) and size \(\mathfrak s\), and let each \(w_\gamma\) be a combination of the \(w'_{\gamma'}\), \(\gamma'\in\mathcal Z'\), with denominator \(\mathfrak d'\) and size \(\mathfrak s'\). Then \(z\) is a combination of the \(w'_{\gamma'}\) with denominator \(\mathfrak d\mathfrak d'\) and size \(\mathfrak s\mathfrak s'\). If \(z_k\) is a combination of the \(w_{k,\gamma}\) with denominator \(\mathfrak d_k\) and size \(\mathfrak s_k\) \((k<l)\), then \(z_{l-1}\otimes\dots\otimes z_0\) is a combination of the \(w_{l-1,\gamma_{l-1}}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{0,\gamma_0}\) with denominator \(\prod_k\mathfrak d_k\) and size \(\prod_k\mathfrak s_k\).

The integral vacuum of \(\operatorname{Hom}(X_j,X_{j+1})\) is \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\,n_jn_{j+1}}\mathrm{vac}\) (Section 37.8). Put \[c_{\mathrm{gl}}:=\prod_{i=0}^{\varrho}\frac{C_i}{c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\,n_{2i}n_{2i+1}}\,c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{\,n_{2i+1}n_{2i+2}}} ,\] a nonzero number. By Problems 56.5.1 and 56.5.2, for points \(y\) integral away from \(q\) the tensor \(\bigotimes_{i=0}^{\varrho}\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_i(\xi,y)\) is a combination of the \(c_{\mathrm{gl}}\,\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\), over the \(\mathfrak t\) with every \(\bar\pi_M\mathfrak t_j\) integral away from \(q\), with denominator \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}\) and size \(1\).

Problem 56.5.3. Each \(\mathrm{mult}_j(c)^{-1}\), \(c\in\mathrm{Cut}_j(\xi)\), lies in \(\mathcal{O}^{[q]}\), and if \(Q_\xi=\varnothing\), then \(\sum_{c_0,\dots,c_{m-1}}\prod_j\mathrm{mult}_j(c_j)^{-1}\le c_{\mathrm{res}}^m\). Hence \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\) is a combination of the \(\bigotimes_i\phi^{\mathrm{ev}}_i(\xi,c)\), over tuples \(c\) of cut points with representatives integral away from \(q\), with denominator \(1\) and size \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^m\).

The pair evaluation, the tensor bound above and Problem 56.5.3, followed by substitution (Problem 56.5.2), give the following theorem.

Theorem 56.5.1. There is \(c_{\mathrm{gl}}\in\mathbb{C}\), depending only on the data, such that for every tame character \(\xi\), with \(q=\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\xi\), the tensor \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\) is a combination of the \(c_{\mathrm{gl}}\,\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\), over the \(\mathfrak t\in\mathcal{T}\) with every \(\bar\pi_M\mathfrak t_j\) integral away from \(q\), with denominator \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}\) and size \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^m\): there are \(r_{\xi,\mathfrak t}\in\mathbb{C}\), finitely many nonzero, with \[\begin{gather*} \mathrm{ss}_\xi=\sum_{\mathfrak t}r_{\xi,\mathfrak t}\,c_{\mathrm{gl}}\,\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t),\qquad c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}r_{\xi,\mathfrak t}\in\mathcal{O}^{[q]},\\ Q_\xi=\varnothing\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_{\mathfrak t}\lvert\sigma(r_{\xi,\mathfrak t})\rvert\le c_{\mathrm{res}}^m\ \text{for all }\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathbb{C}. \end{gather*}\]

§56.6. The unit and the seesaw identity

By Theorem 55.4.1 and Theorem 56.5.1, for every ramified source form \(\varphi\) the composite of the lower lifts on automorphic forms applied to \(\mathcal{P}_{g,\lambda}\varphi\) is \(c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}/c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}\) times \(\mathrm{res}\,\Theta^X_\xi\bigl(\sum_{\mathfrak t}c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}r_{\xi,\mathfrak t}\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\bigr)\varphi\), and these coefficients lie in \(\mathcal{O}^{[q]}\). The scalar may be \(0\).

Problem 56.6.1. Let \(c'=2\), \(c_{\mathrm{gl}}=3\), denominator \(4\) and \(r=(\tfrac14,-\tfrac12)\). Applying Problem 52.5.5 to the scalar \(c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}/4\) gives \(u=\tfrac23\) and \(c=u^{-1}=\tfrac32\). The cleared vector \(\Psi=4r\) is \((1,-2)\) and satisfies \(c\Psi=c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}r=(\tfrac32,-3)\). If \(c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}=0\), the same identity holds with \(c=1\) and \(\Psi=0\).

Problem 56.6.2. Let \(\mathcal V\) be the set of finite places above the primes dividing \(Mq\), and \(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal V}\mathfrak t:=(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal V}\mathfrak t_j)_j\). If every \(\bar\pi_M\mathfrak t_j\) is integral away from \(q\), then \(v_j(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal V}\mathfrak t)=v_j(\mathfrak t)\) for every \(j\), so \(\mathrm{gl}(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal V}\mathfrak t)=\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\).

Used in: Problem 56.6.3

Problem 56.6.3. Let the tame character \(\xi\) be a twist \(\vartheta\), with \(c_{\mathrm{gl}}\) as in Theorem 56.5.1, coefficients \(r_{\vartheta,\mathfrak t}\) for this choice in Theorem 56.5.1, and \(\mathcal V\) as in Problem 56.6.2 for \(q=\mathrm{Nm}\,Q_\vartheta\). If \(c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}\ne0\), put \(\Psi(\mathfrak t):=c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}\sum r_{\vartheta,\mathfrak t'}\) over the \(\mathfrak t'\) with \(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal V}\mathfrak t'=\mathfrak t\); otherwise \(\Psi:=0\). Then \(\Psi\) is a chain datum for \(\vartheta\); for \(Q_\vartheta=\varnothing\) its size is at most \(c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}c_{\mathrm{res}}^{m}=M^{3fmn_m^2}\).

Used in: Chapter 52

Theorem 55.4.1, Theorem 56.5.1, the invariance of \(\mathrm{ss}_\xi\), Problems 52.5.5, 56.6.2 and 56.6.3, and the passage from automorphic to algebraic forms on \(Y_m\) prove Theorem 52.4.1, with \(c=c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}/c_{\mathrm{res}}^{2m}\) if \(c'c_{\mathrm{gl}}\ne0\) and \(c=1\), \(\Psi=0\) otherwise (compare Problem 52.5.6). Passage to algebraic forms on \(Y_m\) carries the restriction on the right of the automorphic identity to the restriction in \(B_{\vartheta,\Psi}\). Nothing is said about the size of \(c\).

Definition 56.6.1. For \(\mathfrak t\in\mathcal{T}\) let \(\mathrm{pl}_0(\mathfrak t):=1\in\mathbb{C}\) and \(\mathrm{pl}_{i+1}(\mathfrak t):=\bigl(v_{2i}(\mathfrak t)\otimes v_{2i+1}(\mathfrak t)\bigr)\otimes\mathrm{pl}_i(\mathfrak t)\) for \(0\le i\le\varrho\). Define the plain vacuum tensor by \(\mathrm{pl}(\mathfrak t):=\mathrm{pl}_{\varrho+1}(\mathfrak t)\).

If every \(\mathfrak t_j\) vanishes at the places above \(N\), then \(\mathrm{gl}(\mathfrak t)\) is the sum of the \(\mathrm{pl}(\mathfrak t+\mathfrak g_y)\) over the residues \(y\) of the gluings, \(\mathfrak g_y\) the tuple of their translations; Chapter 57 uses this expansion of the composite lift.

This proves Theorem 56.5.1, and with it the seesaw identity, from the statements taken as given: the Schrödinger and lattice models with the multiplier bounds, the zero-term lattice, its lift and the pair evaluation quoted above, the passage from automorphic to algebraic forms on \(Y_m\), and the statements marked “in the proof”.
