\title{The cost of one lift}

\paragraph{Prerequisites.}
From Chapter~\ref{sd-chap}: heights of families (Section~\ref{sd-sec:heights}, Problem~\ref{sd-pr:height-mul}).  From Chapter~\ref{at-chap}: sizes read at the trivial twist, valuations at a twist that depends on the prime.  The data of Chapter~\ref{da-chap} and the objects of Chapter~\ref{ob-chap}.  The factorization of Chapter~\ref{dch-chap}.  The statements of Chapters~\ref{l1-chap}, \ref{l2-chap}, \ref{l3-chap} and~\ref{l5-chap} recalled in Section~\ref{sec:general}.

\medskip
Chapter~\ref{dch-chap} expresses the ratio of the chain Gram determinant at an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\) to its value at the trivial twist as a product of single-lift Gram determinant ratios and a correction factor.  The determinant \(Z_j(\vartheta)\) uses the \(j\)-th lift at \(\vartheta^j\).  The proof needs a height bound for \(Z_j(1)/Z_j(\vartheta)\) when \(\vartheta\) varies with the prime \(\ell\) and the isomorphism \(\iota \mid \ell\).  The proof multiplies the Gram determinant of a single lift by the determinant of the compression of a second operator to the image of the lift.  The compression theorem (Theorem~\ref{l1-thm:compression}(a)) expresses this product as a fixed nonzero constant times the \(d_T\)-th power of the ratio of the Hecke value product at the twist to its value at the trivial twist.  Chapter~\ref{l1-chap} gives an archimedean bound for the compression determinant at the trivial twist and a local bound at a twist when \(\ell\) is prime to its modulus norm and a clearing bound is given.  The Hecke quotient is a power of the modulus norm times the square of a product of ratios attached to critical pairs (Chapter~\ref{l3-chap}).  For a regular family (Definition~\ref{l5-def:regular}) the inverse ratios have bounded height, with the estimate for the selected main factors in Chapter~\ref{l5-chap} using Chapter~\ref{l4-chap}.  This chapter deduces Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from these inputs and from the comparison operator of Theorem~\ref{l2-thm:operator}.  The inputs are deduced in Chapters~\ref{l1-chap}, \ref{l2-chap}, \ref{l3-chap}, \ref{l4-chap} and~\ref{l5-chap} from the background and the statements taken from the proof that each of them lists.

\section{The inputs}\label{sec:general}

We use the data of Chapter~\ref{da-chap}: the number \(f\) of infinite places, the scales \(x < x_1 < b\) and \(a = xb\), the number \(m\) of lifts, the ranks \(r\) and \(k\), the direction amount \(\hdir\) and the denominator budget \(B = U/(mk) = fa\log^3 b/(mk)\) (Definition~\ref{da-def:budget}); and the objects of Chapter~\ref{ob-chap}: twists \(\vartheta\) and \(m\)-fold twists, with modulus \(Q_\vartheta\) and \(\Nm Q_\vartheta\), the target dimension \(d_T\), the single lifts \(u_{\vartheta,j}\), and \(Z_j(\vartheta) = \det(u_{\vartheta^j,j}^*u_{\vartheta^j,j})\) for an \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\).

Fix \(1 \le j \le m\).  The Hecke value product \(\Hc_j(\vartheta)\) of lift \(j\) at a twist \(\vartheta\) is that of Definition~\ref{l1-def:hecke-product}.  Comparison operators \((\Upsilon_\vartheta)\), one operator on the target space of \(u_{\vartheta,j}\) for every twist \(\vartheta\), are defined in Definition~\ref{l1-def:comparison}, their compression determinants \(D_\vartheta\) in~\eqref{l1-eq:D}, and the statement that a real \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) clears the projection at \((\iota, \vartheta, j)\) in Definition~\ref{l1-def:clears}.  A family \(\theta = (\theta_\iota)\) of \(m\)-fold twists, one for each prime \(\ell\) and each \(\iota \mid \ell\), is \emph{regular} (Definitions~\ref{l5-def:small-ratios} and~\ref{l5-def:regular}) if: (i) \(\ell\) is prime to \(\Nm Q_{\theta_\iota}\); (ii) for every \(j\), every ramified critical pair \(P\) and \(\epsilon = \pm 1\), the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^j, \epsilon)^{-1}\) has height at most \(x_1\); (iii) for every \(j\) there are reals \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\), one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\sum_{\ell \in P_0}\mathrm{cl}(\ell) \le B\) for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes, such that for every \(\iota\mid\ell\), with \(\vartheta:=\theta_\iota^j\), \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) clears the projection at \((\iota,\vartheta,j)\) and, for every critical pair \(P\) main for \(j\) and any two test forms for \(\ell\) at \((P,\vartheta)\), the pairing of their projections to the line of the lift of \(P\) at \(\vartheta\) has \(\iota\)-absolute value at most \(e^{\mathrm{cl}(\ell)}\) (Definition~\ref{l5-def:regular}).  With (i) and (ii) alone the family has \emph{small ratios}.  Chapter~\ref{dp-chap} treats the denominators of the projection onto the image of a single lift and the sum of the clearing bounds in clause (iii).  Chapter~\ref{tw-chap} states the existence of regular families.

By Theorem~\ref{l2-thm:operator}, for every \(1 \le j \le m\) the operators \(\Upsilon_\vartheta := \mathcal N h^2\, g_\vartheta g_\vartheta^*\), one for each twist \(\vartheta\), form a comparison operator; here \(g_\vartheta\) is the gluing map of Definition~\ref{l2-def:glue}, \(h\) is the hook number of the weight of the tuples on which \(g_\vartheta\) is defined, and \(\mathcal N\) is the order of Section~\ref{ob-sec:chosen}.

By Theorem~\ref{l1-thm:compression}, for every comparison operator every \(D_\vartheta\) is nonzero,
\[
  \lvert\iota(D_\vartheta)\rvert_\ell \le e^{d_T r\,\mathrm{cl}(\ell)}
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \lvert\sigma(D_1)\rvert \le e^{3d_T fmb\log^2 b} ,
\]
the first for every twist \(\vartheta\), every \(\iota \mid \ell\) with \(\ell\) prime to \(\Nm Q_\vartheta\) and every real \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) that clears the projection at \((\iota,\vartheta,j)\), the second for every \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\); and by Problem~\ref{l1-pr:quotient}, for every \(m\)-fold twist \(\vartheta\),
\[
  \frac{Z_j(1)}{Z_j(\vartheta)} = \frac{D_{\vartheta^j}}{D_1}\Bigl(\frac{\Hc_j(\vartheta^j)}{\Hc_j(1)}\Bigr)^{-d_T} .
\]
By Problem~\ref{l3-pr:hecke-product}, for the fixed \(j\) there are a nonnegative integer \(e\), a list of \(k/2\) critical pairs (Definition~\ref{ob-def:pair}) main for \(j\) (Definition~\ref{l3-def:main}), and a list of \(5m\) ramified critical pairs with a sign \(\epsilon = \pm 1\) for each entry.  For every twist \(\vartheta\), the quotient \(\Hc_j(\vartheta)/\Hc_j(1)\) is \((\Nm Q_\vartheta)^e\) times the square of the product of the ratios \(R_P(\vartheta,1)\) from the first list and \(R_P(\vartheta,\epsilon)\) from the second.  By Theorem~\ref{l5-thm:main-pair}, for a regular family \(\theta\) and a critical pair \(P\) main for \(j\), the family \(\iota \mapsto R_P(\theta_\iota^j)^{-1}\) has height at most \(6B - \hdir\).

\section{The deduction}\label{sec:deduction}

A height bound \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c\) for a family \(y = (y_\iota)\) is as in Section~\ref{sd-sec:heights}; products and powers are treated in Problem~\ref{sd-pr:height-mul}.  We need two more operations.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:of-mul}
Let \(H \ne 0\) and \(\alpha \in \R\) with \(\lvert\sigma H\rvert \le e^\alpha\) for all \(\sigma \in \Aut(\C)\), and let \(e(\ell) \in \R\), of any sign, one for each prime \(\ell\), with \(\lvert H y_\iota\rvert_\iota \le \exp(e(\ell))\) for all \(\iota \mid \ell\) and \(\alpha + \sum_{\ell \in P_0} e(\ell) \le c\) for every finite set \(P_0\) of primes.  Then \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.tex#pr:of-mul

\begin{problem}\label{pr:height-ops}
If \(\mathrm{ht}(y) \le c \le c''\) and \(\lvert y''_\iota\rvert_\iota \le \lvert y_\iota\rvert_\iota\) for every \(\iota \mid \ell\) and every prime \(\ell\), then \(\mathrm{ht}(y'') \le c''\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.tex#pr:height-ops

Let \(\theta\) be regular and \(1 \le j \le m\), let \((\Upsilon_\vartheta)\) be the comparison operator of Theorem~\ref{l2-thm:operator}, and write \(\vartheta_\iota := \theta_\iota^j\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:delta-height}
The family \(\iota \mapsto D_{\vartheta_\iota}/D_1\) has height at most \(d_T(3fmb\log^2 b + rB)\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.tex#pr:delta-height

\begin{problem}\label{pr:hecke-height}
The family \(\iota \mapsto \bigl(\Hc_j(\vartheta_\iota)/\Hc_j(1)\bigr)^{-1}\) has height at most \(10mx_1 + k(6B - \hdir)\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.tex#pr:hecke-height

The upper bound on \(m\) controls the sum of the two height bounds.  In the one-sided bound for the prime remainder, \(m^2 \ge x\) gives the factor \(1/m \le x^{-1/2}\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:budget}
Let \(f \ge 1\), \(b > 1\) with \(\log b \ge 16\), \(x \ge 1\), \(0 < m \le 2\sqrt x\), \(r \ge 2m\), \(k = r + 1 - m\), \(1 \le x_1 \le b\), \(a = xb\) and \(B = fa\log^3 b/(mk)\).  Then for every real \(\hdir\)
\[
  3fmb\log^2 b + rB + 10mx_1 + k(6B - \hdir) \le k(B\log b - \hdir) .
\]
The data satisfy these hypotheses.
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.tex#pr:budget

Problems~\ref{pr:delta-height}, \ref{pr:hecke-height}, \ref{l1-pr:quotient}, \ref{pr:height-ops} and~\ref{pr:budget}, with Problem~\ref{sd-pr:height-mul} for the product and for the \(d_T\)-th power of the family of Problem~\ref{pr:hecke-height}, prove the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the data, every regular family \(\theta\) of \(m\)-fold twists, and every \(1 \le j \le m\), the family
\[
  \iota \longmapsto \frac{Z_j(1)}{Z_j(\theta_\iota)}
\]
has height at most \(d_T\, k\,(B\log b - \hdir)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:negative}
For the data, \(B\log b < \hdir\).  So the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is \(-d_Tk(\hdir - B\log b)\) with \(\hdir - B\log b > 0\).
\end{problem}
% End problem: 20261126T030800--the-cost-of-one-lift__workbook.tex#pr:negative

So the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is, per unit of \(d_T\), \(k\) times the budget \(B\log b\) less \(k\) times the direction amount.  Chapter~\ref{tc-chap} combines these bounds for \(1\le j\le m\) with the factorization of Chapter~\ref{dch-chap} and the height bound there for the inverse of the scalar whose \(d_T\)-th power is the correction factor.  The inputs are deduced in Chapters~\ref{l1-chap}, \ref{l2-chap}, \ref{l3-chap}, \ref{l4-chap} and~\ref{l5-chap} from the statements each of them lists as taken from the proof.

\section*{Hints}

\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:of-mul}] Only the primes with \(e(\ell) > 0\) cost anything.
\item[\ref{pr:height-ops}] Keep the multiplier \(H\).
\item[\ref{pr:delta-height}] Problem~\ref{pr:of-mul} with \(H = D_1\), which is nonzero; the two bounds recalled from Theorem~\ref{l1-thm:compression}; and the reals \(\mathrm{cl}(\ell)\) of clause~(iii) of Definition~\ref{l5-def:regular}, whose sums are at most \(B\).
\item[\ref{pr:hecke-height}] Problem~\ref{l3-pr:hecke-product} and Theorem~\ref{l5-thm:main-pair} for the \(k/2\) pairs of the first list, clause~(ii) of Definition~\ref{l5-def:small-ratios} for the \(5m\) of the second, Problems~\ref{sd-pr:height-mul} and~\ref{pr:height-ops}; by~(i), \(\Nm Q_{\vartheta_\iota}\) is a unit at \(\ell\).
\item[\ref{pr:budget}] Cancel the terms in \(\hdir\) and divide by \(kB\).  Use \(m^2 \le 4x\) and \(r \le 2k\).
\item[\ref{pr:negative}] \(\hdir \ge b\,\#\Phi_a\) (Problem~\ref{da-pr:hdir}), \(f = \#\Phi_a\,\varphi(q_0)\) (Problem~\ref{da-pr:degree}); compare \(\varphi(q_0)\,x\log^4 b\) with \(mk\).
\end{description}
