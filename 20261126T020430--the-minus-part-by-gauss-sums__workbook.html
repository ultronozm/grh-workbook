§28.1. Odd classes Tag 236V

For odd primes \(\ell\) and odd \(e\ge1\), Theorem 27.1.2 gives the length \(h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q})\) in terms of \(B_{e+1}/(e+1)\). The proof [GL] needs a bound of this kind over a CM field \(E\), for all \(\ell\) at once, and for cocycles with values in \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^n\). Which module is bounded, and by what?

Recall from Section 27.3 the CM field \(E\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\), \(G_E=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/E)\), complex conjugation \(c\), \(\chi=\chi_\ell|_{G_E}\), the groups \(D_\mathfrak{P}\supseteq I_\mathfrak{P}\) in \(G_E\) of a place \(\mathfrak{P}\) of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\), and \(\mathcal{O}_n=\mathcal{O}/\ell^n\mathcal{O}\). For \(e,n\ge0\), \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_\ell(E,\mathcal{O};e,n)\) is the \(\mathcal{O}\)-module of maps \(z\colon G_E\to\mathcal{O}_n\) such that (Definition 27.3.1)

  1. \(z\) is locally constant and \(z(gh)=z(g)+\chi(g)^{-e}z(h)\) for all \(g,h\in G_E\);

  2. \(z=0\) on \(I_\mathfrak{P}\) for every \(\mathfrak{P}\) above a prime other than \(\ell\);

  3. for every \(\mathfrak{P}\) above \(\ell\): \(z=0\) on \(I_\mathfrak{P}\) if \(e=0\), and \(z=0\) on \(D_\mathfrak{P}\cap\ker\chi\) if \(e>0\);

  4. if \(e>0\), then \(z(cgc)=-z(g)\) for all \(g\in G_E\).

We write \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{e,n}\) when \(\ell\), \(E\) and \(\mathcal{O}\) are fixed. For \(e=0\), Theorem 27.3.1 gives \(\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{0,n}\le v_\mathcal{O}(h_E)\) for every CM field \(E\). This chapter treats \(e>0\).

Tag 236W

Problem 28.1.1. Let \(R\) be a commutative ring with \(2\in R^\times\), \(M\) an \(R\)-module of finite length, and \(\sigma\) an \(R\)-linear involution of \(M\). Then \(M=M^{\sigma=1}\oplus M^{\sigma=-1}\) and \(\operatorname{len}M=\operatorname{len}M^{\sigma=1}+\operatorname{len}M^{\sigma=-1}\).

By Problem 27.3.2, for odd \(\ell\) and \(e>0\) the module of maps satisfying (a)–(c) therefore splits, when it has finite length, into \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{e,n}\) and the part where \(z(cgc)=z(g)\). Over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the odd cocycles are seen through characters. Let \(e>0\) and \(E\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mu_N)\); in this chapter \(N\) is such a modulus, as in Theorem 27.3.2, not the level of Chapter 36. Suppose that \(\mathcal{O}\) contains the values of all characters modulo \(N\). Write \(H=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_N))\). For an \(\mathcal{O}\)-valued Dirichlet character \(\psi\) modulo \(N\), put \(\Upsilon=\chi_\ell^e\cdot(\psi\circ\chi_N)\), where \(\chi_N\) is the cyclotomic character modulo \(N\). We define the component of \(\psi\) to consist of the locally constant \(\Upsilon^{-1}\)-cocycles \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathcal{O}_n\) that vanish at \(c\) and at the elements of \(H\) that lie in an inertia group at a place away from \(\ell\), or in a decomposition group above \(\ell\) with \(\chi_\ell(g)=1\); here inertia and decomposition groups are taken in \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\). The proof associates to each \(z\in\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{e,n}\) a cocycle in each component; if the associated cocycles vanish for every \(\psi\) with \(\Upsilon(c)=-1\), then \(4\varphi(N)z=0\).

§28.2. One integer for all characters Tag 2372

On the components of the critical characters an \(L\)-value is available. How can one integer serve for all the characters modulo \(N\) at once? Fix \(N,e\ge1\); for \(N=1\) the character modulo \(1\) takes the value \(1\) at \(0\). For \(0\le a<N\) put \[\beta_a=(e+2)!\,N^{e+1}B_{e+1}(a/N).\]

Tag 2373

Problem 28.2.1. For integers \(k\ge1\), \(N\ge1\) and \(a\), the number \((k+1)!\,N^kB_k(a/N)\) is an integer. In particular \(\beta_a\in\mathbb{Z}\).

Tag 2374

Problem 28.2.2. Let \(\chi\) be a Dirichlet character modulo \(N\), not necessarily primitive. Then \(\sum_{0\le a<N}\chi(a)\beta_a=-(e+2)!\,(e+1)\,N\,L(-e,\chi)\). For \(N=3\), \(e=1\) and \(\chi\) trivial both sides are \(-6\).

Tag 2375

Problem 28.2.3. For \(k\ge2\) and \(0\le s\le1\), \(\lvert B_k(s)\rvert\le4\,k!/(2\pi)^k\). Hence \(\lvert\beta_a\rvert<(e+2)!\,(e+1)!\,N^{e+1}\).

Let \(U_N=(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times\). For \(t\in U_N\) put \(\kappa(t)=2\beta_t+(-1)^e[t=1]+[t=-1]\), where \(\beta_t\) is taken at the representative of \(t\) in \([0,N)\) and \([\,\cdot\,]\) is \(1\) or \(0\) as the condition holds or not. Let \(T\) be the integer matrix with entries \(T_{s,t}=\kappa(t/s)\), \(s,t\in U_N\). In this chapter \(T\) and \(\kappa\) denote this matrix and this function.

Tag 2376

Problem 28.2.4. For each Dirichlet character \(\psi\) modulo \(N\), the vector \((\psi(t))_{t\in U_N}\) is an eigenvector of \(T\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda_\psi=\sum_{t\in U_N}\psi(t)\kappa(t)\), and \(\det T=\prod_\psi\lambda_\psi\).

Tag 2377

Problem 28.2.5. \(\lambda_\psi=2\sum_{0\le a<N}\psi(a)\beta_a+(-1)^e+\psi(-1)\). It equals \(2\sum_a\psi(a)\beta_a\ne0\) if \(\psi\) is critical for \(e\), and \(2(-1)^e\) otherwise. In particular \(\det T\ne0\).

The correction \((-1)^e[t=1]+[t=-1]\) does nothing on the critical characters and replaces the zeros of Problem 28.1.3 by \(\pm2\). One integer is divisible by every eigenvalue.

Tag 2378

Problem 28.2.6. Let \(R\) be a commutative ring, \(A\) a commutative \(R\)-algebra, free of finite rank as an \(R\)-module, \(z\in A\), and \(\Delta=\det_R(z\mid A)\). Then \(\Delta\in zA\). Hence every \(A\)-module killed by \(z\) is killed by \(\Delta\), and, with \(A=\mathbb{Z}[U_N]\) and \(z=\sum_t\kappa(t)\,t\), every \(\lambda_\psi\) divides \(\det T\) in \(\mathbb{Z}[\psi]\).

Tag 2379

Problem 28.2.7. For \(N=3\) and \(e=1\): \(\beta_1=\beta_2=-3\), \(\kappa(1)=-7\), \(\kappa(2)=-5\), \(\lambda_\psi=-12\) for the trivial character, which is critical, \(\lambda_\psi=-2\) for the other, and \(\det T=24\).

The eigenvalues are tied to Stickelberger sums by a character-weighted form of Voronoi’s congruence, as in the proof [GL]; for the classical congruence see [IR, Ch. 15, §2].

Tag 237A

Problem 28.2.8. Let \(u\ge0\) be an integer with \(u\equiv1\pmod N\), let \(F\ge1\) be prime to \(u\), and let \(\chi\) be a Dirichlet character modulo \(N\). Then, in \(\mathbb{Z}[\chi]\), \[(e+2)!\,(e+1)\,N\,u^e\sum_{0\le a<NF}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{ua}{NF}\Bigr\rfloor a^e\chi(a)\equiv(u^{e+1}-1)\sum_{0\le a<N}\chi(a)\beta_a\pmod F.\] For \(N=1\), \(e=1\), \(u=2\), \(F=5\) the two sides are \(168\) and \(3\); for \(N=3\), \(e=1\), \(u=4\), \(F=5\) and \(\chi\) trivial they are \(22320\) and \(-90\).

Tag 237B

Problem 28.2.9. Let \(\psi\) be critical for \(e\), let \(X\) be an integer divisible by \(\det T\), and let \(u\ge0\), \(u\equiv1\pmod N\). There is \(w\in\mathbb{Z}[\psi]\) such that for every \(F\ge1\) prime to \(u\), \[(u^{e+1}-1)X\equiv2w\sum_{0\le a<NF}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{ua}{NF}\Bigr\rfloor a^e\psi(a)\pmod F.\]

For fixed \(N,e\ge1\), the proof uses the integer \[X=\bigl((1+N)^e-1\bigr)\bigl((1+N)^{e+1}-1\bigr)\bigl((1+2N)^e-1\bigr)\bigl((1+2N)^{e+1}-1\bigr)\,\lvert\det T\rvert,\] so that for every prime \(\ell\) one of \(u=1+N\), \(u=1+2N\) is prime to \(\ell\) and \((u^e-1)(u^{e+1}-1)\mid X\). It bounds \(\lvert\det T\rvert\) through the entries of \(T\), using \(\lvert B_{e+1}(s)\rvert<17\,(e+1)!\) on \([0,1]\), and obtains the following.

Tag 237C

Theorem 28.2.1 (Stickelberger bound). Let \(N,e\ge1\). There is an integer \(X\) with \(1\le X\le2^{(N+e+3)^6}\) such that

  1. for every prime \(\ell\) there is an integer \(u\ge1\) with \(u\equiv1\pmod N\), \(\ell\nmid u\) and \((u^e-1)(u^{e+1}-1)\mid X\);

  2. for every integer \(u\ge0\) with \(u\equiv1\pmod N\), every integral domain \(\Lambda\) of characteristic \(0\), and every Dirichlet character \(\psi\) modulo \(N\) with values in \(\Lambda\) and \(\psi(-1)=-(-1)^e\), there is \(w\in\Lambda\) such that for every integer \(F\ge1\) prime to \(u\), in \(\Lambda\), \[F\ \Big|\ (u^{e+1}-1)X-2w\sum_{0\le a<NF}\Bigl\lfloor\frac{ua}{NF}\Bigr\rfloor a^e\psi(a).\]

For \(N=3\) and \(e=1\) this \(X\) is \(311040\).

§28.3. Gauss sums and the Euler system Tag 237D

How does an integer such as \(X\) bound a module of cocycles from above, without the main conjecture? The proof uses Kolyvagin’s method for Gauss sums, in Rubin’s version for class groups [Ru91], [La, Appendix], [Gr], with a Tate twist as in [BG]. At level \(\ell^n\), let \(u\ge1\) be an auxiliary exponent, \(P\) a finite set of primes \(q\equiv1\pmod{uN\ell^n}\), and \(p\) a prime congruent to \(1\) modulo \(N\ell^n\prod_{q\in P}q\). The Gauss sums at \(p\) give elements of known valuation. For \(S\subseteq P\) the derivative cocycle \(\kappa_S\) has character \(\chi_\ell^{e+1}(\psi\circ\chi_N)\); over \(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_N)\) it is unramified outside \(\ell\), \(p\) and \(S\); and at \(q\in P\setminus S\) its value at a Frobenius is a value of \(\kappa_{S\cup\{q\}}\) on inertia at the same place.

For \(x\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\) let \(\langle x\rangle\in[0,1)\) be its representative, and for an integer \(u\ge0\) put \[\vartheta_u(x)=\lfloor u\langle x\rangle\rfloor-\lfloor u\langle -x\rangle\rfloor .\] For \(F\ge2\) let \(\theta_F=\sum_a\langle a/F\rangle\,\sigma_a^{-1}\in\mathbb{Q}[(\mathbb{Z}/F)^\times]\), the sum over \(a\in(\mathbb{Z}/F)^\times\), be the Stickelberger element at modulus \(F\), extending the prime-modulus definition of Chapter 26.

Tag 237E

Problem 28.3.1. Let \(u\ge1\) be prime to \(F\). For \(a\) prime to \(F\), \(\vartheta_u(a/F)=2\lfloor u\langle a/F\rangle\rfloor-u+1\), and \(\sum_a\vartheta_u(a/F)\,\sigma_a^{-1}=2(u-\sigma_u)\theta_F-(u-1)\sum_a\sigma_a\). For \(F=5\), \(u=2\) the values at \(a=1,2,3,4\) are \(-1,-1,1,1\).

Fix a place \(\mathfrak{P}\) of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\) above \(p\), normalize its valuation by \(v_\mathfrak{P}(p)=1\), and form all Gauss sums \(g(\chi)\) with the same nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb{F}_p\). By Stickelberger’s factorization [Wa, Ch. 6], the characters \(\chi\) of \(\mathbb{F}_p^\times\) can be indexed by the points \(x\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\) of order dividing \(p-1\) so that \(j(\chi)=g(\chi)^u/g(\chi^u)\) satisfies \(v_\mathfrak{P}(j(\chi))=\lfloor u\langle x\rangle\rfloor\) and \(v_\mathfrak{P}(j(\chi)/j(\chi^{-1}))=\vartheta_u(x)\). The proof builds its elements of known valuation from these quotients. The next problem is a cyclic group-ring model for the derivative operator.

Tag 237F

Problem 28.3.2. Let \(\sigma\) generate a cyclic group of order \(M\), \(D=\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}i\sigma^i\) and \(\mathcal N=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}\sigma^i\). Then \((\sigma-1)D=M-\mathcal N\).

The Hilbert symbol over \(\mathbb{Q}\) gives a classical example of global reciprocity [Se, Ch. III].

Tag 237G

Problem 28.3.3. The Hilbert symbol \((-1,-1)_v\) is \(-1\) for \(v=\infty\) and \(v=2\), and \(1\) for odd \(v\). If \(a,b\in\mathbb{Q}^\times\) and \((a,b)_v=1\) for all places \(v\ne r\), then \((a,b)_r=1\).

The reciprocity law used in the proof is Artin reciprocity over \(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{Nm})\) at primes \(q\equiv1\pmod{Nm}\), where \(m\ge1\) kills the component cocycle. Let \(e\ge1\), \(\psi\) critical for \(e\), and \(X\) as in Theorem 28.2.1. Then \(4X^2\) kills the component of \(\psi\), an analogue of Stickelberger’s theorem; and if \(\ell>N+e+3\), Kolyvagin’s descent gives a family \((\gamma_i)_{0\le i\le v_\ell(X)}\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\) such that every cocycle in the component of \(\psi\) vanishing at all the \(\gamma_i\) is zero. For a CM field \(E\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mu_N)\) and \(\mathcal{O}\) containing the values of the characters modulo \(N\), it follows that \(Y_e=4\varphi(N)\cdot4X^2\) kills \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{e,n}\), and that for \(\ell>y^2\), where \(y\ge N+e+3\), the elements of \(G_E\) obtained from triples (a unit modulo \(N\), a character \(\psi\), an index \(0\le i\le v_\ell(X)\)) detect \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{e,n}\). For each \(E\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mu_N)\) and each prime \(\ell\), there is a finite set in \(G_E\) detecting all maps satisfying (a) and (b), simultaneously for all \(\mathcal{O}\), \(n\), \(e\); for every integer \(y\ge N\) with \(\ell\le y^2\), it has at most \(y^{20}\) elements.

Tag 237H

Problem 28.3.4. Let \(Z\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-submodule of the functions \(G_E\to\mathcal{O}_n\), killed by an integer \(m\ge1\), and let \(g_1,\dots,g_k\in G_E\) be such that the only \(z\in Z\) with \(z(g_1)=\dots=z(g_k)=0\) is \(z=0\). Then \(\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}Z\le v_\mathcal{O}(m^k)\).

If \(X\le2^{y^6}\), \(y\ge4\) and \(N\le y\), there are at most \(\varphi(N)^2(y^6+1)\le y^{20}\) triples. So for \(0<e<t\), every \(\ell\) and \(y=N+t+3\), \(\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_{e,n}\le v_\mathcal{O}(Y_e^{y^{20}})\), where \(X_e\) is an integer as in Theorem 28.2.1 for \(N\) and \(e\), and \(Y_e=4\varphi(N)\cdot4X_e^2\). One integer for all \(0<e<t\) is \(\bigl(\prod_{0<e<t}Y_e\bigr)^{y^{20}}\).

Tag 237J

Problem 28.3.5. Let \(N\ge1\), \(t\ge0\), \(y=N+t+3\), and let \(1\le X_e\le2^{(N+e+3)^6}\) for \(0<e<t\). Then \[\Bigl(\prod_{0<e<t}4\varphi(N)\cdot4X_e^2\Bigr)^{y^{20}}\le2^{4y^{27}}.\]

Theorem 27.3.1, Problems 28.3.4 and 28.3.5, Theorem 28.2.1 and the statements above prove Theorem 27.3.2, with \(X_{\mathrm{odd}}=\bigl(\prod_{0<e<t}Y_e\bigr)^{y^{20}}h_E\): one integer \(X_{\mathrm{odd}}\le2^{4(N+t+3)^{27}}h_E\) bounds \(\operatorname{len}_\mathcal{O}\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_\ell(E,\mathcal{O};e,n)\) by \(v_\mathcal{O}(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\) for every prime \(\ell\), \(2\) and the primes dividing \(N\) included, every \(\mathcal{O}\) as in that theorem, every \(0\le e<t\) and every \(n\ge0\).

§28.4. Examples, and the main conjecture Tag 237K

How much is lost? For odd \(\ell\) and odd \(e\ge1\), we can compare the eigenvalue for the principal character modulo \(\ell\) with the exact value of \(h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q})\) in Theorem 27.1.2. First we compute the \(37\)-adic valuations of the eigenvalues for all critical characters modulo \(37\) at \(e=1\). For \(N>1\) and \(\psi\) critical, \(\lambda_\psi=2\,(e+2)!\,N\,B^{(N)}_{e+1,\psi}\) with \(B^{(N)}_{k,\psi}=N^{k-1}\sum_{a=1}^N\psi(a)B_k(a/N)\), which is \(B_{k,\psi}\) when \(\psi\) is primitive of conductor \(N\). Let \(\omega\) be the Teichmüller character modulo \(\ell\).

Tag 237L

Problem 28.4.1. Let \(\ell=N=37\), \(e=1\), and \(\psi=\omega^j\) with \(j\) even, \(0\le j<36\). Then \(v_{37}(\lambda_\psi)=1+v_{37}(B_{2,\omega^j})\) for \(j\ne0\), and \(v_{37}(\lambda_\psi)=1\) for \(j=0\). It equals \(2\) for \(j=30\), \(0\) for \(j=34\), and \(1\) for the other \(j\).

At \(j=30\) the value reflects the irregular pair \((37,32)\) of Problem 26.2.8, through Kummer’s congruences [Wa, Ch. 5].

Tag 237M

Problem 28.4.2. Let \(\ell\) be odd, \(N=\ell\), \(\psi=1\) and \(e\) odd. Then \(v_\ell(\lambda_\psi)=v_\ell(B_{e+1})+1+v_\ell\bigl((e+2)!\bigr)\). For \(\ell=691\), \(e=11\) this is \(2\), while \(h_{691,11}(\mathbb{Q})=1\).

Used in: Problem 28.4.3
Tag 237N

Problem 28.4.3. If \(\ell\) is odd, \(e\) is odd and \((\ell-1)\nmid(e+1)\) (so \(\ell\ge5\)), the value of Problem 28.4.2 exceeds the value \(h_{\ell,e}(\mathbb{Q})\) given by Theorem 27.1.2 by exactly \(1+v_\ell\bigl((e+2)!\bigr)+v_\ell(e+1)\).

For each \(0<e<t\), one may take \(X_e\) to be the determinant multiple of Section 28.2; each eigenvalue for that \(e\) divides \(X_e\) in its character ring, and the witness above contains the squares \(X_e^2\) in a product raised to the power \(y^{20}\). Why is so coarse a bound good enough, and why does a bound at finite level replace the main conjecture? The proof applies Theorem 27.3.2 to choose a witness \(X_{\mathrm{odd}}\) for the data of Chapter 36. At every prime \(\ell\), \(v_\mathcal{O}(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\) bounds the lengths in that theorem for all sufficiently large coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\) in the tower of finite Galois subextensions of \(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_\ell/\mathbb{Q}_\ell\), ordered by inclusion. The denominator error at \(\ell\) includes \(t\,v_\ell(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\) (Chapter 80) [GL]. The main conjecture of Mazur and Wiles [MW] gives the formula of Theorem 27.1.2 for the rational Tate twists; the proof notes that for large \(\ell\) a bound of this kind follows from it, and proves only an upper bound at finite level. To bound the valuations \(v_\ell(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\) with weights \(\log\ell\) it uses the size of \(X_{\mathrm{odd}}\), by Problem 27.2.4: \[\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal L}v_\ell(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\log\ell\le\log X_{\mathrm{odd}}\le4(N+t+3)^{27}\log2+\log h_E\] for every finite set \(\mathcal L\) of primes. For the data, \(\log h_E\le x^{30}\), the sum is at most \(x^{2000}\), and \(x^{10^6}\le x_1\), \(x_1^{10^6}\le b\) [GL]. So the sum is at most \(b^{1/(5\cdot10^8)}\), and its contribution to the denominator errors, weighted by \(\log\ell\), is at most \(t\,x^{2000}\).
