Prerequisites.

The structure of \((\mathbb{Z}/p^c\mathbb{Z})^\times\) for a prime \(p\) [IR, Ch. 4, §1]. Linearity of expectation. From Chapter 3 we use the places \(\Phi\) of \(E\) and their labels, the reduction \(\mathrm{cl}\) of the label modulo \(q_0\), the place \([\nu]\) of an integer \(\nu\), the \(a\)-places \(\Phi_a\) and the \(b\)-places \(\Phi_b\) (Section 3.1), and Problems 3.1.3, 3.1.7 and 3.3.2. From Chapter 36, the admissible data. We write \(U_m := (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times\) for \(m \ge 1\).

In Chapter 3 a set \(\Phi_b\) of places of class \(-1\) modulo \(q_0\) was given the weight \(b\), and Problem 3.3.2 showed why it must be dense: if \(b > 1\) and \(\#\Phi_a \le \#\Phi_b\log b\), then \[R_{q_0}(x) \le \frac{\mathrm{rem}}{a\,\#\Phi_b} + \lvert R_{q_0}(x_1)\rvert + \log b\,\lvert R_1(x_1)\rvert .\] How can \(\Phi_b\) be chosen? The argument asks for a second property, which pulls against density: for every small prime \(\ell\), it restricts which signed powers of \(\ell\) can occur as ratios of labels of \(b\)-places after reduction modulo the prime-to-\(\ell\) part of \(q_1\). Behrend’s construction [Beh] meets both: lattice points on a sphere, written as digits in a large base, become exponents of one element of \(U_{q_1}\).

Throughout, for \(q' \mid q_1\) let \(\mathrm{cl}_{q'} \colon \Phi \to U_{q'}\) be the label reduced modulo \(q'\), so that \(\mathrm{cl} = \mathrm{cl}_{q_0}\); for a prime \(\ell\) let \(q_{(\ell)}\) be the largest divisor of \(q_1\) prime to \(\ell\). For the parameters \((q_0, d)\) and an integer \(s \ge 0\), Chapter 36 sets \[l_0(s) = 3^{\lfloor \log_3 s\rfloor + 4}, \qquad q_1 = q_0 \cdot 3\,l_0(s)\,2^s ,\] where \(\log_3\) is the logarithm to base \(3\), with \(\lfloor\log_3 0\rfloor\) read as \(0\). With \(e := \lfloor\log_3 s\rfloor\) this is the \(q_1 = q_0\,3^{e+5}2^s\) of Chapter 3, and Problem 3.1.3 gives \(\#\Phi_a = q_1/q_0 = 3l_0(s)2^s\). We write \(l_0\) when \(s\) is fixed. Exponents in group powers and congruences are integers, as are the exponents in prime-power moduli.

§4.1. Two requirements Tag 225S

The first requirement is size, measured against the class of \(-1\).

Tag 232J

Problem 4.1.1. Multiplication by \([-1]\) maps \(\Phi_a\) bijectively onto \(\mathrm{cl}^{-1}(-1)\), so \(\mathrm{cl}^{-1}(-1)\) has \(\#\Phi_a\) elements. In the toy example of Chapter 3, \(q_0 = 4\), \(q_1 = 16\), it consists of the places with labels \(3, 7, 11, 15\).

An admissible datum (Chapter 36) requires \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:density}\tag{1} q_1/q_0 \le \#\Phi_b \log b , \end{equation}\] so \(\Phi_b\) must contain at least the proportion \(1/\log b\) of \(\mathrm{cl}^{-1}(-1)\).

The second requirement concerns ratios of places. For \(\ell \nmid q_1\), the label of \([\ell]\) is \(\ell \bmod q_1\). For \(\ell \mid q_1\), the convention of Chapter 3 makes \([\ell]\) the identity. In both cases the sparsity condition compares the reduced label of a ratio with signed powers of \(\ell\) modulo \(q_{(\ell)}\).

Tag 232K

Problem 4.1.2. Let \(\ell\) be a prime, \(\sigma \in \Phi\) and \(i \ge 1\). (i) If \(\ell \nmid q_1\), then \(q_{(\ell)} = q_1\), and the places \(\tau\) with \(\mathrm{cl}_{q_{(\ell)}}(\tau/\sigma) = \pm\ell^i\) are the two places \([\pm\ell^i]\sigma\). (ii) If \(\ell^\nu \Vert q_1\) with \(\nu \ge 1\), then for each sign there are \(\ell^{\nu-1}(\ell-1)\) such places. For \(q_1 = 36\), \(\ell = 3\) this is \(6\).

The proof states the second requirement as follows. For a natural number \(L\), a finite set \(\mathcal{P}\subseteq \Phi\) is sparse for the primes up to \(L\) if for every prime \(\ell \le L\) and all integers \(0 < i < i' < l_0(s)\) there are no \(\sigma, \tau, \tau' \in \mathcal{P}\), distinct or not, and no signs \(\varepsilon, \varepsilon' \in \{\pm1\}\) with \[\mathrm{cl}_{q_{(\ell)}}(\tau/\sigma) = \varepsilon\,\ell^i \quad\text{and}\quad \mathrm{cl}_{q_{(\ell)}}(\tau'/\sigma) = \varepsilon'\ell^{i'} \quad\text{in } \mathbb{Z}/q_{(\ell)}\mathbb{Z}.\] An admissible datum requires \(\Phi_b\) to be sparse for the primes up to \(kt\), where \(k = r+1-m\) and \(t = 5r\), with \(m\) the number of lifts and \(r\) the rank of the target (Chapter 36).

Tag 232L

Problem 4.1.3. Let \(\mathcal{P}\) be sparse for the primes up to \(L\), let \(\ell \le L\) be prime, \(v \in U_{q_{(\ell)}}\), and choose a sign \(\varepsilon_\mu\) for every integer \(\mu\). Among any \(l_0\) consecutive integers \(\mu\), at most two have \(\varepsilon_\mu\ell^\mu v \in \mathrm{cl}_{q_{(\ell)}}(\mathcal{P})\).

Tag 232M

Problem 4.1.4. Let \(\mathcal{P}\) be sparse for the primes up to \(L\), let \(p \le L\) be a prime with \(p \nmid q_1\), and \(0 < i < i' < l_0\). For no \(\sigma\) are all three of \(\sigma\), \([-p]^i\sigma\), \([-p]^{i'}\sigma\) in \(\mathcal{P}\).

Used in: Chapter 19

The sparsity of \(\Phi_b\) is used in two places. Chapter 19 uses Problem 4.1.4 for \(\Phi_b\) when \(p \le k\) and \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\), in the sharpness argument for the divisibility of derivatives (Problem 19.4.3 and the paragraph before it). The condition \(\chi_K(p) \ne 1\) implies \(p \nmid q_1\), since every prime divisor of \(q_1\) divides \(q_0\) and Problem 3.1.7(i) applies. Chapter 85, Section 85.2, states the bound \(4f\,\mathrm{defect}_\ell\) per unit of rank for the local defect above \(\ell\); at primes \(\ell \le kt\) the local argument behind it uses sparsity to exclude a third \(b\)-place on a short arc. That chapter omits the local argument.

Why are the two requirements in tension? We shall look for \(\Phi_b\) among the places \([-g^n]\) for the integer \(g\) of Section 4.3, whose order modulo a power of \(2\) is \(2^s\) (Problem 4.3.2). Section 4.3 uses a set of exponents \(\mathcal{N}\subseteq [0, 2^s)\) with the following property: for all \(n_1, n_2, n_3 \in \mathcal{N}\) and positive integers \(\alpha, \alpha'\) with \(\alpha + \alpha' \le 2l_0\), the congruence \((\alpha+\alpha')n_2 \equiv \alpha n_1 + \alpha' n_3 \pmod{2^s}\) implies \(n_1 = n_2 = n_3\). For \(\alpha = \alpha' = 1\) this excludes nonconstant three-term progressions. The obvious sets without them are thin.

Tag 225W

Problem 4.1.5. For an integer \(\mu \ge 0\), let \(\mathcal{N}_\mu\) be the set of integers in \([0, 3^\mu)\) whose base-\(3\) digits are \(0\) or \(1\). (i) \(\mathcal{N}_\mu\) contains no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression, and \(\lvert \mathcal{N}_\mu\rvert = (3^\mu)^{\log 2/\log 3}\). (ii) For \(\mu \ge 2\), \(\mathcal{N}_\mu\) contains \(0, 1, 3\), and \(3 \cdot 1 = 2 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 3\), a relation with \(\alpha = 2\), \(\alpha' = 1\).

We compare the expected size of a random set with its expected number of progressions.

Tag 225X

Problem 4.1.6. Let \(o \ge 4\) be even, and let \(R \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/o\mathbb{Z}\) contain each element independently with probability \(\delta\). The expected number of pairs \((n, \eta)\) with \(\eta \notin \{0, o/2\}\) and \(n, n+\eta, n+2\eta \in R\) is \(o(o-2)\delta^3\). If \(\delta^2(o-2) > 1\), this exceeds the expected size \(\delta o\) of \(R\).

Used in: Problem 4.1.7
Tag 225Y

Problem 4.1.7. Let \(R\) be as in Problem 4.1.6. For each pair \((n, \eta)\) counted there choose one of \(n, n+\eta, n+2\eta\), and let \(R'\) be obtained from \(R\) by deleting the chosen points. Then \(R'\) contains no three distinct points \(n, n+\eta, n+2\eta\), and its expected size is at least \(\delta o - \delta^3 o^2\). For \(\delta \le (2o)^{-1/2}\) this is at least \(\delta o/2\).

For an admissible datum let \(o = 2^s\). Then \(o = m^{22}\), \(\sqrt{x} \le m \le 2\sqrt{x}\) and \((\log b)^{100} \le x\) (Chapter 36). The density guaranteed by Problem 4.1.7 is of order \(o^{-1/2}\) at best, and \((2o)^{-1/2} < x^{-5}\). For places indexed injectively by exponents modulo \(o\), (1) asks for an exponent density at least \(3l_0/\log b > x^{-1/100}\). The deletion estimate falls short of the required density by a power of \(x\). We now choose the exponents using lattice points on a sphere.

§4.2. Behrend’s construction Tag 225Z

Behrend [Beh] encodes lattice points of fixed squared length by their digits. Let \(D, H \ge 1\) be integers and \(\Sigma_\rho := \{v \in \{0, \dots, H-1\}^D : \lvert v\rvert^2 = \rho\}\).

Tag 2262

Problem 4.2.1. Let \(v, v', v'' \in \mathbb{Z}^D\) have \(\lvert v\rvert^2 = \lvert v'\rvert^2 = \lvert v''\rvert^2\), and let \(\alpha, \alpha'\) be positive integers with \((\alpha + \alpha')v' = \alpha v + \alpha' v''\). Then \(v = v' = v''\).

Tag 2263

Problem 4.2.2. Some \(\rho\) has \(\lvert\Sigma_\rho\rvert \ge H^D/(DH^2)\).

Fix \(\rho\) with \(\lvert\Sigma_\rho\rvert \ge H^D/(DH^2)\) and an integer \(Q \ge 1\). Let \(\beta := QH\), \(\operatorname{enc}(v) := \sum_{\mu=0}^{D-1} v_\mu \beta^\mu\), and \(\mathcal{N}:= \operatorname{enc}(\Sigma_\rho)\).

Tag 2264

Problem 4.2.3. If \(z_0, \dots, z_{D-1}\) are integers with \(\lvert z_\mu\rvert < \beta\) and \(\sum_\mu z_\mu \beta^\mu = 0\), then every \(z_\mu = 0\). In particular \(\operatorname{enc}\) is injective on \(\{0, \dots, H-1\}^D\).

Tag 2265

Problem 4.2.4. Let \(n_1, n_2, n_3 \in \mathcal{N}\), and let \(\alpha, \alpha'\) be positive integers with \(\alpha + \alpha' \le Q\). If \((\alpha + \alpha')n_2 = \alpha n_1 + \alpha' n_3\), then \(n_1 = n_2 = n_3\).

Tag 2266

Problem 4.2.5. For \(D = 3\), \(H = 3\), \(Q = 8\), so \(\beta = 24\), the sphere with the most points is \(\rho = 5\), with the six permutations of \((0, 1, 2)\), and \(\mathcal{N}= \{26, 49, 578, 624, 1153, 1176\}\). With \(Q = 6\), \(\beta = 18\), the same sphere gives \(\{20, 37, 326, 360, 649, 666\}\).

Tag 2267

Problem 4.2.6. Let \(o \ge \beta^D\) be an integer. (i) Every \(n \in \mathcal{N}\) satisfies \(n < o\). (ii) For \(n_1, n_2, n_3 \in \mathcal{N}\) and positive integers \(\alpha, \alpha'\) with \(\alpha + \alpha' \le Q\), the congruence \((\alpha + \alpha')n_2 \equiv \alpha n_1 + \alpha' n_3 \pmod o\) implies \(n_1 = n_2 = n_3\).

Problems 4.2.1–4.2.4 and 4.2.6 give the following theorem for \(D, H \ge 1\); the statement in the proof also covers \(DH = 0\). Here \(D, H, Q, o\) are natural numbers, and the quotient \(H^D/(DH^2)\) is taken in \(\mathbb{R}\), with value \(0\) when \(DH = 0\).

Tag 232N

Theorem 4.2.1. If \(0 < Q\) and \((QH)^D \le o\), there is a finite set \(\mathcal{N}\) of natural numbers such that \(n < o\) for every \(n \in \mathcal{N}\), \(H^D/(DH^2) \le \#\mathcal{N}\), and for all \(n_1, n_2, n_3 \in \mathcal{N}\) and all natural numbers \(\alpha, \alpha'\) with \(0 < \alpha\), \(0 < \alpha'\), \(\alpha + \alpha' \le Q\), the congruence \((\alpha + \alpha')n_2 \equiv \alpha n_1 + \alpha' n_3 \pmod o\) implies \(n_1 = n_2\) and \(n_2 = n_3\).

For \(Q = 2\), the first Exercise below gives the density lower bound \(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log o})\) of Behrend’s construction [Beh]; Shapira [Sh] extends such constructions to other linear equations. No construction gives a density bounded below by a positive constant independent of \(N\): by Roth’s theorem [Ro] a set of integers in \([0, N)\) without nonconstant three-term progressions has \(o(N)\) elements as \(N \to \infty\). The size condition we need is (1).

§4.3. From exponents to places Tag 232P

The exponents of Theorem 4.2.1 become places through one integer \(g\) prime to \(q_1\). Let \(w = v_2(q_0)\), so that \(2^{s+w} \mid q_1\); since \(16 \mid 18480\), \(w \ge 4\), and the problems below use only \(w \ge 2\). Following the construction of the proof, let \[g := 1 + 3l_0q_0, \qquad \sigma_n := [-g^n] \in \Phi \quad (n \ge 0).\] A toy of the same shape has \(q_0 = 4\) and \(l_0 = 3\) in place of the values of the proof, and \(s = 13\): then \(q_1 = 4 \cdot 9 \cdot 2^{13} = 294912\), \(w = 2\) and \(g = 37\). In the toy the prime \(3\) divides \(q_1\) but not \(q_0\), which nothing below uses, and “sparse” is taken with \(l_0 = 3\).

Tag 232Q

Problem 4.3.1. (i) If \(c \ge w \ge 2\) and \(y\) is odd, then \(1 + 2^w y\) has order \(2^{c-w}\) in \(U_{2^c}\). (ii) If \(c \ge 1\), then \(4\) has order \(3^{c-1}\) in \(U_{3^c}\). For example \(5^0, 5^1, 5^2, 5^3 \equiv 1, 5, 9, 13 \pmod{16}\), and \(4\) has order \(3\) modulo \(9\).

Tag 232R

Problem 4.3.2. The integer \(g\) has order \(2^s\) modulo \(2^{s+w}\). The places \(\sigma_n\), \(0 \le n < 2^s\), are distinct, and their labels are \(\equiv -1 \pmod{3l_0q_0}\), in particular modulo \(q_0\). In the toy, \(37\) has order \(2^{13}\) modulo \(2^{15}\).

Tag 232S

Problem 4.3.3. Let \(0 < i < l_0\) and \(n, n' \ge 0\). Then \(\mathrm{cl}_{q_{(2)}}(\sigma_{n'}/\sigma_n) \ne \pm 2^i\).

Tag 232T

Problem 4.3.4. Let \(\ell\) be an odd prime, \(0 < i < i'\), and \(n_1, n_2, n_3 \ge 0\). If \(\mathrm{cl}_{q_{(\ell)}}(\sigma_{n_2}/\sigma_{n_1}) = \pm\ell^i\) and \(\mathrm{cl}_{q_{(\ell)}}(\sigma_{n_3}/\sigma_{n_1}) = \pm\ell^{i'}\), then \[2i'\,n_2 \equiv 2(i' - i)\,n_1 + 2i\,n_3 \pmod{2^s}.\]

Tag 232U

Problem 4.3.5. Let \(\ell > 1\) be an odd integer, \(\lambda \ge 1\) and \(s \ge 0\). If \(\ell^\lambda \equiv 1 \pmod{2^{s+1}}\), then \(2^s \le (\ell + 1)\lambda\).

Tag 232V

Problem 4.3.6. In the toy, the places \(\sigma_n\) for \(n = 20, 37, 326, 360, 649, 666\) have labels \(153935\), \(39851\), \(74807\), \(243359\), \(190331\), \(43271\) modulo \(294912\). These are \(\equiv -1 \pmod{36}\), and the six places form a set sparse for the primes up to \(1364\), the largest \(L\) with \(6(L+1) < 2^{13}\).

Problems 4.3.2–4.3.5, with Theorem 4.2.1 for \(Q = 2l_0\) and \(o = 2^s\), prove the next theorem. It concerns the parameters \((q_0, d)\) and natural numbers \(s, D, H, L\), with the same convention for \(H^D/(DH^2)\) as in Theorem 4.2.1.

Tag 232W

Theorem 4.3.1. If \((2l_0(s)H)^D \le 2^s\) and \(2l_0(s)(L+1) < 2^s\), there is a finite set \(\mathcal{P}\subseteq \Phi\) with \(\mathrm{cl}(\sigma) = -1\) for every \(\sigma \in \mathcal{P}\), with \(H^D/(DH^2) \le \#\mathcal{P}\), and sparse for the primes up to \(L\).

It remains to choose \(D\) and \(H\) so that \(\#\mathcal{P}\) is large compared with \(q_1/q_0\).

Tag 232X

Problem 4.3.7. Let \(D \ge 1\) be an integer and \(W \ge 1\) real, with \(W \le 2^s \le W^D\), \((2l_0)^D \le W\) and \(3l_0 D 2^D \le W^3\), and let \(H\) be the largest power of \(2\) with \(H^D \le 2^s/W\). Then \(H \le W\), \((2l_0 H)^D \le 2^s\), and \(q_1/q_0 \le W^6 H^D/(DH^2)\).

With Theorem 4.3.1 this gives a set of places of class \(-1\) modulo \(q_0\), sparse for the primes up to \(L\), with \(q_1/q_0 \le \#\mathcal{P}\cdot W^6\), under the hypotheses of Problem 4.3.7 and \(2l_0(L+1) < 2^s\). If moreover \(W^6 \le \log b\), then \(\mathcal{P}\) satisfies (1) in place of \(\Phi_b\). This ends the choice of places. From Chapter 5 on, the next part develops the comparison of divisibility with size.

In the Lean formalization.

In the Lean proof [GL], sparsity is the definition New.Parameters.IsSparse (file New/Reduction/Data/AdmissibleData.lean), and Theorems 4.2.1 and 4.3.1 are New.exists_three_term_free and New.Parameters.exists_large_isSparse (file New/Reduction/Data/Existence.lean); the proof of the latter is the construction of Section 4.3.
