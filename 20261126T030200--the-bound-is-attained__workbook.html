§19.1. The least valuation Tag 235H

Chapter 15 proves a lower bound for the divisibility of derivatives on the lemniscate. Is it the truth, or does every function gain more? Throughout this section and the next, \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is a prime, \(q := p^2\), \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}]\), and \(D = d/du\) is the invariant derivation, so that \((D^n g)(0) = n!\,[u^n]\, g(\operatorname{sl}u)\) for \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) (Problem 15.2.3). Recall Theorem 15.4.1: for \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) and every \(n \ge 0\), \[v_p\bigl((D^n g)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ E_p(n) = \sum_{i \ge 1,\ i \text{ odd}} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{n}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor .\] Let \(\mu(n)\) be the least value of \(v_p((D^n g)(0))\) over \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\). How far is \(\mu(n)\) from \(E_p(n)\)?

Tag 235J

Problem 19.1.1. \(\mu(0) = 0\). For \(n \ge 1\) the least value \(\mu(n)\) exists and is attained by a power \(X^i\) with \(1 \le i \le n\): \(\mu(n) = \min_{1 \le i \le n} v_p\bigl(n!\,[u^n]\, \operatorname{sl}^i u\bigr)\).

Used in: Chapter 78
Tag 235K

Problem 19.1.2. For \(p = 3\): \(\mu(11) = 4\), while \(E_3(11) = 3\).

So the bound of Theorem 15.4.1 is not attained in every degree. The rest of this section shows that \(\mu\) is superadditive; Section 19.2 computes it at the powers of \(q\).

Tag 235L

Problem 19.1.3. Let \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\), and write \(g(X \oplus X') = \sum_{i, j} a_{ij} X^i X'^j\) with the group law \(\oplus\) of Problem 15.2.2. Then \(a_{ij} \in \mathcal{O}\), and for all \(n, n' \ge 0\) \[(D^{n+n'} g)(0) = \sum_{i, j} a_{ij}\, (D^n X^i)(0)\, (D^{n'} X^j)(0) .\]

Tag 235M

Problem 19.1.4. \(\mu(n + n') \ge \mu(n) + \mu(n')\) for all \(n, n' \ge 0\).

Tag 235N

Problem 19.1.5. Let \(\phi \colon \mathbb{N}\to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) be superadditive, and let \(\gamma' \ge 0\). If \(\phi(N) \le \gamma' N\) for infinitely many \(N\), then \(\phi(n) \le \gamma' n\) for every \(n \ge 0\).

§19.2. The powers of \(q\) Tag 235P

Let \[\gamma_p := \frac{p}{p^2 - 1} = \frac1p + \frac1{p^3} + \frac1{p^5} + \dotsb .\]

Tag 235Q

Problem 19.2.1. \(E_p(n) \le \gamma_p n\) for every \(n \ge 0\), and \(E_p(q^j) = \gamma_p (q^j - 1)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).

In the degrees \(q^j\) the function \(X\) itself attains the bound.

Tag 235R

Problem 19.2.2. \(v_p\bigl((D^{q^j}X)(0)\bigr) = E_p(q^j)\) for every \(j \ge 0\). (For \(p = 3\): \((D^9X)(0) = 3024\), of valuation \(3\); and \(v_3((D^{81}X)(0)) = 30\).)

Tag 235S

Problem 19.2.3. \(v_p\bigl([u^{q^j}]\, \operatorname{sl}u\bigr) = -(q^j - 1)/(q - 1)\) for every \(j \ge 0\). (For \(p = 3\): \([u^9]\,\operatorname{sl}u = 1/120\); for \(p = 7\): \(v_7([u^{49}]\,\operatorname{sl}u) = -1\).)

Thus \(\mu(q^j) = E_p(q^j) = \gamma_p(q^j - 1)\). Problems 19.1.4, 19.1.5 and 19.2.2, with Theorem 15.4.1, prove the following.

Tag 235T

Theorem 19.2.1. Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\). For every \(n \ge 0\), \[E_p(n) \ \le\ \mu(n) \ \le\ \gamma_p n ,\] and \(\mu(q^j) = E_p(q^j)\), attained by \(g = X\), for every \(j \ge 0\).

Used in: Chapter 78
Tag 235U

Problem 19.2.4. \(0 \le \gamma_p n - E_p(n) < h + 2\), where \(h\) is the number of positive odd integers \(i\) with \(p^i \le n\). Hence \(\mu(n) = \gamma_p n + O(\log n)\).

So \(\gamma_p\) is the exact rate, and Theorem 15.4.1 loses at most a logarithm in every degree. A computation of \(\mu(n)\) by Problem 19.1.1 gives, for instance,

\(p = 3\), \(n\) 9 10 11 20 27 40 80 81
\(E_3(n)\) 3 3 3 6 10 14 28 30
\(\mu(n)\) 3 3 4 7 10 14 29 30
\(3n/8\) 3.375 3.75 4.125 7.5 10.125 15 30 30.375

For \(p = 7\) one finds \(\mu(n) = E_7(n)\) for \(n \le 54\), and \(\mu(55) = 8 > E_7(55) = 7\). We have checked \(E_p(n) \le \mu(n) \le \gamma_p n\) by machine for \(p = 3, 7\) and \(n \le 100\).

§19.3. Curves with complex multiplication by \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\) Tag 235V

The proof works with curves whose complex multiplication is by \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\) for a prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), and with every prime \(p\), split, inert or ramified in \(K\). The lemniscate is not among these curves, since \(\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})\) is not of this form; it serves here as the simplest case. Let \(q_p\) be the norm of a prime of \(K\) above \(p\): \(q_p = p^2\) if \(p\) is inert and \(q_p = p\) otherwise. Let \(\pi_p := \sqrt{-d}\) if \(p = d\) and \(\pi_p := p\) otherwise. In valuations and congruences we use its image under a chosen ring homomorphism \(\eta \colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\); then \(c_p := v(\pi_p)\) is \(\tfrac12\) if \(p = d\) and \(1\) otherwise. Put \[\rho_p := \frac{c_p}{q_p - 1}, \qquad \gamma_p := \frac{1}{p-1} - \rho_p .\] For the lemniscate at \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) we used \(\gamma_p = p/(p^2-1)\); by Problem 19.3.1 the two agree at inert primes.

Tag 235W

Problem 19.3.1. \(\gamma_p = 0\), \(p/(p^2-1)\), \(1/(2(p-1))\) according as \(p\) is split, inert or ramified in \(K\). The inert value is the rate \(\gamma_p\) of Section 19.2.

Used in: Chapter 78

Let \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}\) be a lattice with \(\mathcal{O}_K\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\), let \(t \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(t \notin \Lambda\), \(2t \in \Lambda\), and \(\omega t, \bar\omega t \notin \Lambda\) for \(\omega = (1 + \sqrt{-d})/2\), and let \(a_1 \in \mathbb{C}\) with \(a_1^2 = 12\wp(t)\); assume the normalization \(g_2(\Lambda) = 12\wp(t)^2 - 4\). Let \(y(z)\) be the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(\tfrac12\bigl(\wp'(z + t) - a_1(\wp(z + t) - \wp(t))\bigr)\), with \(\eta\) applied to its coefficients; it begins with \(z\). Let \(\mathfrak o\) be the valuation ring of \(\mathbb{C}_p\), and \(\mathfrak o[[y]]\) the ring of series \(\Psi(y(z))\) with \(\Psi \in \mathfrak o[[W]]\). The proof establishes the following.

Tag 235X

Theorem 19.3.1. Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be prime, \(\Lambda\), \(t\), \(a_1\) as above, \(p\) a prime and \(\eta \colon \mathbb{C}\to \mathbb{C}_p\) a ring homomorphism. For every \(n \ge 0\) there is \(g \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\) with \[v\bigl(n!\,[z^n] g\bigr) \ \le\ \gamma_p n .\]

Used in: Chapter 78

In the Lean formalization.

In the Lean proof [GL] this is CMLattice.katz_divisibility_limit, in New/Derivatives/Sharpness/OneCurve.lean of the readable edition, stated with norms: \(\lvert n!\,[z^n]g\rvert \ge (p^{-\gamma_p})^n\). The Lean proof establishes it as in Sections 19.1 and 19.2: the greatest norm of \(n!\,[z^n]g\) over \(g \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\) is attained by a power of \(y\), it is submultiplicative in \(n\), and a lemma of Fekete’s type (Problem 19.1.5, in multiplicative form) carries a bound at the degrees \(q_p^j\) to all degrees. At those degrees it uses, in place of Problem 19.2.2, the part \(y_1\) of \(y\) in degrees \(\equiv 1 \pmod{q_p - 1}\): the coefficient \([z^{q_p^j}]\, y_1\) has valuation \(-(q_p^j - 1)\rho_p\), as in Problem 19.2.3. It proves this by rescaling: for some \(\varpi\) of valuation \(\rho_p\) the series \(y_1(\varpi z)/\varpi\) is integral and congruent to \(\sum_{j \ge 0} z^{q_p^j}\) modulo \(\pi_p\).

The proof uses Theorem 19.3.1 in many variables, along a linear form whose coefficients are units.

Tag 235Y

Problem 19.3.2. Return to the lemniscate of Section 19.1, with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(\mathcal{O}= \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{i}]\). Let \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\), let \(\beta_j \in \mathcal{O}^\times\) for \(j\) in a finite set \(J\), and let \(F\bigl((u_j)_{j \in J}\bigr) := g\bigl(\operatorname{sl}(\sum_{j \in J} \beta_j u_j)\bigr)\). For every multi-index \(\nu\), \[\nu!\,[u^\nu]\, F = \beta^\nu\, (D^{\lvert\nu\rvert} g)(0),\] so that \(v_p(\nu!\,[u^\nu]\, F) = v_p\bigl((D^{\lvert\nu\rvert}g)(0)\bigr)\); for every \(\nu\) there is a \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X]]\) for which \(v_p\bigl((D^{\lvert\nu\rvert}g)(0)\bigr)\) is at most \(\gamma_p\lvert\nu\rvert = p\lvert\nu\rvert/(p^2-1)\).

Used in: Chapter 78

§19.4. Many places Tag 235Z

In the proof the one-copy variables are indexed by the infinite places \(\tau \in \Phi\) of the CM field \(E\), each with its weight \(\kappa(\tau)\), as in Chapter 36. For a prime \(p\) let \(\mathcal I_p\) be the set of odd \(e \ge 1\) if \(p\) is inert, of all \(e \ge 1\) if \(p = d\), and empty if \(p\) splits. For \(e \in \mathcal I_p\) the partner of \(\tau\) is \([-p^e]\tau\) (Chapter 36). The divisibility predicted at \(p\) for a derivative of order \(\kappa_0\) in the variable of \(\tau\) is \[s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa_0, \tau) = v(\pi_p) \sum_{e \in \mathcal I_p} \Bigl( \frac{\kappa_0}{p^e} - \kappa([-p^e]\tau) \Bigr)_+ ,\] the part at \(p\) of the prime sum of Chapter 36, divided by \(2\log p\), without the cutoff at \(a\). Each term has the shape of the bound in Theorem 16.3.1, with the weight of the partner in the place of \(w\).

Tag 2362

Problem 19.4.1. For real \(\kappa_0 \ge 0\), \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa_0, \tau) \le \gamma_p \kappa_0\), with equality if \(\kappa\) vanishes at all partners of \(\tau\). Hence \(\sum_\tau s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) \le \gamma_p \sum_\tau \kappa(\tau)\).

Used in: Chapter 78

Setting all partner weights to zero in \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa_0, \tau)\) gives \(\gamma_p\kappa_0\). By Theorem 19.3.1, a larger linear rate cannot hold as a divisibility bound for every series on one curve. In independent variables the least valuations add.

Tag 2363

Problem 19.4.2. Use the lemniscate, the prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the ring \(\mathcal{O}\) and the function \(\mu\) of Section 19.1. For \(n, n' \ge 0\), the least value of \(v_p\bigl((D_1^n D_2^{n'} g)(0)\bigr)\) over \(g \in \mathcal{O}[[X_1, X_2]]\), with \(D_1\), \(D_2\) the invariant derivations in the two variables, is \(\mu(n) + \mu(n')\).

Used in: Chapter 78

If \(e \in \mathcal I_p\) and both \(\kappa(\tau)\) and \(\kappa([-p^e]\tau)\) are positive, then \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) < \gamma_p \kappa(\tau)\): a partner lowers the prediction. By Problem 4.1.4, for a prime \(p \le kt\) with \(p \nmid q_1\) and for \(0 < i < j < l_0(s)\), the places \(\tau\), \([-p]^i\tau\), \([-p]^j\tau\) are not all in \(\Phi_b\). Call \((\tau, e)\) a near pair if \(e \in \mathcal I_p\), \(2e < l_0(s)\), and both \(\tau\) and its partner lie in \(\Phi_b\). For each nonsplit prime \(p\le k\), the proof uses the sparsity of \(\Phi_b\) (Chapter 4) to show that distinct near pairs have distinct starts and distinct ends, and that no end is a start.

Tag 2364

Problem 19.4.3. Let \(\Gamma\) be an abelian group, \(\xi \in \Gamma\), \(\mathcal B \subseteq \Gamma\) and \(L \ge 1\), such that for no \(x \in \Gamma\) and \(0 < i < j < L\) are \(x\), \(\xi^ix\), \(\xi^jx\) all in \(\mathcal B\). Call \((x, e)\) near if \(0 < 2e < L\) and \(x, \xi^ex \in \mathcal B\). Then two near pairs with the same start, or with the same end, are equal, and the end of a near pair is never the start of one.

Used in: Chapter 4; Chapter 78

For every degree vector in the range of Theorem 78.5.1, the proof chooses one nonzero scalar \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\) for all primes. For each modulus and \(p\)-adic place in that theorem, some Taylor coefficient on twelve copies, normalized as in the theorem, has valuation at most \(\sum_{\tau\in\Phi}s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau),\tau)\). This sum also enters the divisibility bound for the composite lift in Chapter 57. The proof uses Theorem 19.3.1 in several variables and the sparsity of \(\Phi_b\) (Problem 19.4.3). Chapter 78 takes this up: the version at many places, on twelve copies of the variables.
