\section{The least valuation}\label{sec:least}

Chapter~\ref{c14-chap} proves a lower bound for the divisibility of derivatives on the lemniscate.  Is it the truth, or does every function gain more?  Throughout this section and the next, \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is a prime, \(q := p^2\), \(\Oc = \Z_p[\ii]\), and \(D = d/du\) is the invariant derivation, so that \((D^n g)(0) = n!\,[u^n]\, g(\sll u)\) for \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\) (Problem~\ref{c14-pr:D}).  Recall Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single}: for \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\) and every \(n \ge 0\),
\[
  v_p\bigl((D^n g)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ E_p(n) = \sum_{i \ge 1,\ i \text{ odd}} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{n}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor .
\]
Let \(\mu(n)\) be the least value of \(v_p((D^n g)(0))\) over \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\).  How far is \(\mu(n)\) from \(E_p(n)\)?

\begin{problem}\label{pr:monomial}
\(\mu(0) = 0\).  For \(n \ge 1\) the least value \(\mu(n)\) exists and is attained by a power \(X^i\) with \(1 \le i \le n\):
\(\mu(n) = \min_{1 \le i \le n} v_p\bigl(n!\,[u^n]\, \sll^i u\bigr)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:eleven}
For \(p = 3\): \(\mu(11) = 4\), while \(E_3(11) = 3\).
\end{problem}

So the bound of Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single} is not attained in every degree.  The rest of this section shows that \(\mu\) is superadditive; Section~\ref{sec:powers} computes it at the powers of \(q\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:split}
Let \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\), and write \(g(X \oplus X') = \sum_{i, j} a_{ij} X^i X'^j\) with the group law \(\oplus\) of Problem~\ref{c14-pr:fg}.  Then \(a_{ij} \in \Oc\), and for all \(n, n' \ge 0\)
\[
  (D^{n+n'} g)(0) = \sum_{i, j} a_{ij}\, (D^n X^i)(0)\, (D^{n'} X^j)(0) .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:superadditive}
\(\mu(n + n') \ge \mu(n) + \mu(n')\) for all \(n, n' \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:fekete}
Let \(\phi \colon \N \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) be superadditive, and let \(\gamma' \ge 0\).  If \(\phi(N) \le \gamma' N\) for infinitely many \(N\), then \(\phi(n) \le \gamma' n\) for every \(n \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

\section{The powers of \texorpdfstring{\(q\)}{q}}\label{sec:powers}

Let
\[
  \gamma := \frac{p}{p^2 - 1} = \frac1p + \frac1{p^3} + \frac1{p^5} + \dotsb .
\]

\begin{problem}\label{pr:Eq}
\(E_p(n) \le \gamma n\) for every \(n \ge 0\), and \(E_p(q^j) = \gamma (q^j - 1)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

In the degrees \(q^j\) the function \(X\) itself attains the bound.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:exact}
\(v_p\bigl((D^{q^j}X)(0)\bigr) = E_p(q^j)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).  (For \(p = 3\): \((D^9X)(0) = 3024\), of valuation \(3\); and \(v_3((D^{81}X)(0)) = 30\).)
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:coeff}
\(v_p\bigl([u^{q^j}]\, \sll u\bigr) = -(q^j - 1)/(q - 1)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).  (For \(p = 3\): \([u^9]\,\sll u = 1/120\); for \(p = 7\): \(v_7([u^{49}]\,\sll u) = -1\).)
\end{problem}

Thus \(\mu(q^j) = E_p(q^j) = \gamma(q^j - 1)\).  Problems~\ref{pr:superadditive}, \ref{pr:fekete} and~\ref{pr:exact}, with Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single}, prove the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rate}
Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\).  For every \(n \ge 0\),
\[
  E_p(n) \ \le\ \mu(n) \ \le\ \gamma n ,
\]
and \(\mu(q^j) = E_p(q^j)\), attained by \(g = X\), for every \(j \ge 0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gap}
\(0 \le \gamma n - E_p(n) < h + 2\), where \(h\) is the number of positive odd integers \(i\) with \(p^i \le n\).  Hence \(\mu(n) = \gamma n + O(\log n)\).
\end{problem}

So \(\gamma\) is the exact rate, and Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single} loses at most a logarithm in every degree.  A computation of \(\mu(n)\) by Problem~\ref{pr:monomial} gives, for instance,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccccccc}
\(p = 3\), \(n\) & 9 & 10 & 11 & 20 & 27 & 40 & 80 & 81 \\
\hline
\(E_3(n)\) & 3 & 3 & 3 & 6 & 10 & 14 & 28 & 30 \\
\(\mu(n)\) & 3 & 3 & 4 & 7 & 10 & 14 & 29 & 30 \\
\(3n/8\) & 3.375 & 3.75 & 4.125 & 7.5 & 10.125 & 15 & 30 & 30.375
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For \(p = 7\) one finds \(\mu(n) = E_7(n)\) for \(n \le 54\), and \(\mu(55) = 8 > E_7(55) = 7\).  We have checked \(E_p(n) \le \mu(n) \le \gamma n\) by machine for \(p = 3, 7\) and \(n \le 100\).

\section{Curves with complex multiplication by \texorpdfstring{\(\Q(\sqrt{-d})\)}{Q(sqrt(-d))}}\label{sec:general}

The proof works with curves whose complex multiplication is by \(K = \Q(\sqrt{-d})\) for a prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), and with every prime \(p\), split, inert or ramified in \(K\).  The lemniscate is not among these curves, since \(\Q(\ii)\) is not of this form; it serves here as the simplest case.  Let \(q_p\) be the norm of a prime of \(K\) above \(p\): \(q_p = p^2\) if \(p\) is inert and \(q_p = p\) otherwise.  Let \(\pi_p := \sqrt{-d}\) if \(p = d\) and \(\pi_p := p\) otherwise.  In valuations and congruences we use its image under a chosen ring homomorphism \(\iota_p \colon \C \to \C_p\); then \(v(\pi_p) = \tfrac12\) if \(p = d\) and \(v(\pi_p) = 1\) otherwise.  Put
\[
  \rho_p := \frac{v(\pi_p)}{q_p - 1}, \qquad \gamma_p := \frac{1}{p-1} - \rho_p .
\]

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gamma-cases}
\(\gamma_p = 0\), \(p/(p^2-1)\), \(1/(2(p-1))\) according as \(p\) is split, inert or ramified in \(K\).  The inert formula agrees with the expression for the rate \(\gamma\) of Section~\ref{sec:powers}.
\end{problem}

Let \(\Lambda \subset \C\) be a lattice with \(\Oc_K\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\), let \(t \in \C\) with \(t \notin \Lambda\), \(2t \in \Lambda\), and \(\omega t, \bar\omega t \notin \Lambda\) for \(\omega = (1 + \sqrt{-d})/2\), and let \(a_1 \in \C\) with \(a_1^2 = 12\wp(t)\); assume the normalization \(g_2(\Lambda) = 12\wp(t)^2 - 4\).  Let \(y(z)\) be the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(\tfrac12\bigl(\wp'(z + t) - a_1(\wp(z + t) - \wp(t))\bigr)\), with \(\iota_p\) applied to its coefficients; it begins with \(z\).  Let \(\mathfrak o\) be the valuation ring of \(\C_p\), and \(\mathfrak o[[y]]\) the ring of series \(\Psi(y(z))\) with \(\Psi \in \mathfrak o[[W]]\).  The rewritten Lean proof proves the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:katz}
Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be prime, \(\Lambda\), \(t\), \(a_1\) as above, \(p\) a prime and \(\iota_p \colon \C \to \C_p\) a ring homomorphism.  For every \(n \ge 0\) there is \(g \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\) with
\[
  v\bigl(n!\,[z^n] g\bigr) \ \le\ \gamma_p n .
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{In the Lean formalization.}
In the rewritten Lean proof \cite{GL} this is \texttt{CMLattice.katz\_divisibility\_limit}, in \texttt{New/Derivatives/Sharpness/OneCurve.lean} of the readable edition, stated with norms: \(\lvert n!\,[z^n]g\rvert \ge (p^{-\gamma_p})^n\).  The rewriting proves it as in Sections~\ref{sec:least} and~\ref{sec:powers}: the greatest norm of \(n!\,[z^n]g\) over \(g \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\) is attained by a power of \(y\), it is submultiplicative in \(n\), and a lemma of Fekete's type (Problem~\ref{pr:fekete}, in multiplicative form) carries a bound at the degrees \(q_p^j\) to all degrees.  At those degrees it uses, in place of Problem~\ref{pr:exact}, the part \(y_1\) of \(y\) in degrees \(\equiv 1 \pmod{q_p - 1}\): the coefficient \([z^{q_p^j}]\, y_1\) has valuation \(-(q_p^j - 1)\rho_p\), as in Problem~\ref{pr:coeff}.  The rewriting proves this by rescaling: for some \(\varpi\) of valuation \(\rho_p\) the series \(y_1(\varpi z)/\varpi\) is integral and congruent to \(\sum_{j \ge 0} z^{q_p^j}\) modulo \(\pi_p\).

The proof uses Theorem~\ref{thm:katz} in many variables, along a linear form whose coefficients are units.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:many-variables}
Return to the lemniscate of Section~\ref{sec:least}, with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(\Oc = \Z_p[\ii]\).  Let \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\), let \(\beta_j \in \Oc^\times\) for \(j\) in a finite set \(J\), and let \(F\bigl((u_j)_{j \in J}\bigr) := g\bigl(\sll(\sum_{j \in J} \beta_j u_j)\bigr)\).  For every multi-index \(\nu\),
\[
  \nu!\,[u^\nu]\, F = \beta^\nu\, (D^{\lvert\nu\rvert} g)(0),
\]
so that \(v_p(\nu!\,[u^\nu]\, F) = v_p\bigl((D^{\lvert\nu\rvert}g)(0)\bigr)\); for every \(\nu\) there is such a \(g\) for which this value is at most \(\gamma\lvert\nu\rvert = p\lvert\nu\rvert/(p^2-1)\).
\end{problem}

\section{Many places}\label{sec:places}

In the rewritten proof the one-copy variables are indexed by the infinite places of the CM field \(E = K(\zeta_{q_1})\), a cyclotomic extension of \(K\).  These places form a group \(\Phi \simeq (\Z/q_1)^\times\), and each \(\tau \in \Phi\) has a weight \(\kappa(\tau)\) (Chapter~\ref{p01-chap}).  For a prime \(p\) let \(I_p\) be the set of odd \(e \ge 1\) if \(p\) is inert, of all \(e \ge 1\) if \(p = d\), and empty if \(p\) splits.  For \(e \in I_p\) the \emph{partner} of \(\tau\) is \(-p^e\tau\), the product of \(\tau\) with the place given by the class of \(-p^e\) modulo \(q_1\) (the identity if this class is not a unit).  The predicted divisibility at \(p\) is
\[
  s_p(\kappa_0, \tau) := v(\pi_p) \sum_{e \in I_p} \Bigl( \frac{\kappa_0}{p^e} - \kappa(-p^e\tau) \Bigr)_+ ,
  \qquad
  S_p := \sum_{\tau \in \Phi} s_p\bigl(\kappa(\tau), \tau\bigr) .
\]
In the rewritten Lean proof these are \texttt{localPrimeSum} and \texttt{localPrimeSumTotal}.  Each term has the shape of the bound in Theorem~\ref{c15-thm:main}, with the weight of the partner in the place of \(w\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:prime-sum-rate}
For real \(\kappa_0 \ge 0\), \(s_p(\kappa_0, \tau) \le \gamma_p \kappa_0\), with equality if \(\kappa\) vanishes at all partners of \(\tau\).  Hence \(S_p \le \gamma_p \sum_\tau \kappa(\tau)\).
\end{problem}

Setting all partner weights to zero in \(s_p(\kappa_0, \tau)\) gives \(\gamma_p\kappa_0\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:katz}, a larger linear rate cannot hold as a divisibility bound for every series on one curve.  In independent variables the least valuations add.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:product}
Use the lemniscate, the prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the ring \(\Oc\) and the function \(\mu\) of Section~\ref{sec:least}.  For \(n, n' \ge 0\), the least value of \(v_p\bigl((D_1^n D_2^{n'} g)(0)\bigr)\) over \(g \in \Oc[[X_1, X_2]]\), with \(D_1\), \(D_2\) the invariant derivations in the two variables, is \(\mu(n) + \mu(n')\).
\end{problem}

If \(e \in I_p\) and both \(\kappa(\tau)\) and \(\kappa(-p^e\tau)\) are positive, then \(s_p(\kappa(\tau), \tau) < \gamma_p \kappa(\tau)\).  We use sparsity for the near pairs and the primes specified below.  A set \(B\) of places of weight \(b\) is chosen as in Chapter~\ref{c4-chap}, and the rewritten Lean proof uses that, for a prime \(p \le k = r + 1 - m\) that does not split and for \(0 < i < j < L_0\), with \(L_0 = l_0(s)\) the power of \(3\) in the sparsity condition of Chapter~\ref{c4-chap}, the places \(\tau\), \((-p)^i\tau\), \((-p)^j\tau\) are not all in \(B\) (\texttt{notMem\_bPlaces\_of\_two\_partners}).  A \emph{near pair} is \((\tau, e)\) with \(e \in I_p\), \(2e < L_0\), and both \(\tau\) and its partner in \(B\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:near-pairs}
Let \(\Gamma\) be an abelian group, \(\xi \in \Gamma\), \(B \subseteq \Gamma\) and \(L \ge 1\), such that for no \(x \in \Gamma\) and \(0 < i < j < L\) are \(x\), \(\xi^ix\), \(\xi^jx\) all in \(B\).  Call \((x, e)\) near if \(0 < 2e < L\) and \(x, \xi^ex \in B\).  Then two near pairs with the same start, or with the same end, are equal, and the end of a near pair is never the start of one.
\end{problem}

For a nonsplit prime \(p \le k\), the corresponding statements for the places in \(B\) are \texttt{IsNearPair.exponent\_unique}, \texttt{IsNearPair.eq\_and\_eq\_of\_partner\_eq} and \texttt{IsNearPair.end\_ne\_start} of the rewritten Lean proof.  Here \(\xi\) is the place represented by \(-p\).  Near pairs occur only at inert primes, and by \texttt{IsNearPair.odd} and \texttt{IsNearPair.partner\_eq} the exponent of a near pair is odd and its end is \((-p)^e\tau\).  For a prime \(p \le k\) that does not split, the proof couples the near pairs on six copies of the variables and applies Theorem~\ref{thm:katz} along a linear form with unit coefficients on six more.  Fix the data of Chapter~\ref{p01-chap}, a prime \(p\), and a ring homomorphism \(\iota_p \colon \C \to \C_p\).  Write \(M = p_0p_1\) for the principal level and \(\widehat{\Oc}_E\) for the ring of integral finite adeles.  Let \(\mathfrak a\) be the finite idele of the Weil data, whose component generates the inverse relative different of \(E/K\) at the places dividing \((1 + \sqrt{-d})/2\) and is \(1\) elsewhere.  Let \(w_j \in E \cap \mathfrak a\widehat{\Oc}_E\) and \(c_j \in \N\), indexed by a finite set \(J\), form a tight frame with bound \(M\): writing \(\tau(w)\) for the image of \(w\) under the embedding of \(E\) over \(K\) at the place \(\tau\),
\[
  \sum_{j \in J} c_j\, \tau'(w_j)\, \overline{\tau(w_j)} = M \delta_{\tau'\tau} \qquad (\tau, \tau' \in \Phi).
\]
With \(\Omega_E\) the complex scalar \texttt{periodFactor} at this tower level, write \(\lambda_j(z) = \sum_\tau \iota_p\bigl(\Omega_E\, \overline{\tau(w_j)}\bigr) z_\tau\), and assume that for some \(j\) every coefficient of \(\lambda_j\) has norm one.  Use the chosen curve \texttt{cmCurve}, with lattice \(\Lambda = \Omega_0\Oc_K\), marked point \(t\), and parameter \(y\) as in Section~\ref{sec:general}, and let \(\sigma_\Lambda\) be the function \texttt{PeriodPair.}\(\sigma\) of the rewritten Lean proof (not the Weierstrass sigma function).  Let \(\theta_p\) be the Taylor series at zero of
\[
  z \longmapsto \exp\bigl(-\pi z\bar t/\operatorname{area}(\C/\Lambda)\bigr)\, \frac{\sigma_\Lambda(z + t)}{\sigma_\Lambda(t)},
\]
with \(\iota_p\) applied to its coefficients, and write \(\Theta(z) = \prod_{j \in J} \theta_p(\lambda_j(z))^{c_j}\).  For every \(l \colon \Phi \to \N\) with \(\kappa(\tau) \le l(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for all \(\tau\), \texttt{leastValLE\_twelve} gives twelve series \(G_i\), each an integral power series in the \(y(\lambda_j(z))\), and multi-indices \(\alpha_i\) with \(\sum_i \alpha_i = l\), such that
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^{12} v\bigl([z^{\alpha_i}](G_i\Theta)\bigr) + \sum_\tau v_p(l(\tau)!) \ \le\ S_p + 2\sum_\tau v_p\bigl(\Pi(l(\tau))\bigr) .
\]
Here \(\Pi(h)\) is the product of the \(\lceil 6b/k \rceil\) integers following \(\max(h - \lceil 6b/k \rceil, 0)\).  From this the rewriting deduces the following, which we state as read from the code.  It uses the normalized theta series \texttt{twelveTaylor} of \texttt{indicatorDatum} and the rank-one scalar \texttt{vacuumScale} of the readable edition~\cite{GL}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:attained}
Let the data of Chapter~\ref{p01-chap} be given: the field \(E\) with its set \(\Phi\) of infinite places, \(f = \#\Phi\), the weights \(\kappa\), the scales \(x\) and \(b\), the number \(m\) of lifts, the target rank \(r\), and the source rank \(k = r + 1 - m\).  Write \(\pi_M\) for the conjugate of the element of \(\Oc_K\) of norm \(M\) specified in those data, viewed also in \(\Oc_E\).  Let \(l \colon \Phi \to \N\) satisfy \(\kappa(\tau) \le l(\tau) \le \kappa(\tau) + x^{42}\) for every \(\tau\).  There is \(t_0 \in \C\), \(t_0 \ne 0\), with \(\lvert \varsigma(t_0) \rvert \le \exp(fmb/k)\) for every ring automorphism \(\varsigma\) of \(\C\), such that: for every modulus \(\mathfrak m\) of \(E\) with ideal \((\pi_M)\), every prime \(p\) and every ring homomorphism \(\iota_p \colon \C \to \C_p\), there are a multi-index \(\alpha\) in the variables \(z_{\tau, i}\) (\(\tau \in \Phi\), \(1 \le i \le 12\)) with \(\sum_i \alpha(\tau, i) = l(\tau)\) for every \(\tau\), and a class \(\eta \in (\Oc_E/\mathfrak m)^{12}\), such that
\[
  v\Bigl( \iota_p\Bigl( \frac{c_{\mathrm{vac}}^{12}}{t_0} \prod_{\tau \in \Phi} l(\tau)! \cdot [z^\alpha]\, J_\eta \Bigr) \Bigr) \ \le\ S_p ,
\]
where \(J_\eta\) is the Taylor series at zero, in the twelve copies \(z_{\tau, i}\), of \(z \mapsto \Theta_\eta(\pi_M \delta_0 z)\), with \(\delta_0\) a fixed complex period scale (\texttt{jetScale}) at the tower level of the data.  Here \(\Theta_\eta\) is the theta function for the datum on \(\operatorname{Hom}(E, E^{12})\) with the Gaussian at infinity, the indicator of \(\eta\) at the places dividing \(\mathfrak m\), and the vacuum at the other finite places.  The scalar \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}\) is the chosen nonzero rank-one \texttt{vacuumScale}, for which the scaled vacuum has integral values at finite adelic points and bounded conjugates over \(K\).
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{In the Lean formalization.}
In the rewritten Lean proof \cite{GL} this is \texttt{Data.exists\_rpow\_neg\_localPrimeSumTotal\_le\_norm\_coeff}, in \texttt{New/Derivatives/Sharpness/BoundAttained.lean} of the readable edition, stated with norms; there \(J_\eta\) is \texttt{twelveTaylor (indicatorDatum \(\mathfrak m\) \(\eta\))} and \(c_{\mathrm{vac}}\) is \texttt{vacuumScale}.  The proof takes \(t_0 = M^{12f} \prod_\tau \Pi(l(\tau))^2 \cdot c_0\) with \(c_0\) a nonzero value of the integral vacuum; the one number \(t_0\) serves every prime.

The height bound in Chapter~\ref{p07-chap} uses one multiplier for the whole family of primes and embeddings, with a bound on its complex conjugates (\texttt{HeightLe}).  The common factor \(t_0\) enters the multiplier for the pairings.  In \texttt{exists\_isTestForm\_le\_norm\_inner\_trivialLift}, fix \(1 \le j \le m\), a critical pair \(A = (\nu_A, z_A)\) main for \(j\), and a critical pair \(Q = (\nu_Q, 2)\) with empty row on four indices.  Write \(-c_A(\tau)\) for the weight of \(\nu_A\).  The proof applies Theorem~\ref{thm:attained} with \(l(\tau) = c_A(\tau) - z_A\).  There is \(c_0' \ne 0\) with \(\lvert \varsigma(c_0') \rvert \le \exp(fmb/k + 3x^{34})\) for every ring automorphism \(\varsigma\) of \(\C\).  For every prime \(\ell\), every ring homomorphism \(\iota_\ell \colon \C \to \C_\ell\), and every twist \(\vartheta\) with \(\ell\) prime to the product of the norms of its places, there is a test form \(\Xi\) whose pairing with the normalized lift of \(\nu_A\vartheta\), read through \(\iota_\ell\), has valuation at most \(v(\iota_\ell(c_0' R_Q(\vartheta))) + S_\ell\).  Here \(R_Q(\vartheta)\) is the ratio of the absolute Hecke values of \((\nu_Q\vartheta)^{-1}\) and \(\nu_Q^{-1}\) at \(2\).  Both values omit the Euler factors at the chosen places and their conjugates, and the numerator also omits the factors at the places of \(\vartheta\) and their conjugates.

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:powers}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:Eq}] Geometric series.
\item[\ref{pr:exact}] Start with \((DX)(0) = 1\).  For the induction step take \(j \ge 1\).  \(X \in \Oc[[T]]\); \(D^{q^j} = (p^p\varepsilon)^{q^{j-1}} H^{q^{j-1}}\) by Problem~\ref{c14-pr:Dq-unit}; compare \(H^{q^{j-1}}\) with \(D^{q^{j-1}}\) by Problem~\ref{c14-pr:induction} at level \(2j-2\).
\item[\ref{pr:coeff}] Legendre's formula for \(v_p(q^j!)\).
\item[\ref{pr:gap}] Write \(\gamma n - E_p(n)\) as a sum over odd \(i\) of \(n/p^i - \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor\), and bound the terms with \(p^i > n\) by a geometric series.
\end{description}

\item[\ref{pr:product}] As in Problem~\ref{pr:monomial}, the least value is attained by a monomial \(X_1^iX_2^j\).
\item[\ref{pr:near-pairs}] For a common end \(\xi^e x = \xi^{e'}x'\) with \(e < e'\), consider \(x'\), \(\xi^{e'-e}x'\), \(\xi^{e'}x'\).
