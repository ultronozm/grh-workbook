\section{The least valuation}\label{sec:least}

Chapter~\ref{c14-chap} proves a lower bound for the divisibility of derivatives on the lemniscate.  Is it the truth, or does every function gain more?  Throughout this section and the next, \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is a prime, \(q := p^2\), \(\Oc = \Z_p[\ii]\), and \(D = d/du\) is the invariant derivation, so that \((D^n g)(0) = n!\,[u^n]\, g(\sll u)\) for \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\) (Problem~\ref{c14-pr:D}).  Recall Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single}: for \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\) and every \(n \ge 0\),
\[
  v_p\bigl((D^n g)(0)\bigr) \ \ge\ E_p(n) = \sum_{i \ge 1,\ i \text{ odd}} \Bigl\lfloor \frac{n}{p^i} \Bigr\rfloor .
\]
Let \(\mu(n)\) be the least value of \(v_p((D^n g)(0))\) over \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\).  How far is \(\mu(n)\) from \(E_p(n)\)?

\begin{problem}\label{pr:monomial}
\(\mu(0) = 0\).  For \(n \ge 1\) the least value \(\mu(n)\) exists and is attained by a power \(X^i\) with \(1 \le i \le n\):
\(\mu(n) = \min_{1 \le i \le n} v_p\bigl(n!\,[u^n]\, \sll^i u\bigr)\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:eleven}
For \(p = 3\): \(\mu(11) = 4\), while \(E_3(11) = 3\).
\end{problem}

So the bound of Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single} is not attained in every degree.  The rest of this section shows that \(\mu\) is superadditive; Section~\ref{sec:powers} computes it at the powers of \(q\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:split}
Let \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\), and write \(g(X \oplus X') = \sum_{i, j} a_{ij} X^i X'^j\) with the group law \(\oplus\) of Problem~\ref{c14-pr:fg}.  Then \(a_{ij} \in \Oc\), and for all \(n, n' \ge 0\)
\[
  (D^{n+n'} g)(0) = \sum_{i, j} a_{ij}\, (D^n X^i)(0)\, (D^{n'} X^j)(0) .
\]
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:superadditive}
\(\mu(n + n') \ge \mu(n) + \mu(n')\) for all \(n, n' \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:fekete}
Let \(\phi \colon \N \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) be superadditive, and let \(\gamma' \ge 0\).  If \(\phi(N) \le \gamma' N\) for infinitely many \(N\), then \(\phi(n) \le \gamma' n\) for every \(n \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

\section{The powers of \texorpdfstring{\(q\)}{q}}\label{sec:powers}

Let
\[
  \gamma_p := \frac{p}{p^2 - 1} = \frac1p + \frac1{p^3} + \frac1{p^5} + \dotsb .
\]

\begin{problem}\label{pr:Eq}
\(E_p(n) \le \gamma_p n\) for every \(n \ge 0\), and \(E_p(q^j) = \gamma_p (q^j - 1)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).
\end{problem}

In the degrees \(q^j\) the function \(X\) itself attains the bound.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:exact}
\(v_p\bigl((D^{q^j}X)(0)\bigr) = E_p(q^j)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).  (For \(p = 3\): \((D^9X)(0) = 3024\), of valuation \(3\); and \(v_3((D^{81}X)(0)) = 30\).)
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:coeff}
\(v_p\bigl([u^{q^j}]\, \sll u\bigr) = -(q^j - 1)/(q - 1)\) for every \(j \ge 0\).  (For \(p = 3\): \([u^9]\,\sll u = 1/120\); for \(p = 7\): \(v_7([u^{49}]\,\sll u) = -1\).)
\end{problem}

Thus \(\mu(q^j) = E_p(q^j) = \gamma_p(q^j - 1)\).  Problems~\ref{pr:superadditive}, \ref{pr:fekete} and~\ref{pr:exact}, with Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single}, prove the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rate}
Let \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\).  For every \(n \ge 0\),
\[
  E_p(n) \ \le\ \mu(n) \ \le\ \gamma_p n ,
\]
and \(\mu(q^j) = E_p(q^j)\), attained by \(g = X\), for every \(j \ge 0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gap}
\(0 \le \gamma_p n - E_p(n) < h + 2\), where \(h\) is the number of positive odd integers \(i\) with \(p^i \le n\).  Hence \(\mu(n) = \gamma_p n + O(\log n)\).
\end{problem}

So \(\gamma_p\) is the exact rate, and Theorem~\ref{c14-thm:single} loses at most a logarithm in every degree.  A computation of \(\mu(n)\) by Problem~\ref{pr:monomial} gives, for instance,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccccccc}
\(p = 3\), \(n\) & 9 & 10 & 11 & 20 & 27 & 40 & 80 & 81 \\
\hline
\(E_3(n)\) & 3 & 3 & 3 & 6 & 10 & 14 & 28 & 30 \\
\(\mu(n)\) & 3 & 3 & 4 & 7 & 10 & 14 & 29 & 30 \\
\(3n/8\) & 3.375 & 3.75 & 4.125 & 7.5 & 10.125 & 15 & 30 & 30.375
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For \(p = 7\) one finds \(\mu(n) = E_7(n)\) for \(n \le 54\), and \(\mu(55) = 8 > E_7(55) = 7\).  We have checked \(E_p(n) \le \mu(n) \le \gamma_p n\) by machine for \(p = 3, 7\) and \(n \le 100\).

\section{Curves with complex multiplication by \texorpdfstring{\(\Q(\sqrt{-d})\)}{Q(sqrt(-d))}}\label{sec:general}

The proof works with curves whose complex multiplication is by \(K = \Q(\sqrt{-d})\) for a prime \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\), and with every prime \(p\), split, inert or ramified in \(K\).  The lemniscate is not among these curves, since \(\Q(\ii)\) is not of this form; it serves here as the simplest case.  Let \(q_p\) be the norm of a prime of \(K\) above \(p\): \(q_p = p^2\) if \(p\) is inert and \(q_p = p\) otherwise.  Let \(\pi_p := \sqrt{-d}\) if \(p = d\) and \(\pi_p := p\) otherwise.  In valuations and congruences we use its image under a chosen ring homomorphism \(\eta \colon \C \to \C_p\); then \(c_p := v(\pi_p)\) is \(\tfrac12\) if \(p = d\) and \(1\) otherwise.  Put
\[
  \rho_p := \frac{c_p}{q_p - 1}, \qquad \gamma_p := \frac{1}{p-1} - \rho_p .
\]
For the lemniscate at \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) we used \(\gamma_p = p/(p^2-1)\); by Problem~\ref{pr:gamma-cases} the two agree at inert primes.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:gamma-cases}
\(\gamma_p = 0\), \(p/(p^2-1)\), \(1/(2(p-1))\) according as \(p\) is split, inert or ramified in \(K\).  The inert value is the rate \(\gamma_p\) of Section~\ref{sec:powers}.
\end{problem}

Let \(\Lambda \subset \C\) be a lattice with \(\Oc_K\Lambda \subseteq \Lambda\), let \(t \in \C\) with \(t \notin \Lambda\), \(2t \in \Lambda\), and \(\omega t, \bar\omega t \notin \Lambda\) for \(\omega = (1 + \sqrt{-d})/2\), and let \(a_1 \in \C\) with \(a_1^2 = 12\wp(t)\); assume the normalization \(g_2(\Lambda) = 12\wp(t)^2 - 4\).  Let \(y(z)\) be the Taylor series at \(0\) of \(\tfrac12\bigl(\wp'(z + t) - a_1(\wp(z + t) - \wp(t))\bigr)\), with \(\eta\) applied to its coefficients; it begins with \(z\).  Let \(\mathfrak o\) be the valuation ring of \(\C_p\), and \(\mathfrak o[[y]]\) the ring of series \(\Psi(y(z))\) with \(\Psi \in \mathfrak o[[W]]\).  The proof establishes the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:katz}
Let \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) be prime, \(\Lambda\), \(t\), \(a_1\) as above, \(p\) a prime and \(\eta \colon \C \to \C_p\) a ring homomorphism.  For every \(n \ge 0\) there is \(g \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\) with
\[
  v\bigl(n!\,[z^n] g\bigr) \ \le\ \gamma_p n .
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{In the Lean formalization.}
In the Lean proof \cite{GL} this is \texttt{CMLattice.katz\_divisibility\_limit}, in \texttt{New/Derivatives/Sharpness/OneCurve.lean} of the readable edition, stated with norms: \(\lvert n!\,[z^n]g\rvert \ge (p^{-\gamma_p})^n\).  The Lean proof establishes it as in Sections~\ref{sec:least} and~\ref{sec:powers}: the greatest norm of \(n!\,[z^n]g\) over \(g \in \mathfrak o[[y]]\) is attained by a power of \(y\), it is submultiplicative in \(n\), and a lemma of Fekete's type (Problem~\ref{pr:fekete}, in multiplicative form) carries a bound at the degrees \(q_p^j\) to all degrees.  At those degrees it uses, in place of Problem~\ref{pr:exact}, the part \(y_1\) of \(y\) in degrees \(\equiv 1 \pmod{q_p - 1}\): the coefficient \([z^{q_p^j}]\, y_1\) has valuation \(-(q_p^j - 1)\rho_p\), as in Problem~\ref{pr:coeff}.  It proves this by rescaling: for some \(\varpi\) of valuation \(\rho_p\) the series \(y_1(\varpi z)/\varpi\) is integral and congruent to \(\sum_{j \ge 0} z^{q_p^j}\) modulo \(\pi_p\).

The proof uses Theorem~\ref{thm:katz} in many variables, along a linear form whose coefficients are units.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:many-variables}
Return to the lemniscate of Section~\ref{sec:least}, with \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) and \(\Oc = \Z_p[\ii]\).  Let \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\), let \(\beta_j \in \Oc^\times\) for \(j\) in a finite set \(J\), and let \(F\bigl((u_j)_{j \in J}\bigr) := g\bigl(\sll(\sum_{j \in J} \beta_j u_j)\bigr)\).  For every multi-index \(\nu\),
\[
  \nu!\,[u^\nu]\, F = \beta^\nu\, (D^{\lvert\nu\rvert} g)(0),
\]
so that \(v_p(\nu!\,[u^\nu]\, F) = v_p\bigl((D^{\lvert\nu\rvert}g)(0)\bigr)\); for every \(\nu\) there is a \(g \in \Oc[[X]]\) for which \(v_p\bigl((D^{\lvert\nu\rvert}g)(0)\bigr)\) is at most \(\gamma_p\lvert\nu\rvert = p\lvert\nu\rvert/(p^2-1)\).
\end{problem}

\section{Many places}\label{sec:places}

In the proof the one-copy variables are indexed by the infinite places \(\tau \in \Phi\) of the CM field \(E\), each with its weight \(\kappa(\tau)\), as in Chapter~\ref{p01-chap}.  For a prime \(p\) let \(\mathcal I_p\) be the set of odd \(e \ge 1\) if \(p\) is inert, of all \(e \ge 1\) if \(p = d\), and empty if \(p\) splits.  For \(e \in \mathcal I_p\) the \emph{partner} of \(\tau\) is \([-p^e]\tau\) (Chapter~\ref{p01-chap}).  The divisibility predicted at \(p\) for a derivative of order \(\kappa_0\) in the variable of \(\tau\) is
\[
  s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa_0, \tau) = v(\pi_p) \sum_{e \in \mathcal I_p} \Bigl( \frac{\kappa_0}{p^e} - \kappa([-p^e]\tau) \Bigr)_+ ,
\]
the part at \(p\) of the prime sum of Chapter~\ref{p01-chap}, divided by \(2\log p\), without the cutoff at \(a\).  Each term has the shape of the bound in Theorem~\ref{c15-thm:main}, with the weight of the partner in the place of \(w\).

\begin{problem}\label{pr:prime-sum-rate}
For real \(\kappa_0 \ge 0\), \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa_0, \tau) \le \gamma_p \kappa_0\), with equality if \(\kappa\) vanishes at all partners of \(\tau\).  Hence \(\sum_\tau s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) \le \gamma_p \sum_\tau \kappa(\tau)\).
\end{problem}

Setting all partner weights to zero in \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa_0, \tau)\) gives \(\gamma_p\kappa_0\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:katz}, a larger linear rate cannot hold as a divisibility bound for every series on one curve.  In independent variables the least valuations add.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:product}
Use the lemniscate, the prime \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\), the ring \(\Oc\) and the function \(\mu\) of Section~\ref{sec:least}.  For \(n, n' \ge 0\), the least value of \(v_p\bigl((D_1^n D_2^{n'} g)(0)\bigr)\) over \(g \in \Oc[[X_1, X_2]]\), with \(D_1\), \(D_2\) the invariant derivations in the two variables, is \(\mu(n) + \mu(n')\).
\end{problem}

If \(e \in \mathcal I_p\) and both \(\kappa(\tau)\) and \(\kappa([-p^e]\tau)\) are positive, then \(s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau), \tau) < \gamma_p \kappa(\tau)\): a partner lowers the prediction.  By Problem~\ref{c4-pr:near-pairs}, for a prime \(p \le kt\) with \(p \nmid q_1\) and for \(0 < i < j < l_0(s)\), the places \(\tau\), \([-p]^i\tau\), \([-p]^j\tau\) are not all in \(\Phi_b\).  Call \((\tau, e)\) a \emph{near pair} if \(e \in \mathcal I_p\), \(2e < l_0(s)\), and both \(\tau\) and its partner lie in \(\Phi_b\).  For each nonsplit prime \(p\le k\), the proof uses the sparsity of \(\Phi_b\) (Chapter~\ref{c4-chap}) to show that distinct near pairs have distinct starts and distinct ends, and that no end is a start.

\begin{problem}\label{pr:near-pairs}
Let \(\Gamma\) be an abelian group, \(\xi \in \Gamma\), \(\mathcal B \subseteq \Gamma\) and \(L \ge 1\), such that for no \(x \in \Gamma\) and \(0 < i < j < L\) are \(x\), \(\xi^ix\), \(\xi^jx\) all in \(\mathcal B\).  Call \((x, e)\) near if \(0 < 2e < L\) and \(x, \xi^ex \in \mathcal B\).  Then two near pairs with the same start, or with the same end, are equal, and the end of a near pair is never the start of one.
\end{problem}

For every degree vector in the range of Theorem~\ref{l4-thm:attained}, the proof chooses one nonzero scalar \(c_{\mathrm{att}}\) for all primes.  For each modulus and \(p\)-adic place in that theorem, some Taylor coefficient on twelve copies, normalized as in the theorem, has valuation at most \(\sum_{\tau\in\Phi}s_{\mathrm{fin},p}(\kappa(\tau),\tau)\).  This sum also enters the divisibility bound for the composite lift in Chapter~\ref{p04-chap}.  The proof uses Theorem~\ref{thm:katz} in several variables and the sparsity of \(\Phi_b\) (Problem~\ref{pr:near-pairs}).  Chapter~\ref{l4-chap} takes this up: the version at many places, on twelve copies of the variables.

\section*{Hints}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:least}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:monomial}] Write \(g = \sum_i a_i X^i\) with \(a_i \in \Oc\); \(\sll^i u\) begins with \(u^i\).  Use the ultrametric inequality.
\item[\ref{pr:eleven}] \(\sll^i u\) has only exponents \(\equiv i \pmod 4\), and \([u^{11}]\,\sll^3 u = 11/200\).  As a numerical check, \((D^{11}X^3)(0) = 2195424\).
\item[\ref{pr:split}] Expand \(g(\sll(u + w))\) in two ways and compare the coefficients of \(u^n w^{n'}\).
\item[\ref{pr:superadditive}] Problem~\ref{pr:split}.
\item[\ref{pr:fekete}] Treat \(n = 0\) separately.  For \(n > 0\), write \(N = sn + s'\) with \(0 \le s' < n\).
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:powers}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:Eq}] Geometric series.
\item[\ref{pr:exact}] Start with \((DX)(0) = 1\).  For the induction step take \(j \ge 1\).  \(X \in \Oc[[T]]\); \(D^{q^j} = (p^p\varepsilon)^{q^{j-1}} H^{q^{j-1}}\) by Problem~\ref{c14-pr:Dq-unit}; compare \(H^{q^{j-1}}\) with \(D^{q^{j-1}}\) by Problem~\ref{c14-pr:induction} at level \(2j-2\).
\item[\ref{pr:coeff}] Legendre's formula for \(v_p(q^j!)\).
\item[\ref{pr:gap}] Write \(\gamma_p n - E_p(n)\) as a sum over odd \(i\) of \(n/p^i - \lfloor n/p^i \rfloor\), and bound the terms with \(p^i > n\) by a geometric series.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:general}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:many-variables}] \((D^n g)(0) = n!\,[w^n]\, g(\sll w)\), and the multinomial theorem; Theorem~\ref{thm:rate}.
\end{description}

\subsection*{\S\ref{sec:places}}
\begin{description}
\item[\ref{pr:prime-sum-rate}] Compare each summand with its value when the partner has weight zero.
\item[\ref{pr:product}] As in Problem~\ref{pr:monomial}, the least value is attained by a monomial \(X_1^iX_2^j\).
\item[\ref{pr:near-pairs}] For a common end \(\xi^e x = \xi^{e'}x'\) with \(e < e'\), consider \(x'\), \(\xi^{e'-e}x'\), \(\xi^{e'}x'\).
\end{description}
