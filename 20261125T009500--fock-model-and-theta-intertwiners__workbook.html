Prerequisites.

Fourier series of entire periodic functions. Quadratic Gauss sums: for an odd prime \(p\) and \(p \nmid a\), \(\sum_{t \bmod p} e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}a t^2/p} = \Bigl(\frac{a}{p}\Bigr)\varepsilon_p\sqrt p\), with \(\varepsilon_p = 1\) for \(p \equiv 1 \pmod 4\) and \(\varepsilon_p = \mathrm{i}\) for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) [Dav, Ch. 2]. From Chapter 11, Complex tori, theta functions and periods: Jacobi’s function and its quasi-periodicity (Section 11.2, Problem 11.2.1).

The kernels of the definite theta lifts of later chapters are built from theta functions of lattices of homomorphisms. Here we ask how a theta function of one lattice becomes a theta function of another, and what is lost when one does this twice. For compatible lattices a finite sum of translation operators does this, and for the neighbours below the loss is a root of unity given by a Gauss sum. We work in one complex variable, with \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and its neighbours of index \(2\) and \(p\).

§12.1. The Heisenberg group and the Fock model Tag 22CP

On \(V = \mathbb{C}\) let \(H(z,w) := z\bar w\) and let \(E := \operatorname{Im} H\) be its imaginary part, so that \(E(x + \mathrm{i}y, x' + \mathrm{i}y') = y x' - x y'\) is a symplectic form. For \(x \in \mathbb{C}\) and a function \(G\) on \(\mathbb{C}\) put \[(U_x G)(z) := G(z + x)\, e^{-\pi H(z,x) - \frac\pi2 H(x,x)} .\] Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be the Fock space of entire functions with \(\lVert G\rVert^2 := \int_\mathbb{C}\lvert G(z)\rvert^2 e^{-\pi\lvert z\rvert^2}\, dA(z) < \infty\). The operators \(e^{\mathrm{i}s}U_x\) with \(s \in \mathbb{R}\) form a Heisenberg group, and \(\mathcal{F}\) is its Fock model [Fo, Ch. 1], [Pol, Ch. 2]; for theta functions in one variable from this point of view, see [Mu, Ch. I, §3].

Tag 22CQ

Problem 12.1.1. Each \(U_x\) is a unitary operator on \(\mathcal{F}\), and \[U_x U_y = e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}E(x,y)}\, U_{x+y} .\]

Tag 22CR

Problem 12.1.2. The monomials \(z^n\) are orthogonal in \(\mathcal{F}\), with \(\lVert z^n\rVert^2 = n!/\pi^n\).

So \(f \mapsto f(\sqrt\pi\, z)\) is an isometry from \(\mathbb{C}[z]\) with the Gaussian norm \(\lVert f\rVert^2 := \pi^{-1}\int_\mathbb{C}\lvert f(z)\rvert^2 e^{-\lvert z\rvert^2}\,dA(z)\), for which \(\lVert z^n\rVert^2 = n!\), into \(\mathcal{F}\).

The representation of the Heisenberg group on \(\mathcal{F}\) is irreducible. By the Stone–von Neumann theorem, every strongly continuous irreducible unitary representation on which \(e^{\mathrm{i}s}\) acts by \(e^{\mathrm{i}s}\) is unitarily equivalent to it [LV], [Pol, Ch. 2]. The inverse image of a self-dual lattice in the Heisenberg group is maximal abelian. A theta function is a joint eigenfunction for this group; the centre acts by its given character and the lattice eigenvalues are specified by a semicharacter.

A lattice \(\Lambda \subset \mathbb{C}\) is self-dual if \(E(\Lambda,\Lambda) \subset \mathbb{Z}\) and \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) has area \(1\). Call a function \(\alpha \colon \Lambda \to \{\pm1\}\) a semicharacter of \(\Lambda\) if it satisfies \[\alpha(\lambda + \mu) = \alpha(\lambda)\alpha(\mu)(-1)^{E(\lambda,\mu)} \qquad (\lambda, \mu \in \Lambda) .\] For a self-dual lattice \(\Lambda\) and a semicharacter \(\alpha\) of \(\Lambda\), let \(\mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha)\) be the space of entire functions \(G\) with \(\alpha(\lambda)U_\lambda G = G\) for all \(\lambda \in \Lambda\), that is, \[G(z + \lambda) = \alpha(\lambda)\, e^{\pi z\bar\lambda + \frac\pi2\lvert\lambda\rvert^2}\, G(z) .\] These are the classical theta functions of the line bundle on \(\mathbb{C}/\Lambda\) with Hermitian form \(H\) and semicharacter \(\alpha\) [Pol, Ch. 1]. Let \(\alpha_0(m + n\mathrm{i}) := (-1)^{mn}\) on \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\).

Tag 22CS

Problem 12.1.3. The semicharacters of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) are the four functions \((-1)^{mn + am + bn}\) with \(a, b \in \{0,1\}\). Exactly two of them, \(\alpha_0\) and \((-1)^{mn+m+n}\), are invariant under \(v \mapsto \mathrm{i}v\).

Tag 22CT

Problem 12.1.4. Let \(\alpha\) be a semicharacter of a self-dual \(\Lambda\). Then \(\lambda \mapsto \alpha(\lambda)U_\lambda\) is a homomorphism, and for \(w \in \mathbb{C}\) and \(G \in \mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha)\), \[\alpha(\lambda)U_\lambda\,(U_w G) = e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}E(\lambda,w)}\, U_w G \qquad (\lambda \in \Lambda) .\]

Tag 22CU

Problem 12.1.5. For \(G \in \mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha)\) the function \(z \mapsto \lvert G(z)\rvert^2 e^{-\pi\lvert z\rvert^2}\) is \(\Lambda\)-periodic. Moreover \(\mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha) \cap \mathcal{F}= 0\).

So no nonzero theta function lies in \(\mathcal{F}\). For \(G \in \mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha)\) we use the norm \[\lVert G\rVert_\Lambda^2 := \int_{\mathbb{C}/\Lambda} \lvert G(z)\rvert^2 e^{-\pi\lvert z\rvert^2}\, dA(z) .\]

§12.2. Theta functions of \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) Tag 22CV

How many theta functions does \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) carry? For \(\operatorname{Im}\tau > 0\) let \(\vartheta_\tau(z) := \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{\pi\mathrm{i}n^2\tau + 2\pi\mathrm{i}n z}\), the function \(\vartheta\) of Chapter 11, and let \[G(z) := e^{\pi z^2/2}\, \vartheta_\mathrm{i}(z) = e^{\pi z^2/2} \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} e^{-\pi n^2 + 2\pi\mathrm{i}n z} .\]

For \(t > 0\) let \(\Lambda_t := t\mathbb{Z}+ t^{-1}\mathrm{i}\mathbb{Z}\), a self-dual lattice, with the semicharacter \(\alpha_t(ta + t^{-1}b\mathrm{i}) := (-1)^{ab}\).

Tag 22CZ

Problem 12.2.4. \(\mathcal{T}(\Lambda_t, \alpha_t) = \mathbb{C}\, e^{\pi z^2/2}\, \vartheta_{\mathrm{i}/t^2}(z/t)\).

For every self-dual \(\Lambda\) and every semicharacter \(\alpha\) of it, \(\mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha)\) is a line. More generally, for a positive definite Hermitian form in \(g\) variables whose imaginary part \(E\) is integral on a full lattice \(\Lambda\), the dimension is the absolute value of the Pfaffian of the matrix of \(E\) in a lattice basis [Pol, Ch. 3]. Section 12.3 deduces the line statement from Problem 12.2.1 for pairs compatible with \((\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}],\alpha_0)\).

§12.3. Intertwiners Tag 22D2

Two pairs \((\Lambda_1,\alpha_1)\) and \((\Lambda_2,\alpha_2)\) as in Section 12.1 are compatible if \(\Lambda_1\) and \(\Lambda_2\) are commensurable (that is, \(\Lambda_1 \cap \Lambda_2\) has finite index in both) and \(\alpha_1 = \alpha_2\) on \(\Lambda_{12} := \Lambda_1 \cap \Lambda_2\). Since both lattices have covolume \(1\), \(n_{12} := [\Lambda_2 : \Lambda_{12}]\) equals \([\Lambda_1 : \Lambda_{12}]\). For compatible pairs we define the intertwiner from \((\Lambda_1,\alpha_1)\) to \((\Lambda_2,\alpha_2)\) by \[I_{12}\, G := n_{12}^{-1/2} \sum_{y \in \Lambda_2/\Lambda_{12}} \alpha_2(y)\, U_y G \qquad (G \in \mathcal{T}(\Lambda_1,\alpha_1)) .\] It is a finite sum of translation operators. The theory goes back to Weil [We]; see [Pol, Ch. 4] and [LV].

§12.4. Composition Tag 22D7

Is \(I_{23}I_{12}\) equal to \(I_{13}\)? Let \((\Lambda_i,\alpha_i)\), \(i = 1, 2, 3\), be pairwise compatible pairs, each compatible with \((\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}],\alpha_0)\). For \(z \in \Lambda_3\) and \(y \in \Lambda_2\) with \(z + y \in \Lambda_1\) put \[\psi(z,y) := \alpha_3(z)\,\alpha_2(y)\,\alpha_1(z+y)\, e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}E(z,y)} .\]

§12.5. Neighbours of index \(p\) and \(2\) Tag 22DC

Odd index. Let \(R \subset \mathbb{Q}\) be the ring of fractions with odd denominator, so that \(R/2R = \mathbb{Z}/2\), and for \(r \in R\) let \((-1)^r := (-1)^n\) for any integer \(n \equiv r \pmod{2R}\). For \(v = x + \mathrm{i}y\) with \(x, y \in R\) put \(\alpha_R(v) := (-1)^{xy}\).

Tag 22DD

Problem 12.5.1. \(\alpha_R\) is a semicharacter of every self-dual lattice contained in \(R + R\mathrm{i}\). It equals \(\alpha_0\) on \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(\alpha_{1/p}\) on \(\Lambda_{1/p}\) for \(p\) odd.

So all self-dual lattices in \(R + R\mathrm{i}\), with \(\alpha_R\), are pairwise compatible. Fix an odd prime \(p\), and let \(N := \mathbb{Z}+ p\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{i}\).

Tag 22DE

Problem 12.5.2. The self-dual lattices \(\Lambda\) with \(N \subset \Lambda\) are \(\Lambda^{(\infty)} := \mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\) and \(\Lambda^{(c)} := N + \mathbb{Z}(\frac1p + c\mathrm{i})\) for \(c \in \mathbb{Z}/p\). Moreover \(\Lambda^{(0)} = \Lambda_{1/p}\), and any two of these \(p + 1\) lattices intersect in \(N\).

Used in: Chapter 56
Tag 22DF

Problem 12.5.3. For \(b \ne c\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/p\), with \(\bar 2 := (p+1)/2\), \[\mu(\Lambda^{(\infty)}, \Lambda^{(b)}, \Lambda^{(c)}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt p} \sum_{t \bmod p} e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\, \bar2 (c-b) t^2/p} = \Bigl(\frac{2(c-b)}{p}\Bigr)\, \varepsilon_p .\] For \(p = 3\), \(b = 0\), \(c = 1\) this is \(-\mathrm{i}\).

Tag 22DG

Problem 12.5.4. For distinct \(a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}/p\), \[\mu(\Lambda^{(a)}, \Lambda^{(b)}, \Lambda^{(c)}) = \Bigl(\frac{2(b-a)(c-b)(c-a)}{p}\Bigr)\, \varepsilon_p .\]

Index \(2\). Here \(\alpha_R\) is not available, and the semicharacters must be chosen. Let \(N_2 := \mathbb{Z}+ 2\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{i}\), let \(\Lambda' := \Lambda_{1/2} = \frac12\mathbb{Z}+ 2\mathbb{Z}\mathrm{i}\) with \(\alpha' := \alpha_{1/2}\), and let \(\Lambda'' := \mathbb{Z}(\frac12 + \mathrm{i}) + \mathbb{Z}\) with \(\alpha''(a(\frac12 + \mathrm{i}) + m) := (-1)^{am}\).

Tag 22DH

Problem 12.5.5. \(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}]\), \(\Lambda'\) and \(\Lambda''\) are the self-dual lattices containing \(N_2\), and any two of them intersect in \(N_2\). The pairs \((\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}],\alpha_0)\), \((\Lambda',\alpha')\) and \((\Lambda'',\alpha'')\) are pairwise compatible, and each of \(\Lambda'\), \(\Lambda''\) has exactly two semicharacters compatible with \(\alpha_0\).

Tag 22DJ

Problem 12.5.6. \(\mu(\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}], \Lambda', \Lambda'') = 2^{-1/2}\sum_{t \bmod 2} e^{\pi\mathrm{i}t^2/2} = e^{\pi\mathrm{i}/4}\). If \(\alpha''\) is replaced by the other semicharacter compatible with \(\alpha_0\), then \(\mu = e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}/4}\).

For the distinct neighbours above, the composition scalar is a fourth root of unity at an odd prime and a primitive eighth root of unity at index \(2\). In both calculations the phase \(\psi(z,y)\) contains the semicharacter values and the factor \(e^{-\pi\mathrm{i}E(z,y)}\). At index \(2\) the choice of compatible semicharacter distinguishes the two eighth roots in Problem 12.5.6.

Unitary changes of variable.

Constants of the same kind appear when a lattice is moved by a unitary map \(\varrho\) of \(V\). Let \(G' \in \mathcal{T}(\Lambda,\alpha)\). If \(\varrho\) preserves \(\Lambda\) and \(\alpha\), then \(G' \circ \varrho^{-1}\) lies in the same line. For \(z \mapsto \mathrm{i}z\) on \((\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{i}],\alpha_0)\) the scalar is \(1\) by Problem 12.2.2. If the transported pair \((\varrho\Lambda,\alpha\circ\varrho^{-1})\) is compatible with \((\Lambda,\alpha)\), then \(G' \circ \varrho^{-1}\) and \(I_{\Lambda \to \varrho\Lambda}\,G'\) belong to the same theta line and have the same norm. For \(G' \ne 0\) they differ by a scalar of absolute value one. Related composition factors occur in Weil’s construction of the metaplectic cover [We]. For a completion \(F \ne \mathbb{C}\) of a number field containing the \(n\)-th roots of unity, Kubota gives a factor set for an \(n\)-fold cover of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(F)\) in terms of Hilbert symbols [Kub].

In the Lean formalization.

In the Lean proof [GL], the kernel of the definite lift from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\) for a tame character (liftKernel) takes data in the finite part (finitePart) for the Hermitian space of maps from \(X_j\) to \(X_{j+1}\). This is the complex span of the finite Heisenberg translates of that space’s vacuum. A translation tuple \(x\) (Translations) consists, for every \(j \in \mathbb{N}\), of a matrix \(x_j\) over the finite adeles of the CM field, with rows indexed by the basis of \(X_{j+1}\) and columns by the basis of \(X_j\). The value \(\texttt{translateDatum}\,x\,j\) is the translate by \(x_j\) of the integral vacuum of that Hermitian space (Chapter 37).

The corresponding statements of the Lean proof for the data of the composite lift are gluedDoubleLiftDatum, doubleLiftGluing, gluedVacuum and gluedTensor, and for the composite lift itself compositeLift and restrictToY.

These declarations are in grh/src/New/UpperBound/Objects/Columns.lean, with finitePart in grh/src/Lib/Weil/Construction/VacuumProjections.lean.
