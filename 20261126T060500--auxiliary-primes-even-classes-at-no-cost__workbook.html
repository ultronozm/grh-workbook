Prerequisites.

Kummer theory and the Chebotarev density theorem [Ne, IV §3, VII §13]. From Chapter 80: the notation of Section 80.1 (\(G_E\), \(g^c\), \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\), coefficient rings \(\mathcal{O}\), tame generators \(s_w\)), and Definition 80.1.1. From Chapter 81: Problems 81.2.1, 81.2.2 and 81.3.3. From Chapter 27: Definition 27.3.1, Problem 27.3.2 and Theorem 27.3.2.

The clearing bound \(\mathrm{cl}_\ell\) of Definition 80.1.1 pays for the odd classes through \(X_{\mathrm{odd}}\), and contains no length of even classes. What happens to the even parts of the cocycles that congruences produce? The proof allows ramification at auxiliary places, chosen one after another so that the vanishing conditions at the earlier places force vanishing at the next tame generator up to a controlled power of \(\ell\). We work out the choice in a model over \(\mathbb{Q}\), prove some steps of the general choice, state the theorem used (Theorem 84.3.1), and show how the auxiliary places enter the bound only through powers of \(\ell\).

§84.1. Even and odd parts

Let \(\ell\) be a prime, let \(E\), \(G_E\), \(g^c = cgc\), \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) and \(\mathcal{O}\) be as in Section 80.1, and let \(e, \nu \ge 0\) be integers. Throughout, \(E\) is a CM field containing a primitive root of unity of order \(5040\). A cocycle for \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) with values in \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\) is a map \(z\) with \(z(gh) = z(g) + \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{-e}z(h)\); put \(z^c(g) := z(g^c)\) and \(z^+ := z + z^c\).

Problem 84.1.1. For a cocycle \(z\) for \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) with values in \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\), \(z^+\) and \(z^c - z\) are cocycles for \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\), with \((z^+)^c = z^+\) and \((z^c - z)^c = -(z^c - z)\), and \(2z = z^+ - (z^c - z)\). If \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g) \equiv 1 \pmod{\ell^\nu}\), then \(z\bigl((g^c)^{-1}\bigr) = -z(g^c)\).

Definition 84.1.1. Let \(\mathcal Q\) be a set of finite places of \(E\) and \(e \ge 0\). An even class of exponent \(e\) for \(\mathcal Q\) (at precision \(\nu\), over \(\mathcal{O}\)) is a locally constant cocycle \(z \colon G_E \to \mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\) for \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) that vanishes on the inertia groups above the finite places neither dividing \(\ell\) nor in \(\mathcal Q \cup \bar{\mathcal Q}\), satisfies \(z^c = z\), and vanishes on the elements \(g\) with \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g) = 1\) of the decomposition groups above \(\ell\).

For \(e > 0\), \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_\ell(E,\mathcal{O};e,\nu)\) (Definition 27.3.1) consists of the locally constant cocycles with the local conditions of Definition 84.1.1 for \(\mathcal Q = \varnothing\) and \(z^c = -z\); under the hypotheses of Theorem 27.3.2 its length \(\operatorname{len}\) (throughout this sheet, ordinary \(\mathcal{O}\)-length, with \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal{O}}\) of a uniformizer equal to \(1\)) is at most \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal{O}} X_{\mathrm{odd}}\).

§84.2. A triangle of primes over \(\mathbb{Q}\)

In this section \(\ell = 3\), the base field is \(\mathbb{Q}\), and the coefficients are \(\mathbb{Z}/3\) with trivial action. Let \(\mathcal Q\) be a finite set of primes \(q \equiv 1 \pmod 3\) and \(\mathfrak n := \prod_{q \in \mathcal Q} q\). By the Artin map for \(\mathbb{Q}\), normalized by \(a \mapsto (\zeta \mapsto \zeta^a)\) on roots of unity, continuous homomorphisms \(G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathbb{Z}/3\) unramified outside \(\mathcal Q\) are homomorphisms \(z \colon (\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak n)^\times \to \mathbb{Z}/3\); we call them classes ramified at \(\mathcal Q\). For \(q \in \mathcal Q\) let \(\varpi_q\) be the least primitive root modulo \(q\), and let \(s_q \in (\mathbb{Z}/\mathfrak n)^\times\) be \(\equiv \varpi_q \bmod q\) and \(\equiv 1\) modulo the other primes of \(\mathcal Q\): the image of a tame generator at \(q\). For a prime \(p \nmid \mathfrak n\), \(z(p)\) is the value at an arithmetic Frobenius element at \(p\). For \(p \ne q\) let \(\mathrm{kum}_p(q) \in \mathbb{Z}/3\) be \(i \bmod 3\) where \(p \equiv \varpi_q^i \pmod q\). This is the value of the Kummer class of \(p\) (Problem 81.3.3) at an arithmetic Frobenius element at \(q\), when \(\mu_3(\mathbb F_q)\) is identified with \(\mathbb{Z}/3\) by sending \(\varpi_q^{(q-1)/3}\) to \(1\).

Problem 84.2.1. Every class \(z\) ramified at \(\mathcal Q\) satisfies \(z(p) = \sum_{q \in \mathcal Q} \mathrm{kum}_p(q)\,z(s_q)\) for every prime \(p \nmid \mathfrak n\), and \(\mathrm{kum}_p(q) = 0\) if and only if \(p^{(q-1)/3} \equiv 1 \pmod q\).

Problem 84.2.2. The least primitive roots modulo \(7, 13, 61, 73\) are \(3, 2, 2, 5\); \(3^{(q-1)/3} \bmod q\) is \(2, 3, 1, 1\), and \(\mathrm{kum}_3(q) = 1, 1, 0, 0\), for \(q = 7, 13, 61, 73\); \(7^{20} \equiv 47 \pmod{61}\) and \(7^{24} \equiv 1 \pmod{73}\), with \(\mathrm{kum}_7(61) = 1\) and \(\mathrm{kum}_7(73) = 0\).

Problem 84.2.3. Let \(\mathcal Q = \{7, 61\}\) and let \(z\) be a class ramified at \(\mathcal Q\) with \(z(3) = 0\). Then \(z(s_7) = 0\). So \(z\) is a class ramified at \(\{61\}\); if moreover \(z(7) = 0\), then \(z(s_{61}) = 0\) and \(z = 0\).

Problem 84.2.4. For \(\mathcal Q = \{7, 13\}\), the class with \((z(s_7), z(s_{13})) = (1, 2)\) has \(z(3) = 0\) and \(z(s_7) \ne 0\). For \(\mathcal Q = \{7, 73\}\), the class with \((z(s_7), z(s_{73})) = (0, 1)\) is unramified at \(7\), has \(z(3) = z(7) = 0\), and is not zero.

The model has one infinite place and trivial action. In the proof the base field is \(E\), the classes are even classes of exponent \(e\), and the relation used at the \(j\)-th place comes from a Kummer map through a reciprocity law that uses the local condition above \(\ell\) and the Frobenius conditions at the earlier places of the same exponent; twice the diagonal Kummer value divides \(\ell^{yh}\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu\).

§84.3. Choosing the places

Let \(G_\nu := \ker(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\bmod \ell^\nu) \subseteq G_E\). A map \(\mathrm{kum} \colon G_E \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu\) has Kummer type \(e\) if it is locally constant and, on \(G_\nu\), a homomorphism with \(\mathrm{kum}(a^c) = -\mathrm{kum}(a)\) and \(\mathrm{kum}(gag^{-1}) = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(g)^{e+1}\mathrm{kum}(a)\) for \(g \in G_E\).

Problem 84.3.1. Let \(\nu\) be a positive integer and let \(\psi_1, \dots, \psi_n \colon G_0 \to \mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu\) be homomorphisms on a group \(G_0\) whose reductions modulo \(\ell\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb F_\ell\). Then \(a \mapsto (\psi_i(a))_i\) maps \(G_0\) onto \((\mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu)^n\).

Problem 84.3.2. Let \(t_0\) be a nonnegative integer, \(s_0\) a subset of \(\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:0\le i<t_0\}\), \(\gamma_1 \in G_E\), \(\alpha := \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma_1) \bmod \ell^\nu\), and \(u \in G_\nu\). Some \(u' \in G_\nu\) has \(\mathrm{kum}(u') = \prod_{e' \in s_0}(\alpha^d - \alpha^{e'})\,\mathrm{kum}(u)\) for every \(d \ge 0\) and every \(\mathrm{kum}\) of Kummer type \(d\).

Problem 84.3.3. Let \(\delta, g \in \ker\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\), \(u \in G_\nu\) (the same \(\nu\) as the precision of the classes), and \(\sigma := \delta^{\ell^\nu} g\,g^c\,u\,(u^c)^{-1}\). Then \(\mathrm{kum}(\sigma) = 2\,\mathrm{kum}(u)\) for every \(\mathrm{kum}\) of Kummer type, and \(z(\sigma) = 2z(g)\) for every even class \(z\) for \(\varnothing\), of any exponent.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem 84.3.1 below, fix \(\nu > 0\). The places are chosen in order \(j \in \mathcal{K}\), each with an exponent \(e_j\) and a test point \(g \in \ker\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\). At step \(j\) the proof takes a Kummer map \(\mathrm{kum}_j\) for the earlier places of exponent \(e_j\) (defined below), with reduction on \(G_\nu\) outside the span of the reductions of the earlier maps of that exponent, and \(u \in G_\nu\) killing the earlier maps with \(\mathrm{kum}_j(u) = \prod_{e'}(\alpha^{e_j} - \alpha^{e'})\), over \(0 \le e' < t'\), \(e' \ne e_j\), where \(\alpha = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma_1) \bmod \ell^\nu\) (Problems 84.3.1, 84.3.2). With \(\delta\) given by the hypothesis on \(\mathcal W\) at \(g\,g^c u(u^c)^{-1}\) and \(\sigma\) as in Problem 84.3.3, Chebotarev’s theorem [Ne, VII (13.4)] gives a Frobenius datum in \(\mathcal W\), off \(\ell\), fixing \(\mu_{\ell^\nu}\), above a new prime of \(E^+\) outside finitely many primes to be avoided, whose Frobenius element is so close to \(\sigma\) that the values of Problem 84.3.3 hold there for the finitely many Kummer maps and for all even classes of exponent \(e_j\) for \(\varnothing\), over all coefficient rings. Problem 84.3.4. In an \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\)-module, suppose \(\sum_{i \ge j} \mathrm{kum}_j(\mathrm{Fr}_{w_i})\cdot 2z(\tilde s_{w_i}) = 0\), \(\mathrm{kum}_j(\mathrm{Fr}_{w_i}) = 0\) for \(i > j\), \(2\mathrm{kum}_j(\mathrm{Fr}_{w_j})\) divides the integer \(n\) in \(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^\nu\), and \(z(s_{w_j})\) is an integer multiple of \(z(\tilde s_{w_j})\). A Frobenius datum \((w, \tilde w, \mathrm{Fr})\) is a finite place \(w\) of \(E\) of prime norm \(q_w\), a place \(\tilde w\) of \(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) above \(w\), and \(\mathrm{Fr}\in G_E\) whose inverse is an arithmetic Frobenius element at \(\tilde w\). A set \(\mathcal W\) of data contains most data near \(g \in G_E\) if there are a neighbourhood \(\Omega_g\) of \(g\) and a set \(\mathcal Y\) of data such that every datum in \(\mathcal Y\) whose Frobenius element is in \(\Omega_g\) lies in \(\mathcal W\), and every neighbourhood of a point of \(G_E\) contains a neighbourhood \(\Omega'\) of a point such that, for all but finitely many \(w\), every datum at \(w\) whose Frobenius element is in \(\Omega'\) lies in \(\mathcal Y\).

Definition 84.3.1. Taylor–Wiles places of level \(\ell^\nu\) indexed by a finite set \(\mathcal{K}\) are Frobenius data \((w_j, \tilde w_j, \mathrm{Fr}_{w_j})_{j \in \mathcal{K}}\) such that the \(w_j\) are pairwise distinct, \(\bar w_i \ne w_j\) for all \(i, j\), and \(\ell^\nu \mid q_{w_j} - 1\). When \(w_j\) is not above \(\ell\), \(s_{w_j}\) is the distinguished tame generator at \(\tilde w_j\).

Theorem 84.3.1. Let \(E \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}\) be a CM field with a primitive root of unity of order \(5040\) and at least four infinite places, contained in \(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{N_0})\), \(N_0 > 0\).   3. for \(0 \le e < t'\), \(\prod_{i \ne e}\bigl(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma_1)^{e} - \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma_1)^{i}\bigr)\) divides \(\ell^{yh}\) in \(\mathbb{Z}_\ell\), the product over \(0 \le i < t'\); and for \(0 < e < t'\), \(4(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma_1)^e - 1)^2\) divides \(\ell^{yh}\) in every \(\mathcal{O}\);

§84.4. What the triangle buys

Let \(\mathrm{Fr}'_{w_j} := (\mathrm{Fr}_{w_j}^c)^{-1}\) and \(s'_{w_j} := (s_{w_j}^c)^{-1}\); these serve the conjugate place. Cocycles from congruences are Ribet cocycles (Definition 83.2.1): they obey the linear relations among the families \((U_\pi(g) - 1)_\pi\), \(U_\pi\) the normalized representation of a system \(\pi\) (the normalization of Definition 80.1.8). In the proof, after specializing at a character \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\), the tied rows give, in the quotient presenting the character block over \(\mathcal{O}[\lambda_j : j \in \mathcal{K}]\), the two relations \(Y(s_{w_j}) = \lambda_j Y(\mathrm{Fr}_{w_j})\) and \(Y(s'_{w_j}) = \lambda_j Y(\mathrm{Fr}'_{w_j})\), with one variable for the place and its conjugate; here \(Y(g)\) is the vector that represents evaluation at \(g\). For the length estimate the proof considers submodules of cocycles vanishing above \(\ell\) on the cyclotomic kernel in each decomposition group when \(e > 0\), and on inertia when \(e = 0\), vanishing at \(s_{w_j}\) and \(s'_{w_j}\) for every \(j\), and with \(z(\mathrm{Fr}_{w_j}) = z(\mathrm{Fr}'_{w_j})\) whenever \(e_j = e\).

Problem 84.4.1. Let the places be as in Theorem 84.3.1, \(0 < e < t'\), and let \(\mathcal Z\) be an \(\mathcal{O}\)-module of locally constant cocycles for \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) in \(\mathcal{O}/\ell^\nu\), unramified off \(\ell\), vanishing on the elements of \(\ker\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) in the decomposition groups above \(\ell\), with \((\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\gamma_1)^e - 1)z(\gamma_1^{-1}) = 0\) and \(z^+(\mathrm{Fr}_{w_j}) = 0\) whenever \(e_j = e\). Then \(z \mapsto z^c - z\) maps \(\mathcal Z\) to \(\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_\ell(E, \mathcal{O}; e, \nu)\), its kernel is killed by \(\ell^{yh}\), and \(\operatorname{len}\bigl(\mathcal Z/\mathcal Z[\ell^{yh}]\bigr) \le \operatorname{len}\mathrm{Sha}^{\mathrm{odd}}_\ell(E,\mathcal{O};e,\nu)\), where \(\mathcal Z[\ell^{yh}] = \{z \in \mathcal Z : \ell^{yh}z = 0\}\).

Definition 84.4.1. Call the indeterminates \(\lambda_j\) for \(j \in \mathcal{K}\) the first-order variables; for \(A \subseteq \mathcal{K}\), \(\lambda_A := \prod_{j \in A}\lambda_j\).

For \(0 \le e < t'\) let \(I_e := \{j : e_j = e\}\) and \(\Lambda := \mathcal{O}[\lambda_j : j \in \mathcal{K}]\). Let \(n_{\det}\) be the number of remaining coordinates of the character block, \(\mathrm{Rows}_e \subseteq \Lambda^{n_{\det}}\) the module of the tied rows after evaluation at \(\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) and elimination of the other coordinates, and \(\mathfrak a\) the ideal generated by the monomials that do not divide \(\lambda_{\mathcal{K}}\). Problem 84.4.2. Let \(\mathrm{Nm}_j, \partial_j\) (\(j \in \mathcal{K}\)) be pairwise commuting operators, and write \(\mathrm{Nm}_B\), \(\partial_B\) for products over \(B \subseteq \mathcal{K}\). Problem 84.4.4. For finitely many systems \(\pi_k\) that are not weak lifts choose \(g_k\) with \(b_k := \det(1 - U_{\pi_k}(g_k)) \ne 0\), and let \(\beta_k(\pi) := \det(1 - U_\pi(g_k))\) (in this sheet). Then \(\delta_0 := \prod_k(b_k - \beta_k)\) vanishes at every \(\pi_k\) and equals \(n_0 := \prod_k b_k\) at every weak lift. For the two classes at \(11\), over \(\mathbb{Q}\) with \(\ell = 5\), let \(g\) be an arithmetic Frobenius element at \(2\), \(U = 1 \oplus \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) on the constant function (a weak lift) and \(U = \rho_{11}\) on \(\varphi_{11}\) (Problem 81.2.1), with \(\det\rho_{11} = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}\). Let \(\beta\) also denote the operator acting on each of these lines by its value at the corresponding system. Then \(b = \det(1 - \rho_{11}(g)) = 5\), \(\beta = 3 - B(2)\), and \(b - \beta = B(2) + 2 = 5\,\mathrm{pr}\).

In Problem 84.4.4 the factor \(5\) is an \(n_0\). In the application the characteristic polynomials of the finitely many base-level systems are integral, so each \(b_k\) is a nonzero element of the valuation ring, and it costs nothing: the precision \(\nu > 0\) is chosen after the \(g_k\) and \(\mathcal{K}\), and before the places, so that \(2n'!\,(n_0\mathcal N^2)^{\#\mathcal{K}+1}\) divides \(\ell^\nu\) in the valuation ring; the operator congruence at the raised level holds modulo \(\ell^\nu/(2n'!)\), a multiple of \((n_0\mathcal N^2)^{\#\mathcal{K}+1}\), and the latter factor is cancelled on an integral vector. Status: read from the proof. So, as Definition 80.1.1 shows, the clearing bound has the odd-class term \(t\,\operatorname{val}_\ell(X_{\mathrm{odd}})\), the separation term \(\bigl((4t+1)(\Delta/2)^{22} + \Delta/2\bigr)n_\ell\), and no term for even classes.

What this sheet establishes.

In the model over \(\mathbb{Q}\) the triangle argument runs in full: a class that vanishes at \(3\) and at the Frobenius element of the first prime is unramified at both primes, and each of the two requirements on the primes is needed (Problems 84.2.3 and 84.2.4). In general the sheet proves the steps of the choice of the places that are algebra (Problems 84.3.1–84.3.5), the passage from even classes to the odd module (Problem 84.4.1), and the two formal steps of the extraction (Problems 84.4.2 and 84.4.3). Taken from the proof [GL]: Theorem 84.3.1; the relation count and the reciprocity relation behind it; the Fitting estimate for the character block; the first-order operators \(\partial_j\) at the raised level; and the choice of the precision. Theorem 27.3.2 bounds the odd module. Theorem 84.3.1 supplies the control of even classes in the character-block estimate used for the denominator bound of Theorem 80.1.1.
