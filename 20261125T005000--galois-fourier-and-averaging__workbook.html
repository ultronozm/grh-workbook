Prerequisites.

From Chapter 1, From zeros to a one-sided bound: the weight \(\lambda\), the constant \(c_{d,q}\) and the remainder \(R_{d,q}\) of equation (4), recalled below, and Problem 1.4.7. From Chapter 2, Inert primes and the choice of \(d\): Section 2.1, on the values of \(1 - \chi_K\). Cyclotomic fields and their Galois groups [Wa, Ch. 2]; infinite places of a number field as conjugation orbits of embeddings into \(\mathbb{C}\) [Ne, Ch. II, §8]; quadratic reciprocity [SeA, Ch. I, §3].

In the proof, the main inequality (Theorem 38.2.1) bounds from above a centered sum over the infinite places of class \(1\) modulo \(q_0\) in the CM field obtained by adjoining a root of unity to \(K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\). Chapter 1 needs an upper bound for the remainder of a weighted sum over the prime powers \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}\) up to \(x\). How does the sum over places give such a bound? An exact finite-sum identity gives the answer. In the summand indexed by \(n\), the weight at the translated place sets the cutoff. For an admissible datum the centered total over places of class \(1\) modulo \(q_0\) equals a positive multiple of \(R_{q_0}(x)\) plus two terms at \(x_1\) (Theorem 3.2.1).

Throughout, \(d \equiv 3 \pmod 4\) is a prime, \(K := \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})\), and \(\chi_K(n) := \bigl(\frac nd\bigr)\) is the Legendre symbol (the \(\chi_d\) of Chapter 1), so \(\chi_K(d) = 0\). For an integer \(q \ge 1\) prime to \(d\) we write, as in Chapter 1, \[\lambda_q(n) := \begin{cases} \Lambda(n)\bigl(1 - \chi_K(n)\bigr), & n \equiv 1 \pmod q, \\ 0, & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases} \qquad R_q(z) := \sum_{1 \le n \le z} \lambda_q(n) \Bigl(\frac1n - \frac1z\Bigr) - \frac{\log z}{\varphi(q)} - c_q\] for real \(z > 0\), where \(c_q := c_{d,q}\) and \(R_q := R_{d,q}\) are the constant and the remainder of equation (4), and \(\lambda_q\) is the weight \(\lambda\) there for the modulus \(q\); we drop \(d\), which is fixed. The value of \(c_q\) plays no role in the identities of this chapter. We write \(u_+ := \max(u, 0)\) for real \(u\).

§3.1. The host field and its places Tag 22ZU

Let \(q_0 \ge 3\) and let \(q_1\) be a multiple of \(q_0\) such that every prime factor of \(q_1\) divides \(q_0\), and \(d \nmid q_1\). Let \(E := K(\zeta)\) with \(\zeta\) a primitive \(q_1\)-th root of unity, regarded as a subfield of \(\mathbb{C}\). For \(g \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) let \(e(g) \in (\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times\) be the class with \(g(\zeta) = \zeta^{e(g)}\).

Tag 22ZV

Problem 3.1.1. The map \(e \colon \operatorname{Gal}(E/K) \to (\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times\) is an isomorphism, and \([E : \mathbb{Q}] = 2\varphi(q_1)\).

Used in: Chapter 36

Let \(\Phi\) be the set of infinite places of \(E\) and \(f := \lvert \Phi \rvert\). For \(g \in \operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\) let \(\langle g \rangle \in \Phi\) be the place of the embedding \(g \colon E \to \mathbb{C}\).

Tag 22ZW

Problem 3.1.2. Every infinite place of \(E\) is complex, and \(g \mapsto \langle g \rangle\) is a bijection \(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K) \to \Phi\). Hence \(f = \varphi(q_1)\).

Used in: Chapter 36

We make \(\Phi\) a group through \(g \mapsto \langle g \rangle\), and identify it with \((\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times\) through \(e\): the place \(\sigma \in \Phi\) has label \(v \in (\mathbb{Z}/q_1\mathbb{Z})^\times\) if \(\sigma = \langle g \rangle\) with \(e(g) = v\). The place with label \(1\) is the identity. For \(\nu \in \mathbb{Z}\) let \([\nu] \in \Phi\) be the place with label \(\nu \bmod q_1\) if \(\gcd(\nu, q_1) = 1\), and the identity place otherwise. Let \(\mathrm{cl} \colon \Phi \to (\mathbb{Z}/q_0\mathbb{Z})^\times\) be the label reduced modulo \(q_0\), and let \[\Phi_a := \mathrm{cl}^{-1}(1) ,\] the \(a\)-places. Fix a set \(\Phi_b \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{-1}(-1)\) of \(b\)-places. We write \(\#\) for the number of elements.

Tag 22ZX

Problem 3.1.3. \(\#\Phi_a = q_1/q_0\) and \(f = \#\Phi_a\, \varphi(q_0)\).

Used in: Chapter 4

Partners. For an integer \(n \ge 1\) and a place \(\tau \in \Phi\), the partner of \(\tau\) for \(n\) is the place \([-n]\tau\), the product of \([-n]\) and \(\tau\) in the group \(\Phi\). For \(n\) prime to \(q_1\), \[\text{label of } [-n]\tau \;=\; -n \cdot (\text{label of } \tau) \pmod{q_1} ,\] and for \(n\) not prime to \(q_1\) the partner is \(\tau\) itself. This notation is used throughout the later chapters, mostly for \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) and \(n = p^e\) a prime power.

The minus sign comes from choosing the embedding over \(K\) after applying Frobenius at an inert prime (Problem 3.1.4).

Tag 23ER

Problem 3.1.4. Let \(p\) be an odd prime inert in \(K\) with \(p \nmid q_1\), and let \(A := \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}[\zeta,\sqrt{-d}]\). Modulo \(pA\), the \(p\)-th power map sends \(\zeta\) to \(\zeta^p\) and \(\sqrt{-d}\) to \(-\sqrt{-d}\). For \(\tau \in \Phi\) write \(g_\tau\) for its embedding over \(K\). For odd \(h \ge 1\) and \(u \in A\), \[\bigl(g_\tau(u)\bigr)^{p^h} \equiv \overline{g_{[-p^h]\tau}(u)} \pmod{pA}.\]

Tag 22ZY

Problem 3.1.5. For \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) and every integer \(n \ge 1\), \(\mathrm{cl}\bigl([-n]\tau\bigr) = -1\) if and only if \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}\).

Tag 22ZZ

Problem 3.1.6. For every integer \(n \ge 1\), the number of \(\tau \in \Phi_a\) with \([-n]\tau \in \Phi_b\) is \(\#\Phi_b\) if \(n \equiv 1 \pmod{q_0}\), and \(0\) otherwise.

When \(\gcd(n, q_1) > 1\), our convention gives \([-n]\tau = \tau\). Under the hypotheses of the next problem such \(n\) have coefficient \(\lambda_1(n) = 0\) in the prime sums below, so the convention does not affect them.

Tag 2322

Problem 3.1.7. (i) If \(16 \mid q_0\), \(q_0 \mid 2(d + 1)\) and \(q_0 \nmid d + 1\), then \(d \equiv 7 \pmod 8\) and \(\chi_K(p) = 1\) for every prime \(p \mid q_0\). (ii) In that case \(\lambda_1(n) = 0\) for every \(n \ge 1\) with \(\gcd(n, q_1) > 1\).

Used in: Chapter 4

The toy example is \(q_0 = 4\), \(q_1 = 16\), \(d = 7\), so that \(f = 8\), \(\Phi_a\) consists of the places with labels \(1, 5, 9, 13\), and \(\Phi_b\) consists of the place with label \(15\), so \(\#\Phi_a = 4\) and \(\#\Phi_b = 1\). It does not satisfy the hypotheses of Problem 3.1.7(i), but \(\chi_K(2) = (2/7) = 1\), and the conclusion of Problem 3.1.7(ii) holds.

Tag 2323

Problem 3.1.8. In the toy example, the primes \(p \le 61\) with \(\lambda_1(p) \ne 0\) are \(7\) and the primes inert in \(K\), and the label of \([-p]\) is as follows. \[\begin{array}{c|cccccccc} \text{label} & 1 & 3 & 5 & 7 & 9 & 11 & 13 & 15 \\ \hline p & 31,\, 47 & 13,\, 61 & 59 & 41 & 7 & 5 & 3,\, 19 & 17 \end{array}\] The other \(n \le 61\) with \(\lambda_1(n) \ne 0\) are \(27\) and \(49\), with \(\lambda_1(27) = 2\log 3\) and \(\lambda_1(49) = \log 7\).

§3.2. Two weights and the prime sum Tag 2324

Let \(x < x_1\) and \(b\) be positive integers with \(x_1 \mid b\), and let \(a := xb\). Define the weight of a place \(\sigma\) by \[\kappa(\sigma) := \begin{cases} a/x = b, & \sigma \in \Phi_b, \\ a/x_1, & \sigma \notin \Phi_b, \end{cases}\] an integer; the \(b\)-places carry the larger weight. For real \(y\), define the prime sum at a place \(\sigma\) by \[s_{\mathrm{fin}}(y, \sigma) := \sum_{n = 1}^{a} \lambda_1(n) \Bigl( \frac yn - \kappa\bigl([-n]\sigma\bigr) \Bigr)_+ .\] For real \(z > 0\) and \(n \ge 1\) let \(H_z(n) := (a/n - a/z)_+\).

Tag 2325

Problem 3.2.1. \(H_z(n) = a(1/n - 1/z)\) for \(n < z\), and \(H_z(n) = 0\) for \(n \ge z\). Hence for \(1 \le n \le a\) the \(n\)-th term of \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \sigma)\) is \(\lambda_1(n) H_x(n)\) if \([-n]\sigma \in \Phi_b\), and \(\lambda_1(n) H_{x_1}(n)\) otherwise.

Tag 2326

Problem 3.2.2. Let \(\sigma \in \Phi\). (i) For \(0 \le y \le a\) and \(x_1 \le n \le a\), the \(n\)-th term of \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(y, \sigma)\) is \(0\). (ii) For \(x \le n < x_1\), the \(n\)-th term of \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \sigma)\) is \(0\) if \([-n]\sigma \in \Phi_b\), and \(\lambda_1(n)\, a (1/n - 1/x_1)\) otherwise, which is positive unless \(\lambda_1(n) = 0\).

The first step towards the total over the \(a\)-places is a ramp sum in a progression.

Tag 2327

Problem 3.2.3. For integers \(q \ge 1\) prime to \(d\), \(A_0 \ge 1\), and real \(0 < z \le A_0\), \[\sum_{n = 1}^{A_0} \lambda_q(n) \Bigl( \frac{A_0}{n} - \frac{A_0}{z} \Bigr)_+ = A_0 \Bigl( R_q(z) + \frac{\log z}{\varphi(q)} + c_q \Bigr) .\] For \(d = 7\), \(q = 4\), \(A_0 = 60\), \(z = 20\), both sides equal \(18 \log 5 + \frac{42}{13} \log 13 + \frac{18}{17} \log 17 = 40.2565\ldots\).

In the average over the \(a\)-places, the place \([-n]\tau\) selects the residue class of \(n\).

Tag 2389

Problem 3.2.4. Let \(q_0 = 10\), \(q_1 = 20\), \(x = 2\), \(x_1 = 3\), \(b = 6\) and \(\Phi_b = \{[9]\}\), so that \(a = 12\), \(\Phi_a = \{[1], [11]\}\), and \(\kappa\) is \(6\) at \([9]\) and \(4\) elsewhere. For \(1 \le n \le 12\) and \(\tau \in \Phi_a\), the term \((60/n - \kappa([-n]\tau))_+\) differs from \((60/n - 4)_+\) only for \(n \in \{1, 11\}\), and then for one \(\tau\) only: at \(n = 1\) it is \(54\) instead of \(56\) (for \(\tau = [11]\)), at \(n = 11\) it is \(0\) instead of \(16/11\) (for \(\tau = [1]\)). At \(n = 3\) both terms are \(16\).

Tag 2328

Problem 3.2.5. For every integer \(n \ge 1\), \[\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} \lambda_1(n) \Bigl( \frac an - \kappa\bigl([-n]\tau\bigr) \Bigr)_+ = \#\Phi_a\, \lambda_1(n) H_{x_1}(n) + \#\Phi_b\, \lambda_{q_0}(n) \bigl( H_x(n) - H_{x_1}(n) \bigr) .\]

Tag 2329

Problem 3.2.6. \[\sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) = a \Bigl[ \#\Phi_b \bigl( R_{q_0}(x) - R_{q_0}(x_1) \bigr) + \#\Phi_a R_1(x_1) \Bigr] + a \Bigl[ \#\Phi_a (\log x_1 + c_1) + \#\Phi_b\, \frac{\log (x/x_1)}{\varphi(q_0)} \Bigr] .\]

Used in: Problem 3.2.7; Chapter Overview
Tag 232A

Problem 3.2.7. In the toy example with \(x = 20\), \(x_1 = 60\), \(b = 960\), so that \(a = 19200\) and the weights are \(960\) and \(320\), the four prime sums \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau)\) at the places \(\tau\) with labels \(1, 5, 9, 13\) rounded to the nearest hundredth, are \(45108.01\), \(45591.18\), \(47772.92\), \(46814.23\). Their total and the right side of Problem 3.2.6 both round to \(185286.35\).

The expected value uses the mean of the logarithms of the weights over all places. We define the expected value \[s_{\mathrm{main}}:= a \Bigl( \log a - \frac1f \sum_{\sigma \in \Phi} \log \kappa(\sigma) + c_1 \Bigr) .\]

Tag 232B

Problem 3.2.8. \(\sum_{\sigma \in \Phi} \log \kappa(\sigma) = f \log (a/x_1) - \#\Phi_b \log (x/x_1)\). In the toy example both sides equal \(47.24518\ldots\).

Tag 232C

Problem 3.2.9. \(\#\Phi_a\, s_{\mathrm{main}}= a \bigl[ \#\Phi_a (\log x_1 + c_1) + \#\Phi_b \log(x/x_1)/\varphi(q_0) \bigr]\).

Define the total remainder by \(\mathrm{rem}:= \sum_{\tau \in \Phi_a} \bigl( s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau) - s_{\mathrm{main}}\bigr)\), so that the mean of \(s_{\mathrm{fin}}(a, \tau)\) over \(\Phi_a\) is \(s_{\mathrm{main}}+ \mathrm{rem}/\#\Phi_a\). Subtracting the identity of Problem 3.2.9 from that of Problem 3.2.6 gives \[\begin{equation} \label{eq:rem}\tag{1} \mathrm{rem}= a \Bigl[ \#\Phi_b \bigl( R_{q_0}(x) - R_{q_0}(x_1) \bigr) + \#\Phi_a R_1(x_1) \Bigr] . \end{equation}\] In the toy example, \(\mathrm{rem}+ 76800\, c_1 = -118612.63\ldots\).

In the proof the parameters \((q_0, d)\) are a positive multiple \(q_0\) of \(18480\) and a prime \(d\) with \(q_0 \mid 2(d + 1)\) and \(q_0 \nmid d + 1\) (Definition 36.1.1 of Chapter 36). An admissible datum for them is a datum satisfying all the conditions of Definition 36.3.1 there. We record the components used here. The scales \(x\), \(x_1\), \(b\) are positive integers. The number \(m\) of theta lifts in each chain is a power of \(2\) with \(\sqrt x \le m \le 2\sqrt x\), and the tower exponent is \(s = 22 \log_2 m\) (logarithm to base \(2\)). With \(e_s := \lfloor \log_3 s \rfloor\) (logarithm to base \(3\)), let \(q_1 := q_0\,3^{e_s+5}2^s\); the field \(E\), its embedding into \(\mathbb{C}\), the primitive root \(\zeta\) and the labels of the places are as in §3.1. We have \(q_1 x_1 \mid b\). The datum supplies \(\Phi_b \subseteq \mathrm{cl}^{-1}(-1)\) with \(q_1/q_0 \le \#\Phi_b \log b\). Since \(x_1 \mid b\), the prescribed weight \(\lfloor xb/x_1\rfloor\) outside \(\Phi_b\) equals \(a/x_1\).

§3.3. The remainder at the first scale Tag 232E

Chapter 1 needs an upper bound for \(R_{q_0}(x)\). When \(\Phi_b\) is nonempty, equation (1) isolates \(R_{q_0}(x)\) at the price of two terms at \(x_1\).

Tag 232F

Problem 3.3.1. If \(\#\Phi_b > 0\), then \(R_{q_0}(x) = \dfrac{\mathrm{rem}}{a\,\#\Phi_b} + R_{q_0}(x_1) - \dfrac{\#\Phi_a}{\#\Phi_b} R_1(x_1)\).

Used in: Chapter 38

For \(\#\Phi_b>0\), the coefficient of \(R_1(x_1)\) has magnitude \(\#\Phi_a/\#\Phi_b\), the reciprocal of the proportion of the \(-1\) fibre occupied by \(\Phi_b\); this is why \(\Phi_b\) must be dense.

Tag 232G

Problem 3.3.2. If \(b > 1\) and \(\#\Phi_a \le \#\Phi_b \log b\), then \(\#\Phi_b > 0\) and \[R_{q_0}(x) \le \frac{\mathrm{rem}}{a\,\#\Phi_b} + \lvert R_{q_0}(x_1) \rvert + \log b \, \lvert R_1(x_1) \rvert .\] In the toy example \(\#\Phi_a/\#\Phi_b = 4 \le \log 960 = 6.8669\ldots\).

Used in: Chapter 4

Write \(\theta := \#\Phi_b/\#\Phi_a\) in this section. The main inequality \(m \cdot \mathrm{rem}\le fa(\log b)^5\) bounds the mean excess over the \(a\)-places in units of \(a\), with a saving of \(1/m\). Under the density hypothesis of Problem 3.3.2, isolating \(R_{q_0}(x)\) costs a factor \(1/\theta \le \log b\), which gives the sixth logarithm in the bound at \(x\).

Tag 23EQ

Problem 3.3.3. For a positive integer \(m\), the main inequality implies \[R_1(x_1) + \theta \bigl(R_{q_0}(x) - R_{q_0}(x_1)\bigr) \le \frac{\varphi(q_0)(\log b)^5}{m}.\]
